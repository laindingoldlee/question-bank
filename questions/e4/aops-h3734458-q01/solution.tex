Reflection of $H$ across $AC$ is $F$: the reflected point lies on $BD$, and its angles show it lies on the circumcircle. Similarly reflection across $AB$ sends $H$ to $G$. Because both reflecting lines pass through $A$, we have $AH=AF=AG$. Thus $A$ is the center of the circle through $F,H,G$.

Scale coordinates to $B=(-1,0),C=(1,0),A=(u,v)$ with $v>0$. The assumption $AB<AC$ implies $u<0$. The orthocenter is
\[
H=(u,h),\qquad h=\dfrac{1-u^2}{v},
\]
so $u^2+vh=1$. The circle through $B,C,D,E$ has diameter $BC$ and equation $x^2+y^2=1$. The circle with diameter $AH$ passes through $D,E$ and has equation
\[
x^2+y^2-2ux-(v+h)y+1=0.
\]
Subtracting their equations gives line $DE$: $2ux+(v+h)y=2$. Therefore $X=(\dfrac{1}{u},0)$.

The circumcenter is $\left(0,\dfrac{u^2+v^2-1}{2v}\right)$, so $A'=(-u,-h)=-H$. Thus line $A'H$, which contains chord $HY$, has direction vector $(u,h)$. But
\[
(A-X)\cdot(u,h)=u^2-1+vh=0.
\]
Hence $AX$ is perpendicular to chord $HY$. Since $A$ is the circle's center, $AX$ is the perpendicular bisector of $HY$, and $\boxed{XH=XY}$ follows.
