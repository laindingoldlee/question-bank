Let $u=f(2),v=f(3),w=f(5)$. Applying the product rule to $120=2^3\cdot3\cdot5$, $150=2\cdot3\cdot5^2$, and $45=3^2\cdot5$ gives $3u+v+w=25$, $u+v+2w=14$, $2v+w=5$. Solving yields $(u,v,w)=(7,1,3)$. Therefore $f(30)=u+v+w=\boxed{11}$. Such a function exists: assign these values to the three primes, zero to all other primes, and extend additively across prime factorizations.
