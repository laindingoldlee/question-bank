Let $m=y^2+y+1$; note $m\ge1$ for every integer $y$ (indeed $m=\left(y+\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2+\dfrac{3}{4}>0$). Multiply $x^2+x+1=m^2$ by $4$:
\[(2x+1)^2+3=4m^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (2m-2x-1)(2m+2x+1)=3 .\]
Both factors are integers with sum $4m>0$, so both are positive (their product is positive), and they are $1$ and $3$ in some order. Their sum is then $4=4m$, so $m=1$, and $2x+1=\pm1$, so $x=0$ or $x=-1$.

Finally $y^2+y+1=1$ gives $y(y+1)=0$, so $y=0$ or $y=-1$. All four pairs work, so the solutions are
\[\boxed{(x,y)\in\{(0,0),(0,-1),(-1,0),(-1,-1)\}} .\]
