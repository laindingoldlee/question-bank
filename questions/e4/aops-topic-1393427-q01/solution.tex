Since $1-x^2=(1-x)(1+x)$,
\[
k=\dfrac{1-x}{(1-x)(1+x)}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{1+x}},
\]
valid for all $x$ with $x\ne1$ and $x\ne-1$ (the original fraction is undefined at $x=\pm1$; at $x=1$ the simplified form would give $\dfrac{1}{2}$, but the original is $\dfrac{0}{0}$).
