In similar triangles all corresponding lengths, including the perimeters, are in the same ratio. Let the side of the first triangle be $x$, so the corresponding side of the second is $x+1$ (the second triangle is the larger one).

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(7cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(3.3,0),C=(0.9,2.0);
real k=13/11; pair o=(4.4,0);
pair A2=o+k*A,B2=o+k*B,C2=o+k*C;
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9)); draw(A2--B2--C2--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(A--B,blue+linewidth(1.4)); draw(A2--B2,blue+linewidth(1.4));
label("$x$",(A+B)/2,S,blue); label("$x+1$",(A2+B2)/2,S,blue);
label("perimeter $11t$",(A+B)/2+(0,-0.75),fontsize(8pt));
label("perimeter $13t$",(A2+B2)/2+(0,-0.75),fontsize(8pt));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Then
\[
\dfrac{x}{x+1}=\dfrac{11}{13}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad13x=11x+11\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=\dfrac{11}{2}.
\]
The sides are $\boxed{\dfrac{11}{2}=5.5\text{ m and }\dfrac{13}{2}=6.5\text{ m}}$. Check: $\dfrac{5.5}{6.5}=\dfrac{11}{13}$ and $6.5-5.5=1$.
