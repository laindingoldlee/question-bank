Let $t=\sqrt a\ge0$. Then $a\sqrt a=t^3$ and the equation becomes
\[t^3-10t-3=0 .\]
Trying small integers, $t=-3$ is a root: $-27+30-3=0$. Dividing by $t+3$,
\[t^3-10t-3=(t+3)\left(t^2-3t-1\right).\]
Since $t\ge0$, we need $t^2-3t-1=0$. Then $t\ne0$, and dividing by $t$ gives $t-\dfrac{1}{t}=3$. Squaring,
\[t^2-2+\dfrac{1}{t^2}=9,\qquad\text{so}\qquad a+\dfrac{1}{a}=t^2+\dfrac{1}{t^2}=\boxed{11}.\]
(Such an $a$ exists: $t=\dfrac{3+\sqrt{13}}{2}>0$ and $a=t^2=\dfrac{11+3\sqrt{13}}{2}$.)
