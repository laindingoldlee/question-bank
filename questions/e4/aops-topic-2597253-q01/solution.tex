Suppose such a set $P$ exists. First, $z^2\in P$ for every $z\ne0$: by (i), either $z\in P$, and then $z\cdot z\in P$ by (ii), or $-z\in P$, and then $(-z)(-z)=z^2\in P$.

Taking $z=1$ gives $1\in P$, and taking $z=i$ gives $i^2=-1\in P$. But then, for the number $1$, both $1\in P$ and $-1\in P$ hold, which contradicts (i). So no such $P$ exists. $\blacksquare$

In particular $i$ is neither positive nor negative: if $i$ were positive, then $i\cdot i=-1$ would be positive, and if $i$ were negative, then $(-i)(-i)=-1$ would be positive, while $1=1\cdot1$ is positive too. The complex numbers cannot be ordered in a way compatible with arithmetic.
