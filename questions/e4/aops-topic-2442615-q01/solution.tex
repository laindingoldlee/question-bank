\textbf{(a)} Let the distance from $A$ to $B$ be $d$. The trip takes $\dfrac dk+\dfrac dy$ hours for a distance of $2d$, so the average speed is
\[\dfrac{2d}{\dfrac dk+\dfrac dy}=\boxed{\dfrac{2ky}{k+y}},\]
the harmonic mean of $k$ and $y$ (not their ordinary mean).

\textbf{(b)} For $k=60$ and $y=30$, the true average speed is $\dfrac{2\cdot60\cdot30}{90}=40$, while the formula gives $10+60=70$. So the conjecture is false in general.

When is it right?
\begin{itemize}
\item If $y\ge k$, the formula gives $\dfrac{y-k}{3}+k=\dfrac{y+2k}{3}$, and
\[\dfrac{y+2k}{3}=\dfrac{2ky}{k+y}\iff(y+2k)(y+k)=6ky\iff y^2-3ky+2k^2=0\iff(y-k)(y-2k)=0.\]
\item If $y<k$, the formula gives $\dfrac{4k-y}{3}$, and
\[\dfrac{4k-y}{3}=\dfrac{2ky}{k+y}\iff(4k-y)(k+y)=6ky\iff4k^2-3ky-y^2=0\iff(4k+y)(k-y)=0,\]
which is impossible since $4k+y>0$ and $k-y>0$.
\end{itemize}
So the formula is correct exactly when $\boxed{y=k\text{ or }y=2k}$. (For example $k=30$, $y=60$ gives $40$ both ways, which may be why the formula looked right.)
