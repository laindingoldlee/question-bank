\textbf{(a)} $n=9k+4$ for some integer $k$, so $n=3(3k+1)+1\equiv1\pmod3$.

\textbf{(b)} $n=8m+5$ for some integer $m$, so $n=4(2m+1)+1\equiv1\pmod4$. Together with (a) and $n\equiv1\pmod5$, the number $n-1$ is divisible by $3$, $4$ and $5$. These are pairwise coprime, so $n-1$ is divisible by $3\cdot4\cdot5=60$, that is, $n\equiv1\pmod{60}$. $\blacksquare$

(The three conditions together determine $n$ modulo $360$: $n\equiv301\pmod{360}$, and indeed $301=5\cdot60+1$.)
