Let $c=1188$ km per hour be the speed of sound, $u=V_1$ the soldier's speed and $v$ the terrorist's speed (any consistent units of length and time may be used, since only ratios of speeds and of times will matter). Measure positions along the road in the direction of motion, and put the soldier at position $0$ at the first shot (time $0$), with the terrorist at position $D>0$.

The first sound travels forward from $0$ and reaches the terrorist at the time $t_1$ with $ct_1=D+vt_1$, so $t_1=\dfrac{D}{c-v}$.

The second shot is fired at time $T$ (that is, $335$ seconds later) from position $uT$. Its sound reaches the terrorist at the time $t_2$ with $c(t_2-T)=D+vt_2-uT$, so
\[
t_2=\dfrac{D+(c-u)T}{c-v}\qquad\text{and}\qquad t_2-t_1=\dfrac{(c-u)T}{c-v}.
\]
This interval is $330$ seconds while $T$ is $335$ seconds, so
\[
\dfrac{c-u}{c-v}=\dfrac{330}{335}=\dfrac{66}{67}<1 .
\]
Hence $c-u<c-v$, i.e.\ $u>v$: \textbf{the soldier is faster}. Clearing denominators, $67(c-u)=66(c-v)$, which rearranges to $66(u-v)=c-u$. Therefore
\[
u-v=\dfrac{c-u}{66}=\boxed{\dfrac{1188-V_1}{66}\text{ km per hour}=18-\dfrac{V_1}{66}\text{ km per hour}}.
\]
The soldier is faster by a little less than $18$ km per hour ($5$ metres per second). The often-quoted answer ``$18$ km per hour'' is only the approximation for a soldier whose speed is negligible compared with that of sound. (For the terrorist to be moving forward we need $v=V_1-\dfrac{1188-V_1}{66}>0$, i.e.\ $V_1>\dfrac{1188}{67}\approx17.7$ km per hour.)
