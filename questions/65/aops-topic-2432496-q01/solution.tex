Since $\left(a^b\right)^c=a^{bc}$, the equation is $a^{\left(b^c\right)}=a^{bc}$, where both exponents are positive integers.

\textbf{Case $a\in\{0,1\}$.} Both sides are $0$ (respectively $1$), so every $b,c$ works.

\textbf{Case $a=-1$.} $(-1)^u=(-1)^v$ exactly when $u$ and $v$ have the same parity. Here $b^c$ has the parity of $b$ (as $c\ge1$), and $bc$ is even when $b$ or $c$ is even. They have the same parity exactly when $b$ is even or $c$ is odd.

\textbf{Case $|a|\ge2$.} Then $|a|^u=|a|^v$ forces $u=v$, so we need $b^c=bc$, i.e. $b^{c-1}=c$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $c=1$: $b^0=1$, true for every $b$.
\item $c\ge2$ and $b=1$: $1=c$, impossible.
\item $c\ge2$ and $b=2$: $2^{c-1}=c$ holds for $c=2$, and for $c\ge3$ we have $2^{c-1}>c$ (true for $c=3$ and the left side doubles while the right side grows by $1$).
\item $c\ge2$ and $b\ge3$: $b^{c-1}\ge3^{c-1}>c$.
\end{itemize}
So $c=1$, or $(b,c)=(2,2)$.

\textbf{Answer.} The solutions are exactly the triples with
\[\boxed{c=1;\quad\text{or }(b,c)=(2,2);\quad\text{or }a\in\{0,1\};\quad\text{or }a=-1\text{ with }b\text{ even or }c\text{ odd}}\]
(with $a$ any integer in the first two families and $b,c$ any positive integers in the last two). For example $a^{\left(2^2\right)}=a^4=\left(a^2\right)^2$.
