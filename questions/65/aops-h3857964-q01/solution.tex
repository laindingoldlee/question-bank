A linear equation in one variable simplifies to $mb+c=0$ with constants $m\ne0$ and $c$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $4b+6=14$ simplifies to $4b-8=0$, so it is linear and $\boxed{b=2}$.
\item $b^2+6=14$ simplifies to $b^2-8=0$, which is quadratic, not linear. The squared term does not cancel.
\item Expanding and cancelling gives $(b^2+2b+1)-b^2=7$, or $2b-6=0$. Despite the squares in its original form, this is linear and $\boxed{b=3}$.
\end{enumerate}
