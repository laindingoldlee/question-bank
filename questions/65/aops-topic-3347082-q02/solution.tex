Let the similarity scale be $k>0$. A similarity maps a side line to its corresponding side line and preserves perpendicularity, so it maps the altitude segment to the corresponding altitude segment, scaling its length by $k$. Thus the altitude ratio is $k$. Since triangle area is half base times altitude, both factors scale by $k$, and the area ratio is $k^2$. The same reasoning applies when an altitude foot lies outside its side segment.
\begin{asy}
import geometry; usepackage("amsmath"); size(240);
pair A=(1,2),B=(0,0),C=(3,0),H=(1,0),D=(6,3),Epoint=(4.5,0),F=(9,0),K=(6,0);draw(A--B--C--cycle^^D--Epoint--F--cycle);draw(A--H^^D--K,dashed);label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,SW);label("$C$",C,SE);label("$H$",H,S);label("$D$",D,N);label("$E$",Epoint,SW);label("$F$",F,SE);label("$K$",K,S);
\end{asy}

An obtuse configuration is illustrated separately; the altitude feet lie on the extended side lines.
\begin{asy}
import geometry; usepackage("amsmath"); size(240);
pair A=(-1,2),B=(0,0),C=(3,0),H=(-1,0),D=(5,3),Epoint=(6.5,0),F=(11,0),K=(5,0);
draw(A--B--C--cycle^^D--Epoint--F--cycle);
draw(H--B^^K--Epoint,dashed);
draw(A--H^^D--K,dashed+blue);
label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,S);label("$C$",C,SE);label("$H$",H,SW);
label("$D$",D,N);label("$E$",Epoint,S);label("$F$",F,SE);label("$K$",K,SW);
\end{asy}
