Write $y=\dfrac{ax+b}{cx+d}$ and solve for $x$:
\[
y(cx+d)=ax+b\ \Longrightarrow\ cxy-ax=b-dy\ \Longrightarrow\ x(cy-a)=b-dy\ \Longrightarrow\ x=\dfrac{dy-b}{a-cy}.
\]
Swapping the names of the variables,
\[
\boxed{f^{-1}(x)=\dfrac{dx-b}{-cx+a}} .
\]
The condition $ad-bc\ne0$ is needed: if $ad=bc$, then $f$ is constant (where defined) and has no inverse. If $c\ne0$, the inverse is defined for $x\ne\dfrac {a}{c}$, the one value that $f$ never takes. (Note the pattern: swap $a$ and $d$ and change the signs of $b$ and $c$.)
