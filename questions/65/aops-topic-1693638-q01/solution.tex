The quotient $\dfrac{\text{mass of copper}}{\text{mass of alloy}}=\dfrac{3.5}{4.5}$ is the fraction of the alloy (by mass) that is copper: it tells how many grams of copper there are in each gram of alloy. Since $\dfrac{3.5}{4.5}=\dfrac{35}{45}=\dfrac{7}{9}$, the alloy is $\boxed{\dfrac{7}{9}\approx77.8\%}$ copper (and $\dfrac{2}{9}\approx22.2\%$ zinc). For example, $18$ g of the same alloy contains $\dfrac{7}{9}\cdot18=14$ g of copper.
