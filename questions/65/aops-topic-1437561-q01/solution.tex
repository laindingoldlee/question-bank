Take a collection with sum $k$ and the largest product.
\begin{itemize}
\item No part is $1$ (if $k\ge2$): merging a $1$ with another part $a$ replaces the factor $a$ by $a+1$, a larger product.
\item No part is $5$ or more: replacing $a\ge5$ by $2$ and $a-2$ gives $2(a-2)=2a-4>a$.
\item A $4$ can be replaced by $2+2$ without changing the product, so assume all parts are $2$ or $3$.
\item There are at most two $2$'s: three $2$'s can be replaced by two $3$'s, and $3^2=9>8=2^3$.
\end{itemize}
So the best product uses as many $3$'s as possible with at most two $2$'s:
\[
\text{max product}=\begin{cases}3^{m}, & k=3m,\\ 4\cdot3^{m-1}, & k=3m+1\ (m\ge1),\\ 2\cdot3^{m}, & k=3m+2\end{cases}
\]
(for $k=4$ this gives $4$, from $2+2$ or $4$). For $k=25\equiv1\pmod3$ we use seven $3$'s and two $2$'s: the maximum product is $4\cdot3^7=\boxed{8748}$.
