(a) There are $c+1$ terms but only $c$ residue classes, so the pigeonhole principle applies. (b) Choose $0\le i<j\le c$ with $f^i(a)\equiv f^j(a)$. The additional hypothesis allows repeated application of $f$, giving $f^{i+t}(a)\equiv f^{j+t}(a)$ for every $t\ge0$. Thus the sequence from index $i$ repeats with period $j-i$. Without that hypothesis, a repeated residue alone need not force identical future residues.
