Let $a_k$ be the number of floors of the $k$th house. For $2\le k\le134$ we have $a_k=a_{k-1}a_{k+1}-1$, so (all $a_k$ are positive)
\[a_{k+1}=\dfrac{a_k+1}{a_{k-1}}\qquad(2\le k\le134).\]
Let $a_1=2$ and $a_2=b$. Then
\begin{align*}
a_3&=\dfrac{b+1}{2},\\
a_4&=\dfrac{a_3+1}{a_2}=\dfrac{\dfrac{b+3}{2}}{b}=\dfrac{b+3}{2b},\\
a_5&=\dfrac{a_4+1}{a_3}=\dfrac{\dfrac{3b+3}{2b}}{\dfrac{b+1}{2}}=\dfrac{3}{b},\\
a_6&=\dfrac{a_5+1}{a_4}=\dfrac{\dfrac{b+3}{b}}{\dfrac{b+3}{2b}}=2,\\
a_7&=\dfrac{a_6+1}{a_5}=\dfrac{3}{\dfrac{3}{b}}=b .
\end{align*}
Since $a_6=a_1$ and $a_7=a_2$, and each term is determined by the two before it, the sequence repeats with period $5$.

Now $135=5\cdot27$, so $a_{135}=a_5=\dfrac{3}{b}$. As $a_{135}=3$, we get $b=1$, and the floors are
\[2,\ 1,\ 1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 2,\ 1,\ 1,\ 2,\ 3,\ \dots\]
(Check: $1=2\cdot1-1$, $1=1\cdot2-1$, $2=1\cdot3-1$, $3=2\cdot2-1$, $2=3\cdot1-1$.) Since $68=5\cdot13+3$, the middle house has $a_{68}=a_3=\boxed{1}$ floor.
