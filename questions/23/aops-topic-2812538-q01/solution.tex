Only sums that are at most $\max S$ matter, since larger sums lie outside $[\min S,\max S]$.

Size $1$ ($6$ sets): $T$ is empty, so all are open. Size $2$ ($15$ sets): the single sum $a+b$ exceeds $b$, so nothing in the range is hit; all are open.

Size $3$, $S=\{a<b<c\}$ ($20$ sets): $a+c$ and $b+c$ exceed $c$, so at most one of the at least $3$ integers in $[a,c]$ is hit; all $20$ are open.

Size $4$ ($15$ sets): checking each, the only set where more than half of $[\min S,\max S]$ is hit is $\{1,2,3,5\}$ (it hits $3,4,5$ out of $1,\dots,5$). So $14$ are open.

Size $5$ ($6$ sets): $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$ hits $3,4,5$ out of $5$; $\{1,2,3,4,6\}$ and $\{1,2,3,5,6\}$ hit $3,4,5,6$ out of $6$; these fail. $\{1,2,4,5,6\}$ hits $3,5,6$ of $6$, $\{1,3,4,5,6\}$ hits $4,5,6$ of $6$, and $\{2,3,4,5,6\}$ hits $5,6$ of $5$; these are open. So $3$ are open.

Size $6$: $\{1,\dots,6\}$ hits $3,4,5,6$ out of $6$, not open.

Total $6+15+20+14+3=\boxed{58}$, confirmed by computer enumeration.
