Substitute $x=2\tan\alpha$, $y=20\tan\beta$, $z=9\tan\gamma$ with $\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\left(0,\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$. Then $\dfrac{\sqrt{x^2+4}}{x}=\dfrac{\sec\alpha}{\tan\alpha}=\dfrac{1}{\sin\alpha}$, and similarly for $y,z$, so the first condition says
\[
\dfrac{9}{\sin\alpha}=\dfrac{11}{\sin\beta}=\dfrac{12}{\sin\gamma}. \tag{1}
\]
The second condition becomes $\tan\alpha\tan\beta\tan\gamma=\tan\alpha+\tan\beta+\tan\gamma$. Since
\[
\tan(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)=\dfrac{\tan\alpha+\tan\beta+\tan\gamma-\tan\alpha\tan\beta\tan\gamma}{1-\tan\alpha\tan\beta-\tan\beta\tan\gamma-\tan\gamma\tan\alpha},
\]
the numerator vanishes, so $\alpha+\beta+\gamma$ is a multiple of $\pi$; as it lies in $\left(0,\dfrac{3\pi}{2}\right)$, $\alpha+\beta+\gamma=\pi$ (the denominator is then nonzero, since otherwise the sum would be an odd multiple of $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$). With (1), the law of sines says that $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ are the angles $A',B',C'$ of a triangle with opposite sides $9,11,12$, and conversely those angles satisfy both conditions; the angles of a triangle with given sides are unique, so the solution is unique.

usepackage("amsmath");

defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real a=12,b2=9,c=11;
pair B=(0,0),C=(a,0);
real x=(c^2-b2^2+a^2)/(2a); pair A=(x,sqrt(c^2-x^2));
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
label("$C'$",A,N); label("$A'$",B,SW); label("$B'$",C,SE);
label("$12$",(B+C)/2,S); label("$11$",(A+B)/2,NW); label("$9$",(A+C)/2,NE);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

By the law of cosines,
\[
\cos A'=\dfrac{11^2+12^2-9^2}{2\cdot11\cdot12}=\dfrac{23}{33},\quad \cos B'=\dfrac{9^2+12^2-11^2}{2\cdot9\cdot12}=\dfrac{13}{27},\quad \cos C'=\dfrac{9^2+11^2-12^2}{2\cdot9\cdot11}=\dfrac{29}{99}.
\]
Then $\sin A'=\dfrac{\sqrt{33^2-23^2}}{33}=\dfrac{4\sqrt{35}}{33}$, $\sin B'=\dfrac{\sqrt{27^2-13^2}}{27}=\dfrac{4\sqrt{35}}{27}$, $\sin C'=\dfrac{\sqrt{99^2-29^2}}{99}=\dfrac{16\sqrt{35}}{99}$ (indeed in the ratio $9:11:12$), so
\[
x=2\cdot\dfrac{4\sqrt{35}}{23}=\dfrac{8\sqrt{35}}{23},\qquad y=20\cdot\dfrac{4\sqrt{35}}{13}=\dfrac{80\sqrt{35}}{13},\qquad z=9\cdot\dfrac{16\sqrt{35}}{29}=\dfrac{144\sqrt{35}}{29}.
\]
Hence $a+b+c+d+e+f+g=8+35+23+80+13+144+29=\boxed{332}$.
