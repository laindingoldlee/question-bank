Complete the squares:
\[
f(x)=\sqrt{(x-1)^2+1^2}+\sqrt{(x-2)^2+2^2}.
\]
So $f(x)=XA+XB$, the sum of the distances from the point $X(x,0)$ on the $x$-axis to $A(1,1)$ and $B(2,2)$.

\textbf{Minimum.} Reflect $A$ in the $x$-axis to $A'(1,-1)$. For every $X$ on the axis, $XA=XA'$, so by the triangle inequality
\[
f(x)=XA'+XB\ge A'B=\sqrt{(2-1)^2+(2+1)^2}=\sqrt{10},
\]
with equality exactly when $X$ lies on segment $A'B$. The line $A'B$ has slope $3$ and meets the axis at $P\left(\dfrac{4}{3},0\right)$. Check: $f\left(\dfrac{4}{3}\right)=\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{9}+1}+\sqrt{\dfrac{4}{9}+4}=\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{3}+\dfrac{2\sqrt{10}}{3}=\sqrt{10}$.

\textbf{No maximum.} $f(x)\ge\sqrt{(x-1)^2}=|x-1|$, which is unbounded, so $f$ takes arbitrarily large values and has no greatest value.

Hence the smallest value is $\boxed{\sqrt{10}}$ (at $x=\dfrac{4}{3}$), and $f$ has \textbf{no greatest value}.
