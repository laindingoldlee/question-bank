The primes on a die are $2,3,5$, so each roll is prime with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

Let $p$ be the probability that the player about to roll eventually wins. That player wins at once with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$; otherwise the other player becomes the one about to roll, so
\[
p=\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}(1-p)\iff p=\dfrac{2}{3} .
\]
Bobby rolls first, so he wins with probability $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and Lucy with probability $\boxed{\dfrac{1}{3}}$.

(As a series: Lucy wins on her $k$-th roll with probability $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2k}$, and $\displaystyle\sum_{k\ge1}\dfrac{1}{4^k}=\dfrac{1}{3}$.)
