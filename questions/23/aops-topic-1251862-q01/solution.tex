\emph{Brett.} If Brett's age were $2$, the youngest would have to be $1$ and he would know it. So Brett's age is at least $3$.

\emph{Jorge.} Jorge knew in advance that Brett could not deduce an age. If Jorge's age were $1$, Brett's could be $2$, and then Brett would know Jorge's age; so Jorge could not have been sure. Hence Jorge's age $j\ge2$, and then Brett's age is at least $3$ automatically, so Jorge was sure.

\emph{Jorge's answer.} $j$ is odd, so $j\ge3$; then the middle age is at least $4$ and the oldest at least $5$.

\emph{Matt.} Matt now knows all the ages. If his age $m$ were $6$ or more, the other ages could be, for example, $(3,4)$ or $(3,5)$, and he could not decide. With $m=5$ the only possibility is $j=3$ and middle age $4$.

So the sons are $\boxed{3,\ 4\text{ and }5}$ years old.
