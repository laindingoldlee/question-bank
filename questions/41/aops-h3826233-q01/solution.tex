At $x=10$, $P(10)=0$. Substituting successively $x=9,8,\ldots,0$ forces $P(9)=\cdots=P(0)=0$. Thus
$P(x)=F(x)Q(x)$, where $F(x)=\prod_{j=0}^{10}(x-j)$; this also allows $P=0$.
The identity $(x+1)F(x)=(x-10)F(x+1)$ then implies the polynomial identity $Q(x)=Q(x+1)$. A nonconstant polynomial of degree $d$ and leading coefficient $c$ has $Q(x+1)-Q(x)$ with nonzero leading term $cdx^{d-1}$, a contradiction. Therefore
\[
\boxed{P(x)=C\prod_{j=0}^{10}(x-j),\quad C\in\mathbb R}.
\]
Direct substitution verifies the family.
