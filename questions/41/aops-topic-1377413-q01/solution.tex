\textbf{Each number has its digits in decreasing order.} If a digit of one of the numbers were smaller than a digit to its right in the same number, swapping those two digits would make that number larger and leave the other number unchanged, so the product would increase. Hence in the best arrangement each number is written with its digits in decreasing order, and the arrangement is determined by which two digits form the two-digit number.

There are only $\dbinom52=10$ choices:
\[
\begin{array}{llll}
98\cdot642=62916, & 96\cdot842=80832, & 94\cdot862=81028, & 92\cdot864=79488,\\
86\cdot942=81012, & 84\cdot962=80808, & 82\cdot964=79048, & 64\cdot982=62848,\\
62\cdot984=61008, & 42\cdot986=41412. & &
\end{array}
\]
The largest product is $94\cdot862=81028$, so the two-digit number is $\boxed{94}$.

\textbf{General method.} First argue that each number is in decreasing order, which reduces the problem to choosing which digits go into which number; then compare those few candidates. A useful guide is that the two largest digits should be the leading digits (here $86\cdot942$ and $94\cdot862$ are the two best), and the remaining digits should be placed so that the two factors are as balanced as possible relative to their place values.
