A student applies the rational root theorem to $x^3+3x^2+4x-11=0$. None of the candidates $\pm1,\pm11$ is a root, but a graphing calculator shows a root at $x=1.21$, that is, $\dfrac{121}{100}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Show that the equation has no rational roots, so $\dfrac{121}{100}$ is not a root.
\item[(b)] Show that the equation has exactly one real root, and that it lies strictly between $1.2$ and $1.21$. Explain what the calculator shows.
\end{enumerate}
