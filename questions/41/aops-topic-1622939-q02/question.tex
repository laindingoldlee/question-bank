There are three positive integers. The first is a two-digit number whose two digits are equal. The second is a two-digit number with two different digits, whose units digit is the same as the digit of the first number. The third is a one-digit number equal to the tens digit of the second number. Exactly two of these three numbers are prime. In how many different ways can the three numbers be chosen?
