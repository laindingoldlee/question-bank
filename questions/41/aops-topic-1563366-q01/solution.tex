Group the terms by powers of $x$:
\[
x^2\left(25y^2+10y+1\right)+5x\left(5y^2+6y+1\right)+2y^2+6=0 .
\]
Here $25y^2+10y+1=(5y+1)^2$ and $5y^2+6y+1=(5y+1)(y+1)$. So with $u=x(5y+1)$ the equation is
\[
u^2+5(y+1)u+2y^2+6=0 .
\]
\textbf{A divisibility condition.} Since $5y+1$ divides $u$, it divides $2y^2+6=-u^2-5(y+1)u$. Then it also divides
\[
25\left(2y^2+6\right)-(5y+1)(10y-2)=50y^2+150-\left(50y^2-2\right)=152=2^3\cdot19 .
\]
The divisors of $152$ that are $\equiv1\pmod5$ are $1$, $-4$, $-19$ and $76$, so $y\in\{0,-1,-4,15\}$.

\textbf{Checking each $y$.}
\begin{itemize}
\item $y=0$: $x^2+5x+6=0$, so $x=-2$ or $x=-3$.
\item $y=-1$: $16x^2+8=0$, no real solution.
\item $y=-4$: $361x^2+285x+38=0$, i.e.\ $19x^2+15x+2=0$; the discriminant $225-152=73$ is not a square.
\item $y=15$: $5776x^2+6080x+456=0$, i.e.\ $722x^2+760x+57=0$; the discriminant $760^2-4\cdot722\cdot57=412984$ is not a square ($642^2=412164$, $643^2=413449$).
\end{itemize}
Hence the solutions are $\boxed{(x,y)=(-2,0)\text{ and }(-3,0)}$.
