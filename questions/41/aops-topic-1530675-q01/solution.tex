Factor: $6=2\cdot3$ and $600=2^3\cdot3\cdot5^2$. For each prime $p$, the exponent of $p$ in $\gcd(n,m)$ is the smaller of its exponents in $n$ and $m$, and in $\operatorname{lcm}(n,m)$ the larger.

\textbf{(a)} Every prime factor of $n$ or $m$ divides the lcm, so the primes are $\boxed{2,\ 3,\ 5}$.

\textbf{(b)} The smaller exponents are those of $6$: $\boxed{1\text{ for }2,\ 1\text{ for }3,\ 0\text{ for }5}$.

\textbf{(c)} The larger exponents are those of $600$: $\boxed{3\text{ for }2,\ 1\text{ for }3,\ 2\text{ for }5}$.

\textbf{(d)} For $p=3$ both exponents are $1$. For $p=2$ one number has exponent $1$ and the other $3$; for $p=5$ one has exponent $0$ and the other $2$. That gives $2\cdot2=4$ ordered pairs:
\[
(n,m)=(6,600),\ (600,6),\ (24,150),\ (150,24),
\]
so there are $\boxed{2}$ unordered pairs, $\{6,600\}$ and $\{24,150\}$, and $\boxed{4}$ ordered pairs.
