\textbf{(a)} Drop perpendiculars from $D$ and $C$ to $AB$. By symmetry each cuts off $\dfrac{24-16}{2}=4$ from $AB$, so the height is $h=\sqrt{8^2-4^2}=\sqrt{48}=4\sqrt3$ and
\[
[ABCD]=\dfrac{24+16}{2}\cdot4\sqrt3=\boxed{80\sqrt3}.
\]

\textbf{(b)} An isosceles trapezoid is cyclic, and its circumcircle is the circumcircle of triangle $ABC$. In the right triangle formed by $BC$, the height and the piece $4$ of $AB$, $\cos\angle B=\dfrac{4}{8}=\dfrac{1}{2}$, so $\angle ABC=60^\circ$. The diagonal is
\[
AC=\sqrt{(24-4)^2+(4\sqrt3)^2}=\sqrt{400+48}=\sqrt{448}=8\sqrt7 .
\]
By the extended law of sines in triangle $ABC$,
\[
R=\dfrac{AC}{2\sin\angle ABC}=\dfrac{8\sqrt7}{\sqrt3}=\boxed{\dfrac{8\sqrt{21}}{3}}\approx12.22 .
\]
Check with coordinates $A=(0,0)$, $B=(24,0)$, $D=(4,4\sqrt3)$, $C=(20,4\sqrt3)$: the point $O=\left(12,-\dfrac{4\sqrt3}{3}\right)$ satisfies $OA^2=144+\dfrac{16}{3}=\dfrac{448}{3}$ and $OD^2=64+\left(\dfrac{16\sqrt3}{3}\right)^2=\dfrac{448}{3}$, and by symmetry $OB=OA$, $OC=OD$. The centre lies just below $AB$, outside the trapezoid.
