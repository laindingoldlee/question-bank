Yes. There are $365^{10}$ equally likely lists of birthdays (one for each person, in order). In a list with all birthdays different, the first person's birthday can be any of $365$ days, the second's any of the remaining $364$, and so on down to $356$ for the tenth. So the probability is
\[\dfrac{365\cdot364\cdots356}{365^{10}}=\prod_{k=0}^{9}\left(1-\dfrac{k}{365}\right)\approx\boxed{0.883}.\]
(So the chance that some two of the ten share a birthday is about $0.117$.)
