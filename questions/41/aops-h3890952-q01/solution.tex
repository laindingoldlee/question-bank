Let $AB=c$. By Thales' theorem, triangles $ABM$ and $ABN$ are right-angled at $M$ and $N$. Hence
\[c^2-2AM\cdot BM=(AM-BM)^2=9,\]
\[c^2-2AN\cdot BN=(AN-BN)^2=49.\]
Subtracting gives $AM\cdot BM-AN\cdot BN=20$. The difference of the two right-triangle areas is consequently 10.

The increasing distance differences place the points in circular order $A,M,N,B$, so $P$ is inside both triangles. Their common triangle is $ABP$:
\[[ABM]=[ABP]+[AMP],\qquad [ABN]=[ABP]+[BNP].\]
Subtract to obtain $[AMP]-[BNP]=\boxed{10}$.

\end{asy}
