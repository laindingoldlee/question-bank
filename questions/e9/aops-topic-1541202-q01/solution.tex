Combine the conditions one at a time (instead of listing all three progressions).

\textbf{Mod $13$ and mod $11$.} $n=9+13t$. Since $13\equiv2$ and $9\equiv9\pmod{11}$, we need $9+2t\equiv4$, i.e.\ $2t\equiv-5\equiv6\pmod{11}$, so $t\equiv3\pmod{11}$. Thus $t=3+11s$ and
\[
n=9+13(3+11s)=48+143s .
\]

\textbf{Mod $7$.} $48\equiv6$ and $143\equiv3\pmod7$, so we need $6+3s\equiv1$, i.e.\ $3s\equiv-5\equiv2\pmod7$. As $3\cdot3=9\equiv2$, $s\equiv3\pmod7$. Thus $s=3+7j$ and
\[
n=48+143(3+7j)=477+1001j .
\]

So the numbers are exactly those with $\boxed{n\equiv477\pmod{1001}}$, and the three smallest are $\boxed{477,\ 1478,\ 2479}$.

Check: $477=7\cdot68+1=11\cdot43+4=13\cdot36+9$. (By the Chinese remainder theorem there is exactly one solution modulo $7\cdot11\cdot13=1001$, since the moduli are pairwise coprime.)
