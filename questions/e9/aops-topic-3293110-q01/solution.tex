Since $f(0)=f(-\dfrac{2}{3})=1$, the full function is not injective, hence not invertible. Completing the square gives $f(x)=3(x+\dfrac{1}{3})^2+\dfrac{2}{3}$. On $x\ge-\dfrac{1}{3}$ it is strictly increasing with range $[\dfrac{2}{3},\infty)$. Solving $y=3(x+\dfrac{1}{3})^2+\dfrac{2}{3}$ on that branch gives
\[\boxed{f^{-1}(y)=\dfrac{-1+\sqrt{3y-2}}{3},\quad y\ge\dfrac{2}{3}}.\]
