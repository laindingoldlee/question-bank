Rewrite the equation as $\dfrac{x}{2}-\dfrac{3}{5}=\dfrac{7}{y}$, that is $\dfrac{5x-6}{10}=\dfrac{7}{y}$, or
\[
y(5x-6)=70 .
\]
So $5x-6$ is a positive divisor of $70$ that leaves remainder $4$ on division by $5$. The positive divisors of $70$ are $1,2,5,7,10,14,35,70$, with remainders $1,2,0,2,0,4,0,0$ modulo $5$. Only $14$ works: $5x-6=14$ gives $x=4$ and $y=\dfrac{70}{14}=5$. The only solution is
\[
\boxed{x=4,\ y=5}
\]
(check: $2-\dfrac{7}{5}=\dfrac{3}{5}$).
