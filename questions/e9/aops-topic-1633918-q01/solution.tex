The radius $ON$ is perpendicular to the tangent $PN$, so triangle $PNO$ is right-angled at $N$ and $PN=\sqrt{13^2-5^2}=12$. Also $PQ=PO+OQ=13+5=18$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(7cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair O=(0,0),P=(-13,0),Q=(5,0);
pair Nn=5*dir(180-aCos(5/13));
pair R=P+dot(Q-P,unit(Nn-P))*unit(Nn-P);
draw(circle(O,5));
draw(P--Q,linewidth(0.8));draw(P--R,linewidth(0.8));draw(R--Q,linewidth(0.8));
draw(O--Nn,blue+dashed);
pair u=unit(P-Nn),w=unit(O-Nn);draw(Nn+0.6*u--Nn+0.6*u+0.6*w--Nn+0.6*w);
pair u2=unit(P-R),w2=unit(Q-R);draw(R+0.6*u2--R+0.6*u2+0.6*w2--R+0.6*w2);
dot(O);
label("$O$",O,S);label("$P$",P,W);label("$Q$",Q,SE);label("$N$",Nn,NW);label("$R$",R,N);
label("$5$",(O+Nn)/2,E,blue);label("$13$",(P+O)/2,S);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Triangles $PNO$ and $PRQ$ both have a right angle ($N$ and $R$) and share the angle at $P$, so they are similar with ratio $\dfrac{PQ}{PO}=\dfrac{18}{13}$:
\[
PR=\dfrac{18}{13}\cdot PN=\dfrac{216}{13},\qquad RQ=\dfrac{18}{13}\cdot ON=\dfrac{90}{13}.
\]
Since $R$ lies beyond $N$ on ray $PN$ ($PR>PN$), $NR=PR-PN=\dfrac{216-156}{13}=\dfrac{60}{13}$. Hence
\[
NR+RQ=\dfrac{60}{13}+\dfrac{90}{13}=\boxed{\textbf{(A)}\ \dfrac{150}{13}}.
\]
