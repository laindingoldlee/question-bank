The cosine rule gives $\angle A=60^\circ$. The original triangle has semiperimeter $10$, area $10\sqrt3$, and inradius $\sqrt3$. Its incircle tangency length from $A$ is $3$.

Write $AM=x$, $AN=y$. The other tangency lengths are $x-3,y-3$, so $MN=x+y-6$. The cosine rule in $AMN$ gives
\[
x^2+y^2-xy=(x+y-6)^2,\qquad (x-4)(y-4)=4.
\]
Because $x,y>3$, both $x-4,y-4$ must be positive.

Use unit vectors $u,v$ along $AB,AC$, with $u\cdot v=\dfrac{1}{2}$. Since $BD=2$, $DC=5$,
\[
D=\dfrac{25u+16v}{7},\qquad AD=\dfrac{\sqrt{1281}}{7}.
\]
A circle through the origin has equation $|X|^2=w\cdot X$ for a fixed vector $w$. For the circle through $A,M,N$, $w\cdot u=x$ and $w\cdot v=y$. Intersecting with the ray $AD$ therefore gives
\[
AP=\dfrac{25x+16y}{\sqrt{1281}}.
\]
Put $x=4+t$, $y=4+\dfrac{4}{t}$, where $t>0$. Then
\[
25x+16y=164+25t+\dfrac{64}{t}\ge244
\]
by AM-GM, with equality at $t=\dfrac{8}{5}$. The corresponding triangle has positive tangency lengths and encloses the fixed incircle, so equality is attainable. Thus
\[
\min AP^2=\dfrac{244^2}{1281}=\dfrac{976}{21},
\qquad \boxed{r+s=997}.
\]
