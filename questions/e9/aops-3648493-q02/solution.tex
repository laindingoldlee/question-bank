The conditions describe a kite with equal diagonals, which need not be a square. For example vertices $(0,\dfrac{3}{2}),(1,0),(0,-\dfrac{1}{2}),(-1,0)$ have equal diagonals and exactly one reflection axis, but no nontrivial rotational symmetry. A square also satisfies the conditions and has four reflection axes and rotations through multiples of 90 degrees. Thus the source' s mixed phrase ``axes of rotational symmetry does not have a unique answer. For a nonsquare such kite the reflection-axis count is one and rotational-symmetry order is one; for a square both counts are four.
