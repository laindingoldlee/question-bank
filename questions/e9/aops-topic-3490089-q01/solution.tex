An optimal partition has no $1$: combine it with another summand to increase the product. A part $m\ge5$ can be replaced by $3,m-3$, increasing the product since $3(m-3)>m$. Thus only $2,3,4$ are needed, and $4$ may be replaced by $2,2$ without change. Three twos can be replaced by two threes, increasing $8$ to $9$. An optimum therefore has at most two twos. Since the sum is divisible by $3$, it must have no twos and four threes. The maximum is $3^4=\boxed{81}$.
