Apply the identity to $P(t)=1,t,t^2,t^3$. The resulting conditions are
\[d=\dfrac{2}{3},\qquad a+b+c=0,\qquad a^2+b^2+c^2=1,\qquad a^3+b^3+c^3=0.\]
When $a+b+c=0$, the sum of cubes equals $3abc$, so $abc=0$. If $c=0$, then $b=-a$ and $2a^2=1$, yielding $a=\pm\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$. Thus $d=\dfrac{2}{3}$ and $(a,b,c)$ is any permutation of $\left(-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2},0,\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\right)$. These conditions are sufficient because $1,t,t^2,t^3$ span all the required polynomials and both sides are linear in $P$.
