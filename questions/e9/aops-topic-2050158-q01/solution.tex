Let one side of the square go from vertex $P$ to vertex $Q$ with $\overrightarrow{PQ}=(u,v)$. The next side is $(u,v)$ rotated by $90^\circ$, that is $(-v,u)$ or $(v,-u)$. Taking $u,v\ge0$ (after relabelling the vertices), the square spans $u+v$ units in each direction, so it fits in the grid exactly when $u+v\le3$. Its area is $u^2+v^2$.

\begin{asy}

pair[] o={(0,0),(4.5,0),(9,0),(2.25,-5),(6.75,-5)};
for(int k=0;k<5;++k) grid(o[k]);
draw(o[0]+(0,0)--o[0]+(1,0)--o[0]+(1,1)--o[0]+(0,1)--cycle,red+linewidth(1));

The possible side vectors with $u+v\le3$, up to symmetry, are
\[(1,0),\ (2,0),\ (3,0),\ (1,1),\ (2,1),\]
giving areas $1,4,9,2,5$ (the vector $(1,2)$ gives the same area as $(2,1)$). The sum of the possible areas is
\[1+2+4+5+9=\boxed{21}.\]
The student's argument has two errors. A $4\times4$ square is impossible, since the points span only $3$ units in each direction; the coordinates $0$ to $3$ give $3\times3$ unit cells, not $4\times4$. It also misses the tilted squares, with areas $2$ and $5$.
