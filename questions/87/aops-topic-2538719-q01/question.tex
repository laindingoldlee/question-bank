Let $f(x,y,z)=x^2+y^2+z^2+xyz$. Let $p(x,y,z)$, $q(x,y,z)$, $r(x,y,z)$ be polynomials with real coefficients satisfying
\[f\bigl(p(x,y,z),\,q(x,y,z),\,r(x,y,z)\bigr)=f(x,y,z)\]
identically. Prove or disprove the assertion that $p,q,r$ must be some permutation of $\pm x,\pm y,\pm z$, where the number of minus signs is $0$ or $2$.
