Over coefficients modulo $p$, the binomial theorem gives $(1+t)^{p^2}\equiv1+t^{p^2}$. Since $846<p^2$, coefficients through degree $846$ of $(1+t)^{p^2-1}$ agree with those of $(1+t)^{-1}$, namely $(-1)^j$ in degree $j$. Likewise those of $(1+t)^{p^2-2}$ agree with $(1+t)^{-2}$, whose degree-$j$ coefficient is $(-1)^j(j+1)$. These formal series follow by multiplying the geometric series, so division is legitimate coefficient by coefficient. At even degree $846$, the two coefficients sum to $1+847=848$. Thus the desired condition is $p\mid848=2^4\cdot53$, so $p=53$. The digit sum is $\boxed8$, choice (B).
