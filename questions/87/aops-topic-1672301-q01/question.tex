Call two distinct points $A$ and $Z$ of a line \emph{consecutive} if no point of the line lies strictly between them.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Prove that no two distinct points of a line are consecutive.
\item[(b)] A student tries to find the distance between consecutive points as follows: take $A$ and $B$ at distance $1$, repeatedly halve towards $A$ to get points at distances $\dfrac{1}{2},\dfrac{1}{4},\dfrac{1}{8},\dots$ from $A$, and let $Z$ be ``the last of these points''; then $AZ=1-\left(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{1}{8}+\cdots\right)=0$, contradicting $Z\ne A$. Explain what is wrong.
\end{enumerate}
