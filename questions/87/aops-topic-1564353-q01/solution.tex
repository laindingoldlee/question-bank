The given point is the centroid $G$ of the triangle, the common point of its three medians. (It lies on the median from $A=(x_1,y_1)$ to the midpoint of the opposite side, two thirds of the way along, and similarly for the other medians.)

In an equilateral triangle $ABC$, let $M$ be the midpoint of $AB$. Triangles $AMC$ and $BMC$ are congruent (SSS: $AM=BM$, $AC=BC$, $CM$ common), so $\angle AMC=\angle BMC=90^\circ$. Thus the median $CM$ is also the perpendicular bisector of $AB$, and in the same way each median is the perpendicular bisector of a side.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(4,0),C=(2,2*sqrt(3));
pair G=(A+B+C)/3;
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(circle(G,abs(A-G)),gray);
draw(A--(B+C)/2,dashed);draw(B--(A+C)/2,dashed);draw(C--(A+B)/2,dashed);
pair M=(A+B)/2;
draw(M+(0,0.25)--M+(0.25,0.25)--M+(0.25,0));
dot(G);label("$G$",G+0.32*dir(300));
label("$A$",A,SW);label("$B$",B,SE);label("$C$",C,N);label("$M$",M,S);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

$G$ lies on all three medians, hence on the perpendicular bisectors of all three sides, so $GA=GB=GC$. The point equidistant from the three vertices is the circumcentre. Hence the circumcentre is $G=\left(\dfrac{x_1+x_2+x_3}{3},\dfrac{y_1+y_2+y_3}{3}\right)$. $\blacksquare$

(For a triangle that is not equilateral this is false in general: e.g.\ for $(0,0),(4,0),(0,2)$ the centroid is $\left(\dfrac{4}{3},\dfrac{2}{3}\right)$ but the circumcentre is $(2,1)$.)
