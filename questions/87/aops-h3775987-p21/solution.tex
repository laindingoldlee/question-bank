Fix any real $y>2$. Clearing the nonzero denominators, the equation is equivalent to $x^2((4y+1)x-9)=y^2(2y^2+y-10)$. The right side is positive. For $x>\dfrac{9}{4y+1}$, the left side is continuous and strictly increasing from $0$ to infinity: both positive factors $x^2$ and $(4y+1)x-9$ increase. Thus for every $y>2$ there is exactly one such positive $x$, by the intermediate value theorem. Infinitely many choices of $y$ therefore give infinitely many distinct positive pairs. The equation does $\boxed{\text{not determine a unique pair}}$.
