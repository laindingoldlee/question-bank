Let the weights be $a\le b\le c\le d$. Every pair sum containing $d$ but not $c$ is at most $b+d$, and the sums satisfy
\[
a+b\le a+c\le \{a+d,\ b+c\}\le b+d\le c+d .
\]
So the largest sum is $c+d=136$, the second largest is $b+d=120$, the smallest is $a+b=89$ and the second smallest is $a+c=105$. Therefore
\[
d-a=(b+d)-(a+b)=120-89=\boxed{31}\ \text{kg}.
\]
This does not depend on which of $a+d$, $b+c$ is $112$. In fact, the total of all six sums is $3(a+b+c+d)=675$, so $a+b+c+d=225$. If $a+d=113$ and $b+c=112$, then $c-b=105-89=16$ gives $b=48$, $c=64$, $a=41$, $d=72$; if instead $a+d=112$, the weights are $40.5$, $48.5$, $64.5$, $71.5$. Both sets give all six sums and $d-a=31$.
