Let $x_k$ be the last four digits of $2^{2^k}$, that is, its remainder modulo $10000$. Since $2^{2^{k+1}}=\left(2^{2^k}\right)^2$, each $x_{k+1}$ is the remainder of $x_k^2$ modulo $10000$, starting from $x_0=2$. (For example $x_4=65536\bmod 10000=5536$ and $x_5=5536^2\bmod10000=30647296\bmod10000=7296$.) Squaring $24$ times:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
k & x_k\\\hline
0 & 0002\\
1 & 0004\\
2 & 0016\\
3 & 0256\\
4 & 5536\\
5 & 7296\\
6 & 1616\\
7 & 1456\\
8 & 9936\\
9 & 4096\\
10 & 7216\\
11 & 0656\\
12 & 0336
\end{array}\qquad
\begin{array}{c|c}
k & x_k\\\hline
13 & 2896\\
14 & 6816\\
15 & 7856\\
16 & 6736\\
17 & 3696\\
18 & 0416\\
19 & 3056\\
20 & 9136\\
21 & 6496\\
22 & 8016\\
23 & 6256\\
24 & 7536
\end{array}
\]
So $2^{2^{24}}$ ends in $7536$, and $2^{2^{24}}+1$ ends in $\boxed{7537}$.

(Check by the Chinese remainder theorem: $2^{2^{24}}\equiv0\pmod{16}$; modulo $625$, Euler's theorem with $\varphi(625)=500$ and $2^{24}\equiv216\pmod{500}$ gives $2^{2^{24}}\equiv2^{216}\equiv36\pmod{625}$; and $7536=16\cdot471=12\cdot625+36$ satisfies both.)
