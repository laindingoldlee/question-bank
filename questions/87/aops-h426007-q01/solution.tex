If $n$ is odd, $n^2-1$ is divisible by $8$, so it cannot be a product of distinct primes. For even $n$, factor $(n-1)(n+1)$; the two odd factors are coprime. Checking the even integers in increasing order gives the first five qualifying values:
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
n&n^2-1\\\hline
14&3\cdot5\cdot13\\
16&3\cdot5\cdot17\\
20&3\cdot7\cdot19\\
22&3\cdot7\cdot23\\
32&3\cdot11\cdot31
\end{array}
\]
The other positive even integers below $32$ either give fewer than three prime factors or repeated factors: $2,4,6,12,18,30$ give at most two, while $8,10,24,26,28$ have repetitions. The sum is $\boxed{104}$.
