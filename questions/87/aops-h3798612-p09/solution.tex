The six games award six total points. The winner's three points force wins against everyone else. The three remaining scores sum to three and have minimum $0.5$, with each at most two. The possible multisets are $(2,0.5,0.5)$ and $(1.5,1,0.5)$. Both are feasible: in the first, one player wins both remaining games and the other two draw; in the second, scores follow from one win and one draw, one win and one loss, one draw and one loss. Including the winner, the two score multisets have $\dfrac{4!}{2!}=12$ and $4!=24$ name assignments. Thus $\boxed{36}$ distributions are possible.
