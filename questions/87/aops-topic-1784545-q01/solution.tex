Since $PQ\parallel BC$, triangles $APQ$ and $ABC$ are similar (corresponding angles at $P,B$ and at $Q,C$ are equal), so $\dfrac{AP}{AB}=\dfrac{AQ}{AC}$, or equivalently (basic proportionality theorem) $\dfrac{AP}{PB}=\dfrac{AQ}{QC}$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair B=(0,0),C=(6,0),A=(2,4.5);
pair P=A+(B-A)/3,Q=A+(C-A)/3;
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(P--Q,linewidth(0.9));
dot(P);dot(Q);
label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,SW);label("$C$",C,SE);label("$P$",P,W);label("$Q$",Q,E);
label("$1.5$",(A+P)/2,W);label("$3$",(P+B)/2,W);
label("$1.3$",(A+Q)/2,NE);label("$x$",(Q+C)/2,NE);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

With $QC=x$:
\[
\dfrac{1.5}{3}=\dfrac{1.3}{x}\ \Longrightarrow\ x=\dfrac{3\cdot1.3}{1.5}=2.6 .
\]
So $QC=\boxed{2.6\text{ cm}}$.
