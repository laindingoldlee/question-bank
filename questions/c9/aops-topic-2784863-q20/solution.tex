Let $ s=a+b+c $. Negating and reversing a triple preserves the equations, so it suffices first to consider $ s\ge0 $. Each entry $ r $ satisfies $|s-r|=r^2 $. If $ r\le s $, it is one of $ u_\pm=\dfrac{-1\pm\sqrt{1+4s}}{2}$. If $ r\ge s $, it is one of $ v_\pm=\dfrac{1\pm\sqrt{1-4s}}{2}$, requiring $ s\le\dfrac{1}{4}$. For $ s>\dfrac{1}{4}$ there are only two possible entries, impossible for three distinct numbers. For $ 0<s\le\dfrac{1}{4}$, the three nonnegative candidates are $ u_+,v_-,v_+$; their sum is $ u_++1>1>s $. Any three-entry choice containing $ u_-$ either contains $ u_+$, giving sum $-1+v_\pm\le0 $, or contains both $ v $ values, giving $ u_-+1<0 $. Neither equals $ s>0 $. Thus $ s=0 $, when the possible entries are $-1,0,1 $. These do satisfy all equations, so $\boxed{(a,b,c)=(-1,0,1)}$. The negation symmetry rules out negative $ s $ as well.
