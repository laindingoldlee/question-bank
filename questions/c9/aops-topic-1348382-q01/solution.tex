Divide both sides by $a^2b^2c^2$:
\[
\left(12+\dfrac{b}{a^2}\right)\left(12+\dfrac{c}{b^2}\right)\left(12+\dfrac{a}{c^2}\right)=2016. \tag{$*$}
\]

(a) With $a=b=c=t>0$ this is $\left(12+\dfrac{1}{t}\right)^3=2016$, so $12+\dfrac{1}{t}=\sqrt[3]{2016}$. Since $12^3=1728<2016$, the cube root exceeds $12$, and
\[
\boxed{a=b=c=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]{2016}-12}}\approx1.587 .
\]
This is the only solution with $a=b=c$.

(b) Put $u=\dfrac {b}{a^2}$, $v=\dfrac {c}{b^2}$, $w=\dfrac {a}{c^2}$. Conversely, given any positive $u,v,w$, the system $b=ua^2$, $c=vb^2$, $a=wc^2$ has exactly one positive solution: substituting, $a=w\left(v\left(ua^2\right)^2\right)^2=wv^2u^4a^8$, so
\[
a^7=\dfrac{1}{u^4v^2w},
\]
which determines $a>0$, and then $b$ and $c$. Different triples $(u,v,w)$ give different triples $(a,b,c)$, because $(u,v,w)$ is computed from $(a,b,c)$.

So the solutions of the equation correspond one-to-one to positive triples $(u,v,w)$ with $(12+u)(12+v)(12+w)=2016$. There are infinitely many such triples: for any $s$ with $0<s<\sqrt{168}-12\approx0.96$, take $u=v=s$ and
\[
w=\dfrac{2016}{(12+s)^2}-12>0 .
\]
Hence the equation has infinitely many positive solutions (a two-parameter family), and the problem ``find all $a,b,c$'' cannot be answered by a finite list. $\blacksquare$
