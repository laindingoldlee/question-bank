Measure lengths in seconds: the songs have lengths $L=120,121,\dots,300$, and the long ones have $L=240,\dots,300$ ($61$ songs).

Over a long stretch of $N$ songs, each song is played about $\dfrac{N}{181}$ times (law of large numbers). So the fraction of the time during which a song of length at least $240$ is playing approaches
\[\dfrac{\dfrac{N}{181}\displaystyle\sum_{L=240}^{300}L}{\dfrac{N}{181}\displaystyle\sum_{L=120}^{300}L}=\dfrac{61\cdot270}{181\cdot210}=\dfrac{16470}{38010}=\boxed{\dfrac{549}{1267}}\approx0.433.\]
Waking up ``a long time later'' at a moment unrelated to the playlist means this long-run fraction of time is the required probability.

Note that the answer is larger than $\dfrac{61}{181}\approx0.337$, the fraction of \emph{songs} that are long: a random moment is more likely to fall inside a long song (the inspection paradox).
