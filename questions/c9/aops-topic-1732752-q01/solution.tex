There are $2^7=128$ colourings in all. The two one-coloured colourings are each fixed by every rotation. For any other colouring, a rotation by $k$ steps ($1\le k\le6$) that fixes it would force all vertices to have the same colour, because $\gcd(k,7)=1$ and repeated rotation by $k$ steps visits all seven vertices. So each of the other $126$ colourings has $7$ different rotations, and they fall into $\dfrac{126}{7}=18$ classes. The number of different colourings is $18+2=\boxed{20}$. (Burnside's lemma gives the same: $\dfrac{128+6\cdot2}{7}=20$.)
