\textbf{(a)} Choose the two people for the first theorem ($\dbinom62=15$ ways), then two of the remaining four for the second ($\dbinom42=6$ ways); the last two get the third. There are $15\cdot6\cdot1=\boxed{90}$ ways.

\textbf{(b)} Each theorem independently gets any of the $\dbinom62=15$ pairs, so there are $15^3=\boxed{3375}$ ways.
