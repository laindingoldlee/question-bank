The triangle has legs $4$ and hypotenuse $4\sqrt2$. The common side (the top of the square) is inside the figure, so the boundary consists of the other three sides of the square ($12$), the other leg of the triangle ($4$) and its hypotenuse ($4\sqrt2$).

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0), B=(4,0), C=(4,4), D=(0,4), T=(0,8);
fill(A--B--C--T--cycle,rgb(0.93,0.95,1));
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,dashed); draw(D--C,dashed);
draw(A--B--C--T--cycle,linewidth(1.1));
draw((0,4.35)--(0.35,4.35)--(0.35,4));
label("$4$",(A+B)/2,S); label("$4$",(B+C)/2,E); label("$4$",(D+T)/2,W); label("$4$",(A+D)/2,W);
label("$4\sqrt2$",(C+T)/2,NE); label("$4$",(D+C)/2,S,gray);
\end{asy}

The perimeter is $16+4\sqrt2$, so $a+b+c=16+4+2=\boxed{\textbf{(C) }22}$. (The answer is the same whichever end of the top side carries the right angle.)
