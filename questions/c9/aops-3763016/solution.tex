The exponent is $\sum_{k=1}^{51}v_7(k!)$. For $k\le51$, $v_7 (k!) = \lfloor \dfrac{k}{7} \rfloor + \lfloor \dfrac{k}{49} \rfloor$. The first sum is $7(1+2+3+4+5+6)+3\cdot7=168$, and the second is $3$. Therefore the largest exponent is $\boxed{171}$. Equivalently, every multiple of seven contributes once to all factorials that contain it, and 49 contributes once more.
