\textbf{Two weighings suffice.} Weigh $3$ balls against $3$. If one side is heavier, the heavy ball is among those $3$; if they balance, it is among the other $3$. Then weigh $1$ of the $3$ suspects against another: the heavier one is the ball, and if they balance it is the third.

\textbf{One weighing is not enough.} A weighing has only $3$ outcomes, which cannot distinguish $9$ possibilities. The answer is $\boxed{2}$.
