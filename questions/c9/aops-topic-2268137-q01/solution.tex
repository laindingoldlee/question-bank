\textbf{(a)} The buyer pays $10000\cdot1.025=\$10{,}250$. The fee is $2.5\%$ of this, $0.025\cdot10250=\$256.25$, so the seller keeps $10250-256.25=\$9993.75$. The seller loses $\boxed{\$6.25}$. The reason: the fee is charged on the \emph{raised} price, so $2.5\%$ of the extra $\$250$, which is $\$6.25$, is not covered.

\textbf{(b)} If the buyer pays $P$, the seller keeps $P-0.025P=0.975P$. We need $0.975P=10000$, so
\[P=\dfrac{10000}{0.975}=\dfrac{400000}{39}\approx\$10{,}256.41.\]
This is not a whole number of cents: $0.975\cdot10256.41=9999.99975<10000$, while $0.975\cdot10256.42=10000.0095$. So the least price in whole cents is $\boxed{\$10{,}256.42}$. In general, to keep an amount $X$ after a fee of $f\%$, the price must be $\dfrac{X}{1-\dfrac{f}{100}}$, which is more than $X\left(1+\dfrac{f}{100}\right)$.
