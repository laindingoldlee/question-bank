\textbf{(a)} Let $m$ be the smallest of $a,b,c,d,e$, and let $k$ ($1\le k\le5$) be the number of these exponents equal to $m$. Divide by $5^m$:
\[
5^{a-m}+5^{b-m}+5^{c-m}+5^{d-m}+5^{e-m}=5^{x-m}.
\]
Every exponent on the left is a nonnegative integer; $k$ of the terms equal $1$ and the others are multiples of $5$. So the left side is congruent to $k$ modulo $5$, and it is at least $5$. Then $5^{x-m}\ge5$, so $x-m\ge1$ and the right side is a multiple of $5$. Hence $5\mid k$, so $k=5$: all five exponents equal $m$. The equation becomes $5\cdot5^m=5^{m+1}=5^x$, so $x=m+1=a+1$. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{(b)} Conversely, for every integer $a$, choosing $a=b=c=d=e$ and $x=a+1$ works. Since $a$ can be any integer (for example $a=-100$ gives $x=-99$), $x$ can be as small as we like, so there is no smallest value. If the exponents must be nonnegative, then $x=a+1\ge1$, and $x=1$ is attained by $1+1+1+1+1=5$. The smallest possible value is then $\boxed{x=1}$.
