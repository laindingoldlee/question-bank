Let $s=a+b=\dfrac{2\sqrt3}{3}i$, so $s^2=-\dfrac43$. From $a^2+b^2=s^2-2ab=s$,
\[ab=\dfrac{s^2-s}{2}=-\dfrac23-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{3}i.\]
So $a,b$ are the roots of $t^2-st+ab=0$, namely $\dfrac{s\pm\sqrt\Delta}{2}$ with
\[\Delta=s^2-4ab=-\dfrac43+\dfrac83+\dfrac{4\sqrt3}{3}i=\dfrac83\left(\dfrac12+\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i\right).\]
A square root of $\Delta$ is $\sqrt{\dfrac83}\left(\dfrac{\sqrt3}2+\dfrac12i\right)=\sqrt2+\dfrac{\sqrt6}{3}i$. Since $s$ is purely imaginary, $\operatorname{Re}(a)=\pm\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$. So $p=2$, $q=2$ and $p+q=\boxed{4}$.
