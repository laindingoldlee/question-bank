Since $AP=AB=AD$, triangle $ADP$ is isosceles with $\angle APD=\angle ADP=10^\circ$, so $\angle DAP=160^\circ$. The ray $AP$ makes $160^\circ$ with $AD$, on either side of line $AD$.

label("$A$",A,W);label("$B$",B,E);label("$C$",C,NE);label("$D$",D,NW);label("$P_1$",P1,S);label("$P_2$",P2,S);
\end{asy}

If $P$ is on the opposite side of line $AD$ from $B$, then $\angle BAP=360^\circ-90^\circ-160^\circ=110^\circ$ and $\angle APB=\dfrac{180^\circ-110^\circ}2=35^\circ$. If $P$ is on the same side, then $\angle BAP=160^\circ-90^\circ=70^\circ$ and $\angle APB=55^\circ$. Both points are outside the square, so $\angle APB=\boxed{35^\circ\text{ or }55^\circ}$.
