Scale the square to have area $1$. With probability $1$, no three of the points are collinear, so we may assume this.

\textbf{(a)} Four points, no three collinear, either are the vertices of a convex quadrilateral (their convex hull has all four as vertices) or one of them lies inside the triangle formed by the other three (their convex hull is a triangle).

Let $E_D$ be the event that $D$ lies inside triangle $ABC$. Once $A,B,C$ are chosen, $D$ is uniform in the unit square, so the conditional probability of $E_D$ is the area of triangle $ABC$. Averaging,
\[P(E_D)=\mathbb{E}\left[\text{area of }ABC\right]=\dfrac{11}{144},\]
and similarly for $E_A,E_B,E_C$. These four events are mutually exclusive: if $D$ is inside triangle $ABC$, then the convex hull of the four points is triangle $ABC$, and its vertices $A$, $B$, $C$ are corners of the hull, so none of them can lie inside a triangle formed by the other points (such a triangle lies inside the hull). Hence
\[P(\text{not convex})=4\cdot\dfrac{11}{144}=\dfrac{11}{36},\qquad P(\text{convex})=1-\dfrac{11}{36}=\boxed{\dfrac{25}{36}}\approx0.694 .\]

\textbf{(b)} Three non-collinear points $A,B,C$ lie on exactly one circle. The point $D$ lies on that circle with probability equal to the area of the part of the circle inside the square, which is $0$ because a circle has no area. So the probability is $\boxed{0}$.

(The value $\dfrac{11}{144}$ comes from a double integral; the question in (a) is Sylvester's four-point problem for the square.)
