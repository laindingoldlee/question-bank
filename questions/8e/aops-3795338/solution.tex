Order the factorial exponents $A\le B\le C$. If $A<B$, the power of two dividing the sum is exactly $2^A$, so a cube requires $3\mid A$. Then $A\ge6$, and $A,B,C$ are all multiples of six. Dividing by $2^A$, the odd part is $1+2^{B-A}+2^{C-A}\equiv3\pmod7$, impossible for a cube since cube residues modulo seven are $0,1,6$. If $A=B=C$, the factor $3$ occurs exactly once, also impossible. Finally suppose $A=B<C$. When $C>A+1$, the odd part is $1+2^{C-A-1}$. It cannot be an odd cube $u^3>1$, because $(u-1)(u^2+u+1)$ would be a power of two while the second factor is odd and greater than one. Thus $C=A+1$. The only consecutive factorial values are $1,2$, giving $2+2+4=8$. Each factorial equal to one comes from index zero or one, while the exponent two comes from index two. The unordered triples are $(0,0,2),(0,1,2),(1,1,2)$, so the answer is $\boxed3$.
