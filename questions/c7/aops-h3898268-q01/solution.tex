Cross-multiplication gives $a_na_{n+2}=a_{n+1}a_{n+3}$. No term can be zero: if $a_k=0$ for $k\ge2$, its neighbours are nonzero by the denominator conditions, contradicting the product identity at $k-1$; if $a_1=0$, the identity at $1$ forces $a_4=0$, already impossible. Consecutive product identities then give $a_{n+2}(a_n-a_{n+4})=0$, hence period four. The given values imply $a_1=1,a_2=2,a_3=6$, and $a_4=\dfrac{a_1a_3}{a_2}=3$. Thus $N=506(1+4+36+9)+1+4=25305$, with digit sum $\boxed{15}$.
