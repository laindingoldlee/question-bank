Let $T$ be the intersection of the common tangent with the radical axis $AB$. This intersection is finite: if the two lines were parallel, the line of centers would be perpendicular to the common tangent, forcing the center distance to equal the difference of the radii, contrary to the circles intersecting twice.

Equal powers of $T$ in the two circles give $TC_1^2=TC_2^2$. Since $C_1,C_2$ are distinct points on the same tangent line, $T$ is their midpoint. Their perpendicular distances from any line through $T$, in particular $AB$, are therefore equal. The triangles have the same base $AB$ and equal heights, so
\[
\boxed{[ABC_1]=[ABC_2]}.
\]
