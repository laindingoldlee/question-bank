\textbf{Count.} Glue the four officers into one block. The block and the four other members are $5$ units around a circle; up to rotation they can be arranged in $(5-1)!=24$ ways. Inside the block the officers can be ordered in $4!=24$ ways. Hence there are
\[
24\cdot24=\boxed{576}
\]
distinguishable seating orders.

\textbf{The error in $3!\cdot4!$.} Fixing one person's seat to remove rotations is a valid idea, but it must be carried out fully. Fix officer $O_1$ in a seat. The block of four consecutive seats containing $O_1$ can be placed in $4$ ways ($O_1$ is the first, second, third or fourth seat of the block, going clockwise). Then the other three officers fill the rest of the block in $3!$ ways and the four other members fill the remaining seats in $4!$ ways. This gives $4\cdot3!\cdot4!=576$. The argument $3!\cdot4!$ forgets the factor $4$ for the position of $O_1$ within the block (it silently assumes $O_1$ is always at one end).
