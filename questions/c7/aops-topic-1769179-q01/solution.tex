View the equation as a quadratic in $y$: $5y^2+2x\,y+(10x^2-14x+5)=0$. For a real $y$ the discriminant must be nonnegative:
\[
4x^2-20(10x^2-14x+5)=-196x^2+280x-100=-4(7x-5)^2\ge0 .
\]
So $7x-5=0$, $x=\dfrac{5}{7}$, and then the double root is $y=-\dfrac{2x}{10}=-\dfrac{x}{5}=-\dfrac{1}{7}$. Hence $x+y=\dfrac{5}{7}-\dfrac{1}{7}=\boxed{\dfrac{4}{7}}$.

(Equivalently, $5\cdot\bigl(10x^2+5y^2+2xy-14x+5\bigr)=(5y+x)^2+(7x-5)^2$, which is zero only when $7x=5$ and $5y=-x$.)
