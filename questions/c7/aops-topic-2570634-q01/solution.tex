\textbf{(a)} \emph{Legendre's formula:} for a prime $p$, the exponent of $p$ in $n!$ is
\[\left\lfloor\dfrac np\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\dfrac n{p^2}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\dfrac n{p^3}\right\rfloor+\cdots,\]
a finite sum since the terms become $0$. Proof: the exponent of $p$ in $n!=1\cdot2\cdots n$ is the sum over $m\le n$ of the exponent of $p$ in $m$, and each $m$ with $p^e\,\|\,m$ contributes $1$ for each of $p,p^2,\dots,p^e$. So the total is the sum over $i\ge1$ of the number of $m\le n$ divisible by $p^i$, which is $\left\lfloor\dfrac n{p^i}\right\rfloor$.

For $32!$: $16+8+4+2+1=\boxed{31}$, while the claim gives only $16+8=24$. Higher powers of $p$ must be included.

\textbf{(b)} The formula is for primes. For $6=2\cdot3$, find the exponents of $2$ and $3$ in $10!$:
\[5+2+1=8\qquad\text{and}\qquad3+1=4.\]
So $10!=2^8\cdot3^4\cdots$, and $6^k\mid10!$ exactly when $k\le\min(8,4)$. The largest is $k=\boxed{4}$, while the claim gives $\left\lfloor\dfrac{10}6\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\dfrac{10}{36}\right\rfloor=1$.
