Use induction on $n$. For $n=0$, $\binom00=1$. For $n\ge1$, $\binom n0=\binom nn=1$, and for $1\le k\le n-1$ Pascal's identity holds:
\[\binom{n-1}{k-1}+\binom{n-1}{k}=\dfrac{(n-1)!}{(k-1)!\,(n-k)!}+\dfrac{(n-1)!}{k!\,(n-1-k)!}=\dfrac{(n-1)!\,(k+(n-k))}{k!\,(n-k)!}=\binom nk.\]
By the induction hypothesis the two terms on the left are integers, so $\binom nk$ is an integer. $\blacksquare$

(This is also why a number of combinations can never be a fraction: it counts the $k$-element subsets of an $n$-element set.)
