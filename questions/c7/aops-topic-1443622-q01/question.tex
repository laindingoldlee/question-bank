For vectors $\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf w$ in space, the volume of the tetrahedron with vertices $A$, $A+\mathbf u$, $A+\mathbf v$, $A+\mathbf w$ is
\[
V=\dfrac{1}{6}\left|\det\begin{pmatrix}u_1&u_2&u_3\\ v_1&v_2&v_3\\ w_1&w_2&w_3\end{pmatrix}\right|=\dfrac{1}{6}\left|\mathbf w\cdot(\mathbf u\times\mathbf v)\right|,
\]
a three-dimensional analogue of the shoelace formula.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Prove this formula, using $V=\dfrac{1}{3}\cdot(\text{base area})\cdot(\text{height})$ and the facts that $|\mathbf u\times\mathbf v|$ is the area of the parallelogram spanned by $\mathbf u$ and $\mathbf v$ and that $\mathbf u\times\mathbf v$ is perpendicular to both.
\item[(b)] Find the volume of the tetrahedron with vertices $A(1,2,0)$, $B(3,0,1)$, $C(0,1,4)$, $D(2,3,5)$.
\end{enumerate}
