Adding $1$ to both sides gives $(x-1)^4=2006$. Let $r=\sqrt[4]{2006}>0$. Then $x-1\in\{r,-r,ir,-ir\}$, so the roots are
\[
x=1\pm r\quad\text{(real)},\qquad x=1\pm ir\quad\text{(nonreal)}.
\]
The error: the real roots are $1\pm r$, not $\pm r$. The student solved $x^4=2006$ instead of $(x-1)^4=2006$; their product is $(1+r)(1-r)=1-\sqrt{2006}$, not $-\sqrt{2006}$.

Correctly,
\[
P=(1+ir)(1-ir)=1+r^2=1+\sqrt{2006}.
\]
Check with Vieta: $(1-\sqrt{2006})(1+\sqrt{2006})=1-2006=-2005$, the product of all four roots. Since $44^2=1936<2006<2025=45^2$, we get $44<\sqrt{2006}<45$, so $45<P<46$ and $\lfloor P\rfloor=\boxed{45}$.
