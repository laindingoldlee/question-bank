Let $r=\dfrac{1}{n+1}$, so $0<r\le\dfrac{1}{2}$. The partial sums of this geometric series are
\[S_N=r+r^2+\cdots+r^N=\dfrac{r\left(1-r^N\right)}{1-r}.\]
(Multiply $S_N$ by $1-r$: everything cancels except $r-r^{N+1}$.) Here
\[\dfrac{r}{1-r}=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{n+1}}{\dfrac{n}{n+1}}=\dfrac{1}{n},\qquad\text{so}\qquad S_N=\dfrac{1}{n}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{(n+1)^N}\right).\]
Since $n+1\ge2$, we have $0<\dfrac{1}{(n+1)^N}\le\dfrac{1}{2^N}$, which tends to $0$. Hence $S_N\to\dfrac{1}{n}$, that is, $\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{(n+1)^i}=\dfrac{1}{n}$. $\blacksquare$
