Factor everything:
\[\dfrac{(x-y)^2}{2y(x+3)}\cdot\dfrac{4(x+3)}{(x-y)(x+y)}=\dfrac{4(x+3)(x-y)^2}{2y(x+3)(x-y)(x+y)}=\boxed{\dfrac{2(x-y)}{y(x+y)}}.\]

\textbf{Domain.} The original expression is defined exactly when its two denominators are nonzero:
\[2xy+6y=2y(x+3)\ne0\iff y\ne0\text{ and }x\ne-3,\qquad x^2-y^2=(x-y)(x+y)\ne0\iff x\ne y\text{ and }x\ne-y.\]
So the domain is the set of all pairs $(x,y)$ with
\[\boxed{y\ne0,\quad x\ne-3,\quad x\ne y,\quad x\ne-y}.\]
The cancelled factors still matter: for example, at $x=-3$, $y=1$ the simplified form equals $\dfrac{2\cdot(-4)}{1\cdot(-2)}=4$, but the original expression is undefined there, and at $x=y=1$ the simplified form is $0$ while the original has a zero denominator.
