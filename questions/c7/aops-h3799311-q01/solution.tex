It is $\boxed{\text{not constant}}$. Set $O=(0,0)$ and $E=(2,0)$. A point $T$ is the perpendicular foot from $O$ to the line $ET$ exactly when $(O-T)\cdot(E-T)=0$. If also $|T|<1$, that line is a secant of the unit circle and $T$ is its chord midpoint.

First take $F=\left(\dfrac{1}{5},\dfrac{3}{5}\right)$ and $G=\left(\dfrac{1}{5},-\dfrac{3}{5}\right)$. They satisfy this foot condition and are inside the circle. Here $M=\left(\dfrac{1}{5},0\right)$, and symmetry gives $\angle GFE=\angle FGE$, while $\angle FME=90^\circ$. The expression is $90^\circ$.

Next keep $F$ and take $G=\left(\dfrac{2}{5},\dfrac{4}{5}\right)$, again a valid interior foot. Now $M=\left(-\dfrac{2}{5},0\right)$ and $\angle FME=45^\circ$. Put $\theta=\arctan\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\phi=\arctan\dfrac{1}{2}$. The line directions give
\[
\angle GFE=45^\circ+\theta,\qquad
\angle FGE=135^\circ-\phi.
\]
Since $\tan(\theta+\phi)=1$ and the sum is acute, $\theta+\phi=45^\circ$. The expression is therefore $0^\circ$, different from the first value.
The missing choice of relative positions cannot be ignored.

\end{asy}
