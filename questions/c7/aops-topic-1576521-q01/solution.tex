Let the sides be $a\le b\le c$ with $a+b+c=20$. The semiperimeter is $s=10$, and the triangle inequality $a+b>c$ means $c<10$, i.e.\ $c\le9$. By Heron's formula
\[
K^2=10\,(10-a)(10-b)(10-c).
\]
Put $x=10-a$, $y=10-b$, $z=10-c$. These are positive integers with
\[
x+y+z=30-20=10,
\]
and $K^2=10xyz$. So we must make the product $xyz$ of three positive integers with sum $10$ as large as possible. By AM-GM the product is largest when the three numbers are as nearly equal as possible: if two of them differ by $2$ or more, replacing $u,v$ (with $u\ge v+2$) by $u-1,v+1$ changes $uv$ to $uv+u-v-1>uv$. So the best choice is $\{x,y,z\}=\{3,3,4\}$, with $xyz=36$.

This gives sides $10-3,10-3,10-4$, that is $7,7,6$, and
\[
K=\sqrt{10\cdot36}=\boxed{6\sqrt{10}}\approx18.97 .
\]

usepackage("amsmath");

draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));

label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,SW);label("$C$",C,SE);
label("$6$",(3,0),S);label("$7$",(A+B)/2,W);label("$7$",(A+C)/2,E);
label("$\sqrt{40}$",(3,2.4),E);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Check with the altitude: the altitude to the side $6$ has length $\sqrt{7^2-3^2}=\sqrt{40}=2\sqrt{10}$, so the area is $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot6\cdot2\sqrt{10}=6\sqrt{10}$. (The nearby triangles $6,6,8$ and $5,7,8$ have areas $\sqrt{320}$ and $\sqrt{300}$, both smaller.)
