The small semicircles have diameters $2$ each, so together they cover a length $2N$ of the large diameter, which has length $2N+2$; they fit and do not overlap. Each small semicircle lies inside the large one (its points are at distance at most $N$ from the centre of the large semicircle when it is placed as in the figure, and in any case it lies within the large semicircle because it stands on the diameter inside it).

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(7cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real R=5;
path big=arc((0,0),R,0,180)--cycle;
fill(big,gray(0.82));
for(int k=0;k<4;++k){ pair c=(-3+2k,0); fill(arc(c,1,0,180)--cycle,white); draw(arc(c,1,0,180),linewidth(0.8)); }
draw(big,linewidth(1));
draw((0,0)--(0,R),dashed);
label("$N+1$",(0,0.62*R),E);
draw((3,0)--(3,1),dashed);
label("$1$",(3,0.5),E);
dot((0,0));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The shaded area is the area of the large semicircle minus the areas of the $N$ small ones:
\[
\dfrac{\pi(N+1)^2}{2}-N\cdot\dfrac{\pi\cdot1^2}{2}=\dfrac{\pi\left(N^2+2N+1-N\right)}{2}=\boxed{\dfrac{\pi\left(N^2+N+1\right)}{2}}.
\]
For example, with $N=4$ the shaded area is $\dfrac{21\pi}{2}$.
