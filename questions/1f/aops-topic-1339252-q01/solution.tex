\textbf{(a)} There are $n!$ ways to put the people in $n$ labelled seats. Each circular arrangement appears exactly $n$ times among these (once for each of the $n$ rotations, all different because the people are different). So there are $\dfrac{n!}{n}=\boxed{(n-1)!}$ arrangements. Equivalently: fix one person's seat; the others can then be placed in $(n-1)!$ ways.

\textbf{(b)} For $n\ge3$ the mirror image of a circular arrangement is a different circular arrangement (the neighbour order clockwise is reversed, and with at least three distinct people the reversed order is not a rotation of the original). So the $(n-1)!$ arrangements of (a) fall into pairs, giving $\boxed{\dfrac{(n-1)!}{2}}$.

The people being different does not by itself force division by $2$: at a table, seatings that are mirror images are different (each person has different neighbours on the left and on the right), so the answer for a table is $(n-1)!$. Dividing by $2$ is right only when turning the whole circle over is allowed.

\textbf{(c)} For $n=6$: $5!=\boxed{120}$ and $\dfrac{120}{2}=\boxed{60}$.
