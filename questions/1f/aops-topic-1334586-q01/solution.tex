If $x\ge1$ then $\sqrt{|x+4|}\ge\sqrt5$ and $|x+4|>|x-4|$, so the left side exceeds the right side; there are no solutions with $x\ge1$. Two cases remain.

\textbf{Case $-4\le x<1$.} The equation is $\sqrt{x+4}+\sqrt{1-x}=\sqrt{4-x}$. Squaring,
\[
5+2\sqrt{(x+4)(1-x)}=4-x\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2\sqrt{(x+4)(1-x)}=-1-x,
\]
which needs $x\le-1$. Squaring again, $4(-x^2-3x+4)=x^2+2x+1$, that is $5x^2+14x-15=0$, so $x=\dfrac{-7\pm2\sqrt{31}}{5}$. Only $x=\dfrac{-7-2\sqrt{31}}{5}\approx-3.63$ lies in $[-4,-1]$.

\textbf{Case $x<-4$.} The equation is $\sqrt{-x-4}+\sqrt{1-x}=\sqrt{4-x}$. Squaring,
\[
-2x-3+2\sqrt{(x+4)(x-1)}=4-x\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2\sqrt{(x+4)(x-1)}=x+7,
\]
which needs $x\ge-7$. Squaring again, $4(x^2+3x-4)=x^2+14x+49$, so $3x^2-2x-65=0=(3x+13)(x-5)$. Only $x=-\dfrac{13}{3}$ lies in $[-7,-4)$.

Both values satisfy the original equation (each squaring step was reversible under the sign conditions checked). The sum of the solutions is
\[
-\dfrac{13}{3}+\dfrac{-7-2\sqrt{31}}{5}=\boxed{-\dfrac{86+6\sqrt{31}}{15}}.
\]
