A divisibility rule for $7$ says: remove the last digit of a positive integer, double it, and subtract the result from the number formed by the remaining digits; the original number is divisible by $7$ exactly when the result is divisible by $7$ (possibly $0$ or negative).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Apply the rule correctly to $14$ and to $2016$.
\item[(b)] Prove the rule: for integers $a\ge0$ and $0\le b\le9$, $7\mid 10a+b$ if and only if $7\mid a-2b$.
\end{enumerate}
