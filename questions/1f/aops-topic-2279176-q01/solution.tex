\textbf{(a)} The denominator of the first fraction is again the whole continued fraction, so $N=X+\dfrac {Y}{N}$. Multiplying by $N\ne0$ gives $N^2=XN+Y$, i.e. $N^2-XN-Y=0$. By the quadratic formula,
\[N=\dfrac{X\pm\sqrt{X^2+4Y}}{2}.\]
The product of the two roots is $-Y<0$, so one root is negative and one is positive. Since $N>0$, $N=\dfrac{X+\sqrt{X^2+4Y}}{2}$.

\textbf{(b)} For $X=Y=1$: $N^2-N-1=0$, so $N=\boxed{\dfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2}}$, the golden ratio. For $X=2$, $Y=3$: $N^2-2N-3=(N-3)(N+1)=0$, so $N=\boxed{3}$. (Check: $3=2+\dfrac{3}{3}$.)
