Find an inverse of $7$ modulo $11$: $7\cdot8=56=5\cdot11+1\equiv1\pmod{11}$. Multiplying both sides of the congruence by $8$,
\[
y\equiv8\cdot5=40\equiv7\pmod{11}.
\]
Check: $7\cdot7=49=44+5$. So the solutions are $\boxed{y\equiv7\pmod{11}}$, i.e.\ $y=7+11k$ for integers $k$.

(A quick alternative: add multiples of $11$ to $5$ until the result is divisible by $7$: $5,16,27,38,49$; then $7y\equiv49$ gives $y\equiv7$, as $\gcd(7,11)=1$.)
