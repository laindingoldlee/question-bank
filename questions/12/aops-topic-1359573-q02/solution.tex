Each draw is a queen with probability $\dfrac{5}{15}=\dfrac{1}{3}$, independently of the other draws. Rex wins in round $k$ if the first $3(k-1)$ draws and the two bandit draws of round $k$ are not queens and Rex's draw is a queen:
\[
P(\text{Rex wins})=\sum_{k\ge1}\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{3(k-1)}\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{2}\dfrac{1}{3}
=\dfrac{\dfrac{4}{27}}{1-\dfrac{8}{27}}=\boxed{\dfrac{4}{19}} .
\]
(Without replacement the answer would be different: the first queen would be in position $3,6$ or $9$ with probability $\dfrac{\binom{12}{4}+\binom94+\binom64}{\binom{15}{5}}=\dfrac{212}{1001}$.)
