Let $\sigma(n)$ denote the sum of \emph{all} positive divisors of $n$, including $n$ itself. A positive integer $n$ is called \emph{abundant} if $\sigma(n)>2n$, \emph{perfect} if $\sigma(n)=2n$ and \emph{deficient} if $\sigma(n)<2n$; it is called \emph{almost perfect} if $\sigma(n)=2n-1$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Classify $12$, $16$ and $28$.
\item[(b)] A student writes ``$n$ is abundant if $\sigma(n)>n$''. Explain why, with $\sigma$ as defined above, this condition is wrong.
\item[(c)] Prove that every power of $2$ is almost perfect.
\end{enumerate}
