Here $r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$, $x=r\cos\theta$ and $y=r\sin\theta$. Since the point is not on the non-positive $x$-axis, $\cos\theta\ne-1$, so $x+r>0$. By the half-angle formula,
\[\tan\dfrac\theta2=\dfrac{\sin\theta}{1+\cos\theta}=\dfrac{\dfrac yr}{1+\dfrac xr}=\dfrac{y}{x+r}.\]
\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real r=2;pair O=(0,0),P=r*dir(60),K=(-r,0);draw(circle(O,r),gray);draw((-2.6,0)--(2.6,0),Arrow);draw(O--P);draw(K--P,dashed);
draw((P.x,0)--P,dotted);label("$O$",O,S);label("$K(-r,0)$",K,SW);label("$P(x,y)$",P,NE);draw(arc(O,0.4,0,60));draw(arc(K,0.8,0,30));label("$\theta$",O+0.55*dir(30));label("$\theta/2$",K+(1.25,0.3));
\end{asy}

(Geometrically, with $K=(-r,0)$, the inscribed angle $\angle OKP$ is $\dfrac\theta2$ and $\tan\angle OKP=\dfrac{y}{x+r}$.) Since $\dfrac\theta2\in\left(-\dfrac\pi2,\dfrac\pi2\right)$, the range of $\arctan$, we get $\dfrac\theta2=\arctan\dfrac{y}{x+r}$. $\blacksquare$
