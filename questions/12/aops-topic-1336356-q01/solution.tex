\textbf{Proof.} Let the triangle be $ABC$ with angles $A$, $B$, $C$ (in radians, $A+B+C=\pi$). By the extended law of sines its sides are $a=2R\sin A$, $b=2R\sin B$, $c=2R\sin C$, so
\[
[ABC]=\dfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C=2R^2\sin A\sin B\sin C .
\]

By AM-GM (all three sines are positive),
\[
\sin A\sin B\sin C\le\left(\dfrac{\sin A+\sin B+\sin C}{3}\right)^3 .
\]
On $[0,\pi]$ the sine function is concave, so its graph lies below the tangent line at $x=\dfrac{\pi}{3}$:
$\sin x\le\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\left(x-\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$. Adding this for $x=A,B,C$ gives
\[
\sin A+\sin B+\sin C\le\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\left(A+B+C-\pi\right)=\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{2}.
\]
Hence
\[
[ABC]\le2R^2\left(\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)^3=\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{4}R^2 .
\]
Equality in the tangent-line bound requires $A=B=C=\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, and the equilateral triangle does reach the value $\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{4}R^2$. So the equilateral triangle, and only it, has the largest area.
