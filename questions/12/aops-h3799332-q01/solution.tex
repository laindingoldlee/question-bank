Let $P(n)$ be the displayed prime product, with an empty product equal to $1$. Use strong induction. The cases $n=1,2$ are immediate.

If $n=2m\ge4$, it is composite, so $P(n)=P(n-1)<4^{n-1}<4^n$. If $n=2m+1\ge3$, every prime $m+1<p\le2m+1$ divides $\binom{2m+1}{m}$: it occurs in the numerator factorial and not in either denominator factorial. As these primes are distinct,
\[
P(2m+1)\le P(m+1)\binom{2m+1}{m}.
\]
The two equal central coefficients in row $2m+1$ of the binomial expansion, together with its other positive coefficients, sum to $2^{2m+1}$. Hence $\binom{2m+1}{m}<2^{2m}$. By the induction hypothesis,
\[
P(2m+1)<4^{m+1}2^{2m}=4^{2m+1}.
\]
This completes both cases.
