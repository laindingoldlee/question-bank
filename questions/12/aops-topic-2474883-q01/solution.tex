Place the board with $6$ rows and $8$ columns, and number the rows $1$ to $6$ from the top.

\textbf{States.} Cover the board column by column from the left. When columns $1,\dots,k$ are completely covered, some horizontal dominoes may stick out into column $k+1$; let $X\subseteq\{1,\dots,6\}$ be the set of rows where this happens. Let $h_k(X)$ be the number of ways to cover columns $1,\dots,k$ so that, outside these columns, exactly the cells of column $k+1$ in the rows of $X$ are covered. Equivalently, $h_k(X)$ is the number of tilings of the $6\times k$ board with the cells of column $k$ in the rows of $X$ removed (those cells belong to the dominoes sticking out).

\textbf{Transitions.} Given the state $X$ after column $k$, each cell of column $k+1$ outside the rows of $X$ is covered either by a horizontal domino reaching into column $k+2$ (let $Y$ be the set of these rows) or by a vertical domino inside column $k+1$. So $X\cap Y=\varnothing$, and the rows in neither $X$ nor $Y$ must split into pairs of adjacent rows. This is possible, and then in exactly one way, if and only if these rows form blocks of consecutive rows of even length. Call the step $X\to Y$ \emph{allowed} in that case. Then
\[h_{k+1}(Y)=\sum_{X:\ X\to Y\text{ allowed}}h_k(X),\]
and $h_1(Y)=1$ if $\varnothing\to Y$ is allowed, $h_1(Y)=0$ otherwise. The rule is symmetric in $X$ and $Y$, and it is unchanged when the rows are turned upside down ($r\leftrightarrow7-r$), so a state and its mirror image always have the same counts.

For example, for $Y=\{2,5\}$: rows $1$ and $6$ cannot be paired with a neighbour outside $Y$, so they must be in $X$, and rows $3,4$ are either both in $X$ or both covered by one vertical domino. So $h_2(\{2,5\})=h_1(\{1,6\})+h_1(\{1,3,4,6\})=1+0=1$.

\textbf{Cutting the board in half.} In a tiling of the $6\times8$ board, let $X$ be the set of rows in which a domino crosses the line between columns $4$ and $5$. The part to the left of the line is a tiling of the $6\times4$ board with the cells of column $4$ in the rows of $X$ removed: $h_4(X)$ possibilities. Reflected in the line, the part to the right is a tiling of the same kind of region: again $h_4(X)$ possibilities. The two parts can be chosen independently, so the number of tilings is
\[T=\sum_{X}h_4(X)^2.\]

\textbf{The computation.} Applying the transition rule four times gives the following values. A row lists a state together with its mirror image when they differ (``states'' is $1$ or $2$); all other states have $h_1=h_2=h_3=h_4=0$.
\[\begin{array}{lcrrrr}
\text{state }X & \text{states} & h_1 & h_2 & h_3 & h_4\\ \hline
\varnothing & 1 & 1 & 13 & 41 & 281\\
\{1,2\},\ \{5,6\} & 2 & 1 & 5 & 26 & 132\\
\{2,3\},\ \{4,5\} & 2 & 0 & 3 & 4 & 59\\
\{1,4\},\ \{3,6\} & 2 & 1 & 2 & 22 & 73\\
\{3,4\} & 1 & 1 & 4 & 25 & 110\\
\{1,2,3,4\},\ \{3,4,5,6\} & 2 & 1 & 2 & 18 & 67\\
\{2,5\} & 1 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 22\\
\{1,2,4,5\},\ \{2,3,5,6\} & 2 & 0 & 1 & 2 & 22\\
\{2,3,4,5\} & 1 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 21\\
\{1,6\} & 1 & 1 & 1 & 21 & 51\\

(The first row gives the numbers of tilings of the $6\times k$ boards for $k=1,2,3,4$: $1,13,41,281$.) Hence
\begin{align*}

\end{align*}
