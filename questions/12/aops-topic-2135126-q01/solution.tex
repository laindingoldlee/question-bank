\textbf{(a)} $-7=2\cdot(-4)+1$ is odd and $-10=2\cdot(-5)$ is even; the definitions do not require $n$ or $k$ to be positive. For any integer $n$, division by $2$ gives $n=2q+r$ with an integer $q$ and $r\in\{0,1\}$ (take $q=\left\lfloor\dfrac {n}{2}\right\rfloor$), so $n$ is even ($r=0$) or odd ($r=1$). It cannot be both: $2k=2j+1$ would give $2(k-j)=1$, and $1$ is not a multiple of $2$.

\textbf{(b)} The positive divisors of $24=2^3\cdot3$ are
\[1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 12,\ 24,\]
so there are $\boxed{8}$: the $2$ odd ones $1,3$ and the $6$ even ones.

\textbf{(c)} Each positive divisor $d$ gives a negative divisor $-d$ of the same parity, so there are $\boxed{16}$ divisors: $4$ odd ($\pm1,\pm3$) and $12$ even.

In competitions ``divisors'' means positive divisors unless the problem says otherwise, so the usual answer to ``how many divisors does $24$ have'' is $8$.
