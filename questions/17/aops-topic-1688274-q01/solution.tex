Suppose such $x,y,z$ exist and put $n=z+1\ge2$ and $t=(2x)^x$, an even integer with $t\ge2$. Then
\[
y^n=t^2-1=(t-1)(t+1).
\]
The factors $t-1$ and $t+1$ are odd and differ by $2$, so any common divisor divides $2$ and is odd; hence $\gcd(t-1,t+1)=1$. If two coprime positive integers have a product that is a perfect $n$-th power, each of them is a perfect $n$-th power (every prime of $y^n$ appears with exponent a multiple of $n$, and all of it lies in one factor). So
\[
t-1=u^n,\qquad t+1=v^n
\]
for positive integers $u<v$ (note $t-1\ge1$). Then
\[
2=v^n-u^n\ge(u+1)^n-u^n\ge(u+1)^2-u^2=2u+1\ge3,
\]
a contradiction. (Here $(u+1)^n-u^n\ge(u+1)^2-u^2$ for $n\ge2$, since $(u+1)^n-u^n=(u+1)^{n-2}(u+1)^2-u^{n-2}u^2$ and $(u+1)^{n-2}\ge u^{n-2}$.) Hence no such $x,y,z$ exist. $\blacksquare$

(The condition $z\ge1$ matters: for $z=0$ every $x$ gives the solution $y=(2x)^{2x}-1$.)
