Rational coefficients force $6-\sqrt2$ to accompany $6+\sqrt2$: divide by the irreducible polynomial $(x-6)^2-2$, whose linear remainder cannot vanish at $6+\sqrt2$ unless it is zero. Real coefficients also force the complex conjugate $3-i$. Thus there are at least five distinct roots. The polynomial
\[
\boxed{(x-3)(x^2-12x+34)(x^2-6x+10)}
\]
has rational coefficients, all five roots, and degree $5$, which is odd. The least odd degree is $\boxed5$.
