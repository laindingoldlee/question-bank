On the stated interval, $0\le4y\le\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so $\sin4y=\dfrac{1}{2}$ implies $\boxed{y=\dfrac{\pi}{24}}$.
Differentiating,
\[
\boxed{\dfrac{dx}{dy}=8\sin4y\cos4y=4\sin8y}.
\]
In the open interval both factors are positive, so
\[
\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{1}{8\sqrt{x(1-x)}}
=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{16-64(x-\dfrac{1}{2})^2}}.
\]
Thus $\boxed{q=16,\ r=-64,\ s=-\dfrac{1}{2}}$. The denominator is largest at $x=\dfrac{1}{2}$, where it is $4$. Hence the minimum derivative is $\boxed{\dfrac{1}{4}}$ at $\boxed{x=\dfrac{1}{2}}$; the derivative is unbounded at the endpoints.
