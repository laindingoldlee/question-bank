\textbf{(a)} The point $(x,y)$ is on the graph of $f$ exactly when $\left(x,\dfrac y2\right)$ is on the graph of $a$: the graph of $a$ is the graph of $f$ compressed vertically by the factor $\dfrac12$ (towards the $x$-axis). Also $b(2x)=f(x)$, so $(x,y)$ is on the graph of $f$ exactly when $(2x,y)$ is on the graph of $b$: the graph of $b$ is the graph of $f$ stretched horizontally by the factor $2$ (away from the $y$-axis).

\textbf{(b)} $a(4)=\dfrac12\cdot16=8$ and $b(4)=2^2=4$, so in general $a$ and $b$ are different functions.

\textbf{(c)} $a(x)=\dfrac m2x+\dfrac c2$ and $b(x)=\dfrac m2x+c$. These agree for all $x$ exactly when $\dfrac c2=c$, i.e. $c=0$. So $\boxed{f(x)=mx\text{ for any real }m}$.
