Choose the $4$-person committee in $\dbinom{12}4=495$ ways and then the $3$-person committee from the remaining $8$ people in $\dbinom83=56$ ways:
\[
\dbinom{12}{4}\dbinom83=495\cdot56=\boxed{27720}.
\]
Choosing the $3$-person committee first gives $\dbinom{12}3\dbinom94=220\cdot126=27720$ as well; each product already counts every pair of committees exactly once. The student's expression is the average of two equal numbers, so its value $27720$ is right, but the division by $2$ is not needed: adding the two orders and halving just undoes double counting that the student introduced. (Halving would be needed if the two committees had the same size and were not distinguished, for example two unlabelled committees of $3$.)
