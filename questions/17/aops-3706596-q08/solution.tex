The angle-bisector property gives equal arcs $AR,RB$ not containing $C$, so $RA=RB$. Therefore the perpendicular from $R$ to $AB$ is the perpendicular bisector of $AB$, and $DA=DB$. Thus $\angle KAD=\angle BAD=\angle ABD$. Meanwhile $KB=KN$ makes triangle $KBN$ isosceles, so $\angle KND=\angle KNB=\angle KBN=\angle ABD$. Hence $\angle KAD=\angle KND$, and these equal angles subtend the same segment $KD$. This is the converse of the same-segment circle theorem, proving concyclicity.
