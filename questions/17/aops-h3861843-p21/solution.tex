Write $m=\lfloor ab\rfloor$, $n=\lfloor bc\rfloor$, $p=\lfloor ca\rfloor$, all positive. Then $c=\dfrac{3}{m},a=\dfrac{4}{n},b=\dfrac{5}{p}$. With $K=mnp$, the floor conditions give $K\le20<K+np$, $K\le15<K+mp$, and $K\le12<K+mn$. In particular $20<K(1+\dfrac{1}{m})\le12(1+\dfrac{1}{m})$, forcing $m=1$. Then $10<K\le12$, so $K=11$ or $12$. For $K=11$, its two factor pairs $(n,p)=(1,11),(11,1)$ each violate one strict upper inequality. For $K=12$, the conditions become $p>3$ and $n>0$, leaving $(n,p)=(1,12),(2,6),(3,4)$. These give $a=4,2,\dfrac{4}{3}$, all valid. Their sum is $\boxed{\dfrac{22}{3}}$.
