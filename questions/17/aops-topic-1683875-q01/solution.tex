\textbf{Two-digit numbers.} The special two-digit numbers are
\[
10,12,18,20,21,24,27,30,36,40,42,45,48,50,54,60,63,70,72,80,81,84,90,
\]
which can be found by testing, for each tens digit $t$, the numbers $10t+u$ against $t+u$. The only consecutive pairs are $20,21$ and $80,81$, and neither extends to three ($19$, $22$, $79$, $82$ are not special). So there are no three consecutive special two-digit numbers (and $99$ is not special, so no run crosses $100$ from below).

\textbf{From $100$ on.} Testing in order: $100$ is special, $101$ (sum $2$) is not, $102$ is, $103,104,105,106,107$ (sums $4,5,6,7,8$) are not, $108$ (sum $9$) is, $109$ (sum $10$) is not, and
\[
110=2\cdot55,\qquad 111=3\cdot37,\qquad 112=4\cdot28
\]
are all special. So the smallest three consecutive special numbers are $\boxed{110,111,112}$.
