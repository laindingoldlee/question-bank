A positive integer has exactly three positive divisors precisely when it is the square of a prime: the divisor-count formula requires one prime exponent equal to two. Each such integer is at least $4$, so a sum of two is at least $8$. But $1\equiv1\pmod M$ for every positive $M$, and $1<8$. Thus $\boxed{\text{no such }M\text{ exists}}$. Goldbach concerns sums of two primes and cannot remove this elementary counterexample.
