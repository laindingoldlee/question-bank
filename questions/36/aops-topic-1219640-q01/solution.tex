Let $x=b+c$, $y=a+c$, $z=a+b$. Then $a=\dfrac{y+z-x}{2}$ and similarly for $b,c$, so we need $x+y+z$ even and each of $x,y,z$ smaller than the sum of the other two; also $x,y,z\ge2$ and $xyz=144=2^4\cdot3^2$.

Take $2\le x\le y\le z$ with $z<x+y$. Since $z\ge\sqrt[3]{144}>5$, check $z\ge6$: the factorisations of $144$ with $z<x+y$ are $(2,8,9)$, $(3,6,8)$ and $(4,6,6)$ (others such as $(4,4,9)$, $(3,4,12)$, $(2,6,12)$ violate $z<x+y$). The first two have odd sums $19$ and $17$. So $\{x,y,z\}=\{4,6,6\}$, which gives one of $a,b,c$ equal to $\dfrac{6+6-4}{2}=4$ and the other two equal to $\dfrac{4+6-6}{2}=2$.

The solutions are $\boxed{(4,2,2),\ (2,4,2),\ (2,2,4)}$.
