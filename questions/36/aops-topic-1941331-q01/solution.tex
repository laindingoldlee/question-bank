The mean is $\mu=\dfrac{1}{n}\cdot\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}=\dfrac{n+1}{2}$. The variance is the mean of the squares minus the square of the mean:
\[
\sigma^2=\dfrac{(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}-\dfrac{(n+1)^2}{4}=\dfrac{(n+1)\bigl(2(2n+1)-3(n+1)\bigr)}{12}=\dfrac{(n+1)(n-1)}{12}=\dfrac{n^2-1}{12}.
\]
So the mean is $\boxed{\dfrac{n+1}{2}}$ and the standard deviation is $\boxed{\sqrt{\dfrac{n^2-1}{12}}=\dfrac{\sqrt{3n^2-3}}{6}}$.

The answer key is wrong: $\dfrac{\sqrt{3n^2-9}}{6}=\sqrt{\dfrac{n^2-3}{12}}$. For $n=2$ the data $1,2$ have mean $\dfrac{3}{2}$ and both deviations equal $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the standard deviation is $\dfrac{1}{2}$; our formula gives $\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{12}}=\dfrac{1}{2}$ while the key gives $\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{12}}$. (For $n=1$ the key is not even real.) The sample standard deviation $\sqrt{\dfrac{n(n+1)}{12}}$ does not match the key either.
