A radical expression is in simplest form when the radicand has no square factor other than $1$, there is no radical in a denominator, and the fraction is in lowest terms. In $\dfrac{\sqrt2}{8}$ the radicand $2$ is square-free, the denominator $8$ is rational, and $\sqrt2$ and $8$ have no common factor to cancel. So $\boxed{\text{yes}}$, $\dfrac{\sqrt2}{8}$ is in simplest radical form.

Also $\sqrt{32}=\sqrt{16\cdot2}=4\sqrt2$, and
\[
\dfrac{1}{4\sqrt2}=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{4\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt2}=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{8}.
\]
