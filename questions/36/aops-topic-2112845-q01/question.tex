Dhoni has four distinct digits $A,B,C,D$. He must arrange them in a row so that $A$ and $D$ are at the two ends. In how many ways can he do this? A student argues: ``There are $2$ choices for the first place, $2$ for the second, and then $1$ each for the third and fourth, so $2\cdot2\cdot1\cdot1=4$.'' Is the student right?
