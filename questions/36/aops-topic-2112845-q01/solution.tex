Yes. Fill the places from left to right. The first place must be $A$ or $D$: $2$ choices. The second place is a middle place, so it must be $B$ or $C$: $2$ choices. The third place gets the remaining one of $B,C$, and the fourth place the remaining one of $A,D$: $1$ choice each. So there are
\[2\cdot2\cdot1\cdot1=\boxed{4}\]
arrangements: $ABCD$, $ACBD$, $DBCA$, $DCBA$. (Equivalently, $2!$ ways to place $A,D$ at the ends times $2!$ ways to order $B,C$ in the middle.)
