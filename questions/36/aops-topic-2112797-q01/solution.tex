Let $p_N$ be the probability that the total number of cards moved is exactly $N$ at some moment, and set $p_0=1$. To reach exactly $N\ge1$, the last move before reaching $N$ has some size $k$ with $1\le k\le N$, and before it the total was exactly $N-k$. Hence
\[p_N=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\dfrac{1}{2^k}\,p_{N-k}.\]
We show by strong induction that $p_N=\dfrac{1}{2}$ for all $N\ge1$. For $N=1$: $p_1=\dfrac{1}{2}p_0=\dfrac{1}{2}$. If $p_1=\cdots=p_{N-1}=\dfrac{1}{2}$, then
\[p_N=\dfrac{1}{2^N}+\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\dfrac{1}{2^k}=\dfrac{1}{2^N}+\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{2^{N-1}}\right)=\dfrac{1}{2} .\]
So the probability is $\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}}$.

\emph{A direct view.} Moving $n$ cards with probability $\dfrac{1}{2^n}=\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{n-1}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}$ is the same as moving the cards one at a time and tossing a fair coin after each card, ending the current move at the first head. The second pile contains exactly $52$ cards at the end of some move precisely when the coin tossed after the $52$nd card shows heads, which has probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
