\textbf{(a)} For $n=1$, $n^{10}-10=-9$, which is not divisible by $10$. (Likewise $n=2$ gives $1014$.)

\textbf{(b)} Since $10$ is divisible by $10$, the number $n^{10}-10$ is divisible by $10$ exactly when $n^{10}$ is. Now $10=2\cdot5$ with $2$ and $5$ prime: $2\mid n^{10}$ forces $2\mid n$, and $5\mid n^{10}$ forces $5\mid n$ (a prime dividing a product divides a factor). So $10\mid n$. Conversely, if $10\mid n$ then $10\mid n^{10}$. Hence $n^{10}-10$ is divisible by $10$ exactly when $\boxed{n\text{ is a multiple of }10}$.
