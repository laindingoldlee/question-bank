Multiplying the three congruences modulo $n$ gives
\[
(abc)^3\equiv-(abc)^n\pmod n.
\]
For odd $n>1$, division by the unit $(abc)^3$ shows that $-1$ is an even power modulo every prime divisor $p$ of $n$, since $n-3$ is even. Thus $-1$ must be a quadratic residue modulo $p$, forcing $p\equiv1\pmod4$.

Now $12!=2^{10}3^5 5^2 7\cdot11$. Its only odd prime factor congruent to $1$ modulo $4$ is $5$. The only possible odd divisors are therefore $1,5,25$. All three work: the case $1$ is automatic; for $5$ take $a=b=c=2$, and for $25$ take $a=b=c=7$, using $7^2\equiv-1\pmod{25}$ and $25-3=22$.

Every even divisor works by taking $a=b=c=-1$, because each expression is $-1+1=0$. The number of even divisors is $10\cdot6\cdot3\cdot2\cdot2=720$. Including the three odd cases gives $\boxed{723}$.
