Up to interchanging $x,y$, all solutions are $\boxed{x=d(m^2-n^2),\ y=2dmn,\ z=d(m^2+n^2)}$, where $d,m,n$ are positive integers, $m>n$, $\gcd(m,n)=1$, and $m,n$ have opposite parity. Expansion verifies every listed triple. Conversely divide a triple by its common gcd to make it primitive. The two legs cannot both be odd, by reduction modulo 4, and cannot both be even; take $y$ even and $x,z$ odd. Then $\left(\dfrac{z+x}{2}\right)\left(\dfrac{z-x}{2}\right)=\left(\dfrac{y}{2}\right)^2$. These positive integer factors are coprime: a common divisor would divide both $x,z$, hence also $y$. Coprime factors of a square are separately squares, say $m^2,n^2$. Adding and subtracting gives $z=m^2+n^2,x=m^2-n^2,y=2mn$. Coprimality and opposite parity follow from primitiveness. Restore the common factor $d$.
