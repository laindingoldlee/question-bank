(a) For $\gcd(d,10)=1 $, period dividing $ k $ is equivalent to $ d\mid10^k-1 $. Excluding proper periods 1, 2, 3 from period dividing 6 gives $\tau(999999)-\tau(999)-\tau(99)+\tau(9)$. Here $ 999999=3^3\cdot7\cdot11\cdot13\cdot37 $, $ 999=3^3\cdot37 $, and $ 99=3^2\cdot11 $. Thus the count is $ 64-8-6+3=\boxed{53}$. The denominator 1 cancels automatically in inclusion-exclusion. (b) There are infinitely many: $ d=7\cdot2^k $ for $ k\ge0 $. Removing factors 2 and 5 determines the repeating period, and the remaining denominator 7 has order 6 for 10, since $ 10^6\equiv1\pmod7 $ while $ 10,10^2,10^3 $ are not 1 modulo 7. Thus each has least period 6.
