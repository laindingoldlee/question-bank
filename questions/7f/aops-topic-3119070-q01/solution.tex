Write the terms $a,a+d,\ldots,a+9d$. Each of $2,3,5$ must divide $d$: otherwise every block of $p$ consecutive indices contains a term divisible by $p$, and two disjoint such blocks fit among ten terms. Thus $d\ge30$ and the sum $10a+45d\ge1360$. Equality is attained by $a=1,d=30$. To verify pairwise coprimality, any common prime divisor divides $30(j-i)$ with $1\le j-i\le9$. It cannot be $2,3,5$ because every term is $1$ modulo these primes; the only remaining possibility is $7$. Two indices then differ by $7$, so the lower index is $0,1,$ or $2$, whose terms $1,31,61$ are not divisible by $7$. Thus all terms are pairwise coprime, and the answer is $\boxed{1360}$.
