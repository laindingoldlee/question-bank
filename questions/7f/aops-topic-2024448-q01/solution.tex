We have $a+b=2-c$ and $a^2+b^2=12-c^2$. For real $a,b$,
\[
(a+b)^2\le 2(a^2+b^2),
\]
because $2(a^2+b^2)-(a+b)^2=(a-b)^2\ge0$. Conversely, given $s$ and $q$ with $s^2\le 2q$, the numbers $a,b=\dfrac{s\pm\sqrt{2q-s^2}}{2}$ are real with $a+b=s$ and $a^2+b^2=q$. So $c$ is possible exactly when
\[
(2-c)^2\le 2(12-c^2)\iff 3c^2-4c-20\le0\iff (3c-10)(c+2)\le 0\iff -2\le c\le \dfrac{10}{3}.
\]
Both ends occur: $c=-2$ with $a=b=2$, and $c=\dfrac{10}{3}$ with $a=b=-\dfrac{2}{3}$. The difference is
\[
\dfrac{10}{3}-(-2)=\boxed{\dfrac{16}{3}}.
\]
