Triangle $BDC$ is isosceles with $BD=DC$, so its base angles are equal: $\angle DBC=\angle DCB=70^\circ$. Hence
\[\angle BDC=180^\circ-70^\circ-70^\circ=40^\circ.\]
Since $D$ lies on segment $AC$, the angles $\angle ADB$ and $\angle BDC$ are supplementary, so
\[\angle ADB=180^\circ-40^\circ=140^\circ.\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0), C=(6,0), D=(2,0);
pair B=D+4*dir(40);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9)); draw(B--D,linewidth(0.9));
dot(A); dot(B); dot(C); dot(D);
label("$A$",A,SW); label("$B$",B,N); label("$C$",C,SE); label("$D$",D,S);
draw(arc(C,0.8,110,180)); label("$70^\circ$",C+0.85*dir(145),NW,fontsize(8pt));
draw(arc(B,0.7,220,290)); label("$70^\circ$",B+0.75*dir(255),dir(255),fontsize(8pt));
draw(arc(D,0.55,0,40)); label("$40^\circ$",D+0.6*dir(20),E,fontsize(8pt));
draw(arc(D,0.4,40,180),red); label("$140^\circ$",D+0.45*dir(110),N,red+fontsize(8pt));
pair m1=(B+D)/2, m2=(D+C)/2;
draw(shift(m1)*rotate(130)*((-0.12,0)--(0.12,0))); draw(shift(m2)*rotate(90)*((-0.12,0)--(0.12,0)));
\end{asy}

The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(D) }140}$.
