Let $g=\gcd(x,y)$ and write $x=gx'$, $y=gy'$. Then $\gcd(x',y')=1$ (a common divisor $d>1$ of $x'$ and $y'$ would make $gd$ a common divisor of $x$ and $y$ larger than $g$).

\textbf{Step 1: $a=x'$ and $b=y'$.} We have $\dfrac {a}{b}=\dfrac {x}{y}=\dfrac{x'}{y'}$, so $ay'=bx'$. Then $x'\mid ay'$ and $\gcd(x',y')=1$, so $x'\mid a$; similarly $a\mid bx'$ and $\gcd(a,b)=1$ give $a\mid x'$. Both are positive, so $a=x'$, and then $b=y'$. (A fraction has only one representation in lowest terms with positive denominator.)

\textbf{Step 2: $\operatorname{lcm}(x,y)=gx'y'$.} The number $L=gx'y'$ is a common multiple, since $L=xy'=yx'$. Let $M$ be any positive common multiple of $x$ and $y$. Since $x\mid M$, write $M=xk=gx'k$. Since $y=gy'$ divides $M=gx'k$, we get $y'\mid x'k$, and as $\gcd(x',y')=1$, $y'\mid k$. So $M=gx'k\ge gx'y'=L$. Hence $L$ is the least common multiple.

Combining the steps,
\[\operatorname{lcm}(x,y)=gx'y'=x\cdot y'=xb\qquad\text{and}\qquad\operatorname{lcm}(x,y)=y\cdot x'=ay. \qquad\blacksquare\]
So the poster's formula is always correct for positive integers. (For example, $\dfrac{12}{18}=\dfrac{2}{3}$ gives $\operatorname{lcm}(12,18)=12\cdot3=36$.)
