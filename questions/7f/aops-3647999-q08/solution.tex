Each numerator equals its denominator plus the common quantity $K=16-x^2-y^2-z^2$. Hence the displayed equation is equivalent to $\dfrac{K\sum1}{(y+z)^2}=0$, so $x^2+y^2+z^2=16$. For positive variables, $xyz>0$, but taking $x=\varepsilon$ and $y=z=\sqrt{\dfrac{(16-\varepsilon^2)}{2}}$ makes the product tend to zero. Thus there is $\boxed{\text{no minimum; the infimum is }0}$. If maximum had been intended, AM--GM on the squares gives $\dfrac{64\sqrt3}{9}$, attained when all three variables equal $\dfrac{4}{\sqrt3}$.
