Measure the job in \emph{man-days}: one man working for one day does one man-day of work. Twelve men working for $25$ days do $12\times25=300$ man-days, so reaping the field takes $300$ man-days of work.

A single man does $1$ man-day per day, so alone he needs $300=25\times12$ days; this is the line ``$1$ man can reap the field in $25\times12$ days''. Twenty men together do $20$ man-days per day, so they need
\[
\dfrac{300}{20}=\dfrac{25\times12}{20}=\boxed{15}\text{ days}.
\]
The number of men and the number of days are inversely proportional: their product stays $300$ (check: $20\times15=300$).
