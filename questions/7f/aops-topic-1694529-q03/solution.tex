Write $n^2+8n+44=(n+4)^2+28=k^2$ with $k$ a positive integer. Then
\[
(k-n-4)(k+n+4)=28 .
\]
The two factors have the same parity (they differ by $2n+8$) and the second is larger than $5$. The factorisations of $28$ into two factors of the same parity are $2\cdot14$ only (as $1\cdot28$ and $4\cdot7$ have mixed parity). So $k+n+4=14$ and $k-n-4=2$, giving $k=8$ and $n=2$. Check: $4+16+44=64=8^2$. The sum is $\boxed{2}$.
