Let the primes be $p,q,r$ with $pqr=7(p+q+r)$. Then $7$ divides $pqr$, so one of them, say $r$, is $7$. Dividing by $7$:
\[
pq=p+q+7\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (p-1)(q-1)=8 .
\]
With $p\le q$ the factorisations are $1\cdot8$ and $2\cdot4$, giving $(p,q)=(2,9)$ or $(3,5)$. Since $9$ is not prime, $(p,q)=(3,5)$. The primes are $\boxed{3,\ 5,\ 7}$: indeed $3\cdot5\cdot7=105=7\cdot15$.
