The divisor-count formula can be evaluated exactly for all 999 candidates; each divisor is counted once by the following exhaustive check:
\begin{verbatim}
tau=[0]*1000
for d in range(1,1000):
    for n in range(d,1000,d):tau[n]+=1
M=max(tau[1:])
assert M==32 and [n for n in range(1,1000) if tau[n]==M]==[840]
\end{verbatim}
Thus $\boxed{840}$ is the unique maximizer, with 32 divisors. Its factorisation $ 2^3\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7 $ confirms $(3+1)2^3=32 $.
