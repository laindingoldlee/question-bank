Let the triangle be $ABC$ with $AB=6$ and $AC=BC=5$, and let $H$ be the foot of the altitude from $C$. In an isosceles triangle this altitude bisects the base, so $AH=HB=3$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(-3,0),B=(3,0),C=(0,4),H=(0,0);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));draw(C--H,dashed);
real s=0.3;draw(H+(s,0)--H+(s,s)--H+(0,s));
label("$A$",A,SW);label("$B$",B,SE);label("$C$",C,N);label("$H$",H,S);
label("$5$",(A+C)/2,NW);label("$5$",(B+C)/2,NE);
label("$3$",(A+H)/2,S);label("$3$",(B+H)/2,S);label("$4$",(C+H)/2,W);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

In the right triangle $AHC$, by the Pythagorean theorem, $CH=\sqrt{5^2-3^2}=4$. Hence
\[
[ABC]=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot AB\cdot CH=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot6\cdot4=\boxed{12}.
\]
(Heron's formula agrees: $s=8$ and $\sqrt{8\cdot3\cdot3\cdot2}=12$.)
