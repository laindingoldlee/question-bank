Triangles that share a vertex and have bases on the same line have areas in the ratio of their bases.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(-5,0),C=(7,0),Bv=(1,-3.5),D=(-1.4,4.9),X=(0,0);
draw(A--Bv--C--D--cycle,linewidth(1));draw(A--C);draw(Bv--D);
dot(X);
label("$A$",A,W);label("$B$",Bv,S);label("$C$",C,E);label("$D$",D,N);label("$E$",X,(1,1.6));
label("$25$",(A+Bv+X)/3);label("$35$",(Bv+C+X)/3);label("$35$",(A+D+X)/3);label("$?$",(C+D+X)/3);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Triangles $ABE$ and $ADE$ have the same height from $A$ to line $BD$, so $\dfrac{BE}{ED}=\dfrac{[ABE]}{[ADE]}=\dfrac{25}{35}$. Triangles $CBE$ and $CDE$ have the same height from $C$, so
\[
\dfrac{[BCE]}{[CDE]}=\dfrac{BE}{ED}=\dfrac{25}{35},\qquad [CDE]=\dfrac{35\cdot35}{25}=49 .
\]
The area of $ABCD$ is $25+35+35+49=\boxed{144}$.
