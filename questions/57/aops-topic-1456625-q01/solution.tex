\textbf{Recursion.} Let $a_n$ be the number of ways to climb $n$ stairs. The first jump is $1$ or $3$ stairs, so $a_n=a_{n-1}+a_{n-3}$ for $n\ge3$, with $a_0=a_1=a_2=1$ (for $n<3$ only $1$-jumps are possible). Then
\[
a_3=2,\ a_4=3,\ a_5=4,\ a_6=6,\ a_7=9,\ a_8=13,\ a_9=\boxed{19}.
\]

\textbf{Check by counting $3$-jumps.} With $j$ jumps of $3$ there are $9-3j$ jumps of $1$, so $9-2j$ jumps in all, of which the $3$-jumps can be placed in $\dbinom{9-2j}{j}$ ways: $\dbinom90+\dbinom71+\dbinom52+\dbinom33=1+7+10+1=19$.
