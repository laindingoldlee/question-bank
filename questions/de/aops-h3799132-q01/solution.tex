The farthest point of the larger square from its center is at distance $\dfrac{17}{\sqrt2}<15$. Consequently its intersection with the smaller square is exactly its intersection with the $90^\circ$ wedge formed by the two sides through the common center; the smaller square's two far sides impose no further restriction. Rotating this wedge through multiples of $90^\circ$ partitions the larger square into four pieces of equal area, since the larger square has that rotational symmetry. Thus the overlap area is $\boxed{\dfrac{17^2}{4}=\dfrac{289}{4}}$, independent of orientation.
