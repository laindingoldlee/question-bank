Choose the triple's rank in $13$ ways and its suits in $\binom43=4$ ways; then choose the pair's rank in $12$ ways and suits in $\binom42=6$ ways. This gives $3744$ full houses among $\binom{52}{5}=2598960$ hands, for probability $\boxed{\dfrac{6}{4165}}$. In the proposed expression, the initial selected triple card can be any of its three members, and the initially selected pair card can be either of its two members. Each full house is counted $3\cdot2=6$ times.
