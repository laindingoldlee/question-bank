In the classroom game \emph{Greedy Pig}, a fair die is rolled repeatedly and the number shown is added to a player's running total, except that a roll of $2$ wipes out the total: the player then scores $0$ and is out. Before any roll a player may sit down, and then keeps the current total as the score.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] A student argues: ``The chance of a $2$ on each roll is $16.5\%$, so the chance of a $2$ within five rolls is $5\cdot16.5\%=82.5\%$, and within six rolls it is $99\%$.'' Find the correct probabilities of at least one $2$ in five rolls and in six rolls, and explain what is wrong with the argument.
\item[(b)] A player is standing with a total of $t$ points. Show that rolling once more and then sitting down gives a larger expected score than sitting down now if and only if $t<19$.
\end{enumerate}
