For odd $p$, there are $\dfrac{p+1}{2}$ square residues. Each choice of $a,b$ determines at most one $c$, so $|U_p|\le\dfrac{p(p+1)}{2}$. Therefore every prime $p\le61$ fails (and $p=2$ plainly fails).

For a prime $p$, the number of $k$th-power residues including zero is $1+\dfrac{p-1}{\gcd(k,p-1)}$. Indeed the nonzero residues form a cyclic group: writing them as powers of a generator reduces the power map to multiplication by $k$ modulo $p-1$, with kernel size $\gcd(k,p-1)$.
If one of the cube or fifth-power maps is a permutation, the unrestricted variable is determined uniquely, so the count is the product of the other two residue-set sizes.
The next primes give
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
p&\text{unrestricted variable}&|U_p|\\\hline
67&c&34\cdot23=782\\
71&b&36\cdot15=540\\
73&c&37\cdot25=925\\
79&c&40\cdot27=1080\\
83&b,c&42\cdot83=3486
\end{array}
\]
For $83$, both exponents are coprime to $82$. All smaller primes have been excluded, so $\boxed{p=83}$.
