The pair $(x,y)$ is a uniformly random point of the unit square, so probabilities are areas. The event $x+y>1$ is the triangle above the diagonal $x+y=1$:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
fill((1,0)--(1,1)--(0,1)--cycle,paleblue);
draw((0,0)--(1,0)--(1,1)--(0,1)--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw((1,0)--(0,1),blue+linewidth(1));
label("$0$",(0,0),SW);label("$1$",(1,0),S);label("$1$",(0,1),W);
label("$x$",(0.5,0),S);label("$y$",(0,0.5),W);
label("$x+y>1$",(0.7,0.7));label("$x+y=1$",(0.42,0.58),SW,blue+fontsize(8pt));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The diagonal cuts the square into two congruent triangles (the map $(x,y)\mapsto(1-x,1-y)$ swaps them), and the diagonal itself has area $0$. So the probability is
\[
\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}}.
\]
