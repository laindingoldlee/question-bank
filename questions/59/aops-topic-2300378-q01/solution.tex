The squares not exceeding $50$ are $1,4,9,16,25,36,49$. Work by the largest square used.
\begin{itemize}
\item $49$: the rest is $1$, giving $49+1$.
\item $36$: the rest, $14$, must be a sum of distinct squares from $1,4,9$ (as $16,25>14$); the only way is $9+4+1$. This gives $36+9+4+1$.
\item $25$: the rest, $25$, must use distinct squares from $1,4,9,16$. With $16$ the remainder $9$ is $9$ itself ($4+1=5\ne9$); without $16$ the most is $1+4+9=14<25$. This gives $25+16+9$.
\item Largest square at most $16$: the total is at most $16+9+4+1=30<50$.
\end{itemize}
So there are $\boxed{3}$ ways: $49+1$, $36+9+4+1$ and $25+16+9$.
