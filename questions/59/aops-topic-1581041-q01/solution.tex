Call the identical coins "plain" and the others $w,x,y,z$.

\textbf{Step 1: split the four different coins.} Suppose they end up in $k$ groups ($1\le k\le4$). The number of ways to split $\{w,x,y,z\}$ into $k$ unlabelled nonempty blocks is the Stirling number $S(4,k)$:
\[
S(4,1)=1,\quad S(4,2)=7,\quad S(4,3)=6,\quad S(4,4)=1 .
\]
These $k$ groups are distinguishable from each other by the different coins they contain.

\textbf{Step 2: place the four plain coins.} Say $j$ of them join the $k$ groups from Step 1; this can be done in $\dbinom{j+k-1}{k-1}$ ways (stars and bars). The other $4-j$ plain coins form new groups of plain coins only. There are at most $4-k$ such groups, and since they contain only identical coins, they are determined by their sizes: the number of ways is the number $p(4-j,\,4-k)$ of partitions of $4-j$ into at most $4-k$ parts. (Here $p(0,\cdot)=1$, $p(n,0)=0$ for $n\ge1$, and $p(4,3)=4$, $p(4,2)=3$, $p(3,3)=p(3,4)=3$, $p(3,2)=2$, $p(2,\cdot)=2$ for at least $2$ parts, $p(n,1)=1$.)

\textbf{Step 3: add up.}
\begin{itemize}
\item $k=1$ (at most $3$ plain groups): $\sum_j p(4-j,3)=4+3+2+1+1=11$; times $S(4,1)=1$: $11$.
\item $k=2$ (at most $2$ plain groups): $\sum_j (j+1)\,p(4-j,2)=1\cdot3+2\cdot2+3\cdot2+4\cdot1+5\cdot1=22$; times $7$: $154$.
\item $k=3$ (at most $1$ plain group): $\sum_j\dbinom{j+2}{2}=1+3+6+10+15=35$; times $6$: $210$.
\item $k=4$ (no plain group): all four plain coins join the four groups, $\dbinom{7}{3}=35$ ways; times $1$: $35$.
\end{itemize}
The total is
\[
11+154+210+35=\boxed{410}.
\]
