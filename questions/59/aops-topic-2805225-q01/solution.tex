Let $d=\gcd(a,b)$, $a=dx$, $b=dy$ with $\gcd(x,y)=1$. Then $ab=d^2xy$ is a square, so $xy$ is a perfect square. Since $x$ and $y$ are coprime positive integers, each prime appears in only one of them, so each prime's exponent in $x$ equals its (even) exponent in $xy$: $x$ is a perfect square, say $x=u^2$.

Then $a\cdot d=d^2x=(du)^2$ is a perfect square, so $a$ is a friend of $d=\gcd(a,b)$. $\blacksquare$

(The restriction to positive integers matters: $a=-2$, $b=-8$ have $ab=16$, but $a\cdot\gcd(a,b)=-4$.)
