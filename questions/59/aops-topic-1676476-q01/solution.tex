The first equality is correct: $\sqrt{-1}$ denotes $i$, and $i\cdot i=-1$. The error is the step $\sqrt{-1}\cdot\sqrt{-1}=\sqrt{(-1)(-1)}$. The rule $\sqrt a\cdot\sqrt b=\sqrt{ab}$ is proved only for $a,b\ge0$: both sides are then nonnegative and have the same square $ab$. For negative numbers it fails, exactly as here: $\sqrt{-1}\cdot\sqrt{-1}=i^2=-1$, while $\sqrt{(-1)(-1)}=\sqrt1=1$. (Squaring both sides only shows that the two sides are equal or opposite.)

If $a=-a$, then $2a=0$, so $a=0$. Hence no positive number (indeed no nonzero number) equals its negation. $\blacksquare$
