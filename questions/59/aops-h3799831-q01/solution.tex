No digit can exceed six, since $7!>999$. In fact six cannot occur: its factorial alone is $720$, which would force the hundreds digit to be at least seven. If every digit is at most four, the factorial sum is at most $72<100$. Thus at least one digit is five.
If two digits are five, they cannot include the hundreds position, and the only possible hundreds digits are two or three; neither $255$ nor $355$ equals its factorial sum. Three fives also fail. With exactly one five, the sum is at most $120+24+24=168$, so the hundreds digit is one. If the tens digit is five, $150+c=121+c!$ gives $c!-c=29$, which fails for $c=0,1,2,3,4$. If the units digit is five, $105+10b=121+b!$ gives $10b-b!=16$. For $b=0,1,2,3,4$, the values are $-1,9,18,24,16$, so $b=4$. Hence the only answer is $\boxed{145}$, and $1!+4!+5!=145$.
