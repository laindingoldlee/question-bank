Put $ m=n-j $ and interpret $ n-m+1 $ as the number of integers $ t $ from $ m $ through $ n $. Then \[\sum_{m=k}^n(n-m+1)\binom mk=\sum_{t=k}^n\sum_{m=k}^t\binom mk=\sum_{t=k}^n\binom{t+1}{k+1}=\binom{n+2}{k+2}.\]Each equality of binomial sums follows by classifying subsets by their largest element, the hockey-stick identity.
