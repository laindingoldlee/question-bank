Five consecutive odd numbers contain a multiple of five, and at least one multiple of three; thus every product is divisible by $15$. At $n=1$ the product is $3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot9\cdot11$, and at $n=2$ it is $5\cdot7\cdot9\cdot11\cdot13$. Their gcd is $3^2\cdot5\cdot7\cdot11$. At $n=4$ the product $9\cdot11\cdot13\cdot15\cdot17$ removes the factor seven from that gcd. At $n=6$, the product $13\cdot15\cdot17\cdot19\cdot21$ removes eleven. At $n=3$, $7\cdot9\cdot11\cdot13\cdot15$ still has two threes, but $n=5$ gives $11\cdot13\cdot15\cdot17\cdot19$, with just one three. Thus the gcd of these sample products is $15$, proving the universal greatest divisor is $\boxed{15}$.
