The exponent $x^2-x+6=\left(x-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2+\dfrac{23}{4}$ is always positive. A product is $0$ exactly when one factor is $0$ (and the other is defined).
\begin{itemize}
\item $x^2-5x+6=(x-2)(x-3)=0$ gives $x=2$ or $x=3$. For these, the second factor is $(-2)^{8}$ and $(-2)^{12}$ (integer exponents), which are defined, so both are solutions.
\item $\left(x^2-5x+4\right)^{x^2-x+6}=0$ exactly when the base is $0$ (a nonzero number raised to a power is never $0$, and $0$ raised to a positive power is $0$): $x^2-5x+4=(x-1)(x-4)=0$, so $x=1$ or $x=4$, with exponents $6$ and $18$. Both are solutions.
\end{itemize}
The solutions are $1,2,3,4$, with sum $\boxed{10}$.
