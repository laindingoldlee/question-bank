There are $\dbinom82=28$ equally likely pairs. Two of the points are one unit apart exactly when they are neighbours along the boundary of the square: any other pair is at distance $\sqrt2$, $2$ or more. Going round the square there are $8$ neighbouring pairs.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3.2cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
draw((0,0)--(2,0)--(2,2)--(0,2)--cycle,gray);
pair[] P={(0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(2,1),(2,2),(1,2),(0,2),(0,1)};
for(int i=0;i<8;++i) draw(P[i]--P[(i+1)%8],red+linewidth(1.2));
draw(P[1]--P[3],blue+dashed); label("$\sqrt2$",(1.5,0.5),NW,blue);
for(pair p:P) dot(p,linewidth(4));
label("$1$",(0.5,0),S,red); label("$1$",(1.5,0),S,red);
\end{asy}

So the probability is $\dfrac{8}{28}=\boxed{\textbf{(B) }\dfrac{2}{7}}$.
