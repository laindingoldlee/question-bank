We claim
\[
n^8+n^7+1=(n^2+n+1)(n^6-n^4+n^3-n+1).
\]
Expanding the right side: $n^2(n^6-n^4+n^3-n+1)=n^8-n^6+n^5-n^3+n^2$, $n(\cdots)=n^7-n^5+n^4-n^2+n$, and $1\cdot(\cdots)=n^6-n^4+n^3-n+1$; adding, everything cancels except $n^8+n^7+1$. (The factor $n^2+n+1$ can be guessed because a root $\omega$ of $n^2+n+1$ satisfies $\omega^3=1$, so $\omega^8+\omega^7+1=\omega^2+\omega+1=0$.)

For $n\ge2$, the first factor is $n^2+n+1\ge7>1$, and the second is
\[
n^6-n^4+n^3-n+1=n^4(n^2-1)+n(n^2-1)+1\ge1+1>1 .
\]
So $n^8+n^7+1$ is a product of two integers greater than $1$, hence composite. $\blacksquare$
