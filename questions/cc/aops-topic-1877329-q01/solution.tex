Since $\dfrac{1}{(2k-1)2k}=\dfrac{1}{2k-1}-\dfrac{1}{2k}$, the sum of the first $n$ terms is
\[
S_n=\left(1-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)+\left(\dfrac{1}{3}-\dfrac{1}{4}\right)+\dots+\left(\dfrac{1}{2n-1}-\dfrac{1}{2n}\right).
\]
This does not telescope, but it can be rewritten. Adding and subtracting the even terms,
\[
S_n=\left(1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\dots+\dfrac{1}{2n}\right)-2\left(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}+\dots+\dfrac{1}{2n}\right)=\left(1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\dots+\dfrac{1}{2n}\right)-\left(1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\dots+\dfrac{1}{n}\right),
\]
so
\[
S_n=\dfrac{1}{n+1}+\dfrac{1}{n+2}+\dots+\dfrac{1}{2n}.
\]
Now compare with $\ln$. Since $\dfrac{1}{x}$ is decreasing, for each integer $j\ge2$
\[
\int_j^{j+1}\dfrac{dx}{x}<\dfrac{1}{j}<\int_{j-1}^{j}\dfrac{dx}{x}.
\]
Adding these for $j=n+1,\dots,2n$ gives
\[
\ln\dfrac{2n+1}{n+1}<S_n<\ln\dfrac{2n}{n}=\ln2 .
\]
As $n\to\infty$, $\dfrac{2n+1}{n+1}\to2$, so $S_n\to\ln2$. All terms of the series are positive, so its sum is the limit of $S_n$:
\[
\dfrac{1}{1\cdot2}+\dfrac{1}{3\cdot4}+\dfrac{1}{5\cdot6}+\cdots=\boxed{\ln2}\approx0.693 .
\]
