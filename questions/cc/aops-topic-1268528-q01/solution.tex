We need the number of solutions of $a+b+c+d=9$ with $1\le a,b,c,d\le 6$. Without the upper bound, write $9$ as a row of $9$ stones and place $3$ sticks in the $8$ gaps between stones (each roll is at least $1$, so no two sticks share a gap and no stick goes at an end): $\dbinom83=56$ ways.

The upper bound never matters: if one roll were $7$ or more, the other three rolls would total at most $2$, which is impossible. So the answer is $\boxed{56}$.

The poster's ``$7$ forbidden places'' idea was the problem: with each part at least $1$, the sticks go into gaps \emph{between} stones, not into all positions of a row of $10$.
