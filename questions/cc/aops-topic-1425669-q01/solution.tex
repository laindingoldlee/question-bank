\textbf{1.} By the difference of squares,
\[
(x-1)^2-(x-4)^2=\big((x-1)-(x-4)\big)\big((x-1)+(x-4)\big)=3(2x-5)=6x-15 .
\]
The $x^2$ terms cancel, so the expression is linear. It takes every real value; for example it equals $-15-6M$ at $x=-M$, which is below any proposed minimum for $M$ large. So there is no minimum.

\textbf{2.} Expanding and completing the square,
\[
(x-1)^2+(x-4)^2=2x^2-10x+17=2\left(x-\dfrac{5}{2}\right)^2+\dfrac{9}{2} .
\]
The square is at least $0$ and equals $0$ only at $x=\dfrac{5}{2}$. So the minimum value is $\boxed{\dfrac{9}{2}}$, at $x=\dfrac{5}{2}$ (the midpoint of $1$ and $4$).
