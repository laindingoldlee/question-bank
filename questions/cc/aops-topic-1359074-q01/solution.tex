A choice is determined by the numbers $a,b,c\ge0$ of items of the first, second and third flavours, with
\[
a+b+c=4 .
\]
\textbf{Stars and bars.} Represent a choice by a row of $4$ stars (the items) and $2$ bars (separators between the flavours): the stars before the first bar are flavour $1$, those between the bars flavour $2$, and those after the second bar flavour $3$. For example
\[
\star\star\,|\,\star\,|\,\star \;\leftrightarrow\; (2,1,1),\qquad |\,\star\star\star\star\,| \;\leftrightarrow\; (0,4,0).
\]
Every choice gives exactly one such row, and every row of $4$ stars and $2$ bars gives exactly one choice, so this is a bijection. A row is a sequence of $6$ symbols in which we choose the $2$ places for the bars, so the number of choices is
\[
\dbinom{6}{2}=\boxed{15}.
\]
(In general, the number of ways to choose $n$ items from $k$ kinds, repetition allowed, is $\dbinom{n+k-1}{k-1}$: $n$ stars and $k-1$ bars.)
