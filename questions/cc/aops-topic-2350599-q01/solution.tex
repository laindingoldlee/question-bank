The equation requires $x\ne0$ and $x\ne-1$. For such $x$,
\[\dfrac{4x^3+4x^2}{x}=4x(x+1),\qquad\dfrac{x}{x^3+x^2}=\dfrac{1}{x(x+1)}.\]
Put $u=x(x+1)=x^2+x$, which is not $0$. The equation becomes $4u+\dfrac1u=0$, that is, $4u^2=-1$, so $u=\dfrac i2$ or $u=-\dfrac i2$.

So the roots are the roots of $x^2+x-\dfrac i2=0$ and of $x^2+x+\dfrac i2=0$. None of them is $0$ or $-1$ (those give $u=0$), the two quadratics have no common root (they give different values of $u$), and neither has a double root (the discriminants $1\pm2i$ are not $0$). So there are four roots, all non-real (a real $x$ gives a real $u$). By Vieta's formulas each quadratic has root sum $-1$, so the sum of all roots is $\boxed{-2}$.
