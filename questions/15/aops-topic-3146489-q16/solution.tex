The number factors as $2^6 3^6 5^6$. A divisor is $2^u3^v5^w$, with each exponent between $0$ and $6$. Apart from the all-zero triple, which gives $1$, it is a perfect power exactly when the exponents have a common prime divisor. Such a divisor must be $2,3$, or $5$. The triples with all exponents divisible by these primes number $4^3=64$, $3^3=27$, and $2^3=8$, respectively. Intersections require divisibility by $6,10,15$, giving $2^3=8,1,1$ triples; the triple intersection gives one. The all-zero triple is included by this union and counted once. Inclusion--exclusion gives $64+27+8-8-1-1+1=\boxed{90}$.
