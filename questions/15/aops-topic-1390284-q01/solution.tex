Add $2x^2+1$ to both sides of $x^4=-8x+7$ to complete a square on the left:
\[
x^4+2x^2+1=2x^2-8x+8\iff (x^2+1)^2=2(x-2)^2 .
\]
(The constant $1$ was chosen so that the right side is a perfect square: we need $(x^2+t)^2=2tx^2-8x+7+t^2$ to have zero discriminant on the right, i.e. $64=8t(t^2+7)$, which holds for $t=1$.) Hence
\[
x^2+1=\sqrt2(x-2)\qquad\text{or}\qquad x^2+1=-\sqrt2(x-2).
\]
\begin{itemize}
\item $x^2-\sqrt2x+1+2\sqrt2=0$: discriminant $2-4-8\sqrt2<0$, giving $x=\dfrac{\sqrt2\pm i\sqrt{2+8\sqrt2}}{2}$.
\item $x^2+\sqrt2x+1-2\sqrt2=0$: discriminant $2-4+8\sqrt2=8\sqrt2-2>0$, giving $x=\dfrac{-\sqrt2\pm\sqrt{8\sqrt2-2}}{2}$.
\end{itemize}
So the solutions are
\[
\boxed{x=\dfrac{-\sqrt2\pm\sqrt{8\sqrt2-2}}{2},\qquad x=\dfrac{\sqrt2\pm i\sqrt{2+8\sqrt2}}{2}},
\]
the first two real (about $0.819$ and $-2.233$).
