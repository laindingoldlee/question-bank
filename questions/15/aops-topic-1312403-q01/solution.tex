The rule ``$m_1m_2=-1$'' is only valid for two lines that both have slopes, i.e. neither is vertical. Its proof uses the slopes as numbers: the directions $(1,m_1)$ and $(1,m_2)$ are perpendicular exactly when their dot product $1+m_1m_2$ is $0$. A vertical line has direction $(0,1)$ and no slope at all, so the rule does not apply to it. ``Undefined'' is not a number and cannot be multiplied by $0$, so no equation $0=-1$ follows. (The general perpendicularity test for directions $(a_1,b_1)$ and $(a_2,b_2)$ is $a_1a_2+b_1b_2=0$, which works for vertical lines too.)
