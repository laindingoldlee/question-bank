(a) For a fixed value of $x$ with $2x^2+x-3>0$, say $x=3$ (area $18$), many rectangles have that area, for example $3\times6$ (perimeter $18$) and $2\times9$ (perimeter $22$). So without more information the perimeter is not determined; the poster's objection is correct.

(b) Factor: $2x^2+x-3=(2x+3)(x-1)$. Up to order and constant factors $\pm1$, this is the only factorisation into polynomials with integer coefficients (both factors are linear and cannot be factored further), and positive leading coefficients fix the signs. So the sides are $2x+3$ and $x-1$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
draw((0,0)--(6,0)--(6,2.4)--(0,2.4)--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
label("$2x+3$",(3,0),S); label("$x-1$",(6,1.2),E);
label("$2x^2+x-3$",(3,1.2));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The perimeter is
\[
2\left[(2x+3)+(x-1)\right]=\boxed{6x+4}\text{ units},
\]
and both sides are positive exactly when $x>1$.
