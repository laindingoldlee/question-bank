There must be at least one square-root step, since otherwise every step strictly increases the value by $p$. Between outputs $r_i$ and $r_{i+1}$, additions by $p$ lead to the square $r_{i+1}^2$. Thus $r_{i+1}^2\equiv r_i\pmod p$, with cyclic indices. Substituting repeatedly around the cycle gives $r_1^{2^k}\equiv r_1\pmod p$. Since $p\nmid r_1$, cancellation yields $\boxed{r_1^{2^k-1}\equiv1\pmod p}$. In particular $k=1$ forces $r_1\equiv1\pmod p$, and $k=2$ forces $r_1^3\equiv1\pmod p$. These are necessary conditions, not a sufficiency assertion.
