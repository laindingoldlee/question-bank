A ball of radius $6$ inches rests on the floor and touches a wall; the wall is perpendicular to the floor.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] What is the radius of the largest ball that can be slid into the space between the ball and the line where the wall meets the floor (the smaller ball also resting on the floor and touching the wall)? A book answers $18-12\sqrt2$ by working with circles; a student objects that the balls are spheres and the distance from the centre of the ball to the corner should be the space diagonal of a cube of side $6$. Who is right?
\item[(b)] Now suppose the ball sits in a corner of the room where two perpendicular walls and the floor meet, touching all three. Find the radius of the largest ball that fits in the corner between the ball and the point where the two walls and the floor meet, touching the two walls and the floor.
\end{enumerate}
