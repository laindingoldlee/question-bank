Since $5$ is prime, the product is a multiple of $5$ exactly when at least one die shows a $5$. ``At least one'' covers three cases (first die only, second die only, both), while its opposite is a single simple case: \emph{neither} die shows $5$. That happens with probability $\dfrac{5}{6}\cdot\dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{25}{36}$, because the dice are independent. The two events are complementary (exactly one of them happens), so their probabilities add up to $1$:
\[
P(\text{multiple of }5)=1-\dfrac{25}{36}=\boxed{\dfrac{11}{36}}.
\]
Direct count as a check: the outcomes with at least one $5$ are the $6$ with a $5$ on the first die and the $6$ with a $5$ on the second, minus the outcome $(5,5)$ counted twice: $6+6-1=11$ of the $36$.
