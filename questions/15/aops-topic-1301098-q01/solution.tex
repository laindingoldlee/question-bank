Five terms in arithmetic progression with middle term $m$ add to $5m$. So $\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}-5000=5m$, where $m$ is the middle term of a progression $m-2d,\dots,m+2d$ ($d\ge1$) inside $\{1,\dots,n\}$; in particular $3\le m\le n-2$.

We need $\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}>5000$, so $n\ge100$; and $5m\le5(n-2)$ gives $\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}\le5000+5n-10$, which fails for $n\ge105$. Checking $n=100,\dots,104$: the values $\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}-5000$ are $50,151,253,356,460$, of which only $50$ and $460$ are multiples of $5$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $n=100$, $m=10$: need $10-2d\ge1$, so $d=1,2,3,4$: $4$ progressions.
\item $n=104$, $m=92$: need $92+2d\le104$, so $d=1,\dots,6$: $6$ progressions.
\end{itemize}
Answer: $\boxed{n=100\ (4\text{ progressions})\text{ and }n=104\ (6\text{ progressions})}$.
