An infinite sum means the limit of its partial sums. The first line is correct: every bracket is $0$, so both sides have all partial sums $0$. The trouble is the second line. The series $1-1+1-1+\cdots$ has partial sums $1,0,1,0,\dots$, which do not converge, so it has no sum at all.

Grouping terms of a series in brackets keeps only some of its partial sums. The grouping $(1-1)+(1-1)+\cdots$ keeps the even-numbered partial sums ($0,0,\dots$), and $1+(-1+1)+\cdots$ keeps the odd-numbered ones ($1,1,\dots$). For a convergent series every grouping has the same sum, but for a divergent series different groupings can give different values. So removing the brackets (line 2) and regrouping them differently (line 3) is invalid: $\boxed{\text{the series }1-1+1-1+\cdots\text{ diverges, so it cannot be regrouped}}$.
