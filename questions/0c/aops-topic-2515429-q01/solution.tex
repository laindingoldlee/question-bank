The numbers divisible by $3$ but not by $6$ are those $\equiv3\pmod6$: $3,9,15,\dots$. Among $1,\dots,n$ there are $\left\lfloor\dfrac{n+3}{6}\right\rfloor$ of them (the $k$-th one is $6k-3$, and $6k-3\le n\iff k\le\dfrac{n+3}{6}$). So
\[\left\lfloor\dfrac{n+3}{6}\right\rfloor=100\iff600\le n+3\le605\iff597\le n\le602.\]
The numbers divisible by $6$ but not by $12$ are those $\equiv6\pmod{12}$, and there are $\left\lfloor\dfrac{n+6}{12}\right\rfloor$ of them (the $k$-th is $12k-6$). So
\[\left\lfloor\dfrac{n+6}{12}\right\rfloor=50\iff600\le n+6\le611\iff594\le n\le605.\]
Both hold exactly for
\[\boxed{n\in\{597,598,599,600,601,602\}}.\]
