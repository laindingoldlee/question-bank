Long division advances a remainder $ r $ to $ 10r $ modulo $ b $. Because $ a $ is invertible modulo $ b $, the first return to the initial remainder occurs exactly when $ 10^ka\equiv a $, equivalently $ 10^k\equiv1\pmod b $. Repetition of the remainder restarts the same digit sequence, so the least return time is the period. For $ b=7 $, successive residues of $ 10^k $ are $ 3,2,6,4,5,1 $. Hence the period is $\boxed6 $, and $\dfrac{1}{7}=0.\overline{142857}$.
