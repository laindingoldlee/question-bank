\textbf{It works.} If $f(n)=1-n$ then $f(f(n))=1-(1-n)=n$, $f(f(n+2)+2)=f(1-n-2+2)=f(1-n)=n$, and $f(0)=1$.

\textbf{It is the only one.} Apply $f$ to both sides of (ii): $f\bigl(f(f(n+2)+2)\bigr)=f(n)$. By (i) applied to the integer $m=f(n+2)+2$, the left side equals $m$. Hence
\[f(n+2)+2=f(n),\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad f(n+2)=f(n)-2\quad\text{for all }n.\]
From (iii) and (i), $f(1)=f(f(0))=0$. By induction in both directions ($f(n+2)=f(n)-2$ and $f(n)=f(n+2)+2$), for every integer $k$
\[f(2k)=f(0)-2k=1-2k,\qquad f(2k+1)=f(1)-2k=-2k=1-(2k+1).\]
So $f(n)=1-n$ for every integer $n$. $\blacksquare$
