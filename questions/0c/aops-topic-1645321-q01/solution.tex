The last four digits $a,b,c,d$ satisfy $abcd=504=2^3\cdot3^2\cdot7$. None is $0$. The only digit divisible by $7$ is $7$, so exactly one digit is $7$, and the other three have product $72=2^3\cdot3^2$, using digits from $\{1,2,3,4,6,8,9\}$. The multisets of three digits with product $72$ are
\[
\{1,8,9\},\ \{2,4,9\},\ \{3,3,8\},\ \{2,6,6\},\ \{3,4,6\}
\]
(if a $9$ is used the other two multiply to $8$: $1\cdot8$ or $2\cdot4$; if no $9$ but an $8$, the others multiply to $9$: $3\cdot3$; with neither, the digits come from $\{1,2,3,4,6\}$ and multiply to $72$: $2\cdot6\cdot6$ or $3\cdot4\cdot6$).

Adding the $7$ and counting orders of the four digits:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\{7,1,8,9\}$, $\{7,2,4,9\}$, $\{7,3,4,6\}$: four different digits, $4!=24$ orders each;
\item $\{7,3,3,8\}$, $\{7,2,6,6\}$: one repeated digit, $\dfrac{4!}{2!}=12$ orders each.
\end{itemize}
Total: $3\cdot24+2\cdot12=\boxed{96}$.
