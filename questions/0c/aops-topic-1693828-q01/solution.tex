Let $a=\sqrt{16+x^2}$ and $b=\sqrt{81+x^2}$. Then $a+b=13$ and $b^2-a^2=65$. Dividing, $b-a=\dfrac{65}{13}=5$, so $b=9$, $a=4$, and $16+x^2=16$ gives $\boxed{x=0}$.

(Directly: $\sqrt{16+x^2}\ge4$ and $\sqrt{81+x^2}\ge9$, with equality only at $x=0$, so the left side is at least $13$ and equals $13$ only for $x=0$.)
