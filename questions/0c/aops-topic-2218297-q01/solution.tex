\textbf{Factor the denominator.}
\[x^4+x^2+1=\left(x^2+1\right)^2-x^2=\left(x^2-x+1\right)\left(x^2+x+1\right).\]
The two factors differ by $2x$, so
\[\dfrac{1}{x^2-x+1}-\dfrac{1}{x^2+x+1}=\dfrac{2x}{x^4+x^2+1},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad\dfrac{x}{x^4+x^2+1}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{x^2-x+1}-\dfrac{1}{x^2+x+1}\right).\]

\textbf{Telescope.} Since $(x+1)^2-(x+1)+1=x^2+x+1$, the second fraction for $x$ is the first fraction for $x+1$. Adding from $x=1$ to $n$, everything cancels except the first and the last term:
\[\sum_{x=1}^{n}\dfrac{x}{x^4+x^2+1}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{1}-\dfrac{1}{n^2+n+1}\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{2\left(n^2+n+1\right)}<\dfrac{1}{2}.\qquad\blacksquare\]
(So the sums increase towards $\dfrac{1}{2}$ without reaching it; $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is the smallest constant that works for all $n$.)
