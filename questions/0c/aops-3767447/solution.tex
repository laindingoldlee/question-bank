Fix such a basis and set $c=f(0)$. Repeatedly flipping basis coordinates gives $f(\sum t_ib_i)=c+\sum t_i\pmod2$. Thus $f=c+\ell$, where $\ell$ is a nonzero linear functional. Conversely every nonzero $\ell$ admits a basis on which it is identically $1$: take $v$ with $\ell(v)=1$ and a basis $u_1,\ldots,u_7$ of its kernel; then $v,v+u_1,\ldots,v+u_7$ is such a basis, as linear independence follows by comparing kernel coefficients and the coefficient of $v$. There are $2^8-1$ nonzero linear functionals and two constants, giving $\boxed{510}$ distinct functions.
