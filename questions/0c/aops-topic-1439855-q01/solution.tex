Let $N=\dfrac{50!}{10^{12}}$; we need $N\bmod10$. The exponent of $5$ in $50!$ is $10+2=12$ and the exponent of $2$ is $25+12+6+3+1=47$, so $N$ is even, and it is enough to find $N\bmod5$.

Split $50!$ into the multiples of $5$ and the rest. The multiples are $5k$ for $k=1,\dots,10$, with product $5^{10}\cdot10!$, and $10!=5^2\cdot145152$. So
\[
50!=5^{12}\cdot145152\cdot P,\qquad P=\prod_{\substack{m\le50\\5\nmid m}}m,
\]
and $N=\dfrac{145152\cdot P}{2^{12}}$. Modulo $5$, each block of four consecutive non-multiples of $5$ has product $\equiv1\cdot2\cdot3\cdot4=24\equiv-1$, and there are $10$ blocks, so $P\equiv(-1)^{10}=1$. Also $145152\equiv2$ and $2^{12}=4096\equiv1\pmod5$. Hence $N\equiv2\pmod5$.

$N$ is even and $N\equiv2\pmod5$, so $N\equiv2\pmod{10}$. The $13$th digit from the right is $\boxed{2}$.
