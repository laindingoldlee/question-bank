All primitive triples, up to exchanging the legs, are $\boxed{(m^2-n^2,2mn,m^2+n^2)}$ for $m>n\ge1$, $\gcd(m,n)=1$, and opposite parity. Expansion verifies the Pythagorean identity. Conversely a primitive triple has one even leg $y$, one odd leg $x$, and odd hypotenuse $z$. The integers $\dfrac{z+x}{2}$ and $\dfrac{z-x}{2}$ are coprime and multiply to $(\dfrac{y}{2})^2$; hence each is a square, say $m^2,n^2$. Their sum and difference yield the formula. Coprimality and opposite parity exactly prevent a common factor in the resulting triple. Nonprimitive triples are positive integer multiples.
