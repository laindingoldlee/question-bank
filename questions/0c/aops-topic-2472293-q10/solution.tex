\textbf{The points exist.} If $AB\parallel CD$, the arcs between the parallel chords are equal and $ABCD$ is an isosceles trapezoid with $AD=BC$, contrary to $AD<BC$; so $E$ exists. The tangents at $A$ and $D$ are parallel only if $AD$ is a diameter; but no chord is longer than a diameter and $AD<BC$, so $AD$ is not a diameter and $G$ exists. The diagonals of a convex quadrilateral meet, so $O$ exists.

\textbf{Collinearity.} We use Pascal's theorem: if $P_1,\dots,P_6$ lie on a circle, then the three points $P_1P_2\cap P_4P_5$, $P_2P_3\cap P_5P_6$ and $P_3P_4\cap P_6P_1$ are collinear; this remains true when two consecutive points coincide, the line through them being the tangent at that point.

Apply it to the hexagon $A,A,C,D,D,B$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $P_1P_2$ is the tangent at $A$ and $P_4P_5$ is the tangent at $D$; they meet at $G$;
\item $P_2P_3=AC$ and $P_5P_6=DB$ meet at $O$;
\item $P_3P_4=CD$ and $P_6P_1=BA$ meet at $E$.
\end{itemize}
Hence $G$, $O$ and $E$ are collinear. $\blacksquare$

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair O=(0,0);
pair A=dir(125), B=dir(210), C=dir(-20), D=dir(75);
pair Ee=extension(A,B,C,D), Q=extension(A,C,B,D);
pair G=2*A*D/(A+D);
draw(circle(O,1));
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(0.8));
draw(A--C,gray); draw(B--D,gray);
draw(A--Ee,dashed); draw(D--Ee,dashed);
draw(A--G,blue); draw(D--G,blue);
draw((Ee+0.15*(Ee-Q))--(Q+0.6*(Q-Ee)),red+linewidth(0.9));
dot(A); dot(B); dot(C); dot(D); dot(Ee); dot(Q); dot(G);
label("$A$",A,W); label("$B$",B,SW); label("$C$",C,SE); label("$D$",D,NE);
label("$E$",Ee,E); label("$G$",G,dir(45)); label("$O$",Q,dir(-10));
\end{asy}

\emph{Remark.} With the tangents at $A$ and $B$ instead, the same theorem applied to $A,A,D,B,B,C$ shows that their intersection lies on the line through $O$ and $AD\cap BC$, which in general is a different line from $EO$.
