A rotation keeps every point at the same distance from the centre, so the centre is equidistant from $A$ and $A'$, and from $B$ and $B'$: it lies on the perpendicular bisectors of $AA'$ and $BB'$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $AA'$ has midpoint $\left(\dfrac{7}{2},\dfrac{3}{2}\right)$ and direction $(1,1)$, so its perpendicular bisector is $x+y=5$.
\item $BB'$ has midpoint $\left(\dfrac{9}{2},\dfrac{7}{2}\right)$ and direction $(5,-1)$, so its perpendicular bisector is $5x-y=\dfrac{45}{2}-\dfrac{7}{2}=19$.
\end{itemize}
Adding the two equations, $6x=24$, so $x=4$ and $y=1$. The centre is $O=\boxed{(4,1)}$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair ft(pair P,pair U,pair V){return U+dot(P-U,V-U)/dot(V-U,V-U)*(V-U);}
path ra(pair P,pair u,pair v,real s=0.2){return P+s*unit(u)--P+s*unit(u)+s*unit(v)--P+s*unit(v);}
pair A=(3,1),B=(2,4),A1=(4,2),B1=(7,3),O=(4,1);
for(int i=0;i<=8;++i) draw((i,0)--(i,5),gray(0.88));
for(int j=0;j<=5;++j) draw((0,j)--(8,j),gray(0.88));
draw((0,0)--(8.4,0),Arrow(TeXHead)); draw((0,0)--(0,5.4),Arrow(TeXHead));
draw((0.2,4.8)--(4.8,0.2),deepgreen+dashed);
draw((3.8,0)--(4.8,5),deepgreen+dashed);
draw(A--B,blue+linewidth(0.9)); draw(A1--B1,red+linewidth(0.9));
draw(O--A,gray); draw(O--A1,gray);
draw(ra(O,A-O,A1-O,0.25));
dot(A,blue);dot(B,blue);dot(A1,red);dot(B1,red);dot(O);
label("$A$",A,SW,blue); label("$B$",B,W,blue); label("$A'$",A1,NW,red); label("$B'$",B1,E,red);
label("$O$",O,dir(15));
label("$x+y=5$",(0.6,4.4),NE,deepgreen); label("$5x-y=19$",(4.8,5),N,deepgreen);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Check: relative to $(4,1)$, $A$ is $(-1,0)$ and $A'$ is $(0,1)$; $B$ is $(-2,3)$ and $B'$ is $(3,2)$. In both cases $(u,v)\mapsto(v,-u)$, a $90^\circ$ clockwise rotation, so the same rotation about $(4,1)$ takes $A\to A'$ and $B\to B'$.
