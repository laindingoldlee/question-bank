Count the complement. A safe outcome cannot contain both a two and a six, cannot contain both a three and a five, and can contain at most one four. If $c_j$ is the count of face $j$, its number of ordered realizations is $\dfrac{9!}{c_1!\cdots c_6!}$. Therefore the exact probability is $1-\dfrac{1}{6^9}\sum\dfrac{9!}{c_1!\cdots c_6!}$, where the sum runs over nonnegative $c_1+\cdots+c_6=9$ with $c_2c_6=c_3c_5=0$ and $c_4\le1$. Evaluating this small finite sum gives $\dfrac{4887011}{5038848}\approx\boxed{0.970}$. An alternative evaluation uses the coefficient of $x^9$ in $e^x(2e^x-1)^2(1+x)$, multiplied by $9!$, for the safe count.
