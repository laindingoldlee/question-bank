Let $p$ be the original number of people. The first sharing shows that the bag has $5p+10$ candies. In the second, $3p-5$ people would need $2(3p-5)=6p-10$ candies, and the bag has $8$ fewer, $6p-18$. Hence
\[
5p+10=6p-18,\qquad p=28 ,
\]
and the bag has $5\cdot28+10=\boxed{150}$ candies.

Check: $3\cdot28-5=79$ people would need $158$ candies, which is $8$ more than $150$.

(In ``units'' language: the second sharing hands out $6$ candies per original person, minus $10$, and is $8$ short, so $6$ per person minus $18$ equals $5$ per person plus $10$; the extra $1$ per person is worth $28$ candies, so there are $28$ people.)
