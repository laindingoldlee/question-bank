For a positive integer $N$, let $f(N)$ be the number of terms of the sequence that are at most $N$. Among $1,2,\dots,N$ there are $\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$ squares and $\lfloor\sqrt[3]N\rfloor$ cubes, and the numbers that are both are the sixth powers, of which there are $\lfloor\sqrt[6]N\rfloor$. By inclusion-exclusion,
\[f(N)=N-\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor-\lfloor\sqrt[3]N\rfloor+\lfloor\sqrt[6]N\rfloor.\]

\textbf{Estimate.} For $N=500$: $22^2=484\le500<529$, $7^3=343\le500<512$ and $2^6=64\le500<729$, so $f(500)=500-22-7+2=473$. We still need $27$ more terms, so the answer is at least $527$.

\textbf{Exact.} For $N=528$: the squares up to $528$ are $1^2,\dots,22^2$ ($23^2=529$), the cubes are $1^3,\dots,8^3$ ($8^3=512$), and the sixth powers are $1$ and $64$. So
\[f(528)=528-22-8+2=500.\]
Since $528$ is neither a square nor a cube, it is itself in the sequence, so it is the $500$th term: $\boxed{528}$.
