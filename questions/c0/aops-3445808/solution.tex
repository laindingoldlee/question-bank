Every such number has digit sum 45 and is divisible by 9. For divisibility by 11, the sum $S$ of its five odd-position digits must satisfy $2S-45\equiv0\pmod{11}$, hence $S=17$ or 28. We can attain the smallest possible initial prefix 1234. If the fifth digit were 5, the odd-position sum could reach neither 17 nor 28: after the initial odd digits 1,3,5, the two remaining odd digits from 6,7,8,9 have sum between 13 and 17, giving total 22 through 26. If the fifth digit were 6, the maximum would be $1+3+6+8+9=27$ and the minimum exceeds 17. Thus the fifth digit must be at least 7. With 7, the last two odd digits must be 8 and 9 to make 28. Greedily choosing the smallest remaining digits gives $\boxed{123475869}$, which has odd-position sum 28 and is divisible by 99. This also proves minimality.
