$66=2\cdot3\cdot11$. $N$ is even. Its digit sum is $2+0+1+3+A+2+0+1+2+B+2+0+1+0=14+A+B$, so $3\mid N$ means $A+B\equiv1\pmod3$. For $11$, the alternating sum of digits from the right is
\[
0-1+0-2+B-2+1-0+2-A+3-1+0-2=B-A-2,
\]
so $11\mid N$ means $B=A+2$. Together: $2A+2\equiv1\pmod3$, i.e. $A\equiv1\pmod3$, giving $(A,B)=(1,3),(4,6),(7,9)$.

(A) True: $B=A+2$. (B) True: the three numbers leave remainders $1$, $3$, $5$ on division by $7$. (C) True: $N$ ends in $010$, and $010\equiv2\pmod8$. (D) False: for $(1,3)$ the digit sum is $18$, so $9\mid N$.

So the statements that must be true are $\boxed{\text{(A), (B) and (C)}}$.
