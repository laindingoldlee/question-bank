Let $x$ and $y$ be Stanley's and Andrew's arrival times, in minutes after 2:00 pm. The point $(x,y)$ is uniformly distributed in the $60\times60$ square, and the match happens exactly when $|x-y|\le15$, the shaded band:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real u=4;real w=1;
fill((0,0)--(w,0)--(u,u-w)--(u,u)--(u-w,u)--(0,w)--cycle,paleblue);
draw((0,0)--(u,0)--(u,u)--(0,u)--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw((w,0)--(u,u-w),blue);draw((0,w)--(u-w,u),blue);
label("$0$",(0,0),SW);label("$15$",(w,0),S);label("$60$",(u,0),S);label("$15$",(0,w),W);label("$60$",(0,u),W);
label("$x$",(u/2,-0.45));label("$y$",(-0.5,u/2));
label(rotate(45)*"$|x-y|\le15$",(u/2,u/2),blue);
label("$y>x+15$",(0.95,3.1));label("$x>y+15$",(3.05,0.9));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The complement consists of two right triangles with legs $60-15=45$, of total area $45^2=2025$. The square has area $3600$, so
\[
P=1-\dfrac{2025}{3600}=1-\dfrac{9}{16}=\boxed{\dfrac{7}{16}} .
\]
(Leaving at 3:00 pm changes nothing: someone arriving after 2:45 still sees the other if he arrived within the last $15$ minutes.)
