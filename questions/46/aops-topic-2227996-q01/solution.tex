For $x\ne0$,
\[x-\dfrac{1}{x}=y\iff x^2-yx-1=0 .\]
This quadratic has discriminant $y^2+4>0$, so it has two distinct real roots
\[x=\dfrac{y\pm\sqrt{y^2+4}}{2},\]
and neither is $0$ (their product is $-1$; also $x=0$ does not satisfy $x^2-yx-1=0$). Both roots are solutions of the original equation, so $f$ takes the value $y$, in fact at exactly $\boxed{2}$ points (one positive and one negative, since their product is $-1$). Hence $f$ is surjective onto $\mathbb R$. $\blacksquare$
