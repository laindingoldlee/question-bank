Since $a>0$, the first equation has the unique solution $x=\dfrac{15-b}{a}$; the second has the unique solution $x=\dfrac{b-a}{15}$. They are equal exactly when
\[
15(15-b)=a(b-a)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a^2+225=b(a+15)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b=\dfrac{a^2+225}{a+15}.
\]
Dividing, $a^2+225=(a+15)(a-15)+450$, so
\[
b=a-15+\dfrac{450}{a+15}.
\]
So $a+15$ must divide $450=2\cdot3^2\cdot5^2$, with $16\le a+15\le45$. The divisors of $450$ in this range are $18$, $25$, $30$ and $45$, giving
\[
(a,b)=(3,13),\ (10,13),\ (15,15),\ (30,25),
\]
and all of these have $1\le b\le30$. (For example, $(3,13)$: $3x+13=15$ and $15x+3=13$ both give $x=\dfrac{2}{3}$.) The sum of the possible values of $a$ is
\[
3+10+15+30=\boxed{58}.
\]
