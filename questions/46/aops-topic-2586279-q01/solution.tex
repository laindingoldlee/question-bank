Since $c\ne0$, neither root is $0$. If $x$ is a root of the original equation and $y=1-\dfrac1x$, then $y\ne1$ and $x=\dfrac{1}{1-y}$. Substituting,
\[\dfrac{a}{(1-y)^2}+\dfrac{b}{1-y}+c=0\iff a+b(1-y)+c(1-y)^2=0\iff cy^2-(b+2c)y+(a+b+c)=0.\]
So $1-\dfrac1\alpha$ and $1-\dfrac1\beta$ are the roots of
\[\boxed{cx^2-(b+2c)x+(a+b+c)=0}.\]
Check with Vieta's formulas, using $\alpha+\beta=-\dfrac ba$ and $\alpha\beta=\dfrac ca$:
\[\left(1-\dfrac1\alpha\right)+\left(1-\dfrac1\beta\right)=2-\dfrac{\alpha+\beta}{\alpha\beta}=2+\dfrac bc=\dfrac{b+2c}{c},\]
\[\left(1-\dfrac1\alpha\right)\left(1-\dfrac1\beta\right)=1-\dfrac{\alpha+\beta}{\alpha\beta}+\dfrac{1}{\alpha\beta}=1+\dfrac bc+\dfrac ac=\dfrac{a+b+c}{c}.\]
