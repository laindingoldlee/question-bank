Let the two distances be $s<\ell$ and put $\lambda=\dfrac{\ell}{s}>1$. Both distances must occur, since four points cannot be pairwise equidistant in a plane. We first find all possible ratios $\lambda$.

\textbf{Case 1: three of the points form an equilateral triangle} $ABC$ with side $a$ (where $a=s$ or $a=\ell$). Among $DA$, $DB$, $DC$ two are equal, say $DB=DC$, so $D$ lies on the symmetry axis of the triangle through $A$.
\begin{itemize}
\item If $DA=DB=DC$, then $D$ is the centre and these distances are $\dfrac{a}{\sqrt3}$: ratio $\sqrt3$.
\item If $DB=DC\ne DA$, then since only two distances exist, $DB=a$ or $DA=a$. If $DB=DC=a$, then $D$ is the reflection of $A$ in $BC$ (a rhombus with angles $60^\circ$ and $120^\circ$), and $DA=\sqrt3\,a$: ratio $\sqrt3$. If $DA=a$, then $D$ is on the axis at distance $a$ from $A$, on the far side of $BC$ or beyond $A$; with $a=1$ and the axis as the $y$-axis, $A=\left(0,\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)$, $B=\left(-\dfrac{1}{2},0\right)$ and $D=\left(0,\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\mp1\right)$, so $DB^2=\dfrac{1}{4}+\left(\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\mp1\right)^2=2\mp\sqrt3$: ratio $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2-\sqrt3}}=\sqrt{2+\sqrt3}$ in both positions.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Case 2: no equilateral triangle.} Join two points by a \emph{short edge} if their distance is $s$. Neither the short edges nor the long ones contain a triangle. The graphs on four vertices with this property are the path $P_4$, the $4$-cycle and the pair of disjoint edges (any vertex of degree $3$, in either colour, forces a triangle in one colour).
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Short edges form a $4$-cycle $ABCD$:} $A$ and $C$ are both at distance $s$ from $B$ and from $D$, so they are the two intersections of two circles and $ABCD$ is a rhombus; its diagonals are both $\ell$, so it is a square: ratio $\sqrt2$.
\item \emph{Long edges form a $4$-cycle:} as above it is a square whose diagonals are the short distance; but a square's diagonal is longer than its side. Impossible.
\item \emph{Short edges form a path $A$-$B$-$C$-$D$:} take $s=1$, $B=(0,0)$, $C=(1,0)$, $A=(\cos\beta,\sin\beta)$ where $\beta=\angle ABC$, so $\ell^2=AC^2=2-2\cos\beta$. Point $D$ has $DC=1$, $DB=\ell$, so it is the mirror image of $A$ in the line $x=\dfrac{1}{2}$ or the image of $A$ under the half-turn about $\left(\dfrac{1}{2},0\right)$. In the first case $AD=|1-2\cos\beta|$, and $AD=\ell$ gives $4\cos^2\beta-2\cos\beta-1=0$; the condition $\ell>1$ requires $\cos\beta<\dfrac{1}{2}$, so $\cos\beta=\dfrac{1-\sqrt5}{4}$ ($\beta=108^\circ$) and $\ell^2=\dfrac{3+\sqrt5}{2}$, $\ell=\dfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2}$: four vertices of a regular pentagon. In the second case $AD^2=5-4\cos\beta=2-2\cos\beta$ forces $\cos\beta=\dfrac{3}{2}$, impossible.
\end{itemize}

So the possible ratios are $\sqrt2$, $\dfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2}$, $\sqrt3$ and $\sqrt{2+\sqrt3}$, realised by the six configurations below (short distance solid, long distance dashed).

\textbf{The values of $x$.} The given unit can be either the short or the long distance, so $x=\lambda$ or $x=\dfrac{1}{\lambda}$, and all eight values occur. Their sum is
\[
\left(\sqrt2+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\right)+\left(\dfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2}+\dfrac{\sqrt5-1}{2}\right)+\left(\sqrt3+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\right)+\left(\sqrt{2+\sqrt3}+\sqrt{2-\sqrt3}\right)=\dfrac{3\sqrt2}{2}+\sqrt5+\dfrac{4\sqrt3}{3}+\sqrt6,
\]
using $\left(\sqrt{2+\sqrt3}+\sqrt{2-\sqrt3}\right)^2=4+2=6$. Numerically this is $2.1213+2.2361+2.3094+2.4495\approx\boxed{9.12}$.
