\textbf{(a)} Let $d=\gcd(m+3n,\,5m+7n)$. Then $d$ divides
\[5(m+3n)-(5m+7n)=8n\qquad\text{and}\qquad 7(m+3n)-3(5m+7n)=-8m,\]
so $d\mid\gcd(8m,8n)=8\gcd(m,n)=8$. Since $m-n$ is odd, one of $m,n$ is even and the other odd, so $m+3n=(m-n)+4n$ is odd. Hence $d$ is odd, and $d=1$.

\textbf{(b)} Suppose $(m+3n)(5m+7n)=k^2$. The two factors are positive and, by (a), coprime, so each is a perfect square: $m+3n=x^2$ and $5m+7n=y^2$. (Each prime appears to an even power in $k^2$, and all of its copies lie in one factor.) Both $m+3n$ and $5m+7n=5(m-n)+12n$ are odd, so $x$ and $y$ are odd, and the square of an odd number is $1\pmod8$. But
\[y^2-5x^2=(5m+7n)-5(m+3n)=-8n\equiv0\pmod8,\]
while $y^2-5x^2\equiv1-5=-4\pmod8$. This contradiction shows that the product is not a perfect square. $\blacksquare$
