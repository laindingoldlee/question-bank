Draw the segments $BG$ and $CE$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(1.6,4), B=(0,0), C=(5,0);
real t=0.4; pair Ep=A+t*(B-A), G=A+t*(C-A);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(Ep--G,linewidth(0.9));
draw(B--G,red+dashed); draw(C--Ep,blue+dashed);
dot(Ep);dot(G);
label("$A$",A,N); label("$B$",B,SW); label("$C$",C,SE); label("$E$",Ep,W); label("$G$",G,E);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\textbf{Step 1.} Triangles $EGB$ and $EGC$ have the common base $EG$, and their third vertices $B$ and $C$ lie on a line parallel to $EG$, so they have equal heights. Hence $[EGB]=[EGC]$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Triangles $AEG$ and $BEG$ have bases $AE$ and $EB$ on the same line $AB$ and the same apex $G$, so they have the same height and
\[
\dfrac{[AEG]}{[BEG]}=\dfrac{AE}{EB}.
\]
Similarly, triangles $AEG$ and $CEG$ have bases $AG$ and $GC$ on line $AC$ and the same apex $E$, so
\[
\dfrac{[AEG]}{[CEG]}=\dfrac{AG}{GC}.
\]

\textbf{Step 3.} The two left-hand sides are equal by Step 1, so $\dfrac{AE}{EB}=\dfrac{AG}{GC}$. $\blacksquare$

(Alternatively, $EG\parallel BC$ gives equal corresponding angles, so $\triangle AEG\sim\triangle ABC$ by AA, hence $\dfrac{AE}{AB}=\dfrac{AG}{AC}$; subtracting each side from $1$ and taking reciprocals gives the same result.)
