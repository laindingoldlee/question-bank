Anyone above Kaisar overall must beat him in at least one round. There are only $2+3+4=9$ such places, so at most nine students can finish above him. To attain this, give Kaisar 100 in each round. Number nine students $j=1,\ldots,9$ and assign them to disjoint groups of sizes 2,3,4. Student $j$ scores $400+j$ in the group\textquotesingle s round and $j$ in each other round, for total $400+3j$. The remaining 15 students, numbered $10,\ldots,24$, score their own number in every round. All round and total scores are distinct, and precisely nine totals exceed 300. Kaisar\textquotesingle s lowest possible place is $\boxed{10\text{th}}$.
