Over the common denominator $abc$,
\[\dfrac{a-b}{c}+\dfrac{b-c}{a}+\dfrac{c-a}{b}=\dfrac{ab(a-b)+bc(b-c)+ca(c-a)}{abc}.\]
\emph{Claim:} $ab(a-b)+bc(b-c)+ca(c-a)=-(a-b)(b-c)(c-a)$. The left side is a polynomial that vanishes when $a=b$ (it becomes $b^2(b-c)+cb(c-b)=0$), and similarly when $b=c$ or $c=a$. So it is a constant multiple of $(a-b)(b-c)(c-a)$, both being of degree $3$. Comparing the coefficients of $a^2b$ ($+1$ on the left, $-1$ in the product) gives the constant $-1$.

So the given sum equals $-\dfrac{(a-b)(b-c)(c-a)}{abc}$, which is minus the requested product. The product is therefore $\boxed{-10}$.

(Such numbers exist: for $a=6$, $b=-2$, $c=1$ the three fractions are $8$, $-\dfrac12$ and $\dfrac52$, with sum $10$ and product $-10$.)
