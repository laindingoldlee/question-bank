\textbf{Mass points.} Put masses on the vertices so that $E$ and $D$ are balance points. For $E$ on $AB$ with $AE:EB=3:2$ we need $3m_A=2m_B$; for $D$ on $AC$ with $AD:DC=1:4$ we need $m_A=4m_C$. Take $m_A=2$, $m_B=3$, $m_C=\dfrac{1}{2}$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(1.6,4),B=(0,0),C=(5.5,0);
pair Ep=A+3/5*(B-A),D=A+1/5*(C-A);pair F=extension(B,D,C,Ep);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(1));draw(B--D);draw(C--Ep);
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);dot(D);dot(Ep);dot(F);
label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,SW);label("$C$",C,SE);label("$D$",D,NE);label("$E$",Ep,W);label("$F$",F,SE);
label("$3$",(A+Ep)/2,W);label("$2$",(Ep+B)/2,W);label("$1$",(A+D)/2,NE);label("$4$",(D+C)/2,NE);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Then $E$ carries mass $m_A+m_B=5$, and $F$ is the balance point of segment $EC$ with masses $5$ at $E$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$ at $C$. The lever rule gives
\[
\dfrac{EF}{FC}=\dfrac{m_C}{m_E}=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2}}{5}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{10}} .
\]
\textbf{Check with Menelaus.} Line $BFD$ crosses the sides of triangle $AEC$ (with $B$ on the extension of $AE$). Menelaus' theorem gives
\[
\dfrac{AB}{BE}\cdot\dfrac{EF}{FC}\cdot\dfrac{CD}{DA}=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \dfrac{5}{2}\cdot\dfrac{EF}{FC}\cdot4=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\dfrac{EF}{FC}=\dfrac{1}{10} .
\]
