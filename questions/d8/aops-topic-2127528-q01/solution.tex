Dividing by $5$, $y^2+25y+100=(y+5)(y+20)=0$, so $\{t,v\}=\{-5,-20\}$ and $tv=100$ (by Vieta's formulas, whichever root is $t$).

The quadratic $tx^2+ux+v=0$ (with $t\ne0$) has two distinct real roots exactly when its discriminant is positive:
\[u^2-4tv>0\iff u^2>400\iff|u|>20 .\]
So the least positive integer value of $u$ is $21$, the greatest negative integer value is $-21$, and their product is $\boxed{-441}$.

\emph{Remark.} As originally posed, $t$ and $v$ were the roots of $5y^2+50y+500=0$, that is, of $y^2+10y+100=0$, which are the non-real numbers $-5\pm5\sqrt3\,i$. Then $tx^2+ux+v=0$ has at most one real root for every real $u$: if $x$ is a real root, the imaginary part of $tx^2+ux+v$ is $\pm5\sqrt3\left(x^2-1\right)$, so $x=\pm1$, and the real part $-5x^2+ux-5=0$ then forces $u=10$ (for $x=1$) or $u=-10$ (for $x=-1$), never both. So that version has no valid $u$ at all.
