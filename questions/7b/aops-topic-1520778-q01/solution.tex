\textbf{Lower bound.} Fix a point $X$ of the road strictly between the start and the finish. Each child ends up beyond $X$, so crosses $X$ forwards one more time than backwards. Each bicycle starts before $X$ and is carried forward across $X$ at most one more time than it is carried back. So of the $3$ (net) forward crossings of $X$ by the children, the bicycles can account for at most $2$: at least one child must walk forwards across $X$.

For a child, let $a$ be the total length of the parts of the road that the child walks forwards across. The child walks at least $a$ km and must ride forwards across the rest of the road, at least $84-a$ km, so that child needs at least
\[
\dfrac{a}{5}+\dfrac{84-a}{20}=4.2+\dfrac{3a}{20}
\]
hours. By the previous paragraph the three values of $a$ add up to at least $84$, so some child has $a\ge28$ and needs at least $4.2+4.2=8.4$ hours.

\textbf{A schedule taking $8.4$ hours.} Call the children $A$, $B$, $C$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $A$ rides bicycle $1$ for $56$ km ($2.8$ h), leaves it there, and walks the last $28$ km ($5.6$ h): $8.4$ h.
\item $B$ rides bicycle $2$ for $28$ km ($1.4$ h), leaves it at km $28$, walks to km $56$ (reached after $7$ h), picks up bicycle $1$ (left there at $2.8$ h) and rides the last $28$ km ($1.4$ h): $8.4$ h.
\item $C$ walks $28$ km ($5.6$ h), picks up bicycle $2$ (left there at $1.4$ h) and rides $56$ km ($2.8$ h): $8.4$ h.
\end{itemize}
So the least time is $\boxed{8.4\text{ hours}}$, that is, $8$ hours $24$ minutes.
