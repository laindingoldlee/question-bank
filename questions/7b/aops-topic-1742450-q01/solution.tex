Join the centre $O$ to two adjacent vertices. The central angle is $\dfrac{360^\circ}{6}=60^\circ$, and the two radii are equal, so the triangle is isosceles with apex angle $60^\circ$, hence equilateral.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair O=(0,0);pair[] V;for(int i=0;i<6;++i)V[i]=4*dir(60*i);
draw(circle(O,4),linewidth(0.8));draw(V[0]--V[1]--V[2]--V[3]--V[4]--V[5]--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(O--V[0],dashed);draw(O--V[1],dashed);
dot(O);label("$O$",O,SW);
label("$4$",(V[0]+V[1])/2+0.55*dir(210));label("$4$",(O+V[0])/2,S);label("$4$",(O+V[1])/2,dir(150));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

So the radius equals the side: $\boxed{4\text{ cm}}$.
