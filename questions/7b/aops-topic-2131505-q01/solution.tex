We use Burnside's lemma: the number of essentially different colourings is the average, over all rotations of the cube, of the number of colourings left unchanged by the rotation. A colouring is unchanged by a rotation exactly when each cycle of vertices under the rotation is coloured with a single colour, so a rotation whose action on the vertices has $c$ cycles fixes $2^c$ colourings.

The cube has $24$ rotations, of four kinds (the axes are shown below):

\begin{itemize}
\item the identity: $8$ cycles, $2^8=256$ fixed colourings;
\item rotations by $\pm90^\circ$ about a face axis ($3$ axes, $6$ rotations): the vertices form two $4$-cycles, so $2^2=4$ each, total $24$;
\item rotations by $180^\circ$ about a face axis ($3$ rotations): four $2$-cycles, $2^4=16$ each, total $48$;
\item rotations by $\pm120^\circ$ about a vertex axis through two opposite vertices ($4$ axes, $8$ rotations): the two vertices on the axis are fixed and the other six form two $3$-cycles, so $4$ cycles and $2^4=16$ each, total $128$;
\item rotations by $180^\circ$ about an edge axis through the midpoints of two opposite edges ($6$ rotations): four $2$-cycles, $2^4=16$ each, total $96$.
\end{itemize}
These are $1+6+3+8+6=24$ rotations, and the number of colourings is
\[\dfrac{256+24+48+128+96}{24}=\dfrac{552}{24}=\boxed{23}.\]

(Check by the number $k$ of vertices of the first colour: $k=0,1,\dots,8$ give $1,1,3,3,7,3,3,1,1$ classes; for example, for $k=2$ the two vertices are the ends of an edge, of a face diagonal or of a space diagonal.)
