\textbf{(a)} If $a_1+\dots+a_k=x$ with all $a_i>0$, then by AM-GM
\[
a_1a_2\cdots a_k\le\left(\dfrac{a_1+\dots+a_k}{k}\right)^k=\left(\dfrac {x}{k}\right)^k ,
\]
with equality when all $a_i=\dfrac {x}{k}$. So the maximum with $k$ terms is $\left(\dfrac {x}{k}\right)^k$.

\textbf{(b)} It remains to choose the number of terms $k$. Consider $h(t)=\ln\left(\dfrac {x}{t}\right)^t=t\ln x-t\ln t$ for real $t>0$. Then $h'(t)=\ln x-\ln t-1$, which is positive for $t<\dfrac {x}{e}$ and negative for $t>\dfrac {x}{e}$. So $h$ increases up to $t=\dfrac {x}{e}$ and decreases afterwards, and among the integers $k\ge1$ the maximum of $\left(\dfrac {x}{k}\right)^k$ is attained at the integer just below or just above $\dfrac {x}{e}$.

\textbf{(c)} For $x=10$, $\dfrac{10}{e}\approx3.68$. Compare $k=3$ and $k=4$:
\[
\left(\dfrac{10}{3}\right)^3=\dfrac{1000}{27}\approx37.04,\qquad\left(\dfrac{10}{4}\right)^4=\dfrac{625}{16}=39.0625 .
\]
So the largest product is $\boxed{\dfrac{625}{16}}$, from $10=2.5+2.5+2.5+2.5$.
