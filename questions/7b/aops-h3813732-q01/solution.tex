(a) If $P$ is true, $\neg P$ is false; if $P$ is false, $\neg P$ is true. The biconditional is therefore false in either case, so there is no satisfying assignment.
(b) The two constraints both require opposite truth values. Their solutions are exactly $\boxed{(P,Q)=(T,F),(F,T)}$.
Inconsistency in part (a) means that the proposed constraint cannot hold in this two-valued model. Deriving a contradiction from an assumption is a way to reject that assumption; it does not invalidate proof by contradiction.
