The procedure ends with probability $1$ (each round produces a new Impostor with positive probability) and then exactly $2$ players are Impostors. It treats all players alike: relabelling the players does not change how it works, so every player has the same probability $p$ of becoming an Impostor. The expected number of Impostors is the sum of these probabilities, so
\[10p=2,\qquad p=\boxed{\dfrac15}.\]
(The $10\%$ only affects how many rounds the selection takes, not who is chosen.)
