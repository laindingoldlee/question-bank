Place the five nongreen people around the circle. The four green people must occupy four of the five gaps, so there is exactly one adjacent nongreen pair. That pair must contain one red and one blue person. Choose and order it in $3\cdot2\cdot2=12$ ways. Regard it as a block alongside the other three nongreen people: four objects have $3!=6$ circular orders. Insert the four distinct green people into the four remaining gaps in $4!$ ways. The total is $12\cdot6\cdot24=\boxed{1728}$. If seats are labelled, multiply by nine; if only colours distinguish people, the count is six.
