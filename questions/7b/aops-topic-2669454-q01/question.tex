A student claims: ``$2$ points determine a unique line, $3$ points determine a unique circle, $5$ points determine a unique ellipse, and $4$ points determine a unique parabola.''
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Prove that through two distinct points there passes exactly one line.
\item[(b)] Prove that through three points that are not collinear there passes exactly one circle, and through three distinct collinear points there passes none.
\item[(c)] Let $\varphi=\dfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2}$. Show that the four points $(0,-1)$, $(-1,0)$, $(\varphi,\varphi)$, $(1-\varphi,1-\varphi)$ lie on both parabolas $y=x^2-1$ and $x=y^2-1$. (So four points need not determine a unique parabola.)
\item[(d)] Show that no ellipse passes through the five points $(1,1)$, $\left(2,\dfrac12\right)$, $\left(\dfrac12,2\right)$, $(-1,-1)$, $\left(-2,-\dfrac12\right)$.
\end{enumerate}
