No digit may be zero. If a number $N$ has at least three digits, write $N=10a+b$, where $a\ge 11$ is the prefix and $1\le b\le 9$. The subsequence $a$ must divide $N$, hence divide $b$, impossible. Every one-digit positive integer qualifies. For a two-digit number $10a+b$, the requirements reduce to $a\mid b$ and $b\mid 10a$. Write $b=ka$, with $k\mid 10$. Since both digits are between $1$ and $9$, $k$ can only be $1,2,5$. These give $11,22,33,44,55,66,77,88,99$; $12,24,36,48$; and $15$. Together with $1,2,\ldots,9$, these are exactly all $\boxed{23}$ gregorian integers.
