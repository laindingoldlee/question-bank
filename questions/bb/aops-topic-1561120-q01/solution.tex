Factor: $1995=3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot19$, a product of four different primes. Since $x,y,z>1$, each of them is a product of a nonempty set of these primes, and the three sets split the four primes. So one number is a product of two primes and the other two are single primes. The three numbers are then automatically different, and there is exactly one way to list them in increasing order.

The pair of primes that are multiplied together can be chosen in $\binom42=6$ ways:
\[
15\cdot7\cdot19,\quad 21\cdot5\cdot19,\quad 57\cdot5\cdot7,\quad 35\cdot3\cdot19,\quad 95\cdot3\cdot7,\quad 133\cdot3\cdot5 .
\]
So there are $\boxed{6}$ triples. (The number $36$ counts the ordered triples with all three factors greater than $1$ in every order, $6\cdot3!$; the condition $x<y<z$ fixes the order.)
