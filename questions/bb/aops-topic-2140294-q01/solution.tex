Take any $17$ coins to form one pile; the other $13$ coins form the second pile. Then turn over every coin in the $17$-coin pile.

\textbf{Why it works.} Suppose the $17$-coin pile contains $h$ heads before the coins are turned over. Since there are $17$ heads in all, the $13$-coin pile contains $17-h$ heads. Turning over all $17$ coins of the first pile changes its $h$ heads into tails and its $17-h$ tails into heads, so afterwards it shows $17-h$ heads, the same as the other pile. $\blacksquare$

The method works whatever $h$ is, which is why it can be done blindfolded. (In the post the solution writes ``$x$ coins'' where it means ``$x$ heads''; with that correction it is exactly this argument, and yes, it really is that easy. The same idea works with $N$ coins of which $k$ show heads: make a pile of $k$ coins and turn it over.)
