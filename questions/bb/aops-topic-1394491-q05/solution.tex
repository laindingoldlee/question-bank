Two segments with a common endpoint meet only at that endpoint, which lies on $m$ or $n$. Take two segments with four different endpoints, $XU$ and $YV$ with $X,Y$ on $n$ and $U,V$ on $m$. They cross between the lines exactly when the order of $X,Y$ along $n$ is opposite to the order of $U,V$ along $m$ (going in the same direction along both lines). So:
\begin{itemize}
\item each choice of two points $\{X,Y\}$ on $n$ and two points $\{U,V\}$ on $m$ gives exactly one crossing pair of segments (of the two ways to join them, exactly one crosses);
\item segments sharing an endpoint never cross between the lines.
\end{itemize}
So there are exactly $\binom42\binom52=6\cdot10=60$ crossing pairs. They give $60$ distinct intersection points exactly when no three segments pass through one point, and this can be arranged by moving the points slightly (each triple concurrence is a single equation, avoided by a generic choice). The maximum number of intersection points is $\boxed{60}$.
