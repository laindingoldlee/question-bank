Let $a=BC,b=CA,c=AB$, and let $l$ be the bisector length. By the sine rule, for some $K>0$,
\[
a=K\sin40^\circ,\quad b=K\sin20^\circ,\quad c=K\sin120^\circ.
\]
The angle-bisector length formula gives
\[
l=\dfrac{2ab\cos60^\circ}{a+b}
=K\dfrac{\sin40^\circ\sin20^\circ}{\sin40^\circ+\sin20^\circ}.
\]
Since $\sin40^\circ+\sin20^\circ=\cos10^\circ$ and $\sin20^\circ=2\sin10^\circ\cos10^\circ$, this becomes $l=2K\sin40^\circ\sin10^\circ$. But
\[
c-a=K(\sin60^\circ-\sin40^\circ)
=2K\cos50^\circ\sin10^\circ
=2K\sin40^\circ\sin10^\circ=l.
\]
Thus the bisector length equals the given difference, namely $\boxed{4}$.

\end{asy}
