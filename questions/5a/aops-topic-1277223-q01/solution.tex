One cut suffices: cut the \emph{third} link. This leaves pieces of $1$ (the cut link), $2$ and $4$ links. Every total from $1$ to $7$ is a sum of distinct pieces ($1,2,1+2,4,4+1,4+2,4+2+1$), so on night $n$ she can arrange for the inn to hold pieces totalling $n$ links, exchanging pieces each morning:
\begin{itemize}
\item night 1: give $1$; night 2: give $2$, take back $1$; night 3: give $1$;
\item night 4: give $4$, take back $1$ and $2$; night 5: give $1$; night 6: give $2$, take back $1$; night 7: give $1$.
\end{itemize}
With no cut she cannot pay on the first night. So the minimum is $\boxed{1}$ cut, at the third link (or the fifth, by symmetry).
