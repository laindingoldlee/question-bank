Whichever two positions are re-rolled, the other two digits stay fixed, so the comparison of the new and old numbers is decided by the two re-rolled digits, read in their order of position as a two-digit ``number'' $\overline{xy}$ with digits from $1$ to $6$.

The new pair $(x',y')$ is uniformly distributed over the $36$ possible pairs and independent of the old pair $(x,y)$. The new number equals the old one exactly when $(x',y')=(x,y)$, which has probability $\dfrac{1}{36}$. Otherwise it is greater or smaller, and by symmetry (swap the roles of the old and the new pair, which have the same distribution) these two cases are equally likely. Hence
\[
P(\text{new}>\text{old})=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{36}\right)=\boxed{\dfrac{35}{72}}.
\]
