\textbf{Case $x_0>5$.} If the robot is at a point with $x>5$, it moves to $(x+2,y)$, whose $x$-coordinate is again greater than $5$. So, by induction, after $k$ moves it is at $(x_0+2k,\,y_0)$, and the move is always defined.

\textbf{Case $x_0<5$.} If the robot is at a point with $x<5$, it moves to $(x-4,y+2)$, whose $x$-coordinate is again less than $5$. So after $k$ moves it is at $(x_0-4k,\,y_0+2k)$.

In both cases the $x$-coordinates of the successive points are all different (they increase by $2$ or decrease by $4$ at every move), so no point is visited twice and the robot visits $\boxed{\text{infinitely many}}$ points. (The rule does not say what happens at $x=5$, but the robot never reaches $x=5$ from a start with $x_0\ne5$.) $\blacksquare$
