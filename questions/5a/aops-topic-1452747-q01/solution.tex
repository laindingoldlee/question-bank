\textbf{No.} Suppose $c$ is even. Then $a$ and $b$ cannot both be even, since then $2$ would divide $a$, $b$ and $c$. Also $a^2+b^2=c^2$ is even, so $a$ and $b$ have the same parity. Hence $a$ and $b$ are both odd.

Squares of odd numbers leave remainder $1$ when divided by $4$: $(2k+1)^2=4(k^2+k)+1$. So $a^2+b^2\equiv2\pmod4$. But $c$ is even, so $c^2$ is divisible by $4$. This contradiction shows that $c$ is always odd. $\blacksquare$

(So in a primitive triple exactly one of $a,b$ is even and $c$ is odd.)
