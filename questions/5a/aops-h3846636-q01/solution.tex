Central symmetry gives $R=-P$. Thus $[PQR]=2[PQO]$. Because $F$ lies between $P,Q$ and both triangles $PQO,PFO$ have their bases on $\ell$,
\[
\dfrac{[PQR]}{[PFO]}=2\dfrac{PQ}{PF}=3.
\]
It follows that $PF=2FQ$.

Let $\theta\in(0,\dfrac{\pi}{2})$ be the angle of $FP$ with the positive horizontal direction. Write $r=FP$ and use the other focus $F'=(1,0)$. The focal-distance sum is $r+PF'=4$. Squaring $PF'=4-r$ and using $PF'^2=r^2-4r\cos\theta+4$ gives $r=\dfrac{3}{2-\cos\theta}$. In the opposite direction, $FQ=\dfrac{3}{2+\cos\theta}$. Hence
\[
2=\dfrac{PF}{FQ}=\dfrac{2+\cos\theta}{2-\cos\theta},
\]
so $\cos\theta=\dfrac{2}{3}$ and $\tan\theta=\dfrac{\sqrt5}{2}$. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\ell:\ y=\dfrac{\sqrt5}{2}(x+1)}.
\]
