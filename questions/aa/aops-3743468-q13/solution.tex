The square bounds give $46\le k\le99$. For each of these 54 integers, divide $k^2+1$ by successive primes up to its square root; accept exactly when the total number of factors removed (including the final prime cofactor) is two. The qualifying $k$ are $46,48,49,50,51,52,58,59,60,61,62,64,65,69,71,76,78,79,80,85,86,88,92,95,96$. Thus there are $\boxed{25}$ values. This is exhaustive because every composite integer has a prime divisor no larger than its square root.
