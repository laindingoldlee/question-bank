Differentiating shows the necessary and sufficient identity
\[
\dfrac{a-b}{(n+1)^2+(an+b)^2}=\dfrac{p}{1+(pn+q)^2}.
\]
If $a=b$, this holds precisely when $p=0$, with $q$ arbitrary. If $a\ne b$, then $p\ne0$. Comparing quadratic and linear coefficients after cross multiplication gives
\[
\boxed{p=\dfrac{1+a^2}{a-b},\qquad q=\dfrac{1+ab}{a-b}}.
\]
The constant coefficient also agrees, because $(a-b)^2+(1+ab)^2=(1+a^2)(1+b^2)$. Thus the derivative vanishes throughout the connected interval $(-1,\infty)$, proving sufficiency. The first case is $\boxed{a=b,\ p=0,\ q\in\mathbb R}$.
