\textbf{(a)} For $k\ge p$, the product $k!=1\cdot2\cdots k$ contains the factor $p$, so $p\mid k!$. Hence all the terms $p!,(p+1)!,\dots,50!$ are divisible by $p$, and $S\equiv1!+2!+\cdots+(p-1)!\pmod p$.

\textbf{(b)} Compute $k!$ modulo $p$ step by step, multiplying the previous remainder by $k$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $p=2$: $S\equiv1!=1$. Not divisible.
\item $p=3$: $S\equiv1+2=3\equiv0$. Divisible.
\item $p=5$: $S\equiv1+2+6+24=33\equiv3$. Not divisible.
\item $p=7$: the remainders of $1!,\dots,6!$ are $1,2,6,3,1,6$, with sum $19\equiv5$. Not divisible.
\item $p=11$: the remainders of $1!,\dots,10!$ are $1,2,6,2,10,5,2,5,1,10$, with sum $44\equiv0$. Divisible.
\item $p=13$: the remainders of $1!,\dots,12!$ are $1,2,6,11,3,5,9,7,11,6,1,12$, with sum $74\equiv9$. Not divisible.
\end{itemize}
So among these primes, $\boxed{3\text{ and }11}$ divide $S$, and $2,5,7,13$ do not.

\textbf{(c)} Every $k!$ with $k\ge6$ is divisible by $9$ (it contains the factors $3$ and $6$), and $1!+2!+3!+4!+5!=153=9\cdot17$. So $9\mid S$. By (b), $11\mid S$, and since $9$ and $11$ are coprime, $99\mid S$. $\blacksquare$

(These ideas only find small prime factors. The largest prime factor of $S$, which has $65$ digits, cannot be found this way: after removing $3^2\cdot11$, the remaining factor has no prime factor below $50$, and finding its factors needs a computer.)
