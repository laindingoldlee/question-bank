Put $R=\dfrac{lw}{90}$ and $u=\sqrt w$. Squaring $\sqrt{l+u}=R-u$ gives $(2R+1)u=R^2+w-l$, so $u$ is rational. A rational square root of an integer is an integer, hence $u$ is a positive integer; then $v=\sqrt{l+u}=R-u$ is also an integer. The bounds imply $1\le u,v\le10$, and the equation becomes $u^2v^2-90v-u^3-90u=0$. Its discriminant is $D=8100+4u^5+360u^3$. For $u=1,\ldots,10$, respectively, these are
\[8464,11108,18792,35236,65600,116964,198808,323492,506736,768100.\]
Their integer square roots rounded down are $92,105,137,187,256,342,445,568,711,876$. Only the first and sixth discriminants are perfect squares. The positive root $v=\dfrac{90+\sqrt D}{2u^2}$ is 91 for $u=1$, which violates $v\le10$, and 6 for $u=6$. Thus $l=v^2-u=30$ and $w=u^2=36$. Direct substitution gives $30\cdot36=90(6+6)$, so the unique pair is $\boxed{(l,w)=(30,36)}$.
