The radicals require $x,y\ge0$. Put $d=y-x$. The equality gives $\sqrt{x+\sqrt x}=d+\sqrt y$, hence $\sqrt x=d(d+1)+2d\sqrt y$. For $x>0$, the original equality forces $y>x$ because $\sqrt{x+\sqrt x}>\sqrt x$ and $t+\sqrt t$ is increasing. Thus $d\ge1$, and the last equation implies $\sqrt x>\sqrt y$, a contradiction. Consequently $x=0$; then $y+\sqrt y=0$ forces $y=0$. The required ratio is $\boxed2$.
