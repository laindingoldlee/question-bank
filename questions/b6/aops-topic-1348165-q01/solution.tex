Let the bisectors of the angles at $A$, $B$, $C$ meet the opposite sides at $D$, $E$, $F$, and write $a=BC$, $b=CA$, $c=AB$.

\textbf{Ceva's theorem} (with its converse): for points $D,E,F$ on the sides $BC,CA,AB$ (not at vertices), the segments $AD$, $BE$, $CF$ are concurrent if and only if
\[
\dfrac{BD}{DC}\cdot\dfrac{CE}{EA}\cdot\dfrac{AF}{FB}=1 .
\]
\textbf{Angle bisector theorem}: a bisector divides the opposite side in the ratio of the adjacent sides. So
\[
\dfrac{BD}{DC}=\dfrac{AB}{AC}=\dfrac{c}{b},\qquad \dfrac{CE}{EA}=\dfrac{BC}{BA}=\dfrac{a}{c},\qquad \dfrac{AF}{FB}=\dfrac{CA}{CB}=\dfrac{b}{a}.
\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(1.2,4),B=(0,0),C=(6,0);
real a=length(B-C),bb=length(C-A),c=length(A-B);
pair I=(a*A+bb*B+c*C)/(a+bb+c);
pair D=B+(c/(bb+c))*(C-B), Ee=C+(a/(a+c))*(A-C), F=A+(bb/(bb+a))*(B-A);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(A--D,red); draw(B--Ee,red); draw(C--F,red);
dot(D);dot(Ee);dot(F);dot(I,blue);
label("$A$",A,N); label("$B$",B,SW); label("$C$",C,SE);
label("$D$",D,S); label("$E$",Ee,NE); label("$F$",F,W); label("$I$",I,dir(71),blue);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Multiplying,
\[
\dfrac{BD}{DC}\cdot\dfrac{CE}{EA}\cdot\dfrac{AF}{FB}=\dfrac{c}{b}\cdot\dfrac{a}{c}\cdot\dfrac{b}{a}=1,
\]
so by the converse of Ceva's theorem the three bisectors pass through one point $I$, the incentre. $\blacksquare$
