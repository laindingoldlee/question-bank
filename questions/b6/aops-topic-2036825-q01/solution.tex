Let \(d\) be a positive common divisor of \(n\) and \(n+1\). Then \(d\) divides their difference \((n+1)-n=1\), so \(d=1\). Hence \(\gcd(n,n+1)=1\), i.e. \(n\) and \(n+1\) are relatively prime (in fact this holds for every integer \(n\)). \(\blacksquare\)
