Place one vertex at a corner $A$ of the square and the other two vertices $P,Q$ on the two far sides, symmetric about the diagonal through $A$. Then each of $AP$ and $AQ$ makes $15^\circ$ with a side of the square, so $AP=\dfrac{2}{\cos15^\circ}$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(2,0),C=(2,2),D=(0,2);
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(1));
pair P=(2,2*Tan(15)), Q=(2*Tan(15),2);
draw(A--P--Q--cycle,blue+linewidth(1));
label("$A$",A,SW);label("$P$",P,E);label("$Q$",Q,N);label("\scriptsize $15^\circ$",A+(1.3,0.16));label("$2$",(1,0),S);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

This is the largest: a largest triangle can be translated until a vertex is at a corner and rotated until the other two vertices lie on the sides; among such positions, any side making an angle $\theta\le15^\circ$ with a side of the square has length at most $\dfrac{2}{\cos\theta}\le\dfrac{2}{\cos15^\circ}$ (the two sides from the corner make angles adding to $30^\circ$, so one is at most $15^\circ$ from a side, and its length is at most $\dfrac{2}{\cos\theta}$; equality in both needs the symmetric position). With $\cos15^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}$:
\[s=\dfrac{8}{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}=\boxed{2\left(\sqrt6-\sqrt2\right)}\approx2.07,\qquad\text{area}=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}s^2=\boxed{8\sqrt3-12}\approx1.86.\]
