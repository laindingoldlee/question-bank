Usain Bolt runs $100$ metres in $9.58$ seconds; in this problem assume that he runs at a constant speed. A famous paradox says that the run is impossible: before covering $100$ m he must cover $50$ m, before that $25$ m, before that $12.5$ m, and so on forever, so he would have to complete infinitely many stages.

Split the run into stages: stage $1$ is from the $50$ m mark to the $100$ m mark, stage $2$ from $25$ m to $50$ m, stage $3$ from $12.5$ m to $25$ m, and in general stage $k$ runs from the $\dfrac{100}{2^k}$ m mark to the $\dfrac{100}{2^{k-1}}$ m mark.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Find the length of stage $k$ and the time Bolt needs for it.
\item[(b)] Show that stages $1$ to $n$ together cover $100\left(1-\dfrac{1}{2^n}\right)$ metres, and that every point of the track after the start belongs to some stage.
\item[(c)] Show that the total time of all the stages is $9.58$ seconds, and explain why having infinitely many stages is no obstacle to finishing the run.
\end{enumerate}
