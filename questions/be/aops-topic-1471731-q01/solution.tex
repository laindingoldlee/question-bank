The number $\varphi$ is the positive root of $t^2=t+1$, since $\left(\dfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2}\right)^2=\dfrac{6+2\sqrt5}{4}=\dfrac{3+\sqrt5}{2}=\varphi+1$.

\textbf{(a)} \emph{Bound.} $x_1=1<\varphi$. If $0<x_n<\varphi$, then $x_{n+1}=\sqrt{1+x_n}<\sqrt{1+\varphi}=\sqrt{\varphi^2}=\varphi$. By induction $0<x_n<\varphi$ for all $n$.

\emph{Increasing.} For $x>0$, $\sqrt{1+x}>x$ is equivalent to $x^2-x-1<0$, that is, to $x$ lying strictly between the roots $\dfrac{1-\sqrt5}{2}$ and $\varphi$. Since $0<x_n<\varphi$, we get $x_{n+1}>x_n$.

An increasing sequence that is bounded above converges; let $L$ be its limit. Then $1\le L\le\varphi$, and letting $n\to\infty$ in $x_{n+1}=\sqrt{1+x_n}$ gives $L=\sqrt{1+L}$, so $L^2=L+1$ with $L>0$. Hence $L=\varphi$:
\[
\sqrt{1+\sqrt{1+\sqrt{1+\cdots}}}=\varphi .
\]

\textbf{(b)} The expression is $1$ plus the nested radical of (a), so it equals
\[
1+\varphi=\varphi^2=\boxed{\dfrac{3+\sqrt5}{2}}\approx2.618 ,
\]
not $\varphi\approx1.618$. The correct statement is $\varphi=\sqrt{1+\sqrt{1+\sqrt{1+\cdots}}}$ (equivalently, $\varphi=1+\dfrac{1}{\varphi}$).
