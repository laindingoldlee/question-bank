If $a=c$ and $b=d$, then clearly $ab=cd$ and $a+b=c+d$.

Conversely, suppose $a+b=c+d=S$ and $ab=cd=P$. Then for every $t$,
\[
(t-a)(t-b)=t^2-St+P=(t-c)(t-d).
\]
Putting $t=c$ gives $(c-a)(c-b)=0$, so $c=a$ or $c=b$. If $c=a$, then $d=S-c=b$. If $c=b$, then $d=S-c=a$, and $c\ge d$ gives $b\ge a$; together with $a\ge b$ this forces $a=b$, so again $c=a$ and $d=b$. In every case $a=c$ and $b=d$. $\blacksquare$

(In other words, $a$ and $b$ are the two roots of $t^2-St+P=0$, and the condition $a\ge b$ says which root is which.)
