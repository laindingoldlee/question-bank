\textbf{(a)} The digits of $112_3$ stand for $1\cdot3^2$, $1\cdot3^1$ and $2\cdot3^0$, so $112_3=9+3+2=\boxed{14}$.

\textbf{(b)} Divide by $2$ repeatedly and record the remainders:
\[14=2\cdot7+0,\quad 7=2\cdot3+1,\quad 3=2\cdot1+1,\quad 1=2\cdot0+1.\]
Reading the remainders from last to first, $14=\boxed{1110_2}$. Check: $8+4+2+0=14$.

For base $3$: $14=3\cdot4+2$, $4=3\cdot1+1$, $1=3\cdot0+1$, so $14=\boxed{112_3}$, in agreement with (a).

(Each division by $2$ peels off the last binary digit: the remainder is the units digit, and the quotient is the number formed by the remaining digits.)
