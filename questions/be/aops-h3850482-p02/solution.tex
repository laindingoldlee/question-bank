Put $d=x-y$ and $s=x+y$. Since $x^3-y^3=d^3+3dxy$,
\[
xy=\dfrac{61-d^3}{3d-1},\qquad
s^2=d^2+4xy=\dfrac{244-d^3-d^2}{3d-1}.
\]
For every integer $d\le0$, the numerator of the last fraction is positive while the denominator is negative, impossible. Thus $d\ge1$. Nonnegativity then requires $d^3+d^2\le244$, so $d\le5$.

For $d=1,2,3,4,5$, the values of $xy$ are $30,\dfrac{53}{5},\dfrac{17}{4},-\dfrac{3}{11},-\dfrac{32}{7}$. Only $d=1$ gives an integer. Then $s^2=121$, so $s=\pm11$. Recovering $x=\dfrac{(s+d)}{2}$ and $y=\dfrac{(s-d)}{2}$ gives
\[
\boxed{(x,y)=(6,5),\ (-5,-6)}.
\]
Both directly satisfy the equation.
