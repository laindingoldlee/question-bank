If the common remainder is $r$, then $0\le r\le5$ and $6\mid n-r$, $8\mid n-r$, so $24\mid n-r$. Conversely, $n=24q+r$ with $0\le r\le 5$ has remainder $r$ on division by both $6$ and $8$. So we need $n\bmod 24\in\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}$.

The integers from $101$ to $199$: the blocks starting at $120$, $144$, $168$, $192$ give $120$--$125$, $144$--$149$, $168$--$173$, $192$--$197$ ($24$ numbers), and $96$--$101$ contributes only $101$. The total is $\boxed{25}$.
