Represent a solution by a row of $n$ stars and $k-1$ bars: $x_1$ stars, a bar, $x_2$ stars, and so on. Every arrangement of $n$ stars and $k-1$ bars in $n+k-1$ positions gives exactly one solution, so the count is the number of ways to choose the bar positions, $\binom{n+k-1}{k-1}$. Choosing the star positions instead gives $\binom{n+k-1}{n}$, which is the same number by $\binom mr=\binom m{m-r}$. $\blacksquare$

(Formulas that look different, such as $\binom{n+k-1}{k}$, use the letters with swapped meanings: $k$ items into $n$ bins.)
