The partial sums are
\[
s_1=1,\ s_2=2,\ s_3=1,\ s_4=2,\ \dots,
\]
that is, $s_n=1$ for odd $n$ and $s_n=2$ for even $n$.

(a) The partial sums keep jumping between $1$ and $2$, so they have no limit: the series $\boxed{\text{diverges}}$.

(b) Among $s_1,\dots,s_n$ there are $\left\lceil\dfrac {n}{2}\right\rceil$ ones and $\left\lfloor\dfrac {n}{2}\right\rfloor$ twos. For even $n=2m$ the average is $\dfrac{m+2m}{2m}=\dfrac{3}{2}$; for odd $n=2m+1$ it is $\dfrac{3m+1}{2m+1}$, which also tends to $\dfrac{3}{2}$. So the limit is $\boxed{\dfrac{3}{2}}$. (This is the Cesaro sum of the series; it is a way of assigning a value to some divergent series, not an ordinary sum.)
