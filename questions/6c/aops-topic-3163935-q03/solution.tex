If both primes are odd, the two squares are even and hence divisible by 4; their difference $6q$ would then be divisible by 4, impossible for odd $q$. Thus one prime is 2. If the other is odd, $p+q$ is an odd square, hence 1 modulo 8, forcing the other prime to be 7 modulo 8. If $p=2$, the second expression is 3 modulo 8; if $q=2$, it is 5 modulo 8. Neither is a square. Therefore $p=q=2$, which gives squares 4 and 16. Hence $\boxed{pq=4}$.
