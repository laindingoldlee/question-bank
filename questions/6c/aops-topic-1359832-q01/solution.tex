$\boxed{\text{Yes}}$. As it rolls, the bottom face always covers a triangle of the triangular lattice generated by the starting triangle. Write the lattice points as $i\mathbf{u}+j\mathbf{v}$, where $\mathbf{u}$, $\mathbf{v}$ are two sides of the starting triangle, and give the point the label determined by $(i\bmod2,\ j\bmod2)$: four labels $a,b,c,d$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(7cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair u=(1,0), v=dir(60);
string[] nm={"$a$","$b$","$c$","$d$"};
for(int i=-3;i<=4;++i) for(int j=-2;j<=2;++j){
  pair P=i*u+j*v; if(abs(P.x)>3.1) continue;
  pair Q=P+u, R=P+v; if(abs(Q.x)<=3.1) draw(P--Q,gray); if(abs(R.x)<=3.1 && j<2) draw(P--R,gray);
  if(j>-2){pair T=P-v+u; if(abs(T.x)<=3.1) draw(P--T,gray);}
}
for(int i=-3;i<=4;++i) for(int j=-2;j<=2;++j){
  pair P=i*u+j*v; if(abs(P.x)>3.1) continue;
  int k=2*((i%2+2)%2)+((j%2+2)%2);
  label(nm[k],P,fontsize(9pt),Fill(white));
}
fill((0,0)--u--v--cycle,palered+opacity(0.6));
fill(u--(u+v)--v--cycle,paleblue+opacity(0.6));
draw((0,0)--u--v--cycle,red+linewidth(0.9)); draw(u--(u+v)--v,blue+linewidth(0.9));
label("$a$",(0,0),fontsize(9pt),Fill(white)); label("$c$",u,fontsize(9pt),Fill(white)); label("$b$",v,fontsize(9pt),Fill(white)); label("$d$",u+v,fontsize(9pt),Fill(white));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\textbf{Facts about the labelling.} (1) The three vertices of every lattice triangle have three different labels: the triangles are $\{(i,j),(i+1,j),(i,j+1)\}$ and $\{(i+1,j),(i,j+1),(i+1,j+1)\}$, whose parities are all different. (2) If two lattice triangles share an edge, their two opposite vertices have different labels: they differ by $(1,1)$, $(1,-2)$ or $(-2,1)$, which changes at least one parity. Hence the two triangles together carry all four labels (as the red and blue triangles in the figure do).

\textbf{Claim.} Match the four vertices of the tetrahedron with the labels $a,b,c,d$ so that at the start each bottom vertex sits on the lattice point with its own label (the top vertex gets the fourth label). Then after every roll, every bottom vertex again sits on a point with its own label.

Proof by induction on the number of rolls. Suppose the bottom face covers triangle $XYZ$ with matching labels, and the tetrahedron tips over edge $XY$. The vertices on $X$ and $Y$ stay, the vertex on $Z$ goes up, and the former top vertex comes down onto the lattice point $W$ on the other side of $XY$. The labels of $X$, $Y$, $Z$ are three different labels and the top vertex has the fourth; by fact (2), $W$ has a label different from those of $X$, $Y$ and $Z$, so it is exactly the top vertex's label. This proves the claim.

So the position of every vertex is determined by the triangle the tetrahedron stands on: when it is back on the starting triangle, each vertex of that triangle is occupied by the tetrahedron vertex with the same label as at the start, and the top vertex is the remaining one. Every vertex is back at its original place.
