Put $b_n=-a_n$. The nonzero prescribed sum implies $b_1=1$, and the product relation makes $b$ completely multiplicative. Also $b_{67}=-\dfrac{1}{7}$ and $T:=\sum b_n=\dfrac{1}{12}$. If $S$ is finite, the negative-part sum of $b$ is finite; convergence of the total then makes its positive-part sum finite as well. Thus $A:=\sum|b_n|$ is finite and
\[
S=\dfrac{A-T}{2}.
\]

Absolute convergence and unique prime factorization allow Euler products
\[
T=\prod_p\dfrac{1}{1-b_p},\qquad
A=\prod_p\dfrac{1}{1-|b_p|}.
\]
These follow first over any finite set of primes by multiplying geometric series, then by absolute convergence over all primes. For $|u|<1$,
\[
\dfrac{1}{(1-|u|)(1-u)}\ge1.
\]
The factor at $67$ is $\dfrac{1}{(1-\dfrac{1}{7})(1+\dfrac{1}{7})}=\dfrac{49}{48}$. Multiplying the inequalities gives $AT\ge\dfrac{49}{48}$, hence $A\ge\dfrac{49}{4}$. Therefore
\[
S\ge\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{49}{4}-\dfrac{1}{12}\right)=\dfrac{73}{12}.
\]

To prove sharpness, choose $m$ distinct primes other than $67$ and put
\[
u_m=\left(\dfrac{21}{2}\right)^{\dfrac{1}{m}}-1.
\]
For sufficiently large $m$, $0<u_m<1$. Define $b_{67}=-\dfrac{1}{7}$, set $b_p=-u_m$ at these $m$ primes and $b_p=0$ at all other primes, then extend completely multiplicatively with $b_1=1$. Its series converges absolutely, and
\[
T=\dfrac{7}{8}(1+u_m)^{-m}=\dfrac{1}{12},\qquad
A=\dfrac{7}{6}(1-u_m)^{-m}.
\]
As $m\to\infty$, $u_m\to0$ and
$\dfrac{-\ln(1-u_m)}{\ln(1+u_m)}\to1$, so $(1-u_m)^{-m}\to\dfrac{21}{2}$. Thus $A\to\dfrac{49}{4}$ and $S\to\dfrac{73}{12}$. The sequences $a_n=-b_n$ meet every stated condition, so no larger universal bound is possible. Consequently
\[
\boxed{C=\dfrac{73}{12}}.
\]
