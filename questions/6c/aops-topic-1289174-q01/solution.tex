Let $\varphi=\angle BCD<90^\circ$. Since $CD=CB=CE$, triangle $CDE$ is isosceles, and its apex angle is
\[
\angle DCE=\angle DCB+\angle BCE=\varphi+90^\circ .
\]
So $\angle CDE=\dfrac{180^\circ-(\varphi+90^\circ)}{2}=45^\circ-\dfrac{\varphi}{2}$.

The diagonal $CA$ of the rhombus bisects $\angle BCD$, so $\angle GCD=\dfrac{\varphi}{2}$. In triangle $GCD$,
\[
\angle CGD=180^\circ-\dfrac{\varphi}{2}-\left(45^\circ-\dfrac{\varphi}{2}\right)=135^\circ,
\]
and since $A$, $G$, $C$ are collinear with $G$ between them, $\angle AGD=180^\circ-135^\circ=\boxed{45^\circ}$, whatever the shape of the rhombus.
