Consider the following infinite product $P$: \[P =\prod_{n \in S}\sqrt[n]{n} = \sqrt[1]{1} \cdot \sqrt[5]{5}\cdot \sqrt[7]{7}\cdot \sqrt[11]{11}\cdot \sqrt[25]{25}\cdot \sqrt[35]{35} ...\]where $S$ is the infinite set of positive integers with exclusively 5, 7, or $11$ in its prime factorization. In other words, the elements in $S$ are expressed as $5^x  \cdot  7^y \cdot 11^z$ for all nonnegative integers $x$, $y$, $z$. $P$ can be expressed as $5^a  \cdot  7^b  \cdot  11^c$ for rational numbers $a$, $b$, and $c$. If $a = \dfrac{a_1}{a_2}$, $b = \dfrac{b_1}{b_2}$ , and $c = \dfrac{c_1}{c_2}$ , where $a_1$, $a_2$, $b_1$, $b_2$, $c_1$, $c_2$ are positive integers where gcd$(a_1, a_2) = 1$, gcd$(b_1, b_2) = 1$, gcd$(c_1, c_2) = 1$. Find $a_1 + a_2 + b_1 + b_2 + c_1 + c_2$.
