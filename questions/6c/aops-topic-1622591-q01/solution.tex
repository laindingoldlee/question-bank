Solving $y=3x+a$ for $x$ gives $f^{-1}(x)=\dfrac{x-a}{3}$. At an intersection point $f(x)=f^{-1}(x)$:
\[
3x+a=\dfrac{x-a}{3}\iff 9x+3a=x-a\iff x=-\dfrac {a}{2} .
\]
So the graphs meet at exactly one point, and $-\dfrac {a}{2}=-3$ gives $a=6$. The $y$-coordinate is
\[
f(-3)=3(-3)+6=\boxed{-3}.
\]
(As expected, the intersection point $(-3,-3)$ lies on the line $y=x$, the mirror line between the graph of a function and the graph of its inverse.)
