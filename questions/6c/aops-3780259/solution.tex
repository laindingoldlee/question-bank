Every positive cube qualifies. Among noncubes, 2 and 9 qualify, while 3 and 10 fail by cubing the comparison $\sqrt[3]{n} \le k + \dfrac{1}{n}$. For fixed $k=\lfloor\sqrt[3]n\rfloor$, $n(\sqrt[3]n-k)$ increases with $n$, so only the first noncube needs checking. For $k=3$, $28(\sqrt[3]{28}-3)>1$ since $(\dfrac{85}{28})^3 < 28$. For $k\ge4$, writing $u=\sqrt[3]{k^3+1}<k+1$ gives $(k^3+1) (u-k) = \dfrac{k^3+1}{u^2+uk+k^2} > 1$, since $u^2+uk+k^2<3k^2+3k+1\le k^3+1$. Thus precisely the cubes, 2 and 9 qualify. If $m=\lfloor\sqrt[3]k\rfloor$, the self-referential condition is $k = [\dfrac{m (m+1)}{2}]^2 + 11$, with $m^3\le k<(m+1)^3$. For $m=3$ this gives 47, below 50; for $m=4$ it gives 111 and the interval test holds. For $m\ge5$, $m^2>4(m+1)$ implies the sum already exceeds $(m+1)^3$. Hence $\boxed{k=111}$.
