An integer with at most four divisors is $1$, a prime, a prime square, a prime cube, or a product of two distinct primes. Count ordered pairs of inputs. Product $1$ gives one pair. There are $15$ primes at most $50$, so prime products give $30$ pairs $(1,p),(p,1)$. For prime squares, the pairs $(p,p)$ give $15$, and $(1,p^2),(p^2,1)$ for $p=2,3,5,7$ give eight more, total $23$. Prime cubes give four pairs involving $1$ for $p=2,3$, and eight pairs $(p,p^2),(p^2,p)$ for $p=2,3,5,7$, total $12$. For products of distinct primes, there are $15\cdot14=210$ ordered prime-prime pairs. The thirteen such products at most $50$ are $6,10,14,15,21,22,26,33,34,35,38,39,46$, giving another $26$ pairs involving $1$. The total is $1+30+23+12+210+26=302$ out of $2500$, hence $\boxed{\dfrac{151}{1250}}$.
