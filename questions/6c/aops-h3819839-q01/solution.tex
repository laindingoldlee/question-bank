The divisibility rule for $11$ gives $1-B+A-C\equiv0\pmod{11}$, or $C\equiv A+1-B\pmod{11}$. For $A=8$, choose $C=9-B$, a digit for every digit $B$.
For $A=0,1,\ldots,7$, choose $B=A+2$; then the required residue of $C$ is $10$, not a decimal digit. For $A=9$, choose $B=0$, again requiring $C\equiv10$. Hence the only possible digit is $\boxed8$.
