The polynomial is $x(1+x+\cdots+x^5)^2$. Its distinct roots are zero and the five sixth roots of unity other than $1$. Squaring $-1$ gives $1$, contributing $1$. The other four nonzero roots square to $-\dfrac{1}{2}\pm\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i$, each sign occurring twice; each contributes $\dfrac{1+\sqrt3}{2}$. Zero contributes zero. Thus the sum is $\boxed{1+4\cdot\dfrac{1+\sqrt3}{2}=3+2\sqrt3}$.
