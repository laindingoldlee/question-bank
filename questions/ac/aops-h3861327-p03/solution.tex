There are exactly five shapes up to congruence. In each diagram, the vertices $A,B,C,D,E$ are listed counterclockwise. The side tuple means $(AB,BC,CD,DE,EA)$, and the angle tuple gives the interior angles at $(A,B,C,D,E)$, in degrees:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{shape}&\text{tiles}&\text{side tuple}&\text{angle tuple}\\\hline
1&5&(1,1,1,2,2)&(60,240,60,120,60)\\
2&6&(1,1,1,3,2)&(60,240,120,60,60)\\
3&7&(1,2,1,3,2)&(60,240,60,120,60)\\
4&7&(1,1,2,3,2)&(60,300,60,60,60)\\
5&7&(1,1,1,2,2)&(120,120,120,120,60)
\end{array}
\]

Here is a finite exhaustive construction that also proves completeness. Use triangular-grid coordinates $(i,j)$ for the Euclidean point $(i+\dfrac{j}{2},\sqrt3\dfrac{j}{2})$. Start with one unit triangle. From every current shape, add one triangle across each exposed edge, discarding additions already occupied. Identify two shapes when a translation, a multiple-of-$60^\circ$ rotation, or a reflection maps their unit triangles onto each other. Repeat up to seven tiles. The counts of distinct edge-connected shapes at sizes $1$ through $7$ are
\[
1,\ 1,\ 1,\ 3,\ 4,\ 12,\ 24.
\]
This generation is exhaustive: the adjacency graph of any connected collection has a spanning tree; removing a leaf of that tree leaves a connected collection, so every shape is generated from a smaller one.

For each generated shape, retain its exposed unit edges and traverse the boundary. Discard a shape if the boundary is not one simple closed curve. Merge consecutive collinear boundary edges; retain the shape exactly when five edges remain. The five surviving boundary vertex lists are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
1&(2,0)&(1,1)&(1,2)&(0,2)&(0,0)\\
2&(2,0)&(1,1)&(1,2)&(0,3)&(0,0)\\
3&(2,0)&(1,1)&(1,3)&(0,3)&(0,0)\\
4&(2,0)&(1,1)&(2,1)&(0,3)&(0,0)\\
5&(2,0)&(2,1)&(1,2)&(0,2)&(0,0)
\end{array}
\]
These coordinates allow every length and angle in the first table to be checked directly. The diagrams show the actual unit-triangle decompositions.
