Ask: \emph{``If I asked you whether this road leads to the City of Knights, would you say yes?''}

Let $S$ be the statement ``this road leads to the City of Knights''.
\begin{itemize}
\item A \textbf{knight} would answer the inner question truthfully, and he also tells the truth about what he would say. So he says ``yes'' exactly when $S$ is true.
\item A \textbf{knave} would answer the inner question with a lie: ``no'' if $S$ is true and ``yes'' if $S$ is false. Asked what he would say, he lies about that too. So he says ``yes'' exactly when $S$ is true.
\end{itemize}
Either way the answer is ``yes'' if and only if the road leads to the City of Knights. So: \textbf{``yes'' means the City of Knights, ``no'' means the City of Knaves}.

(Another question that works: ``Is it true that you are a knight if and only if this road leads to the City of Knights?'' A knight answers the truth of $S$, and a knave lies about a statement whose truth value is the opposite of $S$.)
