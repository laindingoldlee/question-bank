In music, take the $12$ notes
\[\text{C},\ \text{C}\sharp,\ \text{D},\ \text{D}\sharp,\ \text{E},\ \text{F},\ \text{F}\sharp,\ \text{G},\ \text{G}\sharp,\ \text{A},\ \text{A}\sharp,\ \text{B},\]
listed from lowest to highest, and assume that only this one octave exists. A \emph{vocal range} consists of all the notes from some lowest note up to some highest note, inclusive; the lowest and highest notes may coincide (a range of one note). For example, the range from C to G consists of $8$ notes. Each possible vocal range is equally likely to be given to a person. What is the probability that a random person can sing a song that needs every note from C to G?
