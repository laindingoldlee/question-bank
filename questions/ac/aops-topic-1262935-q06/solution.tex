\emph{Squares with horizontal and vertical sides:} of side $1$: $3\cdot3=9$; side $2$: $2\cdot2=4$; side $3$: $1$. Total $14$.

\emph{Tilted squares.} A tilted square has side vector $(a,b)$ with $a,b\ge1$, and its vertices fit in an $(a+b)\times(a+b)$ box of the grid. With $a+b\le3$: $(1,1)$ fits in a $2\times2$ box, of which there are $4$; $(1,2)$ and $(2,1)$ fit in a $3\times3$ box (one box, and there are $2$ such squares in it).

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
for(int i=0;i<4;++i) for(int j=0;j<4;++j) dot((i,j));
draw((0,0)--(1,0)--(1,1)--(0,1)--cycle,blue);
draw((1,1)--(3,1)--(3,3)--(1,3)--cycle,red);
draw((2,0)--(3,1)--(2,2)--(1,1)--cycle,heavygreen);
draw((0,1)--(2,0)--(3,2)--(1,3)--cycle,orange);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

That gives $4+2=6$ tilted squares, and the total is $14+6=\boxed{20}$.
