Let $N=n^4+n^2+3$. Since $n^4+n^2=n^2(n^2+1)$ is a product of two consecutive integers, it is even, so $N$ is odd.

If $N=p+q$ with primes $p\le q$, then $p$ and $q$ have different parities, so $p=2$ (the only even prime) and $q=N-2=n^4+n^2+1$. But
\[
n^4+n^2+1=(n^2+1)^2-n^2=(n^2-n+1)(n^2+n+1),
\]
and for $n\ge2$ both factors are greater than $1$ (the smaller is $n^2-n+1=n(n-1)+1\ge3$). So $q$ is composite, a contradiction. Hence $N$ is not a sum of two primes. $\blacksquare$

(For $n=1$ the claim fails: $1+1+3=5=2+3$.)
