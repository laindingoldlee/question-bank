\textbf{(a)} Since $p,q,r,s$ are the roots, $ax^4+bx^3+cx^2+dx+e=a(x-p)(x-q)(x-r)(x-s)$. Expanding the product, the coefficient of $x^3$ comes from choosing $x$ in three factors and $-p$ (etc.) in one; the coefficient of $x^2$ from choosing two of the roots, and so on:
\[a(x-p)(x-q)(x-r)(x-s)=a\left[x^4-e_1x^3+e_2x^2-e_3x+e_4\right],\]
where $e_1=p+q+r+s$, $e_2=pq+pr+ps+qr+qs+rs$, $e_3=pqr+pqs+prs+qrs$, $e_4=pqrs$. Comparing coefficients:
\[\boxed{e_1=-\dfrac ba,\quad e_2=\dfrac ca,\quad e_3=-\dfrac da,\quad e_4=\dfrac ea}.\]
The signs alternate, starting with $-$.

\textbf{(b)} Here $a=1$, $c=-7$, $d=8$, so the formulas give $e_2=-7$ and $e_3=-8$. Directly: $e_2=(-2)(-1)+(-2)(2)+(-2)(3)+(-1)(2)+(-1)(3)+(2)(3)=2-4-6-2-3+6=-7$, and $e_3=(-2)(-1)(2)+(-2)(-1)(3)+(-2)(2)(3)+(-1)(2)(3)=4+6-12-6=-8$. Both agree.
