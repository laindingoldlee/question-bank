First, \(\dfrac{7}{5}<\sqrt2<\dfrac{10}{7}\) because \(\dfrac{49}{25}<2<\dfrac{100}{49}\). Since \(x\mapsto 2^x\) is increasing,
\[
2^{\dfrac{7}{5}}<2^{\sqrt2}<2^{\dfrac{10}{7}}.
\]
Lower bound: \(2^{\dfrac{7}{5}}>2.6\iff 2^7>2.6^5\). Now \(2.6^2=6.76\), \(2.6^4=45.6976\), \(2.6^5=118.81376<128=2^7\).

Upper bound: \(2^{\dfrac{10}{7}}<2.7\iff 2^{10}<2.7^7\). Now \(2.7^3=19.683\), \(2.7^6=387.420489\), \(2.7^7=1046.0353203>1024=2^{10}\).

Hence \(2.6<2^{\sqrt2}<2.7\). \(\blacksquare\) (In fact \(2^{\sqrt2}\approx 2.665\); by the Gelfond--Schneider theorem it is even transcendental.)
