Yes. We need $x^3=z^2-y^2=(z-y)(z+y)$. For any integer $k\ge2$, try $z-y=k$ and $z+y=k^2$, that is,
\[
x=k,\qquad y=\dfrac{k(k-1)}{2},\qquad z=\dfrac{k(k+1)}{2}.
\]
Here $y$ and $z$ are positive integers (a product of consecutive integers is even, and $y\ge1$ for $k\ge2$), and
\[
z^2-y^2=(z-y)(z+y)=k\cdot k^2=k^3=x^3 .
\]
For example $k=2,3,4$ give $8+1=9$, $27+9=36$, $64+36=100$. Different $k$ give different triples, so there are $\boxed{\text{infinitely many}}$. (Equivalently, $y$ and $z$ are consecutive triangular numbers, and $1^3+2^3+\cdots+k^3=\left(\dfrac{k(k+1)}{2}\right)^2$.)
