A cut producing two triangles must join a vertex $A$ to a point $D$ on the opposite side $BC$. Write the original angles at $A,B,C$ as $V,U,W$, and let the cut split $V$ into $p,q$, with $p+q=V$.

Triangle $ABD$ is isosceles exactly when
\[
p\in\left\{U,\dfrac{180^\circ-U}{2},180^\circ-2U\right\}.
\]
The corresponding condition on $ACD$ is
\[
q\in\left\{W,\dfrac{180^\circ-W}{2},180^\circ-2W\right\}.
\]
These list all choices of equal angles; all angles must be positive.

If the $36^\circ$ angle is the cut vertex, substitute $V=36^\circ$, $U+W=144^\circ$ in the nine combinations. Only $(U,W)=(12^\circ,132^\circ)$ or its reversal gives positive $p,q$. If instead it is a base angle, put $U=36^\circ$. The choices $p=36^\circ,72^\circ,108^\circ$, combined with the three displayed possibilities for $q$ and $V=144^\circ-W$, give the following complete list, with one valid cut for each:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
V&U&W&p&q\\ \hline
36&12&132&12&24\\
126&18&36&18&108\\
108&36&36&72&36\\
90&36&54&36&54\\
72&36&72&36&36
\end{array}
\]
All table entries are degrees. A ray splitting angle $V$ into the listed positive $p,q$ meets the opposite side, and the equal-angle conditions ensure both pieces are isosceles. Thus the possible largest angles are
\[
\boxed{72^\circ,\ 90^\circ,\ 108^\circ,\ 126^\circ,\ 132^\circ}.
\]

\end{asy}
