If $m=4k+1$, then $3m+1=4(3k+1)$, so at least two factors of 2 occur. If $m=4k+3$, then $3m+1=2(6k+5)$, whose parenthesised factor is odd; exactly one division occurs. The resulting number is $6k+5\equiv2k+1\pmod4$, which is $1\pmod4$ exactly when $k$ is even. Thus the required condition is $\boxed{m\equiv3\pmod8}$. Equivalently, modulo 16 the possibilities are 3 and 11. These local observations do not prove convergence of every Collatz sequence.
