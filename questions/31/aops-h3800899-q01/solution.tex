Since $G$ and $G+K$ are integers, $K$ is an integer. Write $R=\dfrac{p}{q}$ in lowest terms. The second and fourth daily amounts must be multiples of $q$, so write the five amounts as
\[(p+q)a-qb,\quad qa,\quad pa,\quad qb,\quad pb\]
for positive integers $a,b$. Their sum is $S=2(p+q)a+pb$. If $a=1$, positivity of the first amount and $K=qb-pa$ forces $b=1$; then the third and fifth amounts coincide. Hence $a\ge2$, giving $4q+5p\le S\le100$.
If $p\ge2$, then $q\le\dfrac{100-5p}{4}$ and $\dfrac{p}{q}\ge\dfrac{4p}{100-5p}>\dfrac{1}{23}$. If $p=1$, the same bound gives $q\le23$. Thus $R\ge\dfrac{1}{23}$.
At $p=1,q=23$, $48a+b\le100$ forces $a=2$. Positivity of $G=48-23b$ leaves $b=1$ or $2$; $b=2$ repeats the third and fifth amounts, so $b=1$. The daily amounts are $(25,46,2,23,1)$, with $G=25,K=21,S=97$. They are all distinct and valid. Therefore $\boxed{R_{\min}=\dfrac{1}{23}}$ and the unique possible value is $\boxed{X=143}$.
