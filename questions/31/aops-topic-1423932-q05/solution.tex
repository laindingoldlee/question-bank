Compute the prime factorisations and use $d\left(p_1^{e_1}\cdots p_k^{e_k}\right)=(e_1+1)\cdots(e_k+1)$:
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
T_1=1, & T_2=2, & T_3=3,\\
T_4=8=2^3\ (4), & T_5=15\ (4), & T_6=48=2^4\cdot3\ (10),\\
T_7=105=3\cdot5\cdot7\ (8), & T_8=384=2^7\cdot3\ (16), & T_9=945=3^3\cdot5\cdot7\ (16),
\end{array}
\]
with the number of divisors in brackets (and $1$, $2$, $2$ for the first three). All are below $25$. For $n=10$,
\[
T_{10}=10\cdot8\cdot6\cdot4\cdot2=3840=2^8\cdot3\cdot5,
\]
which has $9\cdot2\cdot2=36\ge25$ divisors. The answer is $\boxed{10}$.
