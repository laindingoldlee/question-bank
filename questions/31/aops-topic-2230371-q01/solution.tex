The trick is to multiply by the common ratio and subtract, which turns the series into a geometric one.

\textbf{(a)} Here $a=d=1$, $r=\dfrac{1}{2}$:
\[S=1+\dfrac{2}{2}+\dfrac{3}{4}+\dfrac{4}{8}+\cdots,\qquad\dfrac {S}{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{2}{4}+\dfrac{3}{8}+\cdots .\]
Subtracting term by term,
\[S-\dfrac {S}{2}=1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{1}{8}+\cdots=2,\]
so $S=\boxed{4}$.

\textbf{(b)} Let $T$ be the sum (it converges for $|r|<1$, since the terms are bounded by a constant times $k|r|^{k}$). Then
\[T-rT=a+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\Bigl(\bigl(a+(k-1)d\bigr)-\bigl(a+(k-2)d\bigr)\Bigr)r^{k-1}=a+d\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}r^{k-1}=a+\dfrac{dr}{1-r}.\]
Dividing by $1-r$,
\[T=\dfrac{a}{1-r}+\dfrac{dr}{(1-r)^2}.\]
For (a) this gives $\dfrac{1}{\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)}+\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)}{\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2}=2+2=4$, as found. $\blacksquare$
