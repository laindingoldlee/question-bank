\textbf{Reformulation.} Place the digits $1,2,\ldots,9$ in increasing order. When a digit is placed, every cell to its left and every cell above it must already be filled, so at each moment the filled cells of the rows form left-justified segments of lengths $r_1\ge r_2\ge r_3$. Conversely, any way of adding cells one at a time that keeps $r_1\ge r_2\ge r_3$ gives a valid grid. So we count sequences of moves from $(0,0,0)$ to $(3,3,3)$ that increase one coordinate by $1$ at a time and keep $r_1\ge r_2\ge r_3$.

\textbf{Counting.} Let $f(r_1,r_2,r_3)$ be the number of ways to reach $(r_1,r_2,r_3)$; then $f$ is the sum of $f$ over the allowed predecessors. Working up:
\begin{itemize}
\item States with $r_3=0$: $f(r_1,r_2,0)$ is the number of ballot paths, giving $f(1,0,0)=f(2,0,0)=f(3,0,0)=1$, $f(1,1,0)=1$, $f(2,1,0)=2$, $f(2,2,0)=2$, $f(3,1,0)=3$, $f(3,2,0)=5$, $f(3,3,0)=5$.
\item $f(1,1,1)=f(1,1,0)=1$; $f(2,1,1)=f(2,1,0)+f(1,1,1)=3$; $f(2,2,1)=f(2,2,0)+f(2,1,1)=5$; $f(3,1,1)=f(3,1,0)+f(2,1,1)=6$; $f(3,2,1)=f(3,2,0)+f(3,1,1)+f(2,2,1)=16$; $f(3,3,1)=f(3,3,0)+f(3,2,1)=21$.
\item $f(2,2,2)=f(2,2,1)=5$; $f(3,2,2)=f(3,2,1)+f(2,2,2)=21$; $f(3,3,2)=f(3,3,1)+f(3,2,2)=42$; $f(3,3,3)=f(3,3,2)=42$.
\end{itemize}
(This agrees with the hook length formula: $\dfrac{9!}{5\cdot4\cdot3\cdot4\cdot3\cdot2\cdot3\cdot2\cdot1}=\dfrac{362880}{8640}=42$.)

The answer is $\boxed{42}$.
