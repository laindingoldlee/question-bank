Let $a$ be the number of apples and $p$ the price of an orange in cents. Then there are $48-a$ oranges and
\[
a(p+10)+(48-a)p=568\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad48p+10a=568\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad24p+5a=284 .
\]
Reduce modulo $24$: $5a\equiv284\equiv20\pmod{24}$. Since $5\cdot5=25\equiv1\pmod{24}$, multiplying by $5$ gives $a\equiv100\equiv4\pmod{24}$. With $24<a\le48$ (more apples than oranges), the only possibility is $a=28$, and then $24p=284-140=144$, $p=6$.

So the customer buys $\boxed{28\text{ apples and }20\text{ oranges}}$, at $16$ cents and $6$ cents each. Check: $28\cdot16+20\cdot6=448+120=568$ cents.
