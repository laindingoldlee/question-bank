Condition on the number $k$ of heads among the three coins; $P(k)=\dbinom3k\cdot\dfrac{1}{8}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $k\le1$: no compartment can hold two heads.
\item $k=2$ (probability $\dfrac{3}{8}$): we need both heads in the same compartment. Whichever compartment the first head lands in, the second head lands in the same one with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
\item $k=3$ (probability $\dfrac{1}{8}$): three heads in two compartments always put at least two heads in one compartment (pigeonhole principle).
\end{itemize}
So the probability is
\[\dfrac{3}{8}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{8}\cdot1=\dfrac{3}{16}+\dfrac{2}{16}=\boxed{\dfrac{5}{16}}.\]
(The tails coins do not matter at all, which is why the compartment probability in the case of two heads is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.)
