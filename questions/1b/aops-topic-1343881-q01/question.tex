Work through the following chain; each step uses the result of the previous one.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Find the smallest positive integer that can be written as the sum of two distinct positive perfect squares in two different ways.
\item Do there exist two regular polygons whose interior angles differ by exactly that many degrees? Answer ``yes'' or ``no''.
\item Take the number of letters in your answer and square it.
\item Add the smallest positive integer $a$ such that $a$, $a+1$, $a+2$ are the side lengths of a right triangle.
\item Subtract the number of distinct arrangements of the letters of RACECAR.
\item Multiply by the value of $X^2+\dfrac{1}{X^2}$, given that $X+\dfrac{1}{X}=3$.
\item Subtract the sum of the infinite geometric series $1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{1}{8}+\cdots$.
\end{enumerate}
What is the final number?
