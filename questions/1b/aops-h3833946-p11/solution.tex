For triangle side $s$ and altitude $h=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}s$, a cross-section at height $x$ has width $s(1-\dfrac{x}{h})$. Equating this width to the square side $x$ yields $x=\dfrac{s\sqrt3}{2+\sqrt3}$. With $s=6+4\sqrt3$, this simplifies to $x=6$. Thus the area is $\boxed{36}$. 
\begin{asy}
texpreamble("\usepackage{amsmath,amssymb}");
size(220);
defaultpen(fontsize(9pt));
pair A=(0,3*sqrt(3)),B=(-3,0),C=(3,0); draw(A--B--C--cycle); label("$A$",A,(-1,-1)); label("$B$",B,(1,-1)); label("$C$",C,(0,1)); real x=6*sqrt(3)/(2+sqrt(3));draw((-x/2,0)--(x/2,0)--(x/2,x)--(-x/2,x)--cycle);
\end{asy}
