Write $v_k=\sum_{i=0}^ka_{n,i}x_{n,i}$, with $v_{-1}=0$. Multiplying each equation by $2a_{n,k}$ gives $v_k^2 - v_{k-1}^2 = \dfrac{a_{n,k}}{n}$, hence $v_k=\sqrt{Gn\sum_{j=n-k}^n j^{-2}}$. Since $x_{n,k} = \dfrac{v_k-v_{k-1}}{a_{n,k}}$, summation by parts gives $\sum x_{n,k} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{G} \, n^{\dfrac{3}{2}}} \sum_{j=1}^n (2j-1) \sqrt{\sum_{r=j}^n r^{-2}}$. This is a Riemann sum converging to $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt G}\int_0^1\sqrt{u(1-u)}\,du$. To justify the endpoint, for $j\ge2$ use $\sum_{r=j}^n r^{-2} \le \dfrac{1}{j-1}$; the contribution from $j\le\varepsilon n$ is $O (\varepsilon^{\dfrac{3}{2}})$, uniformly in $n$, and the single $j=1$ term tends to zero. Away from zero, the usual integral bounds give uniform convergence of $n\sum_{r=j}^n r^{-2}$ to $\dfrac{1}{u} - 1$. Finally the integral is $\dfrac{\pi}{8}$ by $u = \dfrac{1+\cos\theta}{2}$, so the limit is $\boxed{\dfrac{\pi}{4\sqrt G}}$.
