Since $\dfrac{\sqrt a}{a}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt a}$, put $u=\dfrac1{\sqrt a}$, $v=\dfrac1{\sqrt b}$, $w=\dfrac1{\sqrt c}$. These are positive with $u+v+w=10$, and
\[\dfrac1a+\dfrac1b+\dfrac1c=u^2+v^2+w^2.\]
\textbf{Minimum.} By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (or the QM-AM inequality),
\[u^2+v^2+w^2\ge\dfrac{(u+v+w)^2}{3}=\dfrac{100}{3},\]
with equality when $u=v=w=\dfrac{10}3$, that is, $a=b=c=\dfrac9{100}$. So $3\left(\dfrac1a+\dfrac1b+\dfrac1c\right)\ge100$, with equality at $a=b=c=\dfrac{9}{100}$.

\textbf{No maximum.} Since $u,v,w>0$, $u^2+v^2+w^2<(u+v+w)^2=100$, so the expression is always less than $300$; but with $u=10-2\varepsilon$ and $v=w=\varepsilon$ it gets arbitrarily close to $300$. So no maximum exists.

The minimum value is $\boxed{100}$.
