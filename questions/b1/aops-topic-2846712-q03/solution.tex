Boxes of $6$ and $15$ give exactly the multiples of $3$ of the form $3(2p+5q)$; since the largest number not of the form $2p+5q$ is $2\cdot5-2-5=3$, they give $0$, $6$ and every multiple of $3$ from $12$ on (not $3$ or $9$).
\begin{itemize}
\item $n\equiv0\pmod3$: the largest impossible is $9$.
\item $n\equiv1\pmod3$: the number $k$ of $100$-boxes satisfies $k\equiv1\pmod3$, so $k\ge1$. If $n$ is buyable with $k\ge4$ boxes of $100$, replace three of them by $50$ boxes of $6$; so $n$ is buyable iff $n-100$ is buyable with $6$s and $15$s. The largest impossible such $n$ is $100+9=109$.
\item $n\equiv2\pmod3$: similarly $n$ is buyable iff $n-200$ is buyable with $6$s and $15$s, so the largest impossible is $200+9=209$.
\end{itemize}
The answer is $\boxed{209}$.
