The pattern repeats every $\operatorname{lcm}(5,11)=55$ seconds, when both lights blink together. In one period, $A$ blinks at $5,10,\dots,55$ seconds and $B$ at $11,22,33,44,55$; so $A$ blinks alone $10$ times, at $5,10,\dots,50$. Lindsey sees one of these $10$ blinks, each equally likely.

For each, look at the next blink:
\begin{itemize}
\item after $5$: next is $10$ ($A$ alone) -- yes;
\item after $10$: next is $11$ ($B$) -- no;
\item after $15$: next is $20$ -- yes; after $20$: next is $22$ ($B$) -- no;
\item after $25$: next is $30$ -- yes; after $30$: next is $33$ ($B$) -- no;
\item after $35$: next is $40$ -- yes; after $40$: next is $44$ ($B$) -- no;
\item after $45$: next is $50$ -- yes; after $50$: next is $55$, where $A$ and $B$ blink together -- no.
\end{itemize}
That is $5$ favourable cases out of $10$, so the probability is $\boxed{50\%}$.
