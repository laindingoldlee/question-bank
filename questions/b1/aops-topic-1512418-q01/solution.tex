Two coats of $2250$ square feet means $4500$ square feet of coverage.

A gallon covers $4$ times as much as a quart but costs less than $2$ quarts ($29<36$), so gallons are far cheaper per square foot. In an optimal purchase there are at most $1$ quart: two quarts cost $\$36$ and cover $160$ square feet, while one gallon costs less and covers more.
\begin{itemize}
\item With no quarts we need $\left\lceil\dfrac{4500}{320}\right\rceil=15$ gallons, costing $15\cdot29=\$435$.
\item With one quart we need gallons covering $4500-80=4420$ square feet, i.e.\ $\left\lceil\dfrac{4420}{320}\right\rceil=14$ gallons (covering $4480$), costing $14\cdot29+18=\$424$.
\end{itemize}
So the minimum cost is $\boxed{\$424}$: $14$ gallons and $1$ quart.

This paint covers $14\cdot320+80=4560$ square feet, $60$ square feet more than needed. That is $\dfrac{60}{80}=\boxed{\dfrac{3}{4}\text{ quart}}$ of paint left over ($\dfrac{3}{16}$ gallon).
