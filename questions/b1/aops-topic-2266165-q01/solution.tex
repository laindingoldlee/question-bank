Let $p(x)=x^4+3x^3-2x^2+5x+1$. The divisor has degree $3$, so $p(x)=(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)q(x)+r(x)$ with $r(x)=ax^2+bx+c$. Putting $x=1,2,3$ kills the first term:
\[r(1)=p(1)=8,\qquad r(2)=p(2)=16+24-8+10+1=43,\qquad r(3)=p(3)=81+81-18+15+1=160.\]
So $a+b+c=8$, $4a+2b+c=43$, $9a+3b+c=160$. Subtracting, $3a+b=35$ and $5a+b=117$, so $a=41$, $b=-88$ and $c=8-41+88=55$. The remainder is
\[\boxed{41x^2-88x+55}.\]
