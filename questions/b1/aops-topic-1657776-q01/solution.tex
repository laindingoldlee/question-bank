We need $513k\equiv1\pmod{2000}$, i.e.\ $k$ is the inverse of $513$ modulo $2000$ (it exists since $\gcd(513,2000)=1$). Run the Euclidean algorithm:
\[
2000=3\cdot513+461,\quad 513=1\cdot461+52,\quad 461=8\cdot52+45,\quad 52=1\cdot45+7,\quad 45=6\cdot7+3,\quad 7=2\cdot3+1 .
\]
Working backwards,
\[
1=7-2\cdot3=7-2(45-6\cdot7)=13\cdot7-2\cdot45=13\cdot52-15\cdot45=13\cdot52-15(461-8\cdot52)=133\cdot52-15\cdot461
\]
\[
=133\cdot513-148\cdot461=133\cdot513-148(2000-3\cdot513)=577\cdot513-148\cdot2000 .
\]
So $513\cdot577=2000\cdot148+1$, and the least positive solution is
\[
k=\boxed{577}\qquad(q=148).
\]
All solutions are $k=577+2000t$, $q=148+513t$ for integers $t$.
