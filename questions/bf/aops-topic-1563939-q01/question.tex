Three numbers $x$, $y$, $z$ are chosen independently and uniformly at random from the interval $[0,1]$. What is the probability that $x$ is \emph{not} the least of the three? Two solutions are proposed:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Solution 1.} Only the ordering matters. There are $3!=6$ equally likely orderings, and in $2$ of them $x$ is the least, so the probability is $1-\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{2}{3}$.
\item \textbf{Solution 2.} First choose $x$. If $x$ is the least, then $y>x$ and $z>x$, and each happens with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the probability is $1-\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{3}{4}$.
\end{itemize}
Which solution is correct? Find the error in the other one.
