Suppose $N=\dfrac{78}{100}a$ and $N=\dfrac{116}{100}b$ for integers $a$ and $b$. Then
\[
N=\dfrac{39a}{50}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 50N=39a,\qquad N=\dfrac{29b}{25}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 25N=29b .
\]
From $50N=39a$ we get $39\mid50N$, and since $\gcd(39,50)=1$, $39\mid N$. Similarly $29\mid25N$ and $\gcd(29,25)=1$ give $29\mid N$. Hence $N$ is a multiple of $\operatorname{lcm}(39,29)=1131$.

Conversely $N=1131$ works: $1131=0.78\cdot1450$ and $1131=1.16\cdot975$. So $N=1131$, and the remainder is $\boxed{131}$.

The student's $1050$ fails both conditions: $\dfrac{1050}{0.78}=\dfrac{35000}{26}$ and $\dfrac{1050}{1.16}=\dfrac{26250}{29}$ are not integers (indeed $1050$ is divisible by neither $39$ nor $29$).
