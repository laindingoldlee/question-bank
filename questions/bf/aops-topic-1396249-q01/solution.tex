Split the rolls into stages: stage $j$ ($j=1,\dots,n$, for an $n$-sided die) starts when $j-1$ different numbers have been seen and ends when the $j$th new number appears. During stage $j$ each roll is new with probability $p_j=\dfrac{n-j+1}{n}$, so the stage length is geometric with mean $\dfrac{1}{p_j}=\dfrac{n}{n-j+1}$. By linearity of expectation the expected total is
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\dfrac{n}{n-j+1}=n\left(1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{n}\right).
\]
For $n=6$:
\[
6\left(1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{1}{5}+\dfrac{1}{6}\right)=6\cdot\dfrac{49}{20}=\boxed{\dfrac{147}{10}=14.7}.
\]
For $n=10$:
\[
10\left(1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{10}\right)=10\cdot\dfrac{7381}{2520}=\boxed{\dfrac{7381}{252}\approx29.29}.
\]
