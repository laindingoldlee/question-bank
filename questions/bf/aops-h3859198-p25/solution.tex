Place $A=(0,0),B=(1,0)$. The small circle\textquotesingle s center is $(r,y)$ because it touches $AD$. Internal tangency to the circle centered at $A$ gives $r^2+y^2=(1-r)^2$, hence $y^2=1-2r$. External tangency to the circle centered at $B$ gives $(1-r)^2+y^2=(1+r)^2$, hence $y^2=4r$. Equating yields $\boxed{r=\dfrac{1}{6}}$, choice E. The center height is $\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{3}}$, and the tangencies lie on the specified quarter arcs.

\end{asy}
