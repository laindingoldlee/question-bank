By (c), $n$ has no odd prime factor (it would be an odd factor other than $1$), so $n=2^k$ with $k\ge1$. Its factors other than $1$ are $2,4,\dots,2^k$, so there are $k$ of them, and (b) requires $k$ to be even. With $2^k<1000$ we get $k\in\{2,4,6,8\}$, that is $n=4,16,64,256$. She writes $\boxed{4}$ numbers.
