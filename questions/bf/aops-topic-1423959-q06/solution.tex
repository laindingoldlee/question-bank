Let the pentagon have side $2$; the semicircles have radius $1$ and centres at the midpoints of the sides.

\textbf{Which semicircles meet.} The midpoints of the sides lie on the incircle, whose radius is the apothem $\dfrac{1}{\tan36^\circ}\approx1.376$. Midpoints of adjacent sides subtend $72^\circ$ at the centre, and those of non-adjacent sides subtend $144^\circ$. Equivalently, if $M$ and $N$ are the midpoints of the sides meeting at vertex $B$, then $BM=BN=1$ and $\angle MBN=108^\circ$, so
\[
MN=2\sin54^\circ=2\cos36^\circ=\varphi=\dfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2}\approx1.618<2,
\]
while non-adjacent midpoints are $\varphi^2\approx2.618>2$ apart. So only semicircles on adjacent sides overlap, and no point lies in three semicircles (it would lie in two non-adjacent ones).

\textbf{One overlap.} The circles centred at $M$ and $N$ both pass through $B$, and their common region near $B$ is a lens. (It lies inside the pentagon: the part of either circle beyond the other side through $B$ is a small cap outside the angle at $B$, which is not in the other disc.) For two unit circles at distance $d$, the lens has area $2\arccos\dfrac{d}{2}-\dfrac{d}{2}\sqrt{4-d^2}$. Here $\dfrac{d}{2}=\cos36^\circ$, so $\arccos\dfrac{d}{2}=\dfrac{\pi}{5}$, and
\[
\dfrac{d}{2}\sqrt{4-d^2}=\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\varphi^2(4-\varphi^2)}=\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{3+\sqrt5}{2}\cdot\dfrac{5-\sqrt5}{2}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{10+2\sqrt5}{4}}=\dfrac{\sqrt{10+2\sqrt5}}{4}.
\]

\textbf{Total.} With five lenses,
\[
\text{Area}=5\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{5}-\dfrac{\sqrt{10+2\sqrt5}}{4}\right)=2\pi-\dfrac{5\sqrt{10+2\sqrt5}}{4}=\dfrac{8\pi-\sqrt{250+\sqrt{12500}}}{4}.
\]

\textbf{The smallest $p+q+r+s$.} In any representation, $\dfrac{p}{s}=2$ and $\sqrt{q+\sqrt r}=\dfrac{5s}{4}\sqrt{10+2\sqrt5}$, so $q+\sqrt r=\dfrac{125s^2}{8}+\dfrac{25s^2}{8}\sqrt5$. This is irrational, so $\sqrt r$ is irrational; writing $\sqrt r=\left(\dfrac{125s^2}{8}-q\right)+\dfrac{25s^2}{8}\sqrt5$ and squaring shows that the rational part must vanish, so $q=\dfrac{125s^2}{8}$ and $r=\dfrac{3125s^4}{64}$. For $q$ to be an integer, $8\mid s^2$, so $4\mid s$. All of $p,q,r,s$ increase with $s$, so the minimum is at $s=4$: $p=8$, $q=250$, $r=12500$, and
\[
p+q+r+s=8+250+12500+4=12762=30\cdot420+162 .
\]
The remainder is $\boxed{162}$.
