The domain is $x\ge\sqrt3$. Both sides are nonnegative, so squaring is equivalent. Expansion and cancellation give $(1+\sqrt2-\sqrt3)x^2=\sqrt6$. The coefficient is positive. Moreover $\sqrt6>3(1+\sqrt2-\sqrt3)$: this is equivalent to $\sqrt3(3+\sqrt2)>3(1+\sqrt2)$, whose positive sides have squared difference $6-0\sqrt2>0$. Hence the one positive root lies above $\sqrt3$; the negative root is outside the domain. There is $\boxed1$ real solution.
