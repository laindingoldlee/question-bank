Let $N=3^{69}-2^{69}$. Neither $2$ nor $3$ divides $N$. For any prime divisor $p$, the multiplicative order of $\dfrac{3}{2}$ modulo $p$ divides $69$, hence is $1,3,23$, or $69$. Order $1$ is impossible. Order $3$ forces $p\mid3^3-2^3=19$, so $p=19$. Orders $23$ or $69$ require $23\mid p-1$; since $p$ is odd, the smallest possible such prime is $47$.

Both $19$ and $47$ divide $N$. For $47$, note $2\equiv7^2$ and $3\equiv12^2\pmod{47}$, so Fermat's theorem gives $2^{23}\equiv3^{23}\equiv1$. Also
\[
N=(8+19)^{23}-8^{23}\equiv23\cdot8^{22}\cdot19\not\equiv0\pmod{19^2}.
\]
Thus $19^2$ does not divide $N$. Any composite divisor either contains two distinct prime factors, at least $19$ and $47$, or a repeated prime factor, which must be at least $47^2$. Therefore the smallest composite divisor is $\boxed{19\cdot47=893}$.
