One move leaves four heads. After two moves, each coin would need to have been flipped once, since zero or two flips leave heads; but two moves make twelve flips rather than ten. Thus at least three moves are required. Divide the coins into a group of four and three groups of two. On each of three moves flip the group of four together with a different group of two. The first four coins are flipped three times and each other coin once, all odd. Hence $\boxed3$ moves suffice.
