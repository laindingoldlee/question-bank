The digits are distinct and increasing, so the more digits, the larger the number (for numbers of the same length, compare from the left).

Five or more distinct digits have squares adding to at least $0^2+1^2+2^2+3^2+4^2=30$; but a leading $0$ is not allowed, so with nonzero digits at least $1+4+9+16+25=55>50$. (A $0$ cannot appear at all: it would have to be the first digit.) So there are at most $4$ digits.

Four digits: we need $a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2=50$ with $1\le a<b<c<d$. Since $1+4+9+16=30$, try $d=6$: $a^2+b^2+c^2=14=1+4+9$, giving $1236$. $d=5$: $a^2+b^2+c^2=25$, impossible with distinct digits below $5$ ($1+4+16=21$, $1+9+16=26$, $4+9+16=29$). $d\ge7$ leaves at most $1$, impossible. So $1236$ is the only four-digit candidate and the largest such integer.

The product of its digits is $1\cdot2\cdot3\cdot6=\boxed{36}$.
