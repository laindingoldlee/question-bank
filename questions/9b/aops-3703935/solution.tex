There are four multiples of three, five numbers congruent to one, and four congruent to two. Ignore the multiples of three temporarily. To avoid residue zero, the first nonzero residue may be one or two; afterwards the nonzero sequence is forced because from residue one only another one is safe, and from residue two only another two is safe. Starting with one gives the nine residues $1,1,2,1,2,1,2,1,2$, which has the required five ones and four twos. Starting with two would have five twos, so is impossible. The four zero-residue numbers may be inserted in any of the nine gaps after these nine entries, but not before the first. This gives $\binom{12}{4}$ residue strings. Assign distinct numbers within residues in $4!5!4!$ ways. Therefore the probability is $\dfrac{\binom{12}{4}4!5!4!}{13!}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{182}}$.
