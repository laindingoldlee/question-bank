First, choosing $k$ pairwise non-adjacent chairs from $n$ chairs in a \emph{row} can be done in $\binom{n-k+1}{k}$ ways: if the chosen positions are $c_1<c_2<\dots<c_k$, the numbers $c_1,c_2-1,\dots,c_k-(k-1)$ are distinct numbers from $1$ to $n-k+1$, and this is a bijection.

Now split by whether chair $1$ is chosen.
\begin{itemize}
\item Chair $1$ chosen: chairs $2$ and $10$ are not chosen, and we need $3$ non-adjacent chairs from the row $3,4,\dots,9$ of $7$ chairs: $\binom{7-3+1}{3}=\binom53=10$ ways.
\item Chair $1$ not chosen: we need $4$ non-adjacent chairs from the row $2,3,\dots,10$ of $9$ chairs: $\binom{9-4+1}{4}=\binom64=15$ ways.
\end{itemize}
The total is
\[10+15=\boxed{25}.\]
