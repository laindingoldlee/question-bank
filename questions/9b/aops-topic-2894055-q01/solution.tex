There are $15$ correct answers. If $k$ students solved at least $2$, the total is at most $(10-k)+3k=10+2k$, so $10+2k\ge15$ and $k\ge3$.

$k=3$ is achievable: two students solve all three, one solves the first two, and the other seven each solve exactly one ($6$ solve the first, $1$ the second). The answer is $\boxed{3}$.
