\textbf{Construction.} Draw the bisector of angle $B$; let it meet side $AC$ at $E$. Through $E$ draw the line parallel to $BC$; let it meet side $AB$ at $D$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair B=(0,0),C=(6,0),A=(1.6,4.2);
real c=length(A-B), a=length(C-B);
pair E=A+c/(c+a)*(C-A), D=A+c/(c+a)*(B-A);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(1));
draw(B--E,red+dashed); draw(D--E,blue+linewidth(1));
draw(arc(B,0.75,0,degrees(E-B)),red); draw(arc(B,0.95,degrees(E-B),degrees(A-B)),red);
draw(arc(E,0.75,180,180+degrees(E-B)),red);
pair m1=(B+D)/2, m2=(D+E)/2;
draw(m1+0.12*unit(rotate(90)*(D-B))--m1-0.12*unit(rotate(90)*(D-B)));
draw(m2+0.12*unit(rotate(90)*(E-D))--m2-0.12*unit(rotate(90)*(E-D)));
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);dot(D);dot(E);
label("$A$",A,N); label("$B$",B,SW); label("$C$",C,SE); label("$D$",D,W); label("$E$",E,NE);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\textbf{Proof.} Since $DE\parallel BC$, the alternate angles along the transversal $BE$ are equal: $\angle DEB=\angle EBC$. Since $BE$ bisects angle $B$, $\angle EBC=\angle DBE$. Hence $\angle DEB=\angle DBE$, so triangle $DBE$ is isosceles with $DB=DE$. As $E$ is on side $AC$ and $DE\parallel BC$, $D$ lies on side $AB$.

\textbf{Uniqueness.} If $DE\parallel BC$, then triangles $ADE$ and $ABC$ are similar, so $DE=\dfrac{AD}{AB}\cdot BC$, while $BD=AB-AD$. The condition $DE=BD$ gives $AD\left(\dfrac{BC}{AB}+1\right)=AB$, so $AD=\dfrac{AB^2}{AB+BC}$: there is only one such pair $D$, $E$, and the construction finds it.
