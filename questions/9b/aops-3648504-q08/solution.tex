For $D>1$, the $n$th digit of $\dfrac{1}{D}$ is $\lfloor\dfrac{10r}{D}\rfloor$, where $r$ is the remainder of $10^{n-1}$ modulo $D$. This follows directly from long division after shifting $n-1$ places. Apply it at $n=2025$ to $D=81,9801,998001,99980001$, computing modular powers by repeated squaring. The resulting digits are $9,2,4,0$, respectively. Their sum is $\boxed{15}$.
