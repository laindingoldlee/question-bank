\textbf{(a)} Expanding, $p(x)=x^2+2ix+i^2=\boxed{x^2+2ix-1}$. Its leading coefficient is $a=1\ne0$ and its degree is $2$, so it \emph{is} a quadratic polynomial, but with complex coefficients ($b=2i$ is not real), so it is not a quadratic with real coefficients.

\textbf{(b)} $p(x)=0$ exactly when $x+i=0$. So $p$ has the single root $\boxed{x=-i}$ of multiplicity $2$. (Check with the quadratic formula: the discriminant is $(2i)^2-4\cdot1\cdot(-1)=-4+4=0$, giving the double root $\dfrac{-2i}{2}=-i$.)
