Divide the members into two houses in any way, and call a pair of enemies \emph{bad} if both are in the same house. Let $B$ be the number of bad pairs.

\textbf{Moving a member.} Suppose some member $M$ has at least two enemies in his or her own house. Since $M$ has at most three enemies, $M$ has at most one enemy in the other house. Move $M$ to the other house: the bad pairs containing $M$ change from at least $2$ to at most $1$, and no other pair changes, so $B$ decreases by at least $1$.

\textbf{The process stops.} $B$ is a non-negative integer, so it cannot decrease forever. Repeat the move as long as some member has two or more enemies in his or her house; after finitely many moves no such member is left, and then every member has at most one enemy in his or her own house. $\blacksquare$

(Equivalently: choose a division with the smallest possible number of bad pairs; by the argument above it already has the required property. In graph terms, the vertices of a graph with maximum degree $3$ can be $2$-coloured so that each vertex has at most one neighbour of its own colour.)
