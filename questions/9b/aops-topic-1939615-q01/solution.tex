If $m=p_1^{e_1}\cdots p_r^{e_r}$, then $m$ has $(e_1+1)\cdots(e_r+1)$ factors. Since $21=21=3\cdot7$, either $m=p^{20}$ or $m=p^2q^6$ with distinct primes $p,q$. Here $m=n+2005$ lies in $[2006,12004]$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $p^{20}\ge2^{20}>12004$: impossible.
\item $q=2$: $m=64p^2$ with $\dfrac{2006}{64}\le p^2\le\dfrac{12004}{64}$, i.e.\ $32\le p^2\le187$: $p=7,11,13$, giving $m=3136,7744,10816$.
\item $q=3$: $m=729p^2$ with $3\le p^2\le16$: $p=2$, giving $m=2916$.
\item $q\ge5$: $q^6\ge15625>12004$.
\end{itemize}
So $n\in\{911,1131,5739,8811\}$ and the sum is $911+1131+5739+8811=\boxed{16592}$.
