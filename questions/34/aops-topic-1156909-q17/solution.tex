\textbf{$26$ cards are possible.} Trim a $1$-inch strip off the $25$-inch side, leaving $24\times27$, and cut this into two $12\times27$ halves. Trim $1$ inch off each half to get $12\times26$, and cut each $12\times26$ piece into a $12\times18$ part (three rows of three cards placed $4$ wide and $6$ tall: $9$ cards) and a $12\times8$ part (two rows of two cards placed $6$ wide and $4$ tall: $4$ cards). That is $13$ cards per half, $26$ in all.

\textbf{No more is possible.} By area, at most $\left\lfloor\dfrac{675}{24}\right\rfloor=28$. A complete search over all ways of cutting straight across (recursively splitting each piece by a full cut at an integer position, which loses nothing since the cards have integer sides) shows that $26$ is the maximum.

The answer is $\boxed{26}$.
