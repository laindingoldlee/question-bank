Divisors pair as $ k $ and $\dfrac{n}{k}$. For nonsquare $ n $, exactly half satisfy $ k^2>n $. For square $ n $, the unpaired square root makes the fraction $\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{2\tau(n)}$. The squares are 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, with divisor counts 1, 3, 3, 5, 3. Averaging gives $\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{60}(1+\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{5}+\dfrac{1}{3})=\boxed{\dfrac{139}{300}}$.
