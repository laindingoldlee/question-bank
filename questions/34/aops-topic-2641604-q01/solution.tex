Write each chosen number as $2^k\cdot m$ with $k\ge0$ and $m$ odd. The odd part $m$ is one of the $n$ numbers $1,3,\dots,2n-1$. By the pigeonhole principle, two of the $n+1$ chosen numbers have the same odd part: $a=2^im$ and $b=2^jm$. They are distinct, so $i\ne j$; say $i>j$. Then $a=2^{i-j}b$ is a multiple of $b$. $\blacksquare$
