Using $0=0+0$ and the distributive law,
\[1=0\cdot\infty=(0+0)\cdot\infty=0\cdot\infty+0\cdot\infty=1+1=2,\]
which is false. So $\infty$ cannot be a number obeying the usual rules together with $0\cdot\infty=1$. $\blacksquare$

This is why statements like ``$\dfrac1n$ tends to $0$ as $n$ grows'' are about limits: they describe what happens to real numbers $n$ as they get large, and never use $\infty$ as a number in arithmetic.
