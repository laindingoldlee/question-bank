\textbf{(a)} By the division algorithm, $f(x)=(x-a)(x-b)q(x)+R(x)$ for polynomials $q$ and $R$ with $\deg R\le1$. Substituting $x=a$ and $x=b$ gives $R(a)=f(a)$ and $R(b)=f(b)$.

The polynomial $r(x)$ also has degree at most $1$, and $r(a)=f(a)\cdot1+f(b)\cdot0=f(a)$, $r(b)=f(b)$. Then $R-r$ has degree at most $1$ and two roots $a\ne b$, so it is the zero polynomial, and $R=r$. $\blacksquare$

(In the same way, the remainder upon division by $(x-a_1)\cdots(x-a_k)$ with distinct $a_i$ is the unique polynomial of degree less than $k$ that agrees with $f$ at $a_1,\dots,a_k$.)

\textbf{(b)} Here $x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)$, and $f(x)=x^{2021}+2x^{10}$ has $f(1)=3$ and $f(-1)=-1+2=1$. By (a) with $a=1$, $b=-1$:
\[r(x)=3\cdot\dfrac{x+1}{2}+1\cdot\dfrac{x-1}{-2}=\dfrac{3x+3-x+1}{2}=\boxed{x+2}.\]
(Check: $r(1)=3=f(1)$ and $r(-1)=1=f(-1)$.)
