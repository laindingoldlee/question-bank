\textbf{An algebraic factorisation.} By the Sophie Germain identity,
\[
4y^4+1=(2y^2+1)^2-(2y)^2=(2y^2+2y+1)(2y^2-2y+1).
\]
With $y=2^{14}$ we have $4y^4=2^{2}\cdot2^{56}=2^{58}$, $2y^2=2^{29}$ and $2y=2^{15}$, so
\[
2^{58}+1=\left(2^{29}+2^{15}+1\right)\left(2^{29}-2^{15}+1\right).
\]
Both factors exceed $1$.

\textbf{A factor $5$.} Powers of $2$ modulo $5$ repeat with period $4$: $2,4,3,1$. Since $29\equiv1$ and $15\equiv3\pmod4$, we get $2^{29}\equiv2$ and $2^{15}\equiv3\pmod5$. Hence
\[
2^{29}-2^{15}+1\equiv2-3+1=0\pmod5 .
\]
(This agrees with the poster's check $2^{58}+1=4^{29}+1\equiv(-1)^{29}+1=0\pmod5$.)

\textbf{Conclusion.} Since $2^{29}-2^{15}+1=536838145$ is divisible by $5$ and is greater than $5$, the quotient $\dfrac{2^{29}-2^{15}+1}{5}=107367629$ is greater than $1$. Therefore
\[
2^{58}+1=5\cdot107367629\cdot536903681,
\]
a product of three integers each greater than $1$, where $536903681=2^{29}+2^{15}+1$. $\blacksquare$
