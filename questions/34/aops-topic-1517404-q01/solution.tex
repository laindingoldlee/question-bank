Since $y>0$, we have $|-y|=y$ and $\operatorname{Arg}(-y)=\pi$, so
\[
\sqrt[x]{-y}=\exp\!\left(\dfrac{\ln y+i\pi}{x}\right)=e^{\dfrac{\ln y}{x}}\,e^{\dfrac{i\pi}{x}}=\sqrt[x]{y}\cdot e^{\dfrac{i\pi}{x}} .
\]
Also $|i|=1$ and $\operatorname{Arg} i=\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so with $r=\dfrac {x}{2}$,
\[
\sqrt[\dfrac {x}{2}]{i}=\exp\!\left(\dfrac{0+\dfrac{i\pi}{2}}{\dfrac {x}{2}}\right)=e^{\dfrac{i\pi}{x}} .
\]
Comparing the two lines gives $\sqrt[x]{-y}=\sqrt[x]{y}\cdot\sqrt[\dfrac {x}{2}]{i}$. $\blacksquare$

For example, $x=1$ gives $-y=y\cdot i^2$, and $x=2$ gives $\sqrt{-y}=\sqrt y\cdot i$.

(The identity depends on using principal roots. If $\sqrt[x]{w}$ meant the set of \emph{all} $x$-th roots, the two sides would generally be different sets: for $x=4$ the left side has $4$ values, while multiplying the $4$ fourth roots of $y$ by the $2$ square roots of $i$ gives $8$ values.)
