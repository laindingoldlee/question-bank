\textbf{Numbers from $1000$ to $1069$ fail.} Changing the last digit of $N$ produces every other number in its decade $10q,\dots,10q+9$. The primes in these decades are
\[
1009;\ \ 1013,1019;\ \ 1021;\ \ 1031,1033,1039;\ \ 1049;\ \ 1051;\ \ 1061,1063,1069 .
\]
Every decade from $1000$ to $1069$ contains a prime, so $N$ fails unless that prime is $N$ itself and it is the only prime of the decade. That leaves $N=1009,1021,1049,1051$, and for these another change gives a prime:
\[
1009\to1109,\quad 1021\to1051,\quad 1049\to1009,\quad 1051\to1021
\]
($1109$ is prime: it is not divisible by any prime up to $31$, and $37^2>1109$).

\textbf{$1070$ works.} Changing the first, second or third digit gives a number ending in $0$, which is even and greater than $2$. Changing the last digit gives $1071=3\cdot357$, $1072$, $1073=29\cdot37$, $1074$, $1075=5\cdot215$, $1076$, $1077=3\cdot359$, $1078$ or $1079=13\cdot83$, all composite.

So the answer is $\boxed{1070}$. (The same answer results if changing the first digit to $0$ is also allowed, since $70$ is not prime.)
