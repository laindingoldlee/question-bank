Choose three-dimensional coordinates
\[
B=(0,0,0),\quad A=(2,0,0),\quad C=(1,\sqrt3,0),\quad D=(3,\sqrt3,0).
\]
Then $H=\left(1,\dfrac{\sqrt3}{3},0\right)$ and $S=\left(1,\dfrac{\sqrt3}{3},\sqrt3\right)$. Direction vectors for the lines are
\[
\mathbf u=C-A=(-1,\sqrt3,0),\quad
\mathbf v=D-S=\left(2,\dfrac{2\sqrt3}{3},-\sqrt3\right).
\]
Their cross product is $\mathbf u\times\mathbf v=\left(-3,-\sqrt3,-\dfrac{8\sqrt3}{3}\right)$, of length $\dfrac{10}{\sqrt3}$. The distance between the skew lines is the absolute component of $S-A$ in this common perpendicular direction:
\[
d=\dfrac{|(S-A)\cdot(\mathbf u\times\mathbf v)|}{|\mathbf u\times\mathbf v|}
=\dfrac{6}{\dfrac{10}{\sqrt3}}
=\boxed{\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{5}\approx1.04}.
\]
