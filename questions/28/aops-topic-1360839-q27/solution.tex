Look at one senior. Their partner is equally likely to be any of the other $5$ students, and exactly one of these is the other senior. So the probability is $\boxed{\dfrac{1}{5}}$.

Check by counting: there are $\dfrac{6!}{2^3\cdot3!}=15$ ways to split six people into three pairs, and if the seniors are paired, the four juniors can be paired in $3$ ways; $\dfrac{3}{15}=\dfrac{1}{5}$.
