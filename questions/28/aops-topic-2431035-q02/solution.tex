The lines $y=1+x$ and $y=1-x$ meet at $(0,1)$. The line $y=5$ meets $y=1+x$ at $(4,5)$ and $y=1-x$ at $(-4,5)$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
import graph;
draw((-6,0)--(6,0),Arrow(6)); draw((0,-1)--(0,6.6),Arrow(6));
label("$x$",(6,0),E); label("$y$",(0,6.6),N);
pair P=(0,1), Q=(4,5), R=(-4,5);
fill(P--Q--R--cycle,rgb(0.85,0.9,1));
draw((-5.6,5)--(6.4,5),blue); draw((-1.4,-0.4)--(5.2,6.2),red); draw((1.4,-0.4)--(-5.2,6.2),heavygreen);
draw(P--Q--R--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
dot(P); dot(Q); dot(R);
label("$(0,1)$",P,E); label("$(4,5)$",Q,S+0.6*E); label("$(-4,5)$",R,S+0.6*W);
label("$y=5$",(6.4,5),E,blue); label("$y=1+x$",(5.2,6.2),N,red); label("$y=1-x$",(-5.2,6.2),N,heavygreen);
draw((0,5)--P,dashed); label("$4$",(0,3),W);
\end{asy}

The side on $y=5$ has length $4-(-4)=8$, and the opposite vertex $(0,1)$ is at height $5-1=4$ below it. The area is
\[\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot8\cdot4=\boxed{16}.\]
