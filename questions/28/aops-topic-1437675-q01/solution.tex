With $c$ cookies and $b$ brownies we need $120\le20c+8b\le160$, that is,
\[
30\le5c+2b\le40 ,
\]
so $c\le8$, and for each $c$ the number $b$ runs from $\left\lceil\dfrac{30-5c}{2}\right\rceil$ (or $0$) to $\left\lfloor\dfrac{40-5c}{2}\right\rfloor$:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccccccc}
c & 0&1&2&3&4&5&6&7&8\\ \hline
b & 15\text{--}20 & 13\text{--}17 & 10\text{--}15 & 8\text{--}12 & 5\text{--}10 & 3\text{--}7 & 0\text{--}5 & 0\text{--}2 & 0\\
\text{count} & 6&5&6&5&6&5&6&3&1
\end{array}
\]
A shortcut: when $c$ increases by $2$, the range for $b$ shifts down by exactly $5$, so the counts alternate $6,5,6,5,\dots$ until the lower limit $0$ is reached. In total there are $6+5+6+5+6+5+6+3+1=\boxed{43}$ combinations. (If at least one cookie and at least one brownie must be bought, remove the $6$ combinations with $c=0$ and the $3$ with $b=0$, leaving $34$.)
