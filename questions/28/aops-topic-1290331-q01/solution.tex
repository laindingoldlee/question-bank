The diagonal $TU$ of a square bisects the right angle at $T$, so $\angle MTK=\angle UTY=\boxed{45^\circ}$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
path ra(pair P,pair u,pair v,real s=0.2){return P+s*unit(u)--P+s*unit(u)+s*unit(v)--P+s*unit(v);}
pair T=(0,0),Y=(2,0),U=(2,2),I=(0,2),M=(1,1); real s=sqrt(2); pair K=(s,0);
draw(T--Y--U--I--cycle); draw(T--U,gray); draw(Y--I,gray); draw(M--K,blue);
draw(arc(T,0.45,0,45),red); label("$45^\circ$",T+0.6*dir(22),red);
label("$T$",T,SW); label("$Y$",Y,SE); label("$U$",U,NE); label("$I$",I,NW); label("$M$",M,N); label("$K$",K,S);
dot(M); dot(K);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Triangle $TKM$ is isosceles with $TK=TM$, so its base angles at $K$ and $M$ are equal:
\[
\angle TMK=\dfrac{180^\circ-45^\circ}{2}=\boxed{67.5^\circ}.
\]
(Since $TM=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\,TY<TY$, the point $K$ really lies on side $TY$.)
