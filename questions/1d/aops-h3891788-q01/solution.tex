Suppose first that $p\ne q$. For ordered triples allowing entries equal to 1, distribute each prime's exponent 3 among three factors. There are $\binom52^2=100$ choices. Requiring all factors greater than 1 by inclusion--exclusion gives $100-3(4^2)+3=55$ ordered triples.

If $a=b$, the common factor is $p^i q^j$ with $i,j\in\{0,1\}$ and $(i,j)\ne(0,0)$. There are three choices: $p,q,pq$. The last yields $a=b=c=pq$; the first two yield exactly two equal factors. Thus the unordered triples consist of one all-equal triple, two with exactly two equal factors, and $\dfrac{55-1-3\cdot2}{6}=8$ with all factors distinct. The total is $\boxed{11}$.

If $p=q$, the product is $p^6$. The unordered exponent triples are $(1,1,4),(1,2,3),(2,2,2)$, so the answer is $\boxed3$.
