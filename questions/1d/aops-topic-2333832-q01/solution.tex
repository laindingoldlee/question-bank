Let $S=\dbinom{21}{0}+\dbinom{21}{1}+\cdots+\dbinom{21}{10}$. By the symmetry $\dbinom{21}{k}=\dbinom{21}{21-k}$, and since $k\mapsto21-k$ maps $\{0,1,\dots,10\}$ onto $\{11,12,\dots,21\}$,
\[\dbinom{21}{11}+\dbinom{21}{12}+\cdots+\dbinom{21}{21}=S\]
as well. Adding, $2S=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{21}\dbinom{21}{k}=2^{21}$ (the number of subsets of a $21$-element set), so $S=2^{20}$. It is not a coincidence.

The same argument shows that for every $n\ge0$,
\[\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dbinom{2n+1}{k}=2^{2n}=4^n,\]
because a row of Pascal's triangle with an odd number $2n+1$ splits into two mirror-image halves with no middle term.
