If $a=0$ the identity reads $-b^n=-b\cdot b^{n-1}$, which is true. If $a\ne0$, the bracket is a geometric series with first term $a^{n-1}$, ratio $q=\dfrac {b}{a}\ne1$ and $n$ terms, so
\[
a^{n-1}\cdot\dfrac{1-q^n}{1-q}=a^{n-1}\cdot\dfrac{1-\dfrac{b^n}{a^n}}{1-\dfrac {b}{a}}=\dfrac{a^n-b^n}{a-b}.
\]
Multiplying by $a-b$ gives the identity. Taking $n=3$ gives $a^3-b^3=(a-b)(a^2+ab+b^2)$. When $a=b$ both sides are $0$, so the identity holds for all $a,b$. $\blacksquare$
