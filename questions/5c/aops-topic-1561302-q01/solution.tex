Number the days so that today is day $0$; it is sunny.

\textbf{Days $1$ to $4$ are rainy.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Days $-1$ and $-2$ are rainy (they precede the sunny day $0$), so days $4$ and $3$ are rainy (five days later).
\item If day $1$ were sunny, days $0$ and $-1$ would both be rainy; but day $0$ is sunny. So day $1$ is rainy.
\item If day $2$ were sunny, days $1$ and $0$ would both be rainy; again day $0$ is sunny. So day $2$ is rainy.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Day $5$ cannot be predicted.} Both possibilities are consistent with all the rules:
\begin{itemize}
\item the pattern of period $5$ in which day $n$ is sunny exactly when $5\mid n$ (each sunny day is preceded by two rainy ones, and a rainy day is followed five days later by a rainy day, since the pattern repeats every five days); here day $5$ is sunny;
\item the same pattern up to day $0$, followed by rain on every day from day $1$ on; the sunny days are as before, each still preceded by two rainy days, and five days after a rainy day is either a rainy day of the old pattern or a day $\ge1$, which is rainy; here day $5$ is rainy.
\end{itemize}
Therefore the weather is certain for exactly the next $\boxed{4}$ days (all rainy), and day $5$ is the first day that cannot be predicted.
