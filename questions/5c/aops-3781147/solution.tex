For fixed total $k$, the sum of products of $r$ positive indices is the coefficient of $z^k$ in $(\dfrac{z}{(1-z)^2})^r$, hence $\binom{k+r-1}{2r-1}$. Therefore the general answer is $\boxed{S_r = \sum_{k=r}^{\infty} \dfrac{\binom{k+r-1}{2r-1}}{k !}}$. This is always a rational multiple of $e$: the numerator is a polynomial in $k$ of degree $2r-1$, and its expansion in falling factorials reduces the sum to multiples of $\sum_{k\ge j} \dfrac{(k)_j}{k !} = e$. For $r=2$, $\binom{k+1}{3} = \dfrac{(k)_3+3(k)_2}{6}$, giving $S_2 = \dfrac{2 e}{3}$. For $r=3$, $\binom{k+2}{5} = \dfrac{(k)_5+10(k)_4+20(k)_3}{120}$, giving $S_3 = \dfrac{31 e}{120}$. The omitted terms below $k=r$ vanish. Hence the requested values are $\boxed{\dfrac{2 e}{3} , \dfrac{\  31 e}{120}}$.
