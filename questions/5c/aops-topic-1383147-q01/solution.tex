Let the side be $s$ and let $AM$ be the altitude from $A$ to $BC$. In an equilateral triangle the altitude is also a median, so $BM=\dfrac{s}{2}$, and triangle $ABM$ is a $30$-$60$-$90$ triangle with hypotenuse $s$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
path ra(pair P,pair u,pair v,real s=0.2){return P+s*unit(u)--P+s*unit(u)+s*unit(v)--P+s*unit(v);}
pair B=(0,0),C=(8,0),A=(4,4*sqrt(3)),M=(4,0);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(A--M,red+dashed); draw(ra(M,C-M,A-M,0.45));
dot(M);
label("$A$",A,N); label("$B$",B,SW); label("$C$",C,SE); label("$M$",M,S);
label("$s$",(A+B)/2,NW); label("$\dfrac{s}{2}$",(B+M)/2,S);
label("$4\sqrt{3}$",(A+M)/2,E,red);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

By the Pythagorean theorem,
\[
AM^2=s^2-\left(\dfrac{s}{2}\right)^2=\dfrac{3s^2}{4},\qquad AM=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}s .
\]
So $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}s=4\sqrt3$ gives $s=8$, and the perimeter is $3s=\boxed{24}$.
