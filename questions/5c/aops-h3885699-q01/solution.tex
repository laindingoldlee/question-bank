Put $m=da,n=db$, where $\gcd(a,b)=1$. Then
\[
g=d^2\gcd(a^2+b^2,d(a^3+b^3)).
\]
For coprime $a,b$, the gcd of $a^2+b^2$ and $a^3+b^3$ is either $1$ or $2$: an odd common prime would give $(\dfrac{a}{b})^2=-1$ and $(\dfrac{a}{b})^3=-1$, forcing $\dfrac{a}{b}=1$ and then $2=0$. Also $v_2(a^2+b^2)\le1$.

For an odd prime dividing $a^2+b^2$, $-1$ is a quadratic residue, so that prime is $1$ modulo $4$ and is at least $5$. If an odd prime has an odd exponent in $g$, its exponent must therefore be at least $3$: the contribution from $d^2$ is even, and any extra odd-prime contribution requires that prime to divide $d$. This would make $g\ge5^3=125$. Thus for $g\le100$, its odd part is a square; $g$ is either a square or twice a square.

Every $d^2$ is attained by $(m,n)=(d,2d^2)$: the coprime reduced pair $(1,2d)$ gives no extra gcd. Every $2d^2$ is attained by $(m,n)=(d,d(2d+1))$: the reduced sum of squares has exactly one factor $2$ and no odd factor in common with $d$ or the sum of cubes. Hence the sum of admissible values is
\[
\sum_{d=1}^{10}d^2+2\sum_{d=1}^{7}d^2=385+280=\boxed{665}.
\]
