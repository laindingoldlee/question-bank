Since $n^3+3=n(n^2+7)-(7n-3)$, the fraction is an integer exactly when $n^2+7$ divides $7n-3$. For $n\ge1$ we have $7n-3>0$, so we need $n^2+7\le7n-3$, that is,
\[
n^2-7n+10=(n-2)(n-5)\le0,\qquad 2\le n\le5 .
\]
Check these:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
n & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5\\ \hline
n^2+7 & 11 & 16 & 23 & 32\\
7n-3 & 11 & 18 & 25 & 32
\end{array}
\]
Only $n=2$ and $n=5$ work, giving $\dfrac{11}{11}=1$ and $\dfrac{128}{32}=4$. The answer is $\boxed{n=2\text{ or }n=5}$.
