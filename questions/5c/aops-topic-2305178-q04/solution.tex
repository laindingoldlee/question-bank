In an equilateral triangle the bisector from $A$ is also the median and the altitude, so $D$ is the midpoint of $BC$ and $\angle ADB=90^\circ$. Let $AB=s$; then $BD=\dfrac s2$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair B=(0,0), C=(2,0), A=(1,sqrt(3)), D=(1,0);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(1));
draw(A--D,blue);
draw(D+(0.1,0)--D+(0.1,0.1)--D+(0,0.1),blue);
draw(arc(A,0.35,240,270),red); draw(arc(A,0.42,270,300),red);
label("$A$",A,N); label("$B$",B,SW); label("$C$",C,SE); label("$D$",D,S,blue);
label("$5$",(A+D)/2,E,blue); label("$s$",(A+B)/2,NW); label("$\dfrac s2$",(B+D)/2,S,fontsize(9pt));
\end{asy}

By Pythagoras in triangle $ABD$, $AD^2=s^2-\dfrac{s^2}4=\dfrac{3s^2}{4}$, so $AD=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}s$. From $\dfrac{\sqrt3}2s=5$,
\[AB=s=\dfrac{10}{\sqrt3}=\boxed{\dfrac{10\sqrt3}{3}}\text{ inches}\approx5.77\text{ inches}.\]
