\textbf{Boundary.} On the segment from $(x_1,y_1)$ to $(x_2,y_2)$, the lattice points are $(x_1,y_1)+\dfrac{t}{g}(x_2-x_1,\,y_2-y_1)$ for $t=0,1,\dots,g$, where $g=\gcd(|x_2-x_1|,|y_2-y_1|)$. Counting each vertex once,
\[
B=\gcd(|x_2-x_1|,|y_2-y_1|)+\gcd(|x_3-x_2|,|y_3-y_2|)+\gcd(|x_1-x_3|,|y_1-y_3|).
\]
\textbf{Interior.} The area is given by the shoelace formula,
\[
A=\dfrac{1}{2}\left|x_1(y_2-y_3)+x_2(y_3-y_1)+x_3(y_1-y_2)\right| ,
\]
and Pick's theorem $A=I+\dfrac {B}{2}-1$ for lattice polygons gives
\[
I=A-\dfrac {B}{2}+1,\qquad I+B=A+\dfrac {B}{2}+1 .
\]
\textbf{Example.} For $(0,0),(6,0),(2,4)$: $B=\gcd(6,0)+\gcd(4,4)+\gcd(2,4)=6+4+2=12$ and $A=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot6\cdot4=12$, so $I=12-6+1=\boxed{7}$ interior points and $\boxed{19}$ points inside or on the triangle.
