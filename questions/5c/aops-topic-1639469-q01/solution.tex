Every nonzero complex number has \emph{two} square roots. For $i$ they are $\pm\dfrac{1+i}{\sqrt2}$, since
\[
\left(\dfrac{1+i}{\sqrt2}\right)^2=\dfrac{1+2i+i^2}{2}=i .
\]
So a symbol such as $i^{0.5}$ or $i^{2.5}$ does not name a single number until we choose a rule (a \emph{branch}). Once a rule is fixed, the laws $\left(z^a\right)^b=z^{ab}$ and $z^{a+b}=z^az^b$, which are true for positive real $z$, are \textbf{no longer valid} for non-integer exponents.

With the usual principal values, $z^w=e^{w\operatorname{Log}z}$ where $\operatorname{Log}i=\dfrac{\pi i}{2}$:
\[
i^{0.5}=e^{\dfrac{\pi i}{4}}=\dfrac{1+i}{\sqrt2},\qquad i^{2.5}=e^{\dfrac{5\pi i}{4}}=-\dfrac{1+i}{\sqrt2}.
\]
So the second computation $i^{2.5}=i^2\cdot i^{0.5}=-\dfrac{1+i}{\sqrt2}$ is correct, and the error is the first step,
\[
i^{2.5}=\left(i^5\right)^{0.5}.
\]
Here $\left(i^5\right)^{0.5}=i^{0.5}=\dfrac{1+i}{\sqrt2}$, which is the \emph{other} square root of $i^5=i$. Both $i^{2.5}$ and $\left(i^5\right)^{0.5}$ square to $i$, but that only shows they are equal up to sign, exactly as in the false argument $-1=\sqrt{(-1)^2}=\sqrt1=1$. Nothing forces $1+i=0$, and of course $1+i\ne0$.
