\begin{enumerate}
\item Pair each divisor $d$ with $\dfrac{n}{d}$. All pairs contain two distinct divisors except possibly $d=\sqrt n$. Thus an unpaired divisor exists exactly when $n$ is a square.
\item For any prime, coprimality means its exponent in $na$ comes entirely from one of the two factors. Since that exponent is even, every exponent in both factors is even.
\item The exponent of a prime in $nk^2$ is its exponent in $n$ plus an even number. If the former sum is even, the exponent in $n$ is even.
\item Completing the square gives $4(x^2+x)+1=(2x+1)^2$ and $4(x^2-x)+1=(2x-1)^2$.
\end{enumerate}
