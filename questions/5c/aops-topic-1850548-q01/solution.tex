A common factor gives a common (possibly complex) root $\alpha$:
\[
3\alpha^2+p=0,\qquad\alpha^3+p\alpha+r=0 .
\]
If $p=0$, then $\alpha=0$ and so $r=0$, and the value is $0$. Otherwise, from the first equation $\alpha^2=-\dfrac {p}{3}$, and the second gives $\alpha\left(\alpha^2+p\right)=-r$, i.e.\ $\alpha\cdot\dfrac{2p}{3}=-r$, so $\alpha=-\dfrac{3r}{2p}$. Then
\[
\dfrac{9r^2}{4p^2}=\alpha^2=-\dfrac {p}{3}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad27r^2=-4p^3\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\dfrac{p^3}{27}+\dfrac{r^2}{4}=\boxed{0}.
\]
(This is the condition for $x^3+px+r$ to have a repeated root, since $3x^2+p$ is its derivative.)
