\textbf{(a)} From $0<a<7$ and $0<b<10$ we get $ab<7b<70$, and then $abc<70c<70\cdot13=910$, using positivity at each step.

\textbf{(b)} If $abc>910>0$, none of $a,b,c$ is $0$ and an even number of them is negative. By (a) they are not all positive, so \emph{exactly two of them are negative} and the third is positive. Conversely, any triple with exactly two negative entries, the third entry positive, each entry below its bound, and $abc>910$ satisfies all the conditions. So the answer is
\[\boxed{\text{exactly two of }a,b,c\text{ are negative, the third is positive, }a<7,\ b<10,\ c<13,\text{ and }abc>910.}\]
For example, $(a,b,c)=(-10,-10,12)$ gives $abc=1200>910$. (There are infinitely many such triples; the product can be made as large as we like.)
