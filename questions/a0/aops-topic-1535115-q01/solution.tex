Multiply by $10xy$: $10y+10x=xy$, that is $xy-10x-10y+100=100$, or
\[
(x-10)(y-10)=100 .
\]
Conversely every solution of this with $x,y\ne0$ solves the original equation.

\textbf{(a)} For positive $x,y$ the equation forces $x>10$ and $y>10$ (as $\dfrac{1}{x}<\dfrac{1}{10}$), so $x-10$ and $y-10$ are positive divisors of $100$ with product $100$. Since $100=2^25^2$ has $3\cdot3=9$ positive divisors, there are $\boxed{9}$ ordered pairs, from $(11,110)$ to $(110,11)$ (including $(20,20)$).

\textbf{(b)} Now $x-10=d$ and $y-10=\dfrac{100}{d}$ for any divisor $d$ of $100$, positive or negative: $18$ choices. We must exclude $x=0$ or $y=0$, i.e.\ $d=-10$ (which gives $x=y=0$); no other $d$ gives a zero. So there are $\boxed{17}$ ordered pairs of nonzero integers; the $8$ new ones have one of $x,y$ negative, for example $(5,-10)$ and $(-90,9)$.
