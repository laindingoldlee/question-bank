Take $P$ as origin, the line $AB$ as the horizontal axis, and write $A=(a,0)$, $B=(-b,0)$, $X=c\mathbf u$, $Y=-d\mathbf u$, where $a,b,c,d>0$ and $\mathbf u$ is a unit vector not horizontal. Invert a nonzero vector $F$ to $F^*=\dfrac{F}{|F|^2}$. If $F$ is the perpendicular foot on line $AX$, then the line equation is $F\cdot Z=|F|^2$, so $F^*\cdot A=F^*\cdot X=1$. Thus $F^*_x=\dfrac{1}{a}$ and $F^*\cdot\mathbf u=\dfrac{1}{c}$. The four inverted feet are the four intersections of lines $x=\dfrac{1}{a},-\dfrac{1}{b}$ and $Z\cdot\mathbf u=\dfrac{1}{c},-\dfrac{1}{d}$, so they form a parallelogram. Inversion takes a circle avoiding the origin to a circle; this follows by substituting $F=\dfrac{F^*}{|F^*|^2}$ into a circle equation. A circle through the origin would become a line, which cannot contain this nondegenerate parallelogram. Therefore the original feet are concyclic exactly when this parallelogram is cyclic, hence rectangular, exactly when $AB\perp XY$. Finally, by the triangle midpoint theorem, the midpoint quadrilateral has adjacent sides parallel to $AB$ and $XY$; it is a rectangle under exactly the same condition.
