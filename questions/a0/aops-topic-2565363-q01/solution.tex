\textbf{(a)} Let $Q(x)=b_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+b_1x+b_0$. Then
\[(x-r)Q(x)=b_{n-1}x^n+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(b_{k-1}-r\,b_k\right)x^k-r\,b_0.\]
By the definition, $b_{n-1}=a_n$ and $b_{k-1}-r\,b_k=a_k$ for $1\le k\le n-1$. So $(x-r)Q(x)$ has the same coefficients as $p(x)$ except the constant term, which is $-r\,b_0$ instead of $a_0$. Hence
\[p(x)-(x-r)Q(x)=a_0+r\,b_0=R,\]
which is the claimed identity. Since $R$ is a constant, $Q$ is the quotient and $R$ the remainder. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{(b)} With coefficients $2,-3,4,-5$ and $r=2$: bring down $2$; $2\cdot2=4$ and $-3+4=1$; $1\cdot2=2$ and $4+2=6$; $6\cdot2=12$ and $-5+12=7$. So
\[2x^3-3x^2+4x-5=(x-2)\left(2x^2+x+6\right)+7,\]
i.e. the quotient is $\boxed{2x^2+x+6}$ and the remainder is $\boxed{7}$. As a check, the remainder theorem gives $p(2)=16-12+8-5=7$.
