Write $\dfrac{a-b}{2a}=\dfrac12-\dfrac b{2a}$. The ratio $\dfrac b{2a}$ is increasing in $b$ and decreasing in $a$. It ranges over $\left(\dfrac4{8},\dfrac5{6}\right)$, with infimum at $b\to4$, $a\to4$ and supremum at $b\to5$, $a\to3$; since both variables vary independently over open intervals, every value in between occurs. So the expression takes exactly the values in $\boxed{\left(-\dfrac13,0\right)}$.

In the posted attempts, method A multiplied $-\dfrac1{2a}$ by the bounds of $b$ in the wrong pairing, and method B multiplied intervals containing negative numbers endpoint by endpoint. Such inequalities can only be multiplied when all quantities are positive.
