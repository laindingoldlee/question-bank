\textbf{(a)} We have $x^2=\dfrac{6-2\sqrt5}{4}=\dfrac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$, so $x^2+x=\dfrac{3-\sqrt5+\sqrt5-1}{2}=1$. Thus $x(1+x)=1$, and since $1+x\ne0$, $x=\dfrac{1}{1+x}$.

\textbf{(b)} All $c_n$ are positive. Using (a),
\[
\left|c_{n+1}-x\right|=\left|\dfrac{1}{1+c_n}-\dfrac{1}{1+x}\right|=\dfrac{\left|x-c_n\right|}{(1+c_n)(1+x)}\le\dfrac{\left|c_n-x\right|}{1+x}=x\left|c_n-x\right| .
\]
Since $0<x<1$ (indeed $x\approx0.618$), induction gives $\left|c_n-x\right|\le x^{n-1}\left|c_1-x\right|\to0$. So $c_n\to x$, and
\[
\dfrac{\sqrt5-1}{2}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{1+\dfrac{1}{1+\dfrac{1}{1+\cdots}}}}
\]
(in the usual notation, $[0;1,1,1,\dots]$). The convergents are ratios of consecutive Fibonacci numbers, $c_n=\dfrac{F_n}{F_{n+1}}$: $1,\dfrac{1}{2},\dfrac{2}{3},\dfrac{3}{5},\dfrac{5}{8},\dots$ $\blacksquare$
