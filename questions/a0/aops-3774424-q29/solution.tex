Write the three principal angles as $A,B,C$. Their cosines are positive. Dividing $\cos(A+B+C)=0$ by $\cos A\cos B\cos C$ gives $1-x(x+1)-x(x+2)-(x+1)(x+2)=0$, or $3(x+1)^2=2$. The sum of arctangents is strictly increasing and is zero at $x=-1$. Thus only the root greater than $-1$ can give $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$: $\boxed{x=-1+\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{3}}}$. At this root the sum is positive and less than $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, so vanishing cosine indeed makes it $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
