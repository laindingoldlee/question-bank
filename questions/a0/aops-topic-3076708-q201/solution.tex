For a positive integer $b$, $ab$ is the sum of $b$ copies of $a$. For $b=1$ there is one copy, giving $a$. For $b=0$ the sum has no terms and is the additive identity $0$. A noninteger real number cannot specify a number of terms in a finite list; multiplication by arbitrary real numbers therefore needs an extension of the integer repeated-addition definition.
