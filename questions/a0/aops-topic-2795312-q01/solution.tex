By unique factorisation, $d\mid n$ iff $d=p_1^{e_1}\cdots p_r^{e_r}$ with $0\le e_i\le k_i$ for each $i$ (a divisor can contain no other primes, and no prime to a higher power than in $n$). Different exponent choices give different divisors, again by unique factorisation. There are $k_i+1$ choices for each $e_i$, independently, so the number of divisors is $\prod(k_i+1)$. $\blacksquare$
