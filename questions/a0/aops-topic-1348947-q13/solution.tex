After one hour $60$ draws have been made and the box holds $64$ books; $43$ black books means that exactly $40$ of the draws were black and $20$ red.

Consider any particular order of $40$ black and $20$ red draws. When the $j$-th black draw happens, the box has $3+(j-1)$ black books; when the $j$-th red draw happens, it has $1+(j-1)$ red books; and at the $m$-th draw the box has $4+(m-1)$ books. So the probability of that order is
\[
\dfrac{(3\cdot4\cdots42)(1\cdot2\cdots20)}{4\cdot5\cdots63}=\dfrac{\dfrac{42!}{2}\cdot20!}{\dfrac{63!}{6}}=\dfrac{3\cdot42!\cdot20!}{63!},
\]
which does not depend on the order. Multiplying by the $\dbinom{60}{40}$ orders,
\[
P=\dbinom{60}{40}\dfrac{3\cdot42!\,20!}{63!}=\dfrac{3\cdot60!\cdot42!}{40!\cdot63!}=\dfrac{3\cdot42\cdot41}{63\cdot62\cdot61}=\dfrac{41}{1891}.
\]
Since $1891=31\cdot61$ and $41$ is prime, the fraction is in lowest terms, and $a=\boxed{41}$.
