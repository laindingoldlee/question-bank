Let $\angle DBC=\angle ABD=y$, so $\angle ABC=2y$, and since $AB=AC$, also $\angle ACB=2y$.

Since $BD=BC$, triangle $BDC$ is isosceles with $\angle BDC=\angle BCD=2y$. The angles of triangle $BDC$ give
\[
y+2y+2y=180^\circ\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=36^\circ .
\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair B=(0,0), C=(1,0), A=(0.5,0.5*Tan(72));
pair D=intersectionpoint(B--B+3*dir(36),A--C);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9)); draw(B--D,linewidth(0.9));
draw(arc(B,0.17,0,36),red); draw(arc(B,0.21,36,72),red);
label("$y$",B+0.3*dir(18),red+fontsize(8pt)); label("$y$",B+0.32*dir(54),red+fontsize(8pt));
draw(arc(A,0.2,252,288),blue); label("$x$",A+0.29*dir(270),blue+fontsize(8pt));
draw(arc(C,0.15,108,180),deepgreen); label("$2y$",C+0.24*dir(144),deepgreen+fontsize(8pt));
draw(arc(D,0.12,216,288),deepgreen); label("$2y$",D+0.22*dir(252),deepgreen+fontsize(8pt));
dot(D);
label("$A$",A,N); label("$B$",B,SW); label("$C$",C,SE); label("$D$",D,E);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Hence $\angle A=180^\circ-2y-2y=180^\circ-144^\circ=\boxed{36}$ degrees. (The triangle is the $36^\circ$-$72^\circ$-$72^\circ$ golden triangle.)
