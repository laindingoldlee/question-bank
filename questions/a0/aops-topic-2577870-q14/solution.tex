Set $k=\sqrt{x-1}>0$. The equation gives $k^3=k+1$, which has exactly one positive root: it is negative after moving all terms left on $0<k\le1$, and strictly increasing from-1 to infinity on $k\ge1$. Since $k(x-2)=1$, squaring yields $(x-1)(x-2)^2=1$, or $x^3-5x^2+8x-5=0$. Put $z=3x-5$; then $z^3-3z-25=0$. If $z=U+V$ with $UV=1$, we need $U^3+V^3=25$ and $U^3V^3=1$. Thus $U^3,V^3=\dfrac{25\pm\sqrt{621}}{2}=\dfrac{25\pm3\sqrt{69}}{2}$. Both are positive; their real cube roots yield the unique admissible solution. Therefore $\boxed{y=69}$.
