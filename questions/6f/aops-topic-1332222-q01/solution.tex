A way is a multiset of three baskets, each described by (reds, greens). Do casework on how the $3$ reds are split, as a partition into at most three parts.

\textbf{Reds $3,0,0$.} The two empty-of-red baskets are alike. The greens can be: both with the red basket; both in one other basket; one with the reds and one in another basket; one in each of the other two baskets. That is $4$ ways.

\textbf{Reds $2,1,0$.} All three baskets are different, so the greens are $2$ identical items in $3$ distinguishable baskets: $\dbinom{4}{2}=6$ ways.

\textbf{Reds $1,1,1$.} The baskets are alike, so only the pattern of greens matters: $(2,0,0)$ or $(1,1,0)$, $2$ ways.

The total is $4+6+2=\boxed{12}$.
