Compared with buying the fruit singly, the first bag ($3\cdot0.40+2\cdot0.50=2.20$) saves $20$ cents and the second ($0.40+1.50=1.90$) saves $10$ cents. Buying exactly $5$ apples and $6$ oranges singly costs $2.00+3.00=5.00$.

Buying extra fruit only costs more, so look at combinations of bags that do not exceed $5$ apples and $6$ oranges: two first bags give $6$ apples (too many, and then $4.00+1.00=5.00$ anyway); one first bag and one second bag give $4$ apples and $5$ oranges, saving $30$ cents; one first bag alone saves $20$; three second bags would need $9$ oranges. A short check of all cases (including buying surplus fruit) shows the best is one bag of each kind plus one apple and one orange:
\[2.00+1.80+0.40+0.50=\boxed{4.70}\ \text{dollars}.\]
