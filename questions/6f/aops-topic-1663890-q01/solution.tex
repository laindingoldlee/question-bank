\textbf{They are not always similar.}

\textbf{The angles.} In $AEPD$ the angles at $E$ and $D$ are right angles and the angle at $A$ is $60^\circ$, so the angle at $P$ is $360^\circ-90^\circ-90^\circ-60^\circ=120^\circ$. The same holds for $BFPE$: angles $60^\circ$ at $B$, $90^\circ$ at $F$ and $E$, $120^\circ$ at $P$. A similarity preserves angles, so a similarity between the two quadrilaterals must send the $120^\circ$ vertex $P$ to $P$, and the two sides at $P$ to the two sides at $P$. Hence the ratio of the longer to the shorter side at $P$ must be the same in both.

\textbf{A counterexample.} Let the triangle have altitude $6$, and let $P$ be the point at distances $1$ from $BC$, $2$ from $CA$ and $3$ from $AB$. Such a point exists: the line parallel to $BC$ at distance $1$ and the line parallel to $CA$ at distance $2$, both on the triangle's side, meet at a point whose distance to $AB$ is $6-1-2=3>0$ by Viviani's theorem (the three distances from an interior point add up to the altitude), so it is inside.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real s=12/sqrt(3);
pair B=(0,0),C=(s,0),A=(s/2,6);
pair P=(7/sqrt(3),1);
pair F=(P.x,0);
pair E=B+dot(P-B,unit(A-B))*unit(A-B);
pair D=C+dot(P-C,unit(A-C))*unit(A-C);
fill(A--E--P--D--cycle,paleblue);fill(B--F--P--E--cycle,palered);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(P--E);draw(P--D);draw(P--F);
real q=0.3;
draw(F+(0,q)--F+(q,q)--F+(q,0));
draw(E+q*unit(B-E)--E+q*unit(B-E)+q*unit(P-E)--E+q*unit(P-E));
draw(D+q*unit(C-D)--D+q*unit(C-D)+q*unit(P-D)--D+q*unit(P-D));
dot(P);
label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,SW);label("$C$",C,SE);label("$P$",P,dir(-40));
label("$E$",E,NW);label("$D$",D,NE);label("$F$",F,S);
label("$3$",(P+E)/2,dir(150),fontsize(9pt));label("$2$",(P+D)/2,N,fontsize(9pt));label("$1$",(P+F)/2,W,fontsize(9pt));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The sides at $P$ are $PE=3$, $PD=2$ in $AEPD$ and $PF=1$, $PE=3$ in $BFPE$, with ratios $\dfrac{3}{2}$ and $3$. These are different, so $AEPD$ and $BFPE$ are not similar.

\textbf{When are they similar?} A quadrilateral with angles $60^\circ$, $90^\circ$, $120^\circ$, $90^\circ$ is determined up to congruence by its two sides at the $120^\circ$ vertex (they fix the two right-angle vertices, and the fourth vertex is where the perpendiculars there meet). So, writing $d_a,d_b,d_c$ for the distances from $P$ to $BC$, $CA$, $AB$, the two quadrilaterals are similar exactly when the pairs $\{d_c,d_b\}$ and $\{d_a,d_c\}$ have the same ratio, that is, when $d_a=d_b$ or $d_c^2=d_ad_b$. For instance, they are similar when $P$ is the centre, but not in general.
