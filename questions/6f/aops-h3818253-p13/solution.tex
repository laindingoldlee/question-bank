Factor the right side as $(x+1)^2(x-1)$. If $x=-1$, then $y=0$. Otherwise nonnegativity requires $x\ge1$, and
$\left(\dfrac{y}{x+1}\right)^2=x-1$.
A rational number whose square is an integer is an integer: reducing its numerator and denominator to coprime terms proves its denominator is $1$. Thus $x-1=k^2$ for an integer $k\ge0$. All solutions are
\[
\boxed{(-1,0)\quad\text{and}\quad (x,y)=(k^2+1,\ \pm k(k^2+2)),\quad k=0,1,2,\ldots}.
\]
Substitution verifies every listed pair.
