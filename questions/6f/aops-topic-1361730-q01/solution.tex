A sequence returns to the origin exactly when the numbers of up and down moves are equal and the numbers of left and right moves are equal. With $4$ moves the possibilities are:
\begin{itemize}
\item two up and two down: $\dbinom42=6$ orders;
\item two left and two right: $6$ orders;
\item one each of up, down, left, right: $4!=24$ orders.
\end{itemize}
Total: $6+6+24=\boxed{36}$.

\textbf{A slicker count.} Rotate the plane by $45^\circ$: each move changes $x+y$ by $\pm1$ and $x-y$ by $\pm1$ independently, and the bug is home exactly when both sums of $\pm1$'s are $0$. Each of the two coordinates is a sequence of four $\pm1$'s with two of each sign, so the count is $\dbinom42^2=36$; in general there are $\dbinom{2n}{n}^2$ returning walks of length $2n$.
