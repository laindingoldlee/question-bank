Let the circumradius be $R$. A chord from tangency point $B$ to a point at perpendicular distance $h$ from the tangent has squared length $2Rh$: this follows by placing the circle centre at $(0,R)$ and the tangent at $y=0$. Hence $AB^2=50R$ and $BC^2=32R$, so $AB\cdot BC=40R$. The extended sine rule gives $\sin\angle ABC=\dfrac{AC}{2R}=\dfrac{10}{R}$. Therefore $S=\dfrac{1}{2} AB\cdot BC\sin B=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot40R\cdot\dfrac{10}{R}=200$. The answer is $\boxed{10}$. This calculation covers both possible placements relative to the normal through B.
