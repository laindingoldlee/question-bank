Let $A=\{a\in\mathbb Z:P(a)=1\}$ and $B=\{b\in\mathbb Z:P(b)=-1\}$. Each has at most $d$ elements. For $a\in A,b\in B$, polynomial divisibility gives $a-b\mid P(a)-P(b)=2$, so $a-b\in\{-2,-1,1,2\}$. If $B$ contains distinct $b,c$, every $a\in A$ belongs to the intersection of $b+\{-2,-1,1,2\}$ and $c+\{-2,-1,1,2\}$. Two distinct translates of this four-element set intersect in at most two elements (check their positive separations $1,2,3,4$, giving $2,1,2,1$ elements). Thus either $|B|\le1$ or $|A|\le2$. In either case $|A|+|B|\le d+2$.
