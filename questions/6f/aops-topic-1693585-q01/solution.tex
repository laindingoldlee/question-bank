Let the rectangle have $3$ rows and $4$ columns, and let $f(n)$ be the number of tilings of a $3\times n$ rectangle; let $g(n)$ be the number of tilings of a $3\times n$ rectangle with one corner cell removed (by symmetry it does not matter which corner of the left side).

Look at the left column of a $3\times n$ board ($n\ge2$). Its three cells are covered either by three horizontal dominoes, which then fill the first two columns and leave a $3\times(n-2)$ board, or by one vertical domino (on the top two or on the bottom two cells) together with a horizontal domino on the remaining cell, which leaves a $3\times(n-1)$ board with a corner cell removed (in two mirror-image ways). Hence
\[
f(n)=2g(n-1)+f(n-2).
\]
For a $3\times n$ board with its top-left cell removed, the two remaining cells of the left column are covered either by one vertical domino, leaving a $3\times(n-1)$ board, or by two horizontal dominoes; in the second case the top cell of the second column can only be covered by a horizontal domino reaching into the third column, which leaves a $3\times(n-2)$ board with its top-left cell removed. Hence
\[
g(n)=f(n-1)+g(n-2).
\]
With $f(0)=1$, $f(1)=0$, $g(0)=0$, $g(1)=1$: $f(2)=2g(1)+f(0)=3$, $g(2)=f(1)+g(0)=0$, $g(3)=f(2)+g(1)=4$, $f(4)=2g(3)+f(2)=8+3=11$.

So there are $\boxed{11}$ tilings.
