The Pythagorean theorem is about \emph{lengths} of sides, which are non-negative real numbers. The leg from $0$ to $i$ has length $|i-0|=|i|=1$, not $i$. The complex number $i$ describes the leg's position and direction, not its length. So the correct computation is
\[
(\text{hypotenuse})^2=|1|^2+|i|^2=1+1=2,
\]
and the hypotenuse has length $\boxed{\sqrt{2}}$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair O=(0,0),X=(1,0),Y=(0,1);
draw((-0.25,0)--(1.4,0),gray,Arrow(5)); draw((0,-0.25)--(0,1.35),gray,Arrow(5));
draw(O--X--Y--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(X--Y,red+linewidth(0.9));
draw((0.1,0)--(0.1,0.1)--(0,0.1));
dot(O); dot(X); dot(Y);
label("$0$",O,SW); label("$1$",X,S); label("$i$",Y,W);
label("$|1|=1$",(0.5,0),S); label("$|i|=1$",(0,0.55),W);
label("$|1-i|=\sqrt{2}$",(0.5,0.5),NE,red);
label("Re",(1.4,0),SE,gray); label("Im",(0,1.35),NE,gray);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

This agrees with the distance between the endpoints computed directly: $|1-i|=\sqrt{1^2+(-1)^2}=\sqrt2$. In general the distance between complex numbers $z$ and $w$ is $|z-w|$, where $|x+yi|=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$; squaring a complex number $z$ gives $z^2$, which is generally not $|z|^2$.
