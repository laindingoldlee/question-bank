The diagonals of a kite are perpendicular, and one of them (the axis of symmetry) bisects the other. They divide the kite into four right triangles:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(7cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
path ra(pair P,pair u,pair v,real s=0.2){return P+s*unit(u)--P+s*unit(u)+s*unit(v)--P+s*unit(v);}
pair A=(0,0),B=(12,8),C=(58,0),D=(12,-8),O=(12,0);
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(A--C,blue); draw(B--D,red);
draw(ra(O,C-O,B-O,2.2));
draw(B--(-5,8),gray+dotted); draw(D--(-5,-8),gray+dotted);
draw(A--(0,-12),gray+dotted); draw(C--(58,-12),gray+dotted);
draw((-4,-8)--(-4,8),red,Arrows(TeXHead)); draw((0,-11)--(58,-11),blue,Arrows(TeXHead));
label("$24$",(-4,0),W,red); label("$58$",(29,-11),S,blue);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

If the axis is split into pieces $p$ and $q$ with $p+q=58$, the two triangles on one side of the axis have total area $\dfrac{12p}{2}+\dfrac{12q}{2}=6\cdot58$, and the same holds on the other side. So the area is
\[
\dfrac{24\cdot58}{2}=\boxed{696}.
\]
