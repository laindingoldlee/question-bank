Cornie walks through a square field with side length $1$ kilometre. She starts at the midpoint of one side and walks in a straight line to the midpoint of the opposite side. Kevin is at a point of the field chosen uniformly at random. At first Cornie can see everything within $200$ metres of her. Exactly halfway through her walk she gets tired, and for the rest of the walk she can see only within $100$ metres of her position. The probability that Cornie sees Kevin (at some moment of her walk) can be written as $\dfrac{a-b\sqrt c+d\pi}{e}$ for positive integers $a,b,c,d,e$ with $\gcd(a,b,d,e)=1$ and $c$ not divisible by the square of any prime. Find $\dfrac12(a+b+c+d+e)-13$.

$\textbf{(A) }112.5\qquad\textbf{(B) }139.5\qquad\textbf{(C) }187\qquad\textbf{(D) }192.5\qquad\textbf{(E) }219.5$
