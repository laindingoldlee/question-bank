Put $E=(0,0),B=(-y,0),C=(x,0)$, and let $A=d(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)$, with $0<\alpha<\pi$. Write $s$ for the semiperimeter of $ABC$. The reflection condition gives $AB=s-y$ and $AC=s-x$, so both small triangles have semiperimeter $\sigma=\dfrac{s+d}{2}$. Comparing their areas gives $\dfrac{x}{y}=\dfrac{11}{4}$.
Let $t=\tan(\dfrac{\alpha}{2})>0$. The small incenters are $(-4t,4)$ and $(\dfrac{11}{t},11)$. Their separation therefore gives $4t+\dfrac{11}{t}=24$, so $t=\dfrac{1}{2}$ or $\dfrac{11}{2}$. Put $L=s-d>0$. Tangent lengths from $E$ in the two small triangles give
\[
y-\dfrac{L}{2}=4t,\qquad x-\dfrac{L}{2}=\dfrac{11}{t}.
\]
Using $\dfrac{x}{y}=\dfrac{11}{4}$ yields $L=\dfrac{88(\dfrac{1}{t}-t)}{7}$. Since $L>0$, we must have $t=\dfrac{1}{2}$. Consequently $L=\dfrac{132}{7}$, $y=\dfrac{80}{7}$, $x=\dfrac{220}{7}$, and $(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)=(\dfrac{3}{5},\dfrac{4}{5})$.
Now $AB=d+L-y=d+\dfrac{52}{7}$. Its squared coordinate length is $d^2+2dy(\dfrac{3}{5})+y^2$. Equating these expressions yields $d=66$. Thus $s=\dfrac{594}{7}$, altitude $h=\dfrac{264}{5}$, and $BC=\dfrac{300}{7}$. The desired inradius is
\[
\boxed{\dfrac{h\,BC}{2s}=\dfrac{40}{3}}.
\]
