$PQ=3+2=5$ miles. Follow both walkers after 1 P.M. (positions measured from $P$).
\begin{itemize}
\item Michelle reaches $Q$ at $\dfrac{5}{3}$ h after noon (1:40 P.M.) and turns back towards $P$.
\item Audrey reaches $P$ at $\dfrac{5}{2}$ h after noon (2:30 P.M.) and turns back towards $Q$.
\end{itemize}
Between 1:40 and 2:30 both walk towards $P$, Michelle behind Audrey; at 2:30 Michelle is at $5-3\cdot\dfrac{5}{6}=2.5$ miles from $P$ while Audrey is at $P$, so Michelle has not caught up (she would need to close a gap of $\dfrac{10}{3}$ miles at $1$ mile per hour). From 2:30 they walk towards each other with a gap of $2.5$ miles and closing speed $5$ miles per hour, so they meet half an hour later, at 3:00 P.M., $1$ mile from $P$. Michelle reaches $P$ only at $\dfrac{10}{3}$ h (3:20 P.M.), so this is indeed their next meeting.

They pass each other for the second time at $\boxed{3\text{ P.M.}}$ Using $d=rt$ directly: from noon until a head-on meeting after both have turned once, together they cover $3\cdot5=15$ miles (three lengths of $PQ$) at a combined $5$ miles per hour, which takes $3$ hours.
