\textbf{The single frog.} You survive if it is male: probability $\dfrac{3}{5}$.

\textbf{The pair.} For two frogs,
\[
P(\text{both male})=0.6^2=0.36,\quad P(\text{one of each})=2\cdot0.6\cdot0.4=0.48,\quad P(\text{both female})=0.4^2=0.16 .
\]
We know that at least one is female, which has probability $0.48+0.16=0.64$. You survive if, in addition, at least one is male, i.e. the pair is one of each. So
\[
P(\text{survive}\mid\text{at least one female})=\dfrac{0.48}{0.64}=\dfrac{3}{4} .
\]
Since $\dfrac{3}{4}>\dfrac{3}{5}$, go to the pair: survival probabilities are $\boxed{\dfrac{3}{5}\text{ (single frog) and }\dfrac{3}{4}\text{ (pair); choose the pair}}$.

(With the original puzzle's $50\%$ the numbers are $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\dfrac{2}{3}$. The calculation assumes the information is exactly ``at least one of the two is female''; a more detailed model of who croaked can change the answer.)
