The route is $1,e_1,o_1,e_2,o_2,e_3,o_3,e_4,1$, where $(o_1,o_2,o_3)$ is an order of $3,5,7$ and $(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4)$ an order of $2,4,6,8$. Each even house is opposite exactly one odd house ($2$ opposite $1$, $4$ opposite $3$, $6$ opposite $5$, $8$ opposite $7$), and an even house in position $e_i$ is next to the odd houses on both sides of it.

Fix the order of the odd houses, say $(o_1,o_2,o_3)=(3,5,7)$; by relabelling, every one of the $3!=6$ orders gives the same count. The neighbours of the even positions are
\[
e_1:\{1,3\},\qquad e_2:\{3,5\},\qquad e_3:\{5,7\},\qquad e_4:\{7,1\}.
\]
So house $2$ (opposite $1$) must go to $e_2$ or $e_3$; house $4$ (opposite $3$) to $e_3$ or $e_4$; house $6$ to $e_4$ or $e_1$; house $8$ to $e_1$ or $e_2$. These choices form a single cycle: if $2\to e_2$ then $8\to e_1$, $6\to e_4$, $4\to e_3$; if $2\to e_3$ then $4\to e_4$, $6\to e_1$, $8\to e_2$. So there are exactly $2$ ways for each order of the odd houses, and
\[
6\cdot2=\boxed{12}
\]
delivery sequences in all.
