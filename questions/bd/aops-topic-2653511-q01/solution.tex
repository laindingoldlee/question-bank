The divisor count of $110n^3$ need not be a multiple of $d(110)=8$, because $n$ may share prime factors with $110=2\cdot5\cdot11$. Write $n=2^a5^b11^cm$ with $\gcd(m,110)=1$. Then
\[110n^3=2^{3a+1}5^{3b+1}11^{3c+1}m^3,\qquad d(110n^3)=(3a+2)(3b+2)(3c+2)\,d(m^3).\]
Each of the three factors is at least $2$, and $110=2\cdot5\cdot11$ is a product of only three primes, so the three factors are $2,5,11$ in some order and $d(m^3)=1$, i.e. $m=1$. Thus $\{a,b,c\}=\{0,1,3\}$, and $n=pq^3$ for two different primes $p,q\in\{2,5,11\}$ (for example $n=5\cdot11^3$, with $110n^3=2\cdot5^4\cdot11^{10}$ having $2\cdot5\cdot11=110$ divisors).

Then $81n^4=3^4p^4q^{12}$, which has $5\cdot5\cdot13=\boxed{325}$ divisors.
