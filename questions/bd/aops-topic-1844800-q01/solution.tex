There are $2^9-1=511$ nonempty subsets. The sum of the elements of a nonempty subset is an integer from $1$ to $1+2+\cdots+9=45$, so there are only $45$ possible sums. If every sum occurred for at most $11$ subsets, there would be at most $45\cdot11=495<511$ subsets. So by the pigeonhole principle some sum occurs for at least $12$ subsets, and in particular for at least $10$. $\blacksquare$
