Let $a=BC=11$, $b=AC=10$, $c=AB=12$, $m=BD=5$, $n=DC=6$ and $d=AD$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair B=(0,0),C=(11,0),A=(7.5,sqrt(87.75)),D=(5,0);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(A--D,blue+linewidth(0.9));
dot(D,blue);
label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,SW);label("$C$",C,SE);label("$D$",D,S,blue);
label("$12$",(A+B)/2,NW);label("$10$",(A+C)/2,NE);
label("$5$",(B+D)/2,S);label("$6$",(D+C)/2,S);
label("$d$",(A+D)/2,W,blue);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Stewart's theorem for the cevian $AD$ states $b^2m+c^2n=a\left(d^2+mn\right)$. So
\[
100\cdot5+144\cdot6=11\left(d^2+30\right)\iff1364=11d^2+330\iff d^2=94 .
\]
Hence $AD=\boxed{\sqrt{94}}$.

(Check without Stewart: by the law of cosines in triangle $ABC$, $\cos B=\dfrac{12^2+11^2-10^2}{2\cdot12\cdot11}=\dfrac{165}{264}=\dfrac{5}{8}$, and then in triangle $ABD$, $d^2=12^2+5^2-2\cdot12\cdot5\cdot\dfrac{5}{8}=169-75=94$.)
