Put $t=S(n)$. Apart from $n=10^{10}$, write $n$ as ten digits with leading zeros allowed, so $0\le t\le90$. Checking the squares $0,1,4,9,16,25,36,49,64,81$ shows $t=S(t)^2$ exactly for $t=0,1,81$. There is one string of sum $0$ and ten of sum $1$. Complementing every digit to $9$ bijects sum-$81$ strings with sum-$9$ strings; stars and bars gives $\binom{18}{9}=48620$, with no digit bound active because the sum is $9$. Finally $10^{10}$ itself contributes one more. Thus the count is $48632$ and the requested remainder is $\boxed{32}$.
