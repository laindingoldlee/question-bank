Set $Q(x)=P(x)-x-51$, also an integer-coefficient polynomial. Then
\[
Q(21)=-55,\quad Q(32)=-330,\quad Q(37)=-55,\quad Q(N)=0.
\]
For integer arguments, their difference divides the difference of polynomial values. Thus $N-21$ and $N-37$ are signed divisors of $55$. They differ by $16$. Among $\pm1,\pm5,\pm11,\pm55$, the only such ordered pairs are $(5,-11)$ and $(11,-5)$, yielding $N=26$ or $32$. But $Q(32)=-330\ne0$, so $\boxed{N=26}$.
