(a) The semiperimeter is $s=\dfrac{3+5+8}{2}=8$, so Heron's formula gives
\[
[ABC]=\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}=\sqrt{8\cdot5\cdot3\cdot0}=\boxed{0}.
\]

(b) Here $AB+BC=3+5=8=AC$: the triangle inequality $AB+BC>AC$ holds with equality. For any point $B$, $AB+BC\ge AC$, with equality exactly when $B$ lies on the segment $AC$ (the straight segment is the shortest path from $A$ to $C$). So $B$ lies on segment $AC$, $3$ units from $A$ and $5$ from $C$:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0), B=(3,0), C=(8,0);
draw(A--C,linewidth(0.9));
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);
label("$A$",A,S); label("$B$",B,S); label("$C$",C,S);
label("$3$",(A+B)/2,N); label("$5$",(B+C)/2,N);
draw((0,-0.75)--(8,-0.75),Arrows(4)); label("$8$",(4,-0.75),S);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The three points are collinear and enclose no area, which matches the area $0$. Such a figure is called a \emph{degenerate} triangle: it is the limiting case of a triangle, not a genuine triangle, because three collinear points do not form a triangle.
