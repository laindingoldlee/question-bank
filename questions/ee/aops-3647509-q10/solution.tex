There are two possible structures. If a cut is a full diagonal, it leaves an area-three triangle and an area-three triangle to divide into areas one and two. Choose the diagonal in two ways, the half to divide in two ways, the vertex from which to divide that triangle in three ways, and which end of the opposite side receives the smaller piece in two ways. This gives 24. Otherwise the two cuts meet at an interior point of one side and run to the two opposite vertices. The triangle with the opposite full side has area three; the two remaining areas one and two specify two possible locations. Four choices of side give eight. A partition into three triangles has exactly one of these structures: any non-corner boundary vertex must join existing corners, and an interior junction would require at least three cuts. Thus the total is $\boxed{32}$.
