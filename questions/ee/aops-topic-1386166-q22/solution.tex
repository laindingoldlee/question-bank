\textbf{The length $CF$.} Triangle $BCF$ has a right angle at $C$ and $\angle CBF=30^\circ$, so $\angle BFC=60^\circ$. The side opposite the $30^\circ$ angle is $CF$, the \emph{short} leg; $BC=10$ is opposite the $60^\circ$ angle, so it is the short leg times $\sqrt3$. Hence
\[
CF=\dfrac{BC}{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{10}{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{10\sqrt3}{3},\qquad BF=2\,CF=\dfrac{20\sqrt3}{3},
\]
and the solution is right; $10\sqrt3$ would make $CF$ the long leg, which is opposite the $60^\circ$ angle.

\textbf{Finding $E$.} Since $BE$ bisects $\angle CBF$, the angle bisector theorem in triangle $BCF$ gives $\dfrac{CE}{EF}=\dfrac{BC}{BF}=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, so
\[
CE=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2+\sqrt3}\,CF=\dfrac{10}{2+\sqrt3}=10(2-\sqrt3)=20-10\sqrt3 .
\]
(Equivalently, $CE=10\tan15^\circ=10(2-\sqrt3)$.) Then $DE=20-CE=10\sqrt3$, and in right triangle $ADE$,
\[
AE=\sqrt{AD^2+DE^2}=\sqrt{100+300}=\boxed{20}.
\]
