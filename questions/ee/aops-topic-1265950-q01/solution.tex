The small pyramid is similar to the original. If $H$ is the original height, the small pyramid has height $H-2$, and areas scale as the square of the ratio of lengths:
\[\left(\dfrac{H-2}{H}\right)^2=\dfrac{1}{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \dfrac{H-2}{H}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}.\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(4,0),T=(2,4); draw(A--B--T--cycle);
pair P=(A+T)/2+(A-T)/2*0.172, Q=(B+T)/2+(B-T)/2*0.172;
pair P2=T+(A-T)*0.7071, Q2=T+(B-T)*0.7071; draw(P2--Q2,red);
draw((4.5,0)--(4.5,4*0.2929),Arrows); label("$2$",(4.5,0.6),E);
draw((-0.5,0)--(-0.5,4),Arrows); label("$H$",(-0.5,2),W);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

So $H-2=\dfrac{H}{\sqrt2}$, giving
\[H=\dfrac{2}{1-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}}=\dfrac{2\sqrt2}{\sqrt2-1}=2\sqrt2(\sqrt2+1)=\boxed{4+2\sqrt2}.\]
