D's miss probability is $d_k=\dfrac{3}{10}$ for $k\le5$ and $d_k=\dfrac{3}{5}$ thereafter. Let $w_k,m_k$ be N's stated winner and miss probabilities, $c_k=1-w_k-m_k$, and $p_k$ the probability of reaching N's $k$th shot. Then $p_1=1$ and $p_{k+1}=p_kc_k(1-d_k)$. Since $c_7=0$, all sums terminate. A shot by N contributes $p_k$ to expected length, and a shot by D contributes $p_kc_k$. A point is won by N either on a clean winner or on D's miss. Consequently (a) $E=\sum_{k=1}^7p_k(1+c_k)=\boxed{\dfrac{703220883191}{200000000000}}\approx3.516104416$ shots. (b) N's point probability is $P=\sum_{k=1}^7p_k(w_k+c_kd_k)=\dfrac{488842509439}{600000000000}$. Thus N and D expect respectively $\boxed{200P\approx162.9475}$ and $\boxed{200(1-P)\approx37.0525}$ points. This predicts a higher expected score for N, not a guaranteed winner. (c) N's clean-winner probability is $W=\sum p_kw_k=\dfrac{111157490561}{300000000000}$, giving $\boxed{200W\approx74.1050}$ clean winners.
