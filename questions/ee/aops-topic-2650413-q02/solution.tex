The original sum is $10n+45$. If $n+i$ and $n+j$ ($0\le i<j\le9$) are multiplied by $10$, the sum grows by $9(2n+i+j)$, so
\[10n+45+9(2n+i+j)=100,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad28n+9s=55,\quad s=i+j\in\{1,\dots,17\}.\]
Reducing mod $28$: $9s\equiv55\equiv27$, and since $\gcd(9,28)=1$, $s\equiv3\pmod{28}$. So $s=3$ and $n=1$ (realised by multiplying $1$ and $4$, or $2$ and $3$). The original sum is forced to be $10\cdot1+45=\boxed{55}$, which is therefore also the greatest possible value.
