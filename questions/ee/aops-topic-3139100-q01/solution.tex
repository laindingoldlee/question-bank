(a) No: the initial term 1 is rational. (b) The remaining terms are $ r^n $ for positive integers $ n $. If some $ r^n=q $ were rational, then $ r $ would solve the nonzero polynomial $ X^n-q=0 $ with rational coefficients, making it algebraic, contrary to transcendence. Thus every positive power of $ r $ is irrational. This provides a sufficient condition, not a unique common ratio.
