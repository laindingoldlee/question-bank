\textbf{The asteroids.} If each of the six moons gets at least $1$ million of the $6$ million asteroids, each gets exactly $1$ million. So there is only $1$ way to share them, and we only count arrangements.

\textbf{Adamantine and selenium.} They must be at least $3$ places apart. Number the places $1$ to $6$. The pairs of places at distance at least $3$ are $\{1,4\},\{1,5\},\{1,6\},\{2,5\},\{2,6\},\{3,6\}$; with $2$ orders each, that is $12$ placements.

\textbf{Silver, gold, bronze and the sixth moon} fill the other four places, with silver not next to gold or bronze. For each pair of places, the four free places and their neighbour relations are:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\{1,4\}$: free $2,3,5,6$ (neighbours $2$--$3$ and $5$--$6$). Silver at any free place has exactly one free neighbour, which must get the sixth moon: $4\cdot1\cdot2=8$ ways. Likewise $\{3,6\}$: $8$ ways.
\item $\{1,5\}$: free $2,3,4,6$ (neighbours $2$--$3$, $3$--$4$). Silver at $2$ or $4$: its free neighbour $3$ gets the sixth moon and gold and bronze fill the other two places, $2$ ways each; at $3$: two free neighbours, impossible; at $6$: no free neighbour, $3!=6$ ways. Total $10$. Likewise $\{2,6\}$: $10$ ways.
\item $\{1,6\}$: free $2,3,4,5$ in a row. Silver at $2$ or $5$: $2$ ways each; at $3$ or $4$: impossible. Total $4$.
\item $\{2,5\}$: free $1,3,4,6$ (neighbours $3$--$4$ only). Silver at $1$ or $6$: $3!=6$ ways each; at $3$ or $4$: $2$ ways each. Total $16$.
\end{itemize}
The total is $2\cdot(8+8+10+10+4+16)=2\cdot56=\boxed{112}$.
