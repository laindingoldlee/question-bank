Use the sum and difference of cubes, $(u+v)(u^2-uv+v^2)=u^3+v^3$ and $(u-v)(u^2+uv+v^2)=u^3-v^3$, with $u=\sqrt[3]{5}$ and $v=\sqrt[3]{2}$. Then $u^3=5$, $v^3=2$, $u^2=\sqrt[3]{25}$, $uv=\sqrt[3]{10}$ and $v^2=\sqrt[3]{4}$.

\textbf{(a)} Multiply the numerator and the denominator by $u^2-uv+v^2$:
\[
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]{5}+\sqrt[3]{2}}=\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{25}-\sqrt[3]{10}+\sqrt[3]{4}}{5+2}=\boxed{\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{25}-\sqrt[3]{10}+\sqrt[3]{4}}{7}} .
\]

\textbf{(b)} Multiply by $u^2+uv+v^2$:
\[
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]{5}-\sqrt[3]{2}}=\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{25}+\sqrt[3]{10}+\sqrt[3]{4}}{5-2}=\boxed{\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{25}+\sqrt[3]{10}+\sqrt[3]{4}}{3}} .
\]

In general, a denominator $\sqrt[3]{a}\pm\sqrt[3]{b}$ is rationalised by the factor $\sqrt[3]{a^2}\mp\sqrt[3]{ab}+\sqrt[3]{b^2}$, which turns it into $a\pm b$.
