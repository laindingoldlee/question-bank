There are $4$ people named Eric in the ABMC problem writing committee. The ``Eric Test'' is commonly used to decide what problems are going to be used on a test. It is defined as follows:

$\bullet$ The four Erics sit in a line.

$\bullet$  The problem is handed to the leftmost Eric. He has a $\dfrac{1}{2}$ probability of approving the problem.

$\bullet$  For the next three Erics, the probability they approve the problem depends on the number of approvals of the Erics to the left of them. Each Eric has a probability of $\dfrac{1}{2} -\dfrac{1}{2a+2}$ of approving the problem, where a is the number of approvals that have come in the Erics to the left of the current Eric.

$\bullet$  A problem is put onto the test if at least $3$ Erics approve of the problem.

For any problem that undergoes the ``Eric Test'', the probability that it is put onto the test is $\dfrac{p}{q}$ , where $p$ and $q$ are relatively prime positive integers. Find $p + q$.
