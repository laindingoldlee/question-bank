All $216$ outcomes are equally likely, so given the condition, the $15$ outcomes $(a,b,a+b)$ with $a+b\le6$ are equally likely:
\[
\begin{array}{l}
(1,1,2),\ (1,2,3),\ (2,1,3),\ (1,3,4),\ (3,1,4),\ (2,2,4),\ (1,4,5),\ (4,1,5),\\
(2,3,5),\ (3,2,5),\ (1,5,6),\ (5,1,6),\ (2,4,6),\ (4,2,6),\ (3,3,6).
\end{array}
\]
The outcomes containing a $2$ are $(1,1,2)$, $(1,2,3)$, $(2,1,3)$, $(2,2,4)$, $(2,3,5)$, $(3,2,5)$, $(2,4,6)$, $(4,2,6)$: eight of them. So the conditional probability is $\dfrac{8}{15}$ and $m+n=\boxed{23}$.

(This agrees with the definition of conditional probability, which is what Bayes' theorem rests on: $P(\text{a }2\mid\text{condition})=\dfrac{P(\text{a }2\text{ and condition})}{P(\text{condition})}=\dfrac{\dfrac{8}{216}}{\dfrac{15}{216}}$; the factor $\dfrac{1}{216}$ cancels, so the count above is exactly that computation.)
