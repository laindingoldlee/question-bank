Their areas are 6, $\dfrac{15\sqrt7}{4}$, and $\dfrac{9\sqrt{943}}{64}$ respectively. Since $\sqrt7>\dfrac{264}{100}$ and $\sqrt{943}>30$, the total area exceeds $6+9.9+\dfrac{270}{64}>20$. Thus an integer rectangle area is at least 21. The 4-5-6 triangle alone cannot fit in a strip of width 3: its diameter is 6, so its span parallel to the strip is at most 6; a triangle in a rectangle of sides $w,L$ has area at most $\dfrac{1}{2}wL$, so a width 3 strip would force its area at most 9, whereas $\dfrac{15\sqrt7}{4}>9$. (The half-rectangle bound follows by maximizing the determinant separately in each vertex coordinate, so a maximum occurs at rectangle corners.) Every integer-sided rectangle of area 21, 22, or 23 has its shorter side at most 3. Therefore all these areas are impossible, proving the bound $\boxed{24}$.
