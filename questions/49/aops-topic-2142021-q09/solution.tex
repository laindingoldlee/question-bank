Let $N=ac$. The roots are rational exactly when the discriminant $b^2-4N$ is the square of an integer $m\ge0$. Then
\[(b-m)(b+m)=4N .\]
The two factors have the same parity and an even product, so both are even: $b-m=2d$ and $b+m=2e$ with $de=N$ and $0<d\le e$, and $b=d+e$. Conversely, every factorisation $N=de$ with $d\le e$ gives such a $b$ (with $m=e-d$).

So the good values of $b$ are the numbers $d+\dfrac {N}{d}$ for the divisors $d\le\sqrt N$. These are all different, because $d+\dfrac {N}{d}$ strictly decreases as $d$ increases up to $\sqrt N$. If $N$ has $k$ divisors, there are $\left\lceil\dfrac {k}{2}\right\rceil$ divisors $d\le\sqrt N$, so there are exactly $13$ good values of $b$ when $\left\lceil\dfrac {k}{2}\right\rceil=13$, that is, $k=25$ or $k=26$.

Both happen: $a=1$, $c=1296=2^4\cdot3^4$ gives $k=25$, and $a=1$, $c=12288=2^{12}\cdot3$ gives $k=26$. The sum is $25+26=\boxed{51}$.
