Let $\dfrac{n}{20-n}=k^2$ with $k\ge0$ an integer ($n\ne20$). Then $n=20k^2-nk^2$, so
\[
n=\dfrac{20k^2}{k^2+1}.
\]
Since $\gcd(k^2,k^2+1)=1$, we need $k^2+1\mid20$, i.e.\ $k^2+1\in\{1,2,4,5,10,20\}$, so $k^2\in\{0,1,3,4,9,19\}$, and $k^2$ must be a perfect square: $k^2\in\{0,1,4,9\}$. These give
\[
n=0,\quad n=10,\quad n=16,\quad n=18,
\]
and indeed $\dfrac{0}{20}=0^2$, $\dfrac{10}{10}=1^2$, $\dfrac{16}{4}=2^2$, $\dfrac{18}{2}=3^2$. There are $\boxed{4}$ such integers.
