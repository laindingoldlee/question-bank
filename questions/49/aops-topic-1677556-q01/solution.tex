\textbf{(a)} Let $r=\dfrac {a}{b}=\dfrac {c}{d}$. Then $a=rb$ and $c=rd$, so
\[
a+kc=rb+krd=r(b+kd),\qquad\text{hence}\qquad\dfrac{a+kc}{b+kd}=r=\dfrac {a}{b}
\]
whenever $b+kd\ne0$. Taking $k=1$ and $k=-1$ gives $\dfrac{a+c}{b+d}=\dfrac {a}{b}$ and $\dfrac{a-c}{b-d}=\dfrac {a}{b}$.

\textbf{(b)} The first equality just factors $a$ out of the numerator and $b$ out of the denominator. In the second, the fraction $\dfrac{a}{b}$ is split off, and in the denominator $\dfrac {d}{b}$ has been replaced by $\dfrac {c}{a}$. This is allowed because the two are equal: cross-multiplying $\dfrac {a}{b}=\dfrac {c}{d}$ gives $ad=bc$, and dividing by $ab$ (nonzero, as $a\ne0$ and $b\ne0$) gives $\dfrac {d}{b}=\dfrac {c}{a}$. After the replacement the numerator and the denominator of the second fraction are the same nonzero number $1+k\cdot\dfrac {c}{a}$ (nonzero because it equals $\dfrac{b+kd}{b}$), so that fraction is $1$. $\blacksquare$

(The proof in (a) avoids the extra assumption $a\ne0$ needed in (b).)
