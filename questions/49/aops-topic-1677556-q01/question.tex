Let $a,b,c,d$ be real numbers with $b\ne0$, $d\ne0$ and $\dfrac {a}{b}=\dfrac {c}{d}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Prove that $\dfrac{a+kc}{b+kd}=\dfrac {a}{b}$ for every real number $k$ with $b+kd\ne0$. Deduce that $\dfrac {a}{b}=\dfrac{a+c}{b+d}=\dfrac{a-c}{b-d}$ whenever the denominators are nonzero.
\item[(b)] A textbook proves (a), when $a\ne0$, by writing
\[
\dfrac{a+kc}{b+kd}=\dfrac{a\left(1+k\cdot\dfrac {c}{a}\right)}{b\left(1+k\cdot\dfrac {d}{b}\right)}=\dfrac {a}{b}\cdot\dfrac{1+k\cdot\dfrac {c}{a}}{1+k\cdot\dfrac {c}{a}}=\dfrac {a}{b} .
\]
Explain why the middle equality holds.
\end{enumerate}
