Let the pirate ship be at $P$, the caravel at $C$ with $PC=10$, and suppose the pirate, sailing in a straight line at speed $v$, meets the caravel at $I$ after $t$ hours. Then $CI=12t$, $PI=vt$, and the angle at $C$ between $CP$ (due west) and the caravel's course is $\angle PCI=60^\circ$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
path ra(pair P,pair u,pair v,real s=0.2){return P+s*unit(u)--P+s*unit(u)+s*unit(v)--P+s*unit(v);}
pair P=(0,0), C=(10,0), I=(0,-10*sqrt(3));
draw(P--C,linewidth(0.9)); draw(C--I,blue+linewidth(0.9),Arrow(6)); draw(P--I,red+dashed,Arrow(6));
draw(ra(P,C-P,I-P,0.9));
draw(arc(C,2.2,180,240));
label("$60^\circ$",C+3.5*dir(207),fontsize(9pt));
dot(P); dot(C); dot(I);
label("pirate $P$",P,NW); label("caravel $C$",C,NE); label("$I$",I,S);
label("$10$",(P+C)/2,N); label("$12t$",(C+I)/2,E,blue); label("$vt$",(P+I)/2,W,red);
draw((12.5,-7)--(12.5,-5),Arrow(5)); label("N",(12.5,-5),N,fontsize(8pt));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

By the law of cosines in triangle $PCI$,
\[
(vt)^2=10^2+(12t)^2-2\cdot10\cdot12t\cos60^\circ=100+144t^2-120t .
\]
Divide by $t^2$ and put $s=\dfrac{1}{t}>0$:
\[
v^2=100s^2-120s+144=100\left(s-\dfrac{3}{5}\right)^2+144-36=100\left(s-\dfrac{3}{5}\right)^2+108 .
\]
So $v^2\ge108$ for every possible meeting time, with equality when $s=\dfrac{3}{5}$, i.e. $t=\dfrac{5}{3}$ hours. Hence the pirate needs at least
\[
v_{\min}=\sqrt{108}=\boxed{6\sqrt3\approx10.4\text{ nautical miles per hour}},
\]
and at exactly this speed it can catch the caravel, by heading due south: the meeting point is $I$ with $CI=12\cdot\dfrac{5}{3}=20$ and $PI=6\sqrt3\cdot\dfrac{5}{3}=10\sqrt3$, so $PC^2+PI^2=100+300=400=CI^2$ and the angle at $P$ is a right angle. (In general the minimum speed is the caravel's speed times the sine of the angle between its course and the line from the caravel to the pirate: $12\sin60^\circ=6\sqrt3$.)
