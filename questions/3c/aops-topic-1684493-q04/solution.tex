Count the largest possible number of intersections for each kind of pair:
\begin{itemize}
\item two lines meet in at most $1$ point, and there are $\binom62=15$ pairs of lines: at most $15$ points;
\item a line and a circle meet in at most $2$ points, and there are $6\cdot2=12$ line-circle pairs: at most $24$ points;
\item the two circles meet in at most $2$ points.
\end{itemize}
So there are at most $15+24+2=41$ intersection points. This is attained when no two lines are parallel, each line cuts both circles, the two circles meet in two points, and no point lies on three of the figures (a small generic perturbation of any such arrangement removes coincidences, so all $41$ points are different). The maximum is $\boxed{41}$.
