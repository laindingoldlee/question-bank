Every rational in $[0,1]$ has a unique reduced representation $\dfrac{h}{d}$, with $d\ge1$. It is representable with a permitted denominator exactly when $d$ divides at least one $2^b-2^c$, $0\le c<b\le10$. For each $d>1$ there are $\varphi(d)$ fractions in $(0,1)$, and denominator 1 contributes the endpoints. Thus the answer is $1+\sum_{d\in D}\varphi(d)$, where $D=\bigcup_{0\le c<b\le10}\{d:d\mid2^b-2^c\}$. This finite computation gives $8351$, hence remainder $\boxed{351}$. To reproduce the count without duplicate fractions, initialise an empty set $D$; for each of the 55 pairs $(b,c)$ insert every divisor of $2^b-2^c$; then sum $\varphi (d) = d \prod_{p\mid d} (1 - \dfrac{1}{p})$ over that set and add one. The divisor-set method counts each reduced fraction exactly once.
