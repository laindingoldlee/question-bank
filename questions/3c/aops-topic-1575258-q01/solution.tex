Compare triangles $AMN$ and $ABC$. They share the angle at $A$, and
\[
\dfrac{AM}{AB}=\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{AN}{AC},
\]
because $M$ and $N$ are midpoints. By the SAS similarity criterion, $\triangle AMN\sim\triangle ABC$ with ratio $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Corresponding sides are in the same ratio, so
\[
\dfrac{MN}{BC}=\dfrac{1}{2},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad BC=2MN .
\]
Moreover, corresponding angles are equal: $\angle AMN=\angle ABC$. These are corresponding angles for the lines $MN$ and $BC$ cut by the transversal $AB$, so $MN\parallel BC$. $\blacksquare$
