\textbf{A necessary count.} There are $9\cdot5=45$ incidences between points and lines. If every point were on exactly $2$ lines there would be $22.5$ points, so some points must lie on $3$ lines. With $x$ points on $3$ lines and $y$ points on $2$ lines, $x+y=19$ and $3x+2y=45$ give $x=7$, $y=12$. This suggests a figure with a lot of symmetry.

\textbf{Construction.} Start with a regular hexagram with tips $T_0,\dots,T_5$ (equally spaced on a circle with centre $O$). Its $6$ side lines are $T_kT_{k+2}$ (blue). Add the $3$ lines $T_0T_3$, $T_1T_4$, $T_2T_5$ through opposite tips (red); they pass through $O$. The $19$ points are
\begin{itemize}
\item the $6$ tips $T_k$,
\item the $6$ vertices of the inner hexagon (crossings of the blue lines),
\item the midpoints of the $6$ sides $T_kT_{k+2}$ of the two big triangles,
\item the centre $O$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
int n=6; pair[] T; for(int k=0;k<n;++k) T.push(dir(90+360k/n));
pair[] I; for(int k=0;k<n;++k) I.push(extension(T[k],T[(k+2)%n],T[(k+1)%n],T[(k+3)%n]));
for(int k=0;k<n;++k){ pair u=T[k],v=T[(k+2)%n]; draw(u+0.1*(u-v)--v+0.1*(v-u),blue+linewidth(0.8)); }
pair O=(0,0); pair[] M; for(int k=0;k<n;++k) M.push((T[k]+T[(k+2)%n])/2);
for(int k=0;k<3;++k){ pair u=T[k],v=T[k+3]; draw(u+0.1*(u-v)--v+0.1*(v-u),red+linewidth(0.8)); }
for(int k=0;k<n;++k) dot(M[k],linewidth(4.5)); dot(O,linewidth(4.5));
for(int k=0;k<n;++k){ dot(T[k],linewidth(4.5)); dot(I[k],linewidth(4.5)); }
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\textbf{Check.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A blue line $T_kT_{k+2}$ contains its $2$ tips, the $2$ inner hexagon vertices where the other triangle crosses it, and its own midpoint: $5$ points. (It meets the red lines at $T_k$, $T_{k+2}$ and the midpoint, and the parallel blue line not at all.)
\item The red line $T_kT_{k+3}$ contains $T_k$, $T_{k+3}$ and $O$. The side of each big triangle opposite to $T_k$ or $T_{k+3}$ is perpendicular to this line, and the line meets it at its midpoint. This gives $2$ more points, $5$ in all. It contains no vertex of the inner hexagon, because those lie at angles halfway between the directions of the tips.
\end{itemize}
So each of the $9$ lines has exactly $5$ of the $19$ points. As predicted, the $6$ tips and $O$ lie on $3$ lines each, and the other $12$ points lie on $2$ lines each.

(Read literally, ``the points determine exactly $9$ lines'' is impossible: any two of the points determine a line, and $9$ lines with $5$ points each cover only $9\cdot\dbinom{5}{2}=90$ of the $\dbinom{19}{2}=171$ pairs of points. So, as in the classical tree-planting puzzles, we only require the stated lines; other lines through two of the points are not counted.)
