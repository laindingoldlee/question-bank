Suppose $u^2+v^2=3$ with $u,v$ rational. Write $u=\dfrac {a}{c}$ and $v=\dfrac {b}{c}$ with integers $a,b$ and a positive integer $c$ (a common denominator). Then
\[
a^2+b^2=3c^2 .
\]
Among all such integer solutions with $c>0$ choose one with $c$ as small as possible.

\textbf{Squares modulo $3$.} Every square is $\equiv0$ or $1\pmod3$ (as $0^2\equiv0$ and $(\pm1)^2\equiv1$), and it is $\equiv0$ only for multiples of $3$. Since $a^2+b^2\equiv0\pmod3$, the possibilities $0+1$, $1+0$, $1+1$ are excluded, so $a^2\equiv b^2\equiv0$, i.e.\ $3\mid a$ and $3\mid b$.

\textbf{Descent.} Write $a=3a'$, $b=3b'$. Then $9(a'^2+b'^2)=3c^2$, so $c^2=3(a'^2+b'^2)$ is divisible by $3$ and therefore $c=3c'$ with $0<c'<c$. Dividing by $9$ gives
\[
a'^2+b'^2=3c'^2 ,
\]
another solution with a smaller positive $c'$, contradicting the choice of $c$. Hence no rational $u,v$ exist. $\blacksquare$
