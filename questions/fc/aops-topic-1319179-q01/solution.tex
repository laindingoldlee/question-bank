Let $AH$ be the altitude from $A$ in triangle $ABC$, with $H$ on line $BC$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
path ra(pair P,pair u,pair v,real s=0.2){return P+s*unit(u)--P+s*unit(u)+s*unit(v)--P+s*unit(v);}
pair A=(0.8,3),B=(0,0),C=(4,0),H=(0.8,0); draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9)); draw(A--H,red+dashed); draw(ra(H,A-H,C-H,0.2));
label("$A$",A,N); label("$B$",B,SW); label("$C$",C,SE); label("$H$",H,S);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

If $H\ne B$, triangle $AHB$ has a right angle at $H$, so $AB$ is its hypotenuse and $AB>AH$ (the hypotenuse is opposite the largest angle, or by Pythagoras $AB^2=AH^2+HB^2>AH^2$). If $H=B$ (right angle at $B$), then $AH=AB$. The same argument applies to $AC$. So the altitude from $A$ is never longer than $AB$ or $AC$, and is strictly shorter unless the triangle has a right angle at $B$ or $C$. $\blacksquare$
