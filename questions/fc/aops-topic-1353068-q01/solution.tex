Since $MK\parallel AC$, the corresponding angles at $M$ and $A$ are equal, and triangles $MBK$ and $ABC$ share the angle at $B$. So triangle $MBK$ is similar to triangle $ABC$, with ratio
\[
\dfrac{MK}{AC}=\dfrac{9}{18}=\dfrac{1}{2} .
\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(7cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair B=(0,0),C=(12,0),A=(-3.3333,9.4281);
pair M=(A+B)/2,K=(B+C)/2;
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(M--K,blue+linewidth(0.9));
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);dot(M);dot(K);
label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,SW);label("$C$",C,SE);label("$M$",M,W);label("$K$",K,S);
label("$5$",(M+B)/2,W);label("$x$",(A+M)/2,W);
label("$6$",(B+K)/2,S);label("$y$",(K+C)/2,S);
label("$18$",(A+C)/2,NE);label("$9$",(M+K)/2+(0.25,0.35),blue);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Hence $BA=2\cdot BM=10$ and $BC=2\cdot BK=12$, so $x=10-5=5$ and $y=12-6=6$: the points $M$ and $K$ are the midpoints of $AB$ and $BC$, and $MK$ is the midline. Therefore
\[
x+y=\boxed{11}.
\]
Such a triangle exists: its sides are $10$, $12$, $18$, which satisfy the triangle inequality.
