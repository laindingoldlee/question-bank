\textbf{Trailing zeros.} The exponent of $5$ in $2021!$ is
\[\left\lfloor\dfrac{2021}{5}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\dfrac{2021}{25}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\dfrac{2021}{125}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\dfrac{2021}{625}\right\rfloor=404+80+16+3=503,\]
and the exponent of $2$ is larger, so $2021!$ ends in exactly $503$ zeros. Let $M=\dfrac{2021!}{10^{503}}$; we want $M\bmod 10$. Since $M$ still contains factors of $2$, $M$ is even, so it suffices to find $M\bmod5$.

\textbf{A recursion modulo $5$.} For $n\ge0$ let $n!_5$ denote $n!$ with all factors $5$ removed, i.e. $\dfrac{n!}{5^{v}}$ where $5^v$ is the highest power of $5$ dividing $n!$. Splitting $1,\dots,n$ into the numbers not divisible by $5$ and the multiples $5j$ ($j\le\left\lfloor\dfrac n5\right\rfloor$),
\[n!_5=\Bigl(\prod_{i\le n,\ 5\nmid i}i\Bigr)\cdot\left\lfloor\dfrac n5\right\rfloor!_5.\]
Modulo $5$, each complete block of five consecutive integers contributes $1\cdot2\cdot3\cdot4=24\equiv-1$ to the first product, and the final incomplete block contributes $r!$ where $r=n\bmod5$. So
\[n!_5\equiv(-1)^{\left\lfloor\dfrac n5\right\rfloor}\,r!\,\left\lfloor\dfrac n5\right\rfloor!_5\pmod5.\]
Applying this repeatedly ($2021=5\cdot404+1$, $404=5\cdot80+4$, $80=5\cdot16$, $16=5\cdot3+1$):
\[3!_5=6\equiv1,\quad 16!_5\equiv(-1)^3\cdot1!\cdot1=-1,\quad 80!_5\equiv(-1)^{16}\cdot0!\cdot(-1)=-1,\]
\[404!_5\equiv(-1)^{80}\cdot4!\cdot(-1)=-24\equiv1,\quad 2021!_5\equiv(-1)^{404}\cdot1!\cdot1=1\pmod5.\]

\textbf{Finishing.} $M=\dfrac{2021!_5}{2^{503}}$, so $2^{503}M=2021!_5\equiv1\pmod5$. Since $2^4=16\equiv1\pmod5$, $2^{503}\equiv2^3=8\equiv3$, so $3M\equiv1$ and $M\equiv2\pmod5$ (as $3\cdot2=6\equiv1$). The last digit of $M$ is even and $\equiv2\pmod5$, so it is $\boxed{2}$.
