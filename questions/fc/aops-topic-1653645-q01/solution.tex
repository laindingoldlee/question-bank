Let there be $g$ girls and $2g$ boys, $3g$ players in all. Every game has exactly one winner, so the total number of wins is the number of games, $\dbinom{3g}{2}=\dfrac{3g(3g-1)}{2}$. The girls won $\dfrac{7}{12}$ of them:
\[
W_g=\dfrac{7}{12}\cdot\dfrac{3g(3g-1)}{2}=\dfrac{7g(3g-1)}{8}.
\]
\textbf{Upper bound.} Girls can win only the games among themselves, $\dbinom g2$, and the $g\cdot2g$ games against boys, so
\[
\dfrac{7g(3g-1)}{8}\le\dfrac{g(g-1)}{2}+2g^2=\dfrac{g(5g-1)}{2}\iff 7(3g-1)\le4(5g-1)\iff g\le3 .
\]
\textbf{Integrality.} $W_g$ must be an integer: for $g=1$ and $g=2$, $\dfrac{7g(3g-1)}{8}=\dfrac{14}{8}$ and $\dfrac{70}{8}$ are not integers, while $g=3$ gives $W_g=21$.

So $g=3$ and there were $\boxed{6}$ boys. This is possible: with $9$ players there are $36$ games; if the girls win every game they play ($3$ among themselves and $18$ against boys) they have $21$ wins and the boys $15$, and $21:15=7:5$.
