Let $\ell$ touch the circle (centre $O$, radius $r$) at $T$. Every other point $Q\ne T$ of $\ell$ lies outside the circle, so $OQ>r=OT$. Hence $T$ is the point of $\ell$ closest to $O$.
\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair O=(0,0),T=dir(90),Q=T+(1.2,0);draw(circle(O,1));draw(T-(1.5,0)--T+(1.5,0));draw(O--T);draw(O--Q,dashed);dot(O);dot(T);dot(Q);
label("$O$",O,S);label("$T$",T,N);label("$Q$",Q,N);label("$\ell$",T+(1.5,0),E);
\end{asy}

If $OT$ were not perpendicular to $\ell$, the foot $F$ of the perpendicular from $O$ would satisfy $OF<OT$ (a leg is shorter than the hypotenuse of right triangle $OFT$), which is a contradiction. So $OT\perp\ell$. $\blacksquare$
