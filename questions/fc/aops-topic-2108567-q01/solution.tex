Let $N=1^9+9^9+10^9+12^9$. The key observation is Ramanujan's taxicab number:
\[1^3+12^3=9^3+10^3=1729=7\cdot13\cdot19 .\]
Since $u^3+v^3$ divides $(u^3)^3+(v^3)^3=u^9+v^9$, both $1^9+12^9$ and $9^9+10^9$ are multiples of $1729$:
\[1^9+12^9=1729\left(1-1728+1728^2\right)=1729\cdot2984257,\]
\[9^9+10^9=1729\left(729^2-729\cdot1000+1000^2\right)=1729\cdot802441 .\]
Adding,
\[N=1729\,(2984257+802441)=1729\cdot3786698=2\cdot7\cdot13\cdot19\cdot1893349 .\]
Now $1893349$ has no prime factor less than $23$: it is odd, its digit sum $37$ is not a multiple of $3$, it does not end in $0$ or $5$, and dividing it by $7$, $11$, $13$, $17$ and $19$ leaves remainders $3$, $7$, $3$, $8$ and $18$. Since $N$ has exactly $5$ distinct prime factors, $1893349$ must be a power $p^e$ of a single prime $p\ge23$. As $23^5>1893349$, we have $e\le4$; but $1893349$ is not a perfect square ($1375^2=1890625<1893349<1893376=1376^2$) and not a perfect cube ($123^3=1860867<1893349<1906624=124^3$). So $e=1$, and the largest prime factor is $1893349$. The remainder on division by $1000$ is $\boxed{349}$.
