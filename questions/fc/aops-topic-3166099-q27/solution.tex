The property passes to every prime-power factor of $n$: any unit modulo one such factor can be extended to a unit modulo $n$ by the Chinese remainder theorem. For an odd prime $p\mid n$, every nonzero residue would be a root of $x^2-1$ modulo $p$. A quadratic over a field has at most two roots, so $p-1\le2$, forcing $p=3$. The power of $3$ cannot exceed one because $2^2-1=3$ is not divisible by $9$. The power of $2$ cannot exceed three because $3^2-1=8$ is not divisible by $16$. Hence $n\mid24$. Conversely, every unit modulo $24$ is odd and not divisible by $3$. Its square is $1$ modulo $8$ (the neighboring even integers contain factors totaling at least three twos), and is $1$ modulo $3$. Therefore its square is $1$ modulo $24$, so the maximum is $\boxed{24}$.
