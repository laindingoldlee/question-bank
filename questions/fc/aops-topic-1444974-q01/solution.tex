Divisibility by both $4$ and $6$ means divisibility by $12$, that is, by $3$ and by $4$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{By $4$:} the last two digits must form a multiple of $4$; of $44,46,64,66$ only $44$ and $64$ qualify.
\item \emph{By $3$:} the digit sum must be a multiple of $3$. With $f$ fours and $s$ sixes the digit sum is $4f+6s$, so we need $3\mid f$.
\end{itemize}
Using both digits and $3\mid f$ requires $f\ge3$ and $s\ge1$, so at least $4$ digits. With exactly $4$ digits, $f=3$ and $s=1$, and the number ends in $44$ or $64$: the candidates are $4464$, $4644$ and $6444$. So the smallest is $\boxed{4464}$ and the second smallest is $\boxed{4644}$.

(If numbers using only the digit $4$ were also allowed, the smallest would be $444=12\cdot37$ and the second $4464$.)
