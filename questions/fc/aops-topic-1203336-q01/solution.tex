Squares modulo $5$ are $0$, $1$ or $4$, and a square is $0$ modulo $5$ only for multiples of $5$. Suppose none of $a,b,c$ is divisible by $5$. Then $a^2,b^2\in\{1,4\}$ modulo $5$, so $a^2+b^2\equiv2,0$ or $3\pmod5$ ($1+1$, $1+4$, $4+4$). It cannot be $0$, since $c^2\not\equiv0$; and $2,3$ are not squares modulo $5$. Contradiction.
