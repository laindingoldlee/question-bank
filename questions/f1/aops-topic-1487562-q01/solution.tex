By the binomial theorem, $\displaystyle\sum_{a=0}^{P-1}\dbinom{P-1}{a}n^{P-1-a}k^a=(n+k)^{P-1}$. So the numerator is
\[
(n+k)^{P-1}-(n+k)^{P-1}+\sum_{a=0}^{P-1}n^{P-1-a}k^{a}=\sum_{a=0}^{P-1}n^{P-1-a}k^{a}=\dfrac{n^P-k^P}{n-k},
\]
a geometric sum (here $n\ne k$). Hence the left side equals
\[
\dfrac{n^P-k^P}{(n-k)\,n^P}=\dfrac{1}{n-k}\left(1-\left(\dfrac{k}{n}\right)^{P}\right).
\]
Since $0<k<n$, the bracket $1-\left(\dfrac{k}{n}\right)^P$ is positive, so the left side equals the right side if and only if $n-k=1$.

So the formula is correct exactly when $\boxed{n=k+1}$, which includes the die case $n=6$, $k=5$; for example, with $n=6$, $k=4$, $P=1$ the left side is $\dfrac{1}{6}$ but the right side is $\dfrac{1}{3}$. (Also, $1-\left(\dfrac{5}{6}\right)^2$ is the probability of getting \emph{at least one} six in two rolls, not of getting no six as the post says.)
