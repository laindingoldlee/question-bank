\textbf{(b)} Roll $25$ times a die with six faces marked $6$, $1.5$, $0$, $0$, $0$, $0$. An outcome with $a$ sixes, $b$ faces $1.5$ and $c$ zeros can occur in $\dfrac{25!}{a!b!c!}\cdot4^c$ ways (choose the positions, then which of the four zero-faces). So $f(n)$ is the number of the $6^{25}$ outcomes whose total is $n$; equivalently, $f(n)$ is the coefficient of $t^n$ in $\left(t^6+t^{1.5}+4\right)^{25}$.

\textbf{(c)} Every total is $6a+1.5b=1.5(4a+b)$ with $0\le4a+b\le100$, so the sum over $j$ counts every outcome exactly once: $6^{25}$. (Equivalently, put $t=1$ in the multinomial expansion.)

\textbf{(a)} For $n=135$: $4a+b=90$ with $a+b\le25$ forces $a=22$, $b=2$, $c=1$, so $f(135)=\dfrac{25!}{22!\,2!\,1!}\cdot4=27600$. For $n=139.5$: $4a+b=93$ forces $a=23$, $b=1$, $c=1$, so $f(139.5)=\dfrac{25!}{23!}\cdot4=2400$. The ratio is $\dfrac{23}{2}$, and $m(n+1)=23\cdot3=\boxed{69}$.
