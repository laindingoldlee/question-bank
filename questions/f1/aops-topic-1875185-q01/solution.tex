A lucky number has an even digit sum. If $n$ does not end in $9$, the digit sum of $n+1$ is one more than that of $n$, so they cannot both be even. If $n$ ends in exactly two $9$s, the digit sum drops by $18$ and rises by $1$, again changing parity. So $n$ ends in a single $9$ (preceded by a digit $c\ne9$) or in $999$; the smallest candidate ending in $999$ is $1999$, so we first look for $n=\overline{1bc9}$ with $c\le8$. Then $n+1=\overline{1b(c+1)0}$.

For $n$: the digit sum $10+b+c$ is even, and the group not containing the $9$ is part of $\{1,b,c\}$ and must have half the sum, so $1+b+c\ge\dfrac{10+b+c}{2}$, i.e. $b+c\ge8$, with $b+c$ even.
\begin{itemize}
\item $b=0$: $c=8$, $n=1089$ is lucky ($9=1+0+8$), but $1090$ has digit sum $10$ and no group of $\{1,0,9,0\}$ sums to $5$.
\item $b=1$: $c=7$, $n=1179$ is lucky, but no group of $\{1,1,8,0\}$ sums to $5$.
\item $b=2$: $c=6$ or $8$. Then $n+1=1270$ (half sum $5$) or $1290$ (half sum $6$): no group of $\{1,2,7\}$ sums to $5$, and none of $\{1,2,9\}$ sums to $6$.
\item $b=3$: $c=5$ or $7$. Then $n+1=1360$ (half sum $5$) or $1380$ (half sum $6$): no group of $\{1,3,6\}$ sums to $5$, and none of $\{1,3,8\}$ sums to $6$.
\item $b=4$: the smallest choice is $c=4$, $n=1449$: here $9=1+4+4$, and $n+1=1450$ has $5=1+4$.
\end{itemize}
So the answer is $\boxed{1449}$.
