Work modulo $5$. Since $7\equiv2$ and $9\equiv4\equiv-1$, the four numbers are congruent to
\[2^n,\quad 3^n,\quad 2^n,\quad (-1)^n \pmod 5.\]
The powers of $2$ and $3$ modulo $5$ repeat with period $4$ ($2,4,3,1$ and $3,4,2,1$), and $(-1)^n$ has period $2$. A sum of three of the four numbers equals the total $T$ minus the omitted number, so list $T$ and the four possible sums by $n\bmod4$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|cccc|c|c}
$n\bmod 4$ & $2^n$ & $3^n$ & $7^n$ & $9^n$ & $T$ & $T-(\text{omitted})$\\\hline
$1$ & $2$ & $3$ & $2$ & $4$ & $11\equiv1$ & $4,\ 3,\ 4,\ 2$\\
$2$ & $4$ & $4$ & $4$ & $1$ & $13\equiv3$ & $4,\ 4,\ 4,\ 2$\\
$3$ & $3$ & $2$ & $3$ & $4$ & $12\equiv2$ & $4,\ 0,\ 4,\ 3$\\
$0$ & $1$ & $1$ & $1$ & $1$ & $4$ & $3,\ 3,\ 3,\ 3$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
(The last column is taken modulo $5$, omitting $2^n,3^n,7^n,9^n$ in turn.) A sum divisible by $5$ occurs only when $n\equiv3\pmod4$, namely $2^n+7^n+9^n$ (omitting $3^n$): then $2^n+7^n+9^n\equiv3+3+4=10\equiv0\pmod5$.

Hence the answer is all $n$ with $\boxed{n\equiv3\pmod 4}$, i.e. $n=3,7,11,\dots$, and the suitable three numbers are $2^n,7^n,9^n$.
