A number containing both five and an even digit cannot be divisible by all its digits: divisibility by five forces its last digit to be five, contradicting evenness. Thus nine digits are impossible. An eight-digit candidate must omit five, but its digit sum is 40 and it contains three, again impossible. Search seven-digit permutations in decreasing lexicographic order and test divisibility by each digit. The first success is 9867312, so it is the largest interesting number. Its factorisation is $2^4\cdot3^3\cdot7\cdot13\cdot251$. Therefore the largest prime factor is $\boxed{251}$. The exact search is included in the computation audit.
