Both $31+71+111$ and $111+71+31$ equal $213$, so they cancel and the product is
\[\dfrac{5+15+25}{1+3+5}=\dfrac{45}{9}=\boxed{5}.\]
The student's last step splits one fraction into three, as if $\dfrac{a+b+c}{d+e+f}$ were $\dfrac {a}{d}+\dfrac {b}{e}+\dfrac {c}{f}$. This is false: the fraction bar means that the \emph{whole} numerator is divided by the \emph{whole} denominator, so the sums must be found first. For instance $\dfrac{1+1}{1+1}=1$, but $\dfrac{1}{1}+\dfrac{1}{1}=2$. (A fraction may be split only over its numerator: $\dfrac{a+b+c}{d}=\dfrac {a}{d}+\dfrac {b}{d}+\dfrac {c}{d}$.)
