The amount of water does not change, so the volumes are equal. With sphere radius $r$ and hemisphere radius $R$,
\[
\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3\quad\Longrightarrow\quad R^3=2r^3\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\dfrac {r}{R}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]2}.
\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real r=1, R=2^(1/3);
draw(circle((0,0),r),blue+linewidth(0.9)); dot((0,0));
draw((0,0)--(r,0),dashed); label("$r$",(r/2,0),N);
pair o=(3.4,-1);
draw(arc(o,R,0,180)--cycle,red+linewidth(0.9)); dot(o);
draw(o--o+R*dir(60),dashed); label("$R$",o+0.55*R*dir(60),NW);
label("sphere",(0,-1.35)); label("hemisphere",o+(0,-0.4));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Rationalising, $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]2}=\dfrac{\sqrt[3]4}{\sqrt[3]8}=\boxed{\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{4}}{2}}$.
