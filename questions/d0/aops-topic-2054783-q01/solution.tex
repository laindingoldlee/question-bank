The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality states that $(x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2)(y_1^2+y_2^2+y_3^2)\ge(x_1y_1+x_2y_2+x_3y_3)^2$ for real numbers. Take $x_1=\sqrt a$, $x_2=\sqrt b$, $x_3=\sqrt c$ and $y_1=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt a}$, $y_2=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt b}$, $y_3=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt c}$, which are real because $a,b,c>0$. Then $x_iy_i=1$ for each $i$, and
\[(a+b+c)\left(\dfrac{1}{a}+\dfrac{1}{b}+\dfrac{1}{c}\right)\ge(1+1+1)^2=9 .\]
Equality holds when $(x_1,x_2,x_3)$ is proportional to $(y_1,y_2,y_3)$, i.e.\ $\sqrt a\cdot\sqrt a=\sqrt b\cdot\sqrt b=\sqrt c\cdot\sqrt c$, which means $a=b=c$. $\blacksquare$
