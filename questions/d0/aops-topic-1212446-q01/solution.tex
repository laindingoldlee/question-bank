Let the roots be $r$ and $r^2$. By Vieta,
\[r+r^2=\dfrac{15}{4},\qquad r\cdot r^2=r^3=a^3.\]
If $r$ is real, then $a=r$ (cube roots of reals are unique), and $4r^2+4r-15=0$ gives $(2r-3)(2r+5)=0$, so $r=\dfrac{3}{2}$ or $r=-\dfrac{5}{2}$.

If $r$ were not real, then $r$ and $r^2$, the two roots of a quadratic with real coefficients, would be complex conjugates: $r^2=\bar r$. Taking absolute values, $|r|^2=|r|$, so $|r|=1$, and then $r+\bar r=2\operatorname{Re}r\le2<\dfrac{15}{4}$, a contradiction.

So $a=\boxed{\dfrac{3}{2}\ \text{or}\ -\dfrac{5}{2}}$. Check: $a=\dfrac{3}{2}$ gives roots $\dfrac{3}{2},\dfrac{9}{4}$ (sum $\dfrac{15}{4}$, product $\dfrac{27}{8}=a^3$); $a=-\dfrac{5}{2}$ gives roots $-\dfrac{5}{2},\dfrac{25}{4}$ (sum $\dfrac{15}{4}$, product $-\dfrac{125}{8}=a^3$).
