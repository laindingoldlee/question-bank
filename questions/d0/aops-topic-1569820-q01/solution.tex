There are $n!$ arrangements in all. Group them by the positions of the other $n-3$ people and by the set of three chairs used by Anna, Bob and Carlos. Within each group, Anna, Bob and Carlos can occupy those three chairs in $3!=6$ ways, and Carlos is in the middle chair in exactly $2$ of them (Anna left and Bob right, or the reverse). So exactly $\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{1}{3}$ of all arrangements are good, and the answer is
\[
\boxed{\dfrac{n!}{3}}.
\]
(For example, $n=3$ gives $2$ arrangements: $ACB$ and $BCA$.)
