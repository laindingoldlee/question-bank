\textbf{(a)} Let the first triangle $T$ have a side $b$ and corresponding altitude $h$, and let $T'$ be the similar triangle. The corresponding side of $T'$ is $kb$. The altitude to it is $kh$. Indeed, let $a$ be another side of $T$ and $\theta$ the angle of $T$ between the sides $a$ and $b$. The altitude $h$ to $b$ is a leg of a right triangle with hypotenuse $a$ and an angle $\theta$ (or $180^\circ-\theta$, if $\theta$ is obtuse and the foot of the altitude lies outside $b$); since $\sin\left(180^\circ-\theta\right)=\sin\theta$, in either case $h=a\sin\theta$. In $T'$ the corresponding side is $ka$ and the corresponding angle is again $\theta$, because similar triangles have equal corresponding angles, so the altitude is $ka\sin\theta=kh$. Hence
\[[T']=\dfrac12(kb)(kh)=k^2\cdot\dfrac12bh=k^2[T].\ \blacksquare\]

\textbf{(b)} Triangle $ABX$ is similar to triangle $BCX$ with ratio $k=\dfrac{AB}{BC}$, so by (a), $\dfrac{[ABX]}{[BCX]}=k^2=\dfrac12$. Hence
\[\dfrac{AB}{BC}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}=\boxed{\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}}.\]
