Let $g(x)=xf(x)-1$. Then $g$ is a polynomial of degree at most $n$ with roots $1,2,\dots,n$, so
\[g(x)=c(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-n)\]
for a constant $c$. At $x=0$: $g(0)=-1=c(-1)^nn!$, so $c=\dfrac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n!}$. At $x=n+1$:
\[(n+1)f(n+1)-1=g(n+1)=c\cdot n!=(-1)^{n+1}.\]
Hence
\[f(n+1)=\boxed{\dfrac{1+(-1)^{n+1}}{n+1}},\]
which is $\dfrac{2}{n+1}$ for odd $n$ and $0$ for even $n$. (For example, $n=2$: $f(x)=\dfrac{3-x}{2}$ and $f(3)=0$.)
