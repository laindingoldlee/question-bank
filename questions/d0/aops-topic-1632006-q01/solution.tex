Recall that a point $P$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of a segment $XY$ if and only if $PX=PY$.

\textbf{If the polygon is inscribed.} Suppose all vertices lie on a circle with centre $O$ and radius $r$. Then $OA_1=OA_2=\cdots=OA_n=r$. For each side $A_iA_{i+1}$ (indices modulo $n$) we have $OA_i=OA_{i+1}$, so $O$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of that side. Hence all $n$ perpendicular bisectors pass through $O$.

\textbf{If the bisectors are concurrent.} Suppose all perpendicular bisectors pass through a point $O$. Since $O$ is on the bisector of $A_1A_2$, $OA_1=OA_2$; since it is on the bisector of $A_2A_3$, $OA_2=OA_3$; and so on around the polygon. Thus $OA_1=OA_2=\cdots=OA_n$, and the circle with centre $O$ and radius $OA_1$ passes through every vertex.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair O=(0,0);real[] t={100,170,235,300,25};pair[] P;
for(int i=0;i<5;++i)P.push(dir(t[i]));
draw(unitcircle,gray);
draw(P[0]--P[1]--P[2]--P[3]--P[4]--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
for(int i=0;i<5;++i){pair M=(P[i]+P[(i+1)%5])/2;draw(O--1.15*unit(M),blue+dashed);
 pair u=unit(P[(i+1)%5]-P[i]);pair v=unit(O-M);real s=0.06;draw(M+s*u--M+s*u+s*v--M+s*v,blue);}
dot(O);label("$O$",O,1.6*dir(22));
for(int i=0;i<5;++i)label("$A_{"+string(i+1)+"}$",P[i],dir(t[i]));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\textbf{Triangles.} For a triangle $ABC$, the perpendicular bisectors of $AB$ and $BC$ are not parallel (the sides $AB$ and $BC$ are not parallel), so they meet at a point $O$. Then $OA=OB$ and $OB=OC$, so $OA=OC$ and $O$ is on the third bisector as well. By the result above, every triangle can be inscribed in a circle (its circumcircle). $\blacksquare$

(For $n\ge4$ the condition can fail: in a non-square rhombus the perpendicular bisectors of the sides do not all meet, and indeed no circle passes through all four vertices.)
