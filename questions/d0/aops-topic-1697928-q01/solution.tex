\textbf{Invariant.} Call two stones \emph{separated} once they are in different piles. When a pile of $p+q$ stones is split into piles of $p$ and $q$, exactly $pq$ pairs of stones become separated: a stone from the first new pile with a stone from the second. Pairs already in different piles stay separated.

At the start no pair is separated; at the end, with $15$ single stones, all $\dbinom{15}{2}=105$ pairs are separated, and each pair was separated at exactly one split. Therefore the total of the products is always
\[
\dbinom{15}{2}=105,
\]
no matter how Kayla splits the piles. In particular the greatest possible total is $\boxed{105}$.

(Equivalently, by induction, $f(n)=\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}$ satisfies $f(p+q)=pq+f(p)+f(q)$.)
