Divide by $P_0b^x$ (which is nonzero):
\[
\left(\dfrac {a}{b}\right)^x=\dfrac{Q_0}{P_0}.
\]
The left side is positive for every real $x$, so if $\dfrac{Q_0}{P_0}<0$ (that is, $P_0$ and $Q_0$ have opposite signs) there is no solution. If $\dfrac{Q_0}{P_0}>0$, take natural logarithms; since $a\ne b$, $\ln\dfrac {a}{b}\ne0$, and
\[
\boxed{x=\dfrac{\ln\left(\dfrac{Q_0}{P_0}\right)}{\ln\left(\dfrac {a}{b}\right)}=\dfrac{\ln Q_0-\ln P_0}{\ln a-\ln b}}
\]
(the second form when $P_0,Q_0>0$). This is the unique solution, because $\left(\dfrac {a}{b}\right)^x$ is strictly monotonic. (If $a=b$, the equation is $P_0=Q_0$: every $x$ works when $P_0=Q_0$ and none otherwise.)
