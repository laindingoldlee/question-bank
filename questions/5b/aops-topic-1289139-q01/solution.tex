Let the distance be $d$ miles. The two travel times differ by $10$ minutes, so
\[
\dfrac {d}{40}-\dfrac {d}{60}=\dfrac{10}{60}\iff\dfrac {d}{120}=\dfrac{1}{6}\iff d=20.
\]
The on-time journey takes $\dfrac{20}{40}-\dfrac{5}{60}=\dfrac{5}{12}$ hour, so the speed must be $20\div\dfrac{5}{12}=\boxed{48\text{ mph}}$.

The on-time \emph{time} is the average of the two times ($30$ and $20$ minutes), not the on-time speed. Since speed is distance divided by time, the right speed is the harmonic mean $\dfrac{2\cdot40\cdot60}{40+60}=48$ of the two speeds, which is less than their arithmetic mean $50$.
