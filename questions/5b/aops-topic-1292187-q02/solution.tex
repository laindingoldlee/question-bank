Let $S(n)$ be the digit sum of $n$. Choose $m$ so large that $10^m>a$. For every $k\ge m$, consider the term $a+d\cdot10^k$. Its decimal representation is that of $d$ followed by $k$ digits, the last digits being those of $a$ padded with zeros; the digits of $a$ and of $d\cdot10^k$ do not overlap. Hence
\[
S\!\left(a+d\cdot10^k\right)=S(d)+S(a)\qquad\text{for all }k\ge m .
\]
These are infinitely many different terms of the progression with the same digit sum. $\blacksquare$
