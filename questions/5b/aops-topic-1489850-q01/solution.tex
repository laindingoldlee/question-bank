Since $6^{111}<10^{111}$, it has at most $111$ digits, so the second term is at most $9\cdot111=999$. (In fact $6^{111}$ has $87$ digits, but the rough bound is enough.)
\begin{itemize}
\item The second term is at most $999$, so the third term is at most $9+9+9=27$.
\item The third term is at most $27$, and among the numbers from $1$ to $27$ the largest digit sum is $1+9=10$ (for $19$), so the fourth term is at most $10$.
\end{itemize}
A number and its digit sum leave the same remainder on division by $9$. Since $6^{111}=2^{111}3^{111}$ is divisible by $9$, every term is a positive multiple of $9$. The fourth term is a positive multiple of $9$ that is at most $10$, so it is $9$, and from then on every term is $9$ (the digit sum of $9$ is $9$). Hence the $111$th term is $\boxed{9}$.
