The first graph is an upside-down V with vertex $(a,b)$ and the second a V with vertex $(c,d)$; all their pieces have slopes $\pm1$. Between the two intersection points the graphs bound a quadrilateral with vertices $(2,5)$, $(a,b)$, $(8,3)$, $(c,d)$ whose sides have slopes $\pm1$: a rectangle.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
import graph; real a=4,b=7,c=6,d=1;
real f(real x){return -abs(x-a)+b;} real g(real x){return abs(x-c)+d;}
draw(graph(f,0,9),blue); draw(graph(g,0,9),red);
pair P=(2,5),Q=(8,3); dot(P);dot(Q); dot((a,b),blue); dot((c,d),red);
label("$(2,5)$",P,W); label("$(8,3)$",Q,E); label("$(a,b)$",(a,b),N,blue); label("$(c,d)$",(c,d),S,red);
draw(P--Q,gray+dashed); draw((a,b)--(c,d),gray+dashed); dot((5,4)); label("$(5,4)$",(5,4),W);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The diagonals of a rectangle bisect each other, so the midpoint of $(a,b)$ and $(c,d)$ equals the midpoint of $(2,5)$ and $(8,3)$, which is $(5,4)$. Hence $a+c=2\cdot5=\boxed{10}$. (Indeed $a=4$, $b=7$, $c=6$, $d=1$ works: $-|2-4|+7=5$, $-|8-4|+7=3$, $|2-6|+1=5$, $|8-6|+1=3$; here $b$ and $d$ are not needed individually, only $a+c$ is forced.)

The student's error: the point $(8,3)$ means $x=8$, $y=3$, so the second pair of equations should be $3=-|8-a|+b$ and $3=|8-c|+d$, not $8=-|3-a|+b$.
