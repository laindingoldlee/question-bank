An angle of $d$ degrees measures
\[
d\cdot\dfrac{400}{360}=\dfrac{10d}{9}\ \text{grads}\qquad\text{and}\qquad d\cdot\dfrac{6400}{360}=\dfrac{160d}{9}\ \text{mils}.
\]
For $d$ a whole number, both are whole numbers exactly when $9\mid10d$ and $9\mid160d$, and since $\gcd(10,9)=\gcd(160,9)=1$ this means $9\mid d$. Conversely, if $d$ is not a whole number, the angle already fails in degrees. So the angles that are whole in all three units are exactly the multiples of $9^\circ$: $\dots,81^\circ,90^\circ,99^\circ,\dots$

The next one after the right angle is $\boxed{99}$ degrees ($110$ grads, $1760$ mils).
