Consider prime exponents separately. The exponent of 2 must be odd and at least 3, divisible by 3, and divisible by 5; its least value is 15. The exponent of 3 must be even, congruent to 2 modulo 3, and divisible by 5; its least value is 20. The exponent of 5 must be even, divisible by 3, and congruent to 2 modulo 5; its least value is 12. Every other prime exponent must be a multiple of 30, so taking zero minimizes the number. Therefore $\boxed{n=2^{15}3^{20}5^{12}}$. The quotient exponents are respectively all even, all multiples of 3, and all multiples of 5, verifying the conditions.
