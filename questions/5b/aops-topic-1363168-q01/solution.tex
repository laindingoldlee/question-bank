\textbf{Key fact.} The circumcircle of $BHC$ has the same radius as the circumcircle of $ABC$. Indeed, in quadrilateral $AFHE$ the angles at $E$ and $F$ are right angles, so $\angle FHE=180^\circ-\angle A$, and hence $\angle BHC=\angle FHE=180^\circ-\angle A$ (vertical angles). By the extended law of sines in triangle $BHC$, its circumradius is
\[\dfrac{BC}{2\sin\angle BHC}=\dfrac{BC}{2\sin(180^\circ-A)}=\dfrac{BC}{2\sin A}=R,\]
the circumradius of $ABC$. (Equivalently, the reflection of $H$ in $BC$ lies on the circumcircle of $ABC$, so the circle through $B$, $H$, $C$ is the reflection of that circumcircle; its centre $O'$ is the reflection of the circumcentre $O$ in $BC$.)

pair B=(0,0),C=(5,0),A=(3.8,sqrt(49-3.8^2));

\textbf{Computing $R$.} The triangle is acute ($5^2+6^2>7^2$), so $H$ is inside it. By Heron's formula with $s=\dfrac{7+5+6}{2}=9$,
\[[ABC]=\sqrt{9\cdot2\cdot4\cdot3}=\sqrt{216}=6\sqrt6 .\]
Then
\[R=\dfrac{abc}{4[ABC]}=\dfrac{5\cdot6\cdot7}{24\sqrt6}=\dfrac{35}{4\sqrt6}=\boxed{\dfrac{35\sqrt6}{24}}\approx3.57 .\]
