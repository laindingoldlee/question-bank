Since $|x|^3=|i|=1$, we have $|x|=1$, so $x=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta$ for some angle $\theta$. By De Moivre's theorem $x^3=\cos3\theta+i\sin3\theta$, and $i=\cos\dfrac{\pi}{2}+i\sin\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so
\[3\theta=\dfrac{\pi}{2}+2\pi m,\qquad\theta=\dfrac{\pi}{6}+\dfrac{2\pi m}{3}\quad(m=0,1,2),\]
that is, $\theta=30^\circ,150^\circ,270^\circ$. Only $\theta=30^\circ$ gives positive real and imaginary parts:
\[x=\cos30^\circ+i\sin30^\circ=\boxed{\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}i}.\]
(Check: $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}+\dfrac {i}{2}\right)^2=\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i$, and multiplying by $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}+\dfrac {i}{2}$ gives $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}+\left(\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{3}{4}\right)i=i$.)
