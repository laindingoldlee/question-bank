Expanding the first square gives $\sin^2x+\cos^2x+2\sin x\cos x=1+\sin2x$.
For the second, $\sin x\ne0$ implies $\cos x\ne\pm1$, so the denominators below are nonzero. Multiply by the conjugate factor:
\[\dfrac{1-\cos x}{\sin x}=\dfrac{1-\cos^2x}{\sin x(1+\cos x)}=\dfrac{\sin^2x}{\sin x(1+\cos x)}=\dfrac{\sin x}{1+\cos x}.\]
