For $m>1$, any representation has $1\le k\le n-1$. In a fixed row these binomial coefficients are at least $n$, so $n\le m$ and the count is finite. The symmetry $(n,k)\leftrightarrow(n,n-k)$ pairs all representations except those with $n=2k$. Hence $R(m)$ is odd precisely when $m$ is a central binomial coefficient $\binom{2k}{k}$. These coefficients strictly increase, since the ratio of consecutive ones is $\dfrac{(2k+2)(2k+1)}{(k+1)^2}>1$. Starting at $k=1$, they are $2,6,20,70,252,924,3432$. Exactly $\boxed6$ are below 1000.
