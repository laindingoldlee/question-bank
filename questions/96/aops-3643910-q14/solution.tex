In six candy-taking positions, exactly one of the five people repeats. The first is Alice. If Alice repeats, her second appearance can occupy positions three through six, and the four other people fill the remaining positions in $4!$ orders: 96 sequences. If one of the other four repeats, choose two nonadjacent positions among positions two through six in six ways, then arrange the remaining three people in $3!$ ways: $4\cdot6\cdot6=144$. Every legal sequence has probability $4^{-5}$. The desired probability is $\dfrac{240}{1024}=\boxed{\dfrac{15}{64}}$.
