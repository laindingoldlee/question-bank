Let $n$ be a perfect cube with $\sigma(n)=400$, where $\sigma$ is the sum of divisors. Since $1$ and $n$ are both divisors, $\sigma(n)\ge n+1$, so $n\le399$, and $n$ is one of the cubes $1,8,27,64,125,216,343$.

Using $\sigma(p^k)=1+p+\dots+p^k$ and multiplicativity:
\[
\sigma(8)=15,\ \sigma(27)=40,\ \sigma(64)=127,\ \sigma(125)=156,\ \sigma(216)=\sigma(8)\sigma(27)=600,\ \sigma(343)=1+7+49+343=400 .
\]
So the cube is $\boxed{343}$.

A structural check: for a prime cube, $\sigma(p^3)=1+p+p^2+p^3=(1+p)(1+p^2)$, and $400=8\cdot50$ with $1+p=8$, $1+p^2=50$ gives $p=7$. A cube with two or more prime factors and $n\le399$ can only be $216=2^3\cdot3^3$, whose divisor sum is $600$.
