\textbf{(a)} Three shoes may all have different colours, so $3$ are not enough. Among any $4$ shoes, by the pigeonhole principle (three colours), two have the same colour. The answer is $\boxed{4}$.

\textbf{(b)} All $60$ left shoes contain no wearable pair, so $60$ shoes are not enough.

Now take any $61$ shoes and suppose there is no wearable pair. Then for each colour, the shoes taken are all left shoes or all right shoes, so at most $20$ shoes of each colour are taken, at most $60$ in total. This contradicts $61$. So $\boxed{61}$ shoes guarantee a wearable pair.
