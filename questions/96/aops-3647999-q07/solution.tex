A set of $n$ increasing real numbers has at least $2n-1$ pair sums, and equality forces an arithmetic progression. Here is the equality argument. Remove the maximum $M$. The smaller set already has at least $2n-3$ sums, and $M+$ its maximum and $2M$ are two new larger sums. Equality forces equality for the smaller set. Inductively, after translation it is $0,d,\ldots,(n-2)d$. The new sums $M$ and $M+(n-3)d$ must lie among these old sums, forcing $M=kd$ with $n-2<k\le n-1$. Hence $M=(n-1)d$.
Thus our integer set is $a,a+d,\ldots,a+12d$, with positive integers $a,d$ and median $a+6d=38$. Maximise $d$ subject to $a\ge1$: $d\le6$. Taking $d=6$ gives $a=2$, so the smallest attainable element is $\boxed2$.
