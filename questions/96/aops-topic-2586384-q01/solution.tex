An integer $n$ has an inverse modulo $m$ exactly when $\gcd(n,m)=1$. So we need $\gcd(n,66)>1$ and $\gcd(n,77)>1$. Since $66=2\cdot3\cdot11$ and $77=7\cdot11$, $n$ must be divisible by one of $2,3,11$ and by one of $7,11$.

For $n\le10$, the only number divisible by $7$ or $11$ is $7$, and $\gcd(7,66)=1$. For $n=11$, $\gcd(11,66)=11$ and $\gcd(11,77)=11$. So the answer is $\boxed{11}$.
