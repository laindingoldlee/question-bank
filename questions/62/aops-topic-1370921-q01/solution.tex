A $b$-separative number is a number whose base-$b$ digits are all $0$ or $1$. Such numbers correspond to binary strings: the number $\sum\varepsilon_ib^i$ with $\varepsilon_i\in\{0,1\}$ corresponds to the binary number $\sum\varepsilon_i2^i$.

This correspondence preserves order: comparing two numbers whose base-$b$ digits are $0$ or $1$ means comparing their digit strings from the top, exactly as for the binary numbers. (If the highest place where they differ is $b^k$, the larger one exceeds the other by at least $b^k-(b^{k-1}+\dots+1)>0$.) So the $n$th smallest $b$-separative number is the number whose base-$b$ digits are the binary digits of $n$.

Since $100=1100100_2=2^6+2^5+2^2$, the $100$th smallest $b$-separative integer is
\[
\boxed{b^6+b^5+b^2}.
\]
For example, for $b=3$ it is $729+243+9=981$.
