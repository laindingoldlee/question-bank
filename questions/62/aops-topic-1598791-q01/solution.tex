Let her have $p$ pennies, $n$ nickels, $d$ dimes and $q$ quarters. An average of $10$ cents means the total deviation from $10$ cents is zero:
\[
-9p-5n+0\cdot d+15q=0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad 15q=9p+5n .
\]
Reducing modulo $5$: $9p\equiv0$, so $5\mid p$. Reducing modulo $3$: $5n\equiv0$, so $3\mid n$. Write $p=5x$, $n=3y$ with $x,y\ge1$; then $15q=45x+15y$, so $q=3x+y$. The number of coins is
\[
p+n+d+q=5x+3y+d+3x+y=8x+4y+d\ge8+4+1=13,
\]
with equality for $x=y=d=1$: $5$ pennies, $3$ nickels, $1$ dime and $4$ quarters, worth $5+15+10+100=130$ cents for $13$ coins. The fewest number is $\boxed{13}$.
