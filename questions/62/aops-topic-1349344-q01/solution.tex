Let the right angle be at $C$, with $CA=60$, $CB=80$, $AB=100$, and let the segment be $CD$ with $D$ on $AB$. The perimeters of triangles $ACD$ and $BCD$ are $60+AD+CD$ and $80+BD+CD$. They are equal when $60+AD=80+BD$; together with $AD+BD=100$ this gives $AD=60$ and $BD=40$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
path ra(pair P,pair u,pair v,real s=0.2){return P+s*unit(u)--P+s*unit(u)+s*unit(v)--P+s*unit(v);}
pair C=(0,0),B=(80,0),A=(0,60),D=(48,24);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9)); draw(C--D,red);
draw(ra(C,B-C,A-C,4.5));
dot(D);
label("$A$",A,N); label("$B$",B,E); label("$C$",C,SW); label("$D$",D,NE);
label("$60$",(A+C)/2,W); label("$80$",(B+C)/2,S);
label("$60$",(A+D)/2,NE); label("$40$",(D+B)/2,NE);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Put $C=(0,0)$, $B=(80,0)$, $A=(0,60)$. Then $D=A+\dfrac{60}{100}(B-A)=(48,24)$, and
\[
CD=\sqrt{48^2+24^2}=24\sqrt{2^2+1^2}=\boxed{24\sqrt5}\approx53.7 .
\]
(Stewart's theorem gives the same: $60^2\cdot40+80^2\cdot60=100\left(CD^2+60\cdot40\right)$, so $CD^2=2880$.)
