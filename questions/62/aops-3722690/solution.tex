For $b\ne0$, write $y=a|b|\left|x-\dfrac{h}{b}\right|+k$. Thus the vertex is $\left(\dfrac{h}{b},k\right)$, and the left and right slopes are $-a|b|$ and $a|b|$. It opens upward for $a>0$ and downward for $a<0$. The minus sign places the zero of the expression inside the absolute value at $x=\dfrac{h}{b}$; $h$ alone is not generally the horizontal shift. If $a=0$, $y=k$; if $b=0$, $y=a|h|+k$. These are horizontal lines, not V-shaped graphs.
