Let the roots be $r,s,t$. They sum to zero, and $rs+st+tr=-111$. No root can be zero, since the other two would then be $\pm\sqrt{111}$. Replacing all roots by their negatives changes only the sign of $n$, so assume the two positive roots are $u\le v$ and the third is $-u-v$. Then
\[u^2+uv+v^2=111.\]
Since $v\ge u$, $3u^2\le111$, hence $1\le u\le6$. Modulo 3 the equation gives $u\equiv v\pmod3$; they cannot both be multiples of 3, because 9 does not divide 111. Therefore $u\in\{1,2,4,5\}$.

Solving the quadratic in $v$ requires $444-3u^2$ to be a square. For those four values it is respectively $441,432,396,369$; only 441 is a square. This gives $u=1,v=10$, with root set $\{-11,1,10\}$ and $n=-rst=110$. Negating the roots gives $n=-110$. Both work, so the answer is $\boxed{\{-110,110\}}$.
