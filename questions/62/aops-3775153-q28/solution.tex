Put $\theta=\angle ACD$. Then $\angle A=180^\circ-3\theta$, so $45^\circ<\theta<60^\circ$. The sine rule in $ACD$ gives $AD=\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\sin3\theta}$ and $AC=\dfrac{\sin2\theta}{\sin3\theta}$. The right triangle $ABC$ also gives $AC=4\cos A=-4\cos3\theta$. Consequently $-2\sin6\theta=\sin2\theta$. The triple-angle identity, with $\sin2\theta>0$, yields $\sin^2 2\theta=\dfrac{7}{8}$. Since $90^\circ<2\theta<120^\circ$, $\cos2\theta=-\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt2}$. Thus $AD=\dfrac{1}{4\cos^2\theta-1}=\dfrac{1}{1-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}}=\boxed{2+\sqrt2}$.
