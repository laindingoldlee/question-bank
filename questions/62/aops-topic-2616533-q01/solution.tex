$(x-1)^2=0$ holds exactly when $x-1=0$, so there is only \emph{one distinct} solution, $x=1$. As a polynomial, $(x-1)^2=(x-1)(x-1)$ has the factor $x-1$ twice, so $1$ is a \emph{double root} (multiplicity $2$); counted with multiplicity the quadratic has two roots, $1$ and $1$. Answer: $\boxed{\text{one distinct root, }x=1\text{, of multiplicity }2}$.
