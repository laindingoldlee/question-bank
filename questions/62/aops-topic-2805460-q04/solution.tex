$\boxed{\text{Jui}}$ (the second player) wins by mirroring in the vertical midline: answer a bishop on $(r,c)$ with one on $(r,9-c)$.

The mirrored square is never attacked by the bishop just placed: they are in the same row, and bishops attack only along diagonals. If some earlier bishop $Y$ attacked the mirror square, the mirror image of $Y$ (also on the board, since the position was symmetric) would have attacked Rui's square, so Rui's move would have been illegal. Since the position is symmetric after each of Jui's moves, Jui always has a legal reply, and Rui eventually cannot move. $\blacksquare$
