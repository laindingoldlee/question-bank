Since $\gcd(k,21)=1$, the residues $0,k,2k,\ldots,20k$ are distinct modulo $21$, so this is a single tour with $21$ edges. Because $2\le k\le10$, every edge is a diagonal.

Two chords with four distinct endpoints cross inside a circle exactly when their endpoints alternate around the circle. Consider the edge joining $0$ to $k$. On its shorter arc there are $k-1$ vertices, labelled $1,\ldots,k-1$.

For each such vertex $j$, both tour edges incident to it cross the edge $0k$: one joins $j$ to $j+k$, and the other joins $j$ to $j-k$ modulo $21$. The other endpoints are outside the shorter arc because $k\le10$. Conversely, a crossing edge must have exactly one endpoint among $1,\ldots,k-1$, so this list is complete. These are $2(k-1)$ distinct crossing edges.

Rotation gives the same count for each tour edge. Dividing by two because each crossing pair was counted at both edges, the total is
\[
C(k)=\dfrac{21\cdot2(k-1)}{2}=21(k-1).
\]
The admissible steps are $2,4,5,8,10$. The minimum is $C(2)=21$, and the maximum is $C(10)=189$. Their difference is therefore $\boxed{168}$. The two extremal configurations are shown below.
