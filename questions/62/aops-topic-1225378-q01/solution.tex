$\left(x^2\right)^{\dfrac{3}{4}}=\left(\left(x^2\right)^{\dfrac{1}{2}}\right)^{\dfrac{3}{2}}=|x|^{\dfrac{3}{2}}=\boxed{\sqrt{|x|^3}}$. (Writing $x^{\dfrac{3}{2}}$ is wrong for negative $x$, where it is not even real.)

The student's $\left(\sqrt{x^2}\right)^3=|x|^3$ is \emph{not} correct: it corresponds to the exponent $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot3=\dfrac{3}{2}$ applied to $x^2$, not $\dfrac{3}{4}$. For example at $x=4$: $16^{\dfrac{3}{4}}=8$, but $\left(\sqrt{16}\right)^3=64$.
