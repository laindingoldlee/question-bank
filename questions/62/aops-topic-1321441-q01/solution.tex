Let the large circle have radius $2$, so the two middle circles have radius $1$ and centres $(\pm1,0)$. By symmetry the fourth circle has centre $(0,y)$ and radius $r$, with
\[
\sqrt{1+y^2}=1+r\quad\text{and}\quad y=2-r .
\]
Then $1+(2-r)^2=(1+r)^2$, i.e. $5-4r=1+2r$, so $r=\dfrac{2}{3}$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
draw(circle((0,0),2),linewidth(0.9)); draw(circle((-1,0),1)); draw(circle((1,0),1)); draw(circle((0,4/3),2/3),blue);
draw((-2,0)--(2,0),gray); dot((0,0)); label("$O$",(0,0),S); label("$A$",(-2,0),W); label("$B$",(2,0),E);
draw((1,0)--(0,4/3),red+dashed); label("$1+r$",(0.5,0.67),E,red+fontsize(8pt));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The smallest circle has radius $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and the largest $2$, so $p=\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^2=\dfrac{1}{9}$ and $1000p\approx111.1$, which rounds to $\boxed{111}$.
