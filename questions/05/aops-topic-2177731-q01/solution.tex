Write $z=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta$ with $\theta$ uniform on $[0^\circ,360^\circ)$. Then $w=z^2=\cos2\theta+i\sin2\theta$, and as $\theta$ runs once around the circle, $2\theta$ runs twice around it at constant speed, so $w$ is also uniformly distributed on the unit circle.

Write $w=x+iy$. The condition is $y\ge1-x$, that is, $x+y\ge1$: the point $w$ lies on or above the line $x+y=1$, which meets the unit circle at $1$ and $i$.

On the circle, $x+y=\sqrt2\sin\left(\varphi+45^\circ\right)$ where $\varphi$ is the argument of $w$, and this is at least $1$ exactly when $45^\circ\le\varphi+45^\circ\le135^\circ$, i.e. $0^\circ\le\varphi\le90^\circ$: the quarter of the circle from $1$ to $i$ (red). So the probability is
\[\dfrac{90^\circ}{360^\circ}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{4}}.\]
