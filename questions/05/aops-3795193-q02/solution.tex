Put $u=x-\dfrac{\pi}{4}$. Then the integrand becomes $\dfrac{\cos u-\sin u}{\sqrt2\sqrt{1+2\cos^2u}}$, with antiderivative $\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\arcsin(\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{3}}\sin u)+\operatorname{arsinh}(\sqrt2\cos u)\right)$. Differentiation verifies this on the interval. The bounds are $u = \dfrac{3 \pi}{4}$ and $\dfrac{4 \pi}{3}$. Thus the integral equals $\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{4}-\arcsin\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}+\operatorname{arsinh}1-\operatorname{arsinh}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\right)}$. Here $\operatorname{arsinh}t=\ln(t+\sqrt{t^2+1})$.
