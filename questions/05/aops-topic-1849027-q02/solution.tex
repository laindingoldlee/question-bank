Every positive integer is congruent to the sum of its digits modulo $9$, because $10^k\equiv1\pmod9$ for every $k\ge0$.

Since $2^6=64=63+1\equiv1\pmod9$,
\[
2^{29}=\left(2^6\right)^4\cdot2^5\equiv2^5=32\equiv5\pmod9 .
\]
Let $m$ be the missing digit. The ten digits $0,1,\dots,9$ add up to $45$, so the digit sum of $2^{29}$ is $45-m$, and
\[
45-m\equiv5\pmod9\quad\Longrightarrow\quad m\equiv40\equiv4\pmod9 .
\]
The only digit from $0$ to $9$ that is congruent to $4$ modulo $9$ is $4$. So the missing digit is $\boxed{4}$. (Check: $2^{29}=536870912$.)
