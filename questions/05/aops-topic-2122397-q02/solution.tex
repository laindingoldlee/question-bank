Let $BC=a$ and let $M$ be the midpoint of $BC$, so that $BM=MC=\dfrac {a}{2}$ and the median is $AM=a$. Let $\theta=\angle AMB$; then $\angle AMC=180^\circ-\theta$ and $\cos\angle AMC=-\cos\theta$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair B=(0,0), C=(sqrt(2),0), A=(-1/(2*sqrt(2)),sqrt(1-1/8)), M=(B+C)/2;
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(1));
draw(A--M,red+dashed+linewidth(0.9));
dot(A); dot(B); dot(C); dot(M,red);
label("$A$",A,N); label("$B$",B,S); label("$C$",C,SE); label("$M$",M,S,red);

label("$\dfrac {a}{2}$",(B+M)/2,S,fontsize(8pt)); label("$\dfrac {a}{2}$",(M+C)/2,S,fontsize(8pt));
label("$a$",(A+M)/2,E,red+fontsize(9pt));
label("$1$",(A+B)/2,W,fontsize(9pt)); label("$2$",(A+C)/2,NE,fontsize(9pt));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

By the law of cosines in triangles $ABM$ and $ACM$,
\[AB^2=AM^2+BM^2-2\cdot AM\cdot BM\cos\theta,\qquad AC^2=AM^2+MC^2+2\cdot AM\cdot MC\cos\theta .\]
Adding (the cosine terms cancel because $BM=MC$) gives Apollonius' theorem,
\[AB^2+AC^2=2AM^2+2BM^2 .\]
Here $1+4=2a^2+2\cdot\dfrac{a^2}{4}=\dfrac{5a^2}{2}$, so $a^2=2$ and $BC=\sqrt2$. The triangle with sides $1$, $2$, $\sqrt2$ exists, since $1+\sqrt2>2$. The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(C)}\ \sqrt2}$.
