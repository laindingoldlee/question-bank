A sphere of radius $r$ has surface area $4\pi r^2$. The new radius is $\dfrac32r$, so the new surface area is
\[4\pi\left(\dfrac32r\right)^2=\dfrac94\cdot4\pi r^2.\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
void ball(pair c,real r,string s){
  draw(circle(c,r),linewidth(0.9));
  draw(shift(c)*xscale(r)*yscale(0.3*r)*arc((0,0),1,180,360));
  draw(shift(c)*xscale(r)*yscale(0.3*r)*arc((0,0),1,0,180),dashed);
  dot(c); draw(c--c+r*dir(60),red); label(s,c+0.78*r*dir(60),W,red);
}
ball((0,0),1,"$r$"); ball((3.2,0),1.5,"$1.5r$");
\end{asy}

The surface area is multiplied by $\dfrac94=2.25$, so it increases by $\boxed{125\%}$. (In general, scaling lengths by a factor $k$ multiplies areas by $k^2$.)
