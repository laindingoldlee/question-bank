Call a pair of adjacent numbers a \emph{change} if the two numbers differ; its product is $-1$, and every other adjacent product is $+1$. If there are $t$ changes, the sum of the products is $(2002-t)-t=2002-2t<0$, so $t>1001$.

Going once around the circle the sign returns to its starting value, so $t$ is even; hence $t\ge1002$. The changes split the circle into $t$ maximal blocks of equal numbers, alternately blocks of $1$'s and blocks of $-1$'s, so there are $\dfrac {t}{2}\ge501$ blocks of each sign, and each block contains at least one number. Hence there are at least $501$ numbers equal to $-1$ and at least $501$ equal to $1$. The number of $1$'s, $P$, satisfies $501\le P\le2002-501=1501$, and the sum of all numbers is
\[
P-(2002-P)=2P-2002,
\]
which lies between $-1000$ and $1000$. So $\boxed{|\text{sum}|\le1000}$. $\blacksquare$

(The poster's argument only examines particular arrangements and is not a proof.)
