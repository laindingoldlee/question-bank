\textbf{(a)} $\dfrac{1}{100}=1\%$.

\textbf{(b)} Use the complement: the probability that neither spin is blue is $\left(\dfrac{99}{100}\right)^2=\dfrac{9801}{10000}$, so the probability of at least one blue is
\[1-\dfrac{9801}{10000}=\boxed{\dfrac{199}{10000}}=1.99\%.\]
Adding $1\%+1\%$ counts the case ``blue on both spins'' (probability $\dfrac{1}{10000}$) twice, which is why the answer is a little less than $2\%$.

\textbf{(c)} In the same way, the probability of at least one blue in $100$ spins is
\[1-\left(\dfrac{99}{100}\right)^{100}\approx1-0.366=\boxed{0.634}.\]
It is not $100\%$ because ``$1\%$ each time'' does not add up to a certainty: all $100$ spins can be red, and that happens with probability about $0.366$. (For comparison, $\left(1-\dfrac1n\right)^n$ is close to $\dfrac1e\approx0.368$ for large $n$.)
