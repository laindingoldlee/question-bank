The only special number is $1009$. Witnesses for its ten representations are $k=1: (x,y)=(28,15)$; $k=2: (x,y)=(19,18)$; $k=3: (x,y)=(31,4)$; $k=4: (x,y)=(15,14)$; $k=5: (x,y)=(17,12)$; $k=6: (x,y)=(25,8)$; $k=7: (x,y)=(1,12)$; $k=8: (x,y)=(19,9)$; $k=9: (x,y)=(28,5)$; $k=10: (x,y)=(3,10)$. Each identity is checked by squaring and adding. To exclude the others, it suffices to fail one $k$. For $k=1$, numbers $1005,2010,2013$ fail modulo $9$; $1006,2014$ fail modulo $8$; $1007,2011,2015$ fail modulo $4$; and $2012$ fails modulo $16$, by listing square residues. Also $1008,2016$ are divisible by $7$ but not $49$; a sum of two squares divisible by $7$ must have both terms divisible by $7$, hence the sum divisible by $49$. Finally, $2017$ fails for $k=5$ modulo $5$, and $2018$ fails for $k=3$ modulo $3$, because their residues are nonsquares. Therefore the count is $\boxed1$.
