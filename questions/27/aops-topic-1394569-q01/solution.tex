The equation means $(2x)^x=216$, with $2x>0$ and $2x\ne1$. Since $216=6^3$, $x=3$ works: $(2\cdot3)^3=216$.

\textbf{Uniqueness.} Take logarithms: $g(x)=x\ln(2x)=\ln216>0$.
\begin{itemize}
\item For $0<x<\dfrac{1}{2}$: $\ln(2x)<0$, so $g(x)<0$; no solution.
\item For $x>\dfrac{1}{2}$: both $x$ and $\ln(2x)$ are positive and increasing, so $g$ is strictly increasing and takes the value $\ln216$ at most once.
\end{itemize}
So the only solution is $\boxed{x=3}$.
