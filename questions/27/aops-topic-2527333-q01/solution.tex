Let $P$ and $Q$ be pieces with $P=Q+5$, and let $R$ be the third piece. The ``twice'' relation involves two of the three pieces.

\textbf{It involves $P$ and $Q$.} $P=2Q$ gives $Q+5=2Q$, so $Q=5$, $P=10$, $R=21$; longest $21$. ($Q=2P$ is impossible since $Q<P$.)

\textbf{It involves $R$.} Then it pairs $R$ with $P$ or with $Q$, in either direction; use $P+Q+R=36$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $R=2P$: $4P-5=36$, $P=10.25$; pieces $10.25,5.25,20.5$; longest $20.5$.
\item $P=2R$: $\dfrac52P-5=36$, $P=16.4$; pieces $16.4,11.4,8.2$; longest $16.4$.
\item $R=2Q$: $4Q+5=36$, $Q=7.75$; pieces $12.75,7.75,15.5$; longest $15.5$.
\item $Q=2R$: $\dfrac52Q+5=36$, $Q=12.4$; pieces $17.4,12.4,6.2$; longest $17.4$.
\end{itemize}
All lengths are positive, and the five sets of pieces are different. The sum of the possible longest lengths is
\[21+20.5+16.4+15.5+17.4=90.8\approx\boxed{91}.\]
