A quadratic $x^2+bx+k=0$ has a real root exactly when its discriminant is nonnegative. Here
\[
\Delta=(-5)^2-4(c-1)=29-4c\ge0\iff c\le\dfrac{29}{4}.
\]
So there is a real solution exactly when $c\le\dfrac{29}{4}$, and the largest such $c$ is $\boxed{\dfrac{29}{4}}$, for which $x=\dfrac{5}{2}$ is a double root.

Equivalently, $c=1+5x-x^2=\dfrac{29}{4}-\left(x-\dfrac{5}{2}\right)^2$, which is at most $\dfrac{29}{4}$, with equality at $x=\dfrac{5}{2}$.
