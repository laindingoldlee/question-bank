The five cards are different objects even though two of them show I; call them $I_1$, $M$, $A$, $N$, $I_2$. There are $\binom53=10$ equally likely sets of three cards.

The favourable sets contain $N$, $A$ and one of the two I cards: $\{N,A,I_1\}$ and $\{N,A,I_2\}$. So the probability is
\[
\dfrac{2}{10}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{5}}.
\]
(Treating the two I's as one card would give the wrong answer $\dfrac{1}{10}$, because the $10$ sets would no longer be equally likely.)
