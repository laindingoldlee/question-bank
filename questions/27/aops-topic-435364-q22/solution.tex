Line the players up in order: there are $9!$ orders. Cut the line into the first three, the middle three and the last three players. Each set of three groups arises from $3!\cdot3!\cdot3!$ orders within the groups times $3!$ orders of the groups themselves. So the number of sets of groups is
\[
\dfrac{9!}{(3!)^3\cdot3!}=\dfrac{362880}{216\cdot6}=\boxed{280}.
\]
(Equivalently: the group containing a fixed player can be completed in $\dbinom82=28$ ways, the group containing the lowest-numbered remaining player in $\dbinom52=10$ ways, and the last group is forced: $28\cdot10=280$.)
