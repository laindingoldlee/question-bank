Rays $OM$, $ON$ and $OP$ coincide at time $t=0$. At that instant $OM$ and $OP$ start to rotate about $O$ in the plane in opposite directions, while $ON$ stays fixed. Relative to $ON$, ray $OM$ rotates at a constant rate of $3^\circ$ per second and ray $OP$ at $6^\circ$ per second.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] What is the smallest positive value of $t$, in seconds, for which $OM$ and $OP$ coincide again?
\item[(b)] When $t=30$ minutes, how many more complete revolutions has $OP$ made than $OM$?
\end{enumerate}
