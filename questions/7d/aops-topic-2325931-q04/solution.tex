If he adds $x$ caramel and $x$ peanut chocolates, there are $140+2x$ chocolates, of which $20+x$ are caramel. We need
\[\dfrac{20+x}{140+2x}=\dfrac15\iff100+5x=140+2x\iff x=\dfrac{40}{3}.\]
So he would have to add $13\dfrac13$ of each, which is $\boxed{\text{not possible with whole chocolates}}$. (Adding $13$ of each gives $\dfrac{33}{166}\approx19.9\%$ caramel, and adding $14$ of each gives $\dfrac{34}{168}\approx20.2\%$.)
