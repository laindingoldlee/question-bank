\textbf{No.} The sum is $2\left(H_n-1-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)=2H_n-3$, where $H_n=1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{n}$. Let $2^k$ be the largest power of $2$ with $2^k\le n$ ($k\ge2$ for $n\ge4$). In $H_n$, the term $\dfrac{1}{2^k}$ is the only one whose denominator contains $2^k$ (any other multiple of $2^k$ up to $n$ would be $\ge2^{k+1}$). Writing all terms over the common denominator $L=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,n)$, every numerator is even except that of $\dfrac{1}{2^k}$; so $H_n=\dfrac{\text{odd}}{L}$ with $2^k\mid L$, and $2H_n=\dfrac{\text{odd}}{M}$ with $M=\dfrac {L}{2}$ even. So $2H_n$ is not an integer, and neither is $2H_n-3$, for every $n\ge4$.
