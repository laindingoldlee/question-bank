Let $y$ be the depth of the bucket below the top, in feet. When the bucket is at depth $y$, the load being lifted is the bucket plus $y$ feet of cable, a force of $800+2y$ pounds. Raising the bucket by $dy$ costs $(800+2y)\,dy$ foot-pounds, so lifting it from depth $y_1$ to depth $y_0<y_1$ needs
\[
W=\int_{y_0}^{y_1}(800+2y)\,dy=\Big[800y+y^2\Big]_{y_0}^{y_1}.
\]
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] From $y=500$ to $y=250$: $W=800\cdot250+(500^2-250^2)=200000+187500=\boxed{387500\text{ ft-lb}}$.
\item[(b)] From $y=250$ to $y=0$: $W=800\cdot250+250^2=200000+62500=\boxed{262500\text{ ft-lb}}$.
\item[(c)] From $y=500$ to $y=0$: $W=800\cdot500+500^2=\boxed{650000\text{ ft-lb}}$, the sum of (a) and (b).
\end{enumerate}
Part (a) costs more than part (b) because more cable hangs below the top during the first half of the lift.
