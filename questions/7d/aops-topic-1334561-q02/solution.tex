A (non-degenerate) triangle exists exactly when each side is shorter than the sum of the other two:
\[
x+13>37,\qquad x+37>13,\qquad 13+37>x .
\]
These give $24<x<50$, so $x\in\{25,26,\dots,49\}$, which is $\boxed{25}$ values.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair B=(0,0), C=(37,0); real L=30; real x=(37^2+L^2-169)/(2*37); pair A=(x,sqrt(L^2-x^2));
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
label("$x$",(A+B)/2,NW); label("$13$",(A+C)/2,NE); label("$37$",(B+C)/2,S);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

(The sketch shows the case $x=30$.)
