The $10$-digit numbers run from $1\,000\,000\,000$ to $9\,999\,999\,999$; there are $9\cdot10^9$ of them. Group them by their first nine digits ($9\cdot10^8$ groups of $10$ numbers each, with last digit $0,1,\ldots,9$). Within a group, the digit sum is $S+d$, where $S$ is the sum of the first nine digits and $d$ the last digit; exactly $5$ of the ten values of $d$ make $S+d$ even (the even $d$ if $S$ is even, the odd $d$ if $S$ is odd). So exactly half of each group, and hence half of all $10$-digit numbers, have an even digit sum:
\[\dfrac{9\cdot10^9}{2}=\boxed{4\,500\,000\,000}.\]
(``Half the numbers'' is the right answer, but it needs an argument like this one: for example, among the $2$-digit numbers $10$ to $19$ exactly half have an even digit sum only because the last digit runs through a full set $0,\ldots,9$.)
