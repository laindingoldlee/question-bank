\textbf{$a$ is even.} If $a$ were odd, $a^n+1$ would be even and at least $3+1=4$, so not prime.

\textbf{$n$ is a power of $2$.} Suppose $n$ has an odd divisor $m>1$; write $n=mk$. For odd $m$ we have the factorisation
\[
y^m+1=(y+1)\left(y^{m-1}-y^{m-2}+\cdots-y+1\right),
\]
because $y=-1$ is a root of $y^m+1$. With $y=a^k$,
\[
a^n+1=(a^k)^m+1\ \text{ is divisible by }\ a^k+1 .
\]
Here $1<a^k+1<a^{mk}+1=a^n+1$ (as $a\ge2$ and $m>1$), so $a^k+1$ is a proper divisor greater than $1$, and $a^n+1$ is not prime. So $n$ has no odd divisor greater than $1$, i.e. $n$ is a power of $2$. $\blacksquare$

(For example $2^3+1=9=3\cdot3$ and $2^6+1=65=5\cdot13$, while $2^{1}+1$, $2^2+1$, $2^4+1$, $2^8+1$, $2^{16}+1$ are the Fermat primes. Whether infinitely many numbers $a^{2^j}+1$ are prime is an open problem, so the post's statement cannot be proved as written.)
