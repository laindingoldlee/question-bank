Two indistinguishable farmers, two indistinguishable lawyers and two indistinguishable mailmen sit around a circular table with six seats, so that the two people with the same job sit next to each other. Seatings that differ by a rotation of the table are considered the same (reflections are not). How many different seatings are there? A student argues: ``$3$ ways for the first pair, $2$ for the second, $1$ for the last, divided by $6$ for rotations, gives $1$.'' Find the error.
