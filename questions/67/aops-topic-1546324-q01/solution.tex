The three numbers $p$, $p+2$, $p+4$ leave the remainders $p$, $p+2$, $p+1$ modulo $3$ (as $4\equiv1$). These are three consecutive residues, so exactly one of the three numbers is divisible by $3$. A prime divisible by $3$ equals $3$, so one of the numbers is $3$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $p=3$ gives $3,5,7$, all prime;
\item $p+2=3$ gives $p=1$, not prime;
\item $p+4=3$ gives $p=-1$, not prime.
\end{itemize}
Hence $3,5,7$ is the only such triple. $\blacksquare$
