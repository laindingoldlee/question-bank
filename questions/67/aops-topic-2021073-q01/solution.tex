Suppose, for contradiction, that no language is spoken by three or more of the mathematicians, so every language is spoken by at most two of them.

Then a mathematician who speaks at most three languages shares each of them with at most one other person, so he or she has a common language with at most $3$ of the other $8$ mathematicians.

Pick any mathematician $A$. At least $8-3=5$ of the others share no language with $A$; call this set $T$. Pick $B\in T$. Then $B$ shares a language with at most $3$ others, so at least one of the remaining $4$ members of $T$, say $C$, shares no language with $B$.

Now look at $A,B,C$: $A$ and $B$ have no common language (as $B\in T$), $A$ and $C$ have none (as $C\in T$), and $B$ and $C$ have none by the choice of $C$. This contradicts the hypothesis that among any three, two speak a common language.

Hence some language is spoken by at least three of the mathematicians. $\blacksquare$
