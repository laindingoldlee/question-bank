Without the upper bound, stars and bars gives $\dbinom{19}{2}=171$ positive solutions.

Now remove the solutions where some variable is at least $10$. If $x\ge10$, put $x'=x-9\ge1$; then $x'+y+z=11$ in positive integers, which has $\dbinom{10}{2}=45$ solutions. The same count holds for $y\ge10$ and for $z\ge10$. Two variables cannot both be at least $10$, since then the sum would be at least $10+10+1=21>20$.

By inclusion and exclusion the answer is
\[
171-3\cdot45=\boxed{36}.
\]
This method (complementary counting with stars and bars, using inclusion and exclusion when several variables can exceed the bound) handles upper bounds in general without listing cases.
