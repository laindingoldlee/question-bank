The rotation group has $60$ elements: the image of a chosen vertex has $12$ choices and, once it is chosen, a chosen incident edge has $5$ rotational placements. These choices specify a unique orientation-preserving symmetry.
There are $12!$ labelled vertex placements, and no nonidentity rotation fixes a placement of distinct people. Each rotational equivalence class therefore contains $60$ placements, giving $\boxed{\dfrac{12!}{60}}$. The same reasoning applies to the thirty edge positions, giving $\boxed{\dfrac{30!}{60}}$.
