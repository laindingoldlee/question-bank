Take $A=(0,0)$, $B=(13,0)$, $C=(\dfrac{99}{13},\dfrac{168}{13})$. Then $M=(\dfrac{13}{2},0)$ and $N=\dfrac{C}{2}$. A circle through $B,M$ has equation
\[
x^2+y^2-\dfrac{39}{2}x+vy+\dfrac{169}{2}=0.
\]
A circle through $C,N$ has constant term $\dfrac{225}{2}$, since its power at $A$ is $AC\cdot AN=\dfrac{225}{2}$. Their radical axis is horizontal exactly when the $x$ coefficients agree. Substituting $C$ then gives the second circle as
\[
x^2+y^2-\dfrac{39}{2}x-\dfrac{117}{8}y+\dfrac{225}{2}=0.
\]
Write $P=M+t(N-M)$. Substitution gives $7(t-1)(7t-4)=0$. The root $t=1$ is $N$ and degenerates the construction, so $t=\dfrac{4}{7}$. Thus $P=(\dfrac{129}{26},\dfrac{48}{13})$ and
\[
AP^2=\dfrac{153}{4},\qquad \boxed{m+n=157}.
\]
The first circle then has $v=-\dfrac{169}{24}$; the horizontal radical line has a second intersection $Q=(\dfrac{189}{13},\dfrac{48}{13})$ distinct from $P$, verifying the required configuration.
