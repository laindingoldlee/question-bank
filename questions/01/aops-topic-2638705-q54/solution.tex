Let $u=\sqrt[3]{3x-7}$, so $3x-7=u^3$ and $\sqrt[3]{(3x-7)^2}=u^2$. The right side is $-u^3-1$. (a) The equation becomes $u^3+u^2+u+1=(u+1)(u^2+1)=0$, forcing $u=-1$. (b) It becomes $u^3+3u^2+3u+1=(u+1)^3=0$, again $u=-1$. Both give $3x-7=-1$, so $\boxed{x=2}$ in each case. Substitution gives left and right sides $0$ in both original equations.
