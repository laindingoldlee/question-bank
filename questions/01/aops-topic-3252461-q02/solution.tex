Use perpendicular coordinates along the square's diagonals, with centre $O$ as origin. The square has vertices $(\pm4\sqrt2,0),(0,\pm4\sqrt2)$. The kite's full first diagonal has length $8\sqrt2$. Its other diagonal is perpendicular to it and bisected by it. Their intersection lies on the incircle of radius four, hence at $(4,0)$ or $(-4,0)$. In either case the perpendicular chord of the square has length $2(4\sqrt2-4)=8\sqrt2-8$. The kite area is half the product of its perpendicular diagonals:
\[\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}(8\sqrt2)(8\sqrt2-8)=64-32\sqrt2}.\]

\end{asy}
