\textbf{(a)} Giving away $2\%$ of what you have leaves $98\%$ of it, so after $n$ days you have
\[
150\cdot(0.98)^n>0 .
\]
This is positive for every $n$, so you $\boxed{\text{never run out}}$ (although the amount tends to $0$; for example, after $100$ days about $19.9$ remain, and after $500$ days about $0.006$).

\textbf{(b)} $2\%$ of $150$ is $3$ a day, so after $n$ days $150-3n$ remain. This reaches $0$ when $n=50$: you run out on day $\boxed{50}$.

(With whole candies only, the rule in (a) is not well defined once $2\%$ of the amount left is not a whole number, which is why fractions are allowed there.)
