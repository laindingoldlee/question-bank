Let $p_1=2,p_2=3,\dots$ be the primes. Prove that $p_1p_2\cdots p_n+1$ is never a perfect square, and check the following argument: ``If $p_1\cdots p_n=k^2-1=(k-1)(k+1)$, then $k-1$ and $k+1$ have the same parity, but any factorisation of $p_1\cdots p_n$ into two parts has one even and one odd part.''
