The radius-$r$ centers have coordinates $(10-r-2j,0)$ for $j=0,\ldots,10-r$. This follows by induction: the radius-$10$ center is $0$, and every next center is obtained by shifting a preceding center left or right by $1$. The listed consecutive parity-compatible coordinates are exactly the resulting set. Thus there are $11-r$ circles of radius $r$, and the total is $1+2+\cdots+10=\boxed{55}$. The sketch illustrates the first three generations.
\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(7cm);
for(int r=8;r<=10;++r){for(int j=0;j<=10-r;++j){pair O=(10-r-2*j,0);draw(circle(O,r),r==10?black:r==9?blue:red);dot(O);}}draw((-10,0)--(10,0),dashed);label("$r=10,9,8$",(0,11));label("$O$",(0,0),S);
\end{asy}
