Suppose a prime $p$ divides both $n^3-n+1$ and $n^2$. Since $p\mid n^2$ and $p$ is prime, $p\mid n$. Then $p$ divides $n^3-n=n(n^2-1)$, and so $p$ divides
\[
\left(n^3-n+1\right)-\left(n^3-n\right)=1,
\]
which is impossible. So no prime divides both numbers, and $\gcd\left(n^3-n+1,\,n^2\right)=1$.
