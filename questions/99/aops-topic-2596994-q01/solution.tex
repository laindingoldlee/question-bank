Let $A_n$ be the event that Kevin succeeds at his $n$-th proposal; in any reading of the statement, $P(A_n)\le\dfrac1{187^n}$. Then
\[P(\text{he ever succeeds})\le\sum_{n\ge1}P(A_n)\le\sum_{n\ge1}\dfrac1{187^n}=\dfrac{\dfrac1{187}}{1-\dfrac1{187}}=\dfrac1{186}.\]
So he never succeeds with probability at least $1-\dfrac1{186}=\boxed{\dfrac{185}{186}}$.

The waiting time is therefore infinite with positive probability, so its expected value is infinite: there is \emph{no finite expected time} until Kevin succeeds. (This holds whether $\dfrac1{187^n}$ is the chance of success on the $n$-th try given the earlier failures, or the overall chance that the $n$-th try is the first success.)
