Let the fraction be $\dfrac {x}{x+3}$. The condition is
\[
\dfrac{x+7}{x+8}=\dfrac{x}{x+3}+\dfrac{1}{2} .
\]
Multiplying by $2(x+8)(x+3)$:
\[
2(x+7)(x+3)=2x(x+8)+(x+3)(x+8),
\]
\[
2x^2+20x+42=3x^2+27x+24\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^2+7x-18=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad(x+9)(x-2)=0 .
\]
\begin{itemize}
\item $x=2$ gives $\dfrac{2}{5}$, which is in simplest form, and $\dfrac{9}{10}=\dfrac{2}{5}+\dfrac{1}{2}$. It works.
\item $x=-9$ gives $\dfrac{-9}{-6}$, which is not in simplest form (it equals $\dfrac{3}{2}$), so it is rejected.
\end{itemize}
The original fraction is $\boxed{\dfrac{2}{5}}$.
