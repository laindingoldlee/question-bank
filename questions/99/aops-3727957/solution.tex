Write the digits as $a,b,c$, with $1\le a\le9$ and $0\le b,c\le9$. Put $S=a+b+c$ and $A=a-b+c$. Since $1\le S\le27$, the allowed sums are 11 and 22. Also $-8\le A\le18$, so $A\in\{0,9,18\}$. The quantities $S$ and $A$ have the same parity because $S-A=2b$. The possible pairs are therefore $(11,9),(22,0),(22,18)$. The latter two give $b=11$ or $a+c=20$, impossible. For $(11,9)$, we get $b=1$ and $a+c=10$. There are nine choices $a=1,\ldots,9$, giving $119,218,317,416,515,614,713,812,911$. The answer is $\boxed9$.
