Count the factors of $2$ in $1\cdot2\cdots34$ (Legendre's formula). Every multiple of $2$ contributes one factor, every multiple of $4$ one more, and so on:
\[
n=\left\lfloor\dfrac{34}{2}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\dfrac{34}{4}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\dfrac{34}{8}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\dfrac{34}{16}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\dfrac{34}{32}\right\rfloor=17+8+4+2+1=\boxed{32}.
\]
For example, $32=2^5$ is counted in all five terms, which is exactly its five factors of $2$. (This is a number theory problem: the exponent of a prime in a factorial.)
