\textbf{(a)} For odd $p$, $p+1$ is even and $i^{p+1}=(i^2)^{\dfrac{p+1}{2}}=(-1)^{\dfrac{p+1}{2}}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item If $p\equiv1\pmod4$, then $\dfrac{p+1}{2}$ is odd, $i^{p+1}=-1$ and $f(p)=2p+1-(p-5)=p+6$.
\item If $p\equiv3\pmod4$, then $\dfrac{p+1}{2}$ is even, $i^{p+1}=1$ and $f(p)=2p+1+(p-5)=3p-4$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{(b)} Compute in order:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccccc}
p & 3 & 5 & 7 & 11 & 13 & 17 & 19 & 23\\\hline
f(p) & 5 & 11 & 17 & 29 & 19 & 23 & 53 & 65
\end{array}
\]
All values are prime until $p=23$ ($\equiv3\pmod4$), where $f(23)=3\cdot23-4=65=5\cdot13$. So the claim is false, and the smallest counterexample is $\boxed{p=23}$. (Another: $p=29\equiv1\pmod4$ gives $35$.)
