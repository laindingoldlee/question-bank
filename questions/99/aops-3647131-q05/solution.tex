The product is $\dfrac{100!}{100^{100}}=\dfrac{100!}{10^{200}}$. Since $v_5(100!)=20+4=24$ and there are more factors of two, cancelling trailing zeros leaves a denominator $10^{176}$ and a numerator not divisible by ten. Thus there are 176 decimal places through the final nonzero digit. There are 42 zeros immediately after the decimal point, so the nonzero-ending block has length $176-42=\boxed{134}$. The count of 42 follows, for example, from $10^{-43}<\dfrac{100!}{10^{200}}<10^{-42}$, which is verified by exact integer comparison with $10^{157}$ and $10^{158}$.
