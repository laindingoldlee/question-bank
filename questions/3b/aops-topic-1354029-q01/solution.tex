Let $g=\gcd(a,b)$, $a=gx$, $b=gy$ with $\gcd(x,y)=1$. Then $\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)=gxy$, and the equation becomes
\[
g^2\left(x^2+y^2\right)=gxy+7g\iff g\left(x^2+y^2\right)=xy+7 .
\]
Since $g\ge1$, $x^2+y^2\le xy+7$, i.e.\ $(x-y)^2+xy\le7$. With $x\le y$ coprime, the possibilities are $(1,1),(1,2),(1,3),(2,3)$ (for $(1,4)$: $9+4>7$; $(1,5)$, $(2,5)$, $(3,4)$ and larger are too big). Then $g=\dfrac{xy+7}{x^2+y^2}$:
\[
(1,1):\ g=\dfrac{8}{2}=4;\quad (1,2):\ \dfrac{9}{5}\ \text{no};\quad (1,3):\ \dfrac{10}{10}=1;\quad (2,3):\ \dfrac{13}{13}=1 .
\]
So $(a,b)$ is one of $\boxed{(4,4),\ (1,3),\ (3,1),\ (2,3),\ (3,2)}$. Check: $16+16=4+28$, $1+9=3+7$, $4+9=6+7$.
