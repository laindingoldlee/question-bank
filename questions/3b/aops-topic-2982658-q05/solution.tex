Let $A_k$ be the probability that Alfred wins when he has $k$ tokens (Brendan $4-k$) and it is Alfred's turn; by symmetry, when Brendan is to move with Alfred holding $k$ tokens, Alfred wins with probability $1-A_{4-k}$. Then
\[A_1=\dfrac12(1-A_3)+\dfrac12,\qquad A_2=\dfrac12(1-A_2)+\dfrac12(1-A_3),\qquad A_3=\dfrac12(1-A_1)+\dfrac12(1-A_2).\]
(E.g. from $A_2$: heads leaves $2$ tokens with Brendan to move; tails leaves Alfred $1$ token, Brendan $3$, Brendan to move.) Let $t=A_3$. Then $A_1=1-\dfrac t2$ and $A_2=\dfrac{2-t}3$. Substituting into the third equation: $t=1-\dfrac12\left(1-\dfrac t2+\dfrac{2-t}3\right)$, so $12t=6+3t-4+2t$ and $t=\dfrac27$. Therefore $A_2=\dfrac13\left(2-\dfrac27\right)=\boxed{\dfrac47}$.
