A solution is nonzero, so write $z=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)$ with $r>0$. By De Moivre's theorem,
\[z^n=r^n\left(\cos n\theta+i\sin n\theta\right).\]
This equals $1$ exactly when $r^n=1$ and $\cos n\theta=1$, $\sin n\theta=0$, i.e. $r=1$ and $n\theta=2\pi k$ for an integer $k$. So the solutions are
\[\boxed{z_k=\cos\dfrac{2\pi k}{n}+i\sin\dfrac{2\pi k}{n},\qquad k=0,1,\dots,n-1.}\]
These $n$ numbers are different, because their arguments $\dfrac{2\pi k}{n}$ are different angles in $[0,2\pi)$. Any other integer $k$ gives the same number as its remainder mod $n$, since the angle changes by a multiple of $2\pi$. So there are exactly $n$ roots of unity; they are the vertices of a regular $n$-gon inscribed in the unit circle, starting at $1$.
