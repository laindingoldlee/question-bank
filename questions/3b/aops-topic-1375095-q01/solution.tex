By symmetry (swap the faces $1$ and $2$), ``more $2$'s than $1$'s'' and ``more $1$'s than $2$'s'' are equally likely. So it is enough to count the $6^4=1296$ outcomes with equally many $1$'s and $2$'s.
\begin{itemize}
\item No $1$'s and no $2$'s: each die shows $3,4,5$ or $6$: $4^4=256$.
\item One $1$ and one $2$: choose the die with the $1$ and the die with the $2$ ($4\cdot3=12$ ways), the other two dice from $\{3,4,5,6\}$ ($4^2=16$): $192$.
\item Two $1$'s and two $2$'s: $\dbinom42=6$.
\end{itemize}
That is $256+192+6=454$ outcomes. The required probability is
\[
\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1296-454}{1296}=\dfrac{421}{1296},
\]
so the student's answer $\boxed{\dfrac{421}{1296}}$ is right.
