Write $a=da'$ and $b=db'$, where $a'=\dfrac {a}{d}$ and $b'=\dfrac {b}{d}$ are positive integers. Let $e=\gcd(a',b')$. Since $e\mid a'$ and $e\mid b'$, the number $de$ divides both $a=da'$ and $b=db'$. So $de$ is a common divisor of $a$ and $b$, and therefore $de\le d$, the greatest common divisor. Hence $e\le1$, i.e.\ $e=1$. $\blacksquare$

The quoted argument has the right idea but is not a proof as written: ``the most they have in common'' and ``removing all the common stuff'' are not precise statements. The step that needs justification is exactly the one above: a common divisor $e>1$ of $\dfrac {a}{d}$ and $\dfrac {b}{d}$ would give the common divisor $de>d$ of $a$ and $b$. Written in that form, the intuitive argument is a complete proof.
