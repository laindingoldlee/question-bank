The two right-triangle identities, valid also when a horizontal leg has length zero, are
\[
AB^2=AD^2+BD^2,\qquad AC^2=AD^2+CD^2.
\]
Subtracting gives $AB^2-AC^2=BD^2-CD^2$. Since all side lengths are nonnegative, squaring preserves their comparisons. Thus the two differences have the same sign, proving all three assertions. The reasoning does not require $D$ to lie between $B$ and $C$.

\end{asy}
