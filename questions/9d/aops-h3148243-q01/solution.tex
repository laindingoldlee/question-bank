Let $k$ be the number of crossing pairs. Each pair determines a quadrilateral. Its interior cannot contain another selected edge: an edge entering it would cross one of the two diagonals already used in a crossing. The interiors of these quadrilaterals are therefore disjoint. Add their boundary edges, remove the crossing diagonals, and triangulate all remaining regions. If the resulting dissection has $t$ triangular and $k$ quadrilateral regions, counting polygon angles gives $t+2k=48$, so $k\le24$. This dissection has $47-k$ internal edges; restoring the two diagonals of every quadrilateral gives at most $47+k\le71$ selected diagonals. To attain this, dissect the polygon into the $24$ quadrilaterals $(1,2,3,4),(1,4,5,6),\ldots,(1,48,49,50)$. Select the $23$ separating diagonals and both diagonals of each quadrilateral, for $23+48=\boxed{71}$.
