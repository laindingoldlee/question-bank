Let the roots be $r$ and $s$. By Vieta's formulas, $r+s=\dfrac{13}{11}$ and $rs=\dfrac{15}{11}$. Then
\[(r+2)(s+2)=rs+2(r+s)+4=\dfrac{15}{11}+\dfrac{26}{11}+\dfrac{44}{11}=\boxed{\dfrac{85}{11}}.\]
Equivalently, $11x^2-13x+15=11(x-r)(x-s)$; putting $x=-2$ gives $44+26+15=11(r+2)(s+2)$, so $(r+2)(s+2)=\dfrac{85}{11}$.

(The discriminant $169-660$ is negative, so $r$ and $s$ are non-real complex conjugates; Vieta's formulas, and hence the answer, hold all the same.)
