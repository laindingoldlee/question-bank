Write $3n+1=m^2$. Since $m$ is not divisible by $3$, write $m=3a+1$ or $3a-1$. Then $n+1=3a^2\pm2a+1=a^2+a^2+(a\pm1)^2$, so three squares always suffice. For $n=21$, the hypothesis holds because $3n+1=64$, but $n+1=22$ is not the sum of two nonnegative squares: the possible squares at most $22$ are $0,1,4,9,16$, and subtracting each from $22$ never leaves a square. Thus $\boxed{k=3}$, choice C.
