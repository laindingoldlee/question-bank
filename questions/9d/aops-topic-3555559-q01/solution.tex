There cannot be two zero digits, since their pair sum would make $P=0$. Thus there is exactly one zero. Each nonzero digit occurs as a factor paired with that zero, so each must be one of 1,2,4,8. The sum of two distinct powers of 2 is not a power of 2, so all three nonzero digits must equal the same digit d. Conversely three copies of any $d\in\{1,2,4,8\}$ and one zero give only power-of-two factors. The zero has 3 allowed positions, so there are $4\cdot 3=\boxed{12}$ numbers.
