$x^2+5x+6=(x+2)(x+3)$, since $2+3=5$ and $2\cdot3=6$. So the solutions are $x=-2$ and $x=-3$, and their sum is $\boxed{-5}$. (By Vieta's formulas the sum of the roots of $x^2+bx+c$ is $-b=-5$.)

The student made two errors. First, $(x+1)(x+5)=x^2+6x+5$, not $x^2+5x+6$: the factors must have \emph{product} $6$ and \emph{sum} $5$. Second, even for $(x+1)(x+5)=0$ the solutions would be $x=-1$ and $x=-5$, not $1$ and $5$: a factor $x+a$ is zero when $x=-a$.
