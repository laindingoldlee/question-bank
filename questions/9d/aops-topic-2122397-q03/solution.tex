Since $P$ is inside the convex quadrilateral, the segments $PA,PB,PC,PD$ split it into the triangles $PAB$, $PBC$, $PCD$, $PDA$, and the angles of these triangles at $P$ add up to $360^\circ$. Using the area formula $\dfrac{1}{2}xy\sin\varphi\le\dfrac{1}{2}xy$,
\[[ABCD]=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot1\cdot2\sin\angle APB+\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot2\cdot3\sin\angle BPC+\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot3\cdot4\sin\angle CPD+\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot4\cdot1\sin\angle DPA\le\dfrac{1}{2}(2+6+12+4)=12 .\]
The area is exactly $12$, so equality holds in every term: all four angles at $P$ are right angles. Then $\angle APB+\angle BPC=180^\circ$, so $A$, $P$, $C$ are collinear, and similarly $B$, $P$, $D$ are collinear: the diagonals are perpendicular and meet at $P$.

usepackage("amsmath");

fill(A--B--C--D--cycle,paleyellow);
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(1));
draw(A--C,blue); draw(B--D,blue);
draw((0.25,0)--(0.25,0.25)--(0,0.25),blue);
dot(P); dot(A); dot(B); dot(C); dot(D);

label("$1$",(A+P)/2,N,blue+fontsize(8pt)); label("$3$",(P+C)/2,N,blue+fontsize(8pt));
label("$2$",(P+B)/2,E,blue+fontsize(8pt)); label("$4$",(P+D)/2,E,blue+fontsize(8pt));
label("$P$",P,dir(-135),fontsize(9pt)); label("$A$",A,W); label("$B$",B,N); label("$C$",C,E); label("$D$",D,S);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

By the Pythagorean theorem,
\[AB=\sqrt{1+4}=\sqrt5,\quad BC=\sqrt{4+9}=\sqrt{13},\quad CD=\sqrt{9+16}=5,\quad DA=\sqrt{16+1}=\sqrt{17},\]
so the perimeter is
\[\boxed{5+\sqrt5+\sqrt{13}+\sqrt{17}}\approx15.89 .\]
