Since $n\cdot n!=(n+1)\cdot n!-n!=(n+1)!-n!$, the sum telescopes:
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{2017}n\cdot n!=\sum_{n=1}^{2017}\bigl((n+1)!-n!\bigr)=2018!-1! .
\]
Adding $1$ gives $2018!$, so $k=\boxed{2018}$ (and $k$ is unique, because $m!$ is strictly increasing for $m\ge1$).
