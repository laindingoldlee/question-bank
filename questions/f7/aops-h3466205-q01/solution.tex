We have $f(1)=1$ and $f(\dfrac{1}{2})=2$. Multiplicativity gives $1=f(2)f(\dfrac{1}{2})$, so $f(2)=\dfrac{1}{2}$. Therefore $f(\sqrt2)^2=\dfrac{1}{2}$.

The positive sign is forced: $\sqrt2=(\sqrt[4]2)^2$, so $f(\sqrt2)=f(\sqrt[4]2)^2\ge0$. Thus
\[
f(\sqrt2)=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\qquad\boxed{a+b=4}.
\]
