Let the hexagon have side $1$ and centre $O$. The three long diagonals meet at $O$ and cut the hexagon into six small equilateral triangles; the six short diagonals add more crossing points. Every triangle in the figure has angles that are multiples of $30^\circ$, and we count the isosceles ones by type, using the symmetry of the figure.
\begin{itemize}
\item The six triangles $OV_iV_{i+1}$ of side $1$ are equilateral: $6$.
\item The two large triangles $V_0V_2V_4$ and $V_1V_3V_5$ are equilateral: $2$.
\item Inside each small triangle $OV_iV_{i+1}$ the short diagonals from $V_i$ and $V_{i+1}$ cross; together with the side $V_iV_{i+1}$ they make one $30$-$30$-$120$ triangle: $6$.
\item Each pair of adjacent small triangles (a rhombus $OV_{i-1}V_iV_{i+1}$) contains two $30$-$30$-$120$ triangles ($V_{i-1}V_iV_{i+1}$ and $V_{i-1}OV_{i+1}$) and one more equilateral triangle with a vertex at $V_i$ and its opposite side on a long diagonal: $3$ per rhombus, $18$ in all.
\item Each half of the hexagon contains one large $30$-$30$-$120$ triangle with its base on a long diagonal and its apex at a vertex: $6$.
\end{itemize}
The total is $6+2+6+18+6=\boxed{38}$.

A computer check over all $19$ points of the figure confirms this: there are $110$ triangles in all, and exactly $38$ of them are isosceles (equilateral ones included).
