Three identical-looking cats are named Truecat, Liecat and Randomcat. Truecat always answers truthfully and Liecat always lies; both know everything. Randomcat answers each question by choosing at random between its two words. All three cats answer yes-or-no questions only with the words ``Meow'' and ``Nya''; one of these words means ``yes'' and the other means ``no'', but you do not know which. You ask questions one at a time, each addressed to one cat of your choice, and your choice of cat and question may depend on the earlier answers. A question that would lead to a paradox may not be asked.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Show that you can always find out which cat is which with three questions.
\item[(b)] Show that two questions are not always enough.
\end{enumerate}
