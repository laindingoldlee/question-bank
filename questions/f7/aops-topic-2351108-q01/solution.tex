By the multiplication principle there are $4\cdot6\cdot4\cdot4=384$ different meals. Since $400>384$, the $400$ people cannot all have different meals (pigeonhole principle: $384$ ``boxes'', $400$ ``objects''), so at least two people have the same meal. $\blacksquare$

(More precisely, if the guests choose one after another, at least $400-384=16$ of them choose a meal that someone before them has already chosen.)
