The nine numbers add up to $333$, so each of the three rows sums to $\dfrac{333}{3}=111$.

\textbf{The centre.} The middle row, the middle column and the two diagonals all pass through the centre $c$; together they cover every cell once and the centre three extra times, so $4\cdot111=333+3c$ and $c=37$. Then each line through the centre pairs two numbers with sum $111-37=74$: the pairs are $1+73$, $7+67$, $13+61$, $31+43$.

\textbf{Where is $1$?} If $1$ were in a corner, its row and its column would each need two further numbers with sum $110$; but among the remaining numbers only $43+67=110$, so one pair is not enough. Hence $1$ is in the middle of a side, with $73$ in the middle of the opposite side, and the two corners next to $1$ are $43$ and $67$. The rest is forced by the pairs above:
\[\begin{array}{|c|c|c|}\hline 67&1&43\\\hline 13&37&61\\\hline 31&73&7\\\hline\end{array}\]
Check: rows $67+1+43=13+37+61=31+73+7=111$; columns $67+13+31=1+37+73=43+61+7=111$; diagonals $67+37+7=43+37+31=111$. Up to rotations and reflections this is the only solution.
