Put the large centre $O$ at the origin; the small centres are at distance $5-1=4$ from $O$ on a line through $O$, so they are $(\pm4,0)$. The fourth circle (radius $r$) is equally far, $r+1$, from both small centres, so its centre is $(0,y)$ with, say, $y\ge0$. Internal tangency to the large circle gives $y+r=5$, and tangency to a small circle gives $16+y^2=(r+1)^2$. Hence
\[16+(5-r)^2=(r+1)^2\iff40=12r\iff r=\boxed{\dfrac{10}{3}}.\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair O=(0,0),P=(-4,0),Q=(4,0);real r=10/3;pair Cc=(0,5-r);
draw(circle(O,5),linewidth(0.8));draw(circle(P,1));draw(circle(Q,1));filldraw(circle(Cc,r),lightgray);
draw(P--Cc,dashed);draw(P--Q,dashed);draw(O--(0,5),dashed);dot(O);dot(P);dot(Q);dot(Cc);
label("$O$",O,S,fontsize(8pt));label("$4$",(-2,0),S,fontsize(7pt));label("$r+1$",(-2,(5-r)/2),NW,fontsize(7pt));label("$y$",(0,(5-r)/2),E,fontsize(7pt));
\end{asy}
