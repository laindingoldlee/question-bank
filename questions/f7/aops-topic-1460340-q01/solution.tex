$\lfloor\sqrt{x}\rfloor=k$ exactly when $k\le\sqrt{x}<k+1$, that is $k^2\le x<(k+1)^2$, an interval of length $2k+1$. For $0<x<100$ the possible values are $k=0,1,\dots,9$, and the even ones give
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\lfloor\sqrt{x}\rfloor & x\text{-interval} & \text{length}\\
0&0<x<1&1\\
2&4\le x<9&5\\
4&16\le x<25&9\\
6&36\le x<49&13\\
8&64\le x<81&17
\end{array}
\]
(The row ``$1$, $1\le x<4$, length $3$'' in such a table lists the $x$ with $\lfloor\sqrt{x}\rfloor=1$, an \emph{odd} value, so it is not counted.) The favourable length is $1+5+9+13+17=45$, and the probability is $\dfrac{45}{100}=\boxed{\dfrac{9}{20}}$.
