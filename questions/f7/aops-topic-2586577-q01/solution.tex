$103$ is prime and $n^3-1=(n-1)\left(n^2+n+1\right)$, so for $n\not\equiv1$ we need $n^2+n+1\equiv0\pmod{103}$. Since $46^3\equiv1$ and $46\not\equiv1$, the number $46$ is a root of $x^2+x+1$ modulo $103$.

If $r$ is the other root, then $x^2+x+1\equiv(x-46)(x-r)$, and comparing the $x$-coefficients gives $46+r\equiv-1$, so $r\equiv-47\equiv56\pmod{103}$. A quadratic has at most two roots modulo a prime, so the solutions of $n^3\equiv1$ are $n\equiv1,46,56$. The required integer is $\boxed{56}$.

Check: $56^3-1=175615=103\cdot1705$. (Also $46\cdot56=2576=25\cdot103+1$, so $56\equiv46^{-1}\equiv46^2$.)
