\textbf{(a)} A rotation of the cube is determined by where it sends the bottom face and how it then turns about the vertical axis: the bottom face can go to any of the $6$ faces, and then the cube can be turned into $4$ positions about the axis through that face. So there are $6\cdot4=24$ rotations. Listed by axis: the identity ($1$); rotations by $90^\circ,180^\circ,270^\circ$ about the $3$ axes through centres of opposite faces ($9$); rotations by $120^\circ,240^\circ$ about the $4$ axes through opposite vertices ($8$); and rotations by $180^\circ$ about the $6$ axes through midpoints of opposite edges ($6$). Indeed $1+9+8+6=24$. (Reflections are not allowed: a physical cube cannot be turned into its mirror image.)

\textbf{(b)} First choose the $6$ colours: $\dbinom{7}{6}=7$ ways. With $6$ different colours there are $6!=720$ ways to paint the faces of a cube held in a fixed position. A rotation other than the identity moves some face to another face, so it changes such a painting; hence the $24$ rotations of a painting give $24$ different paintings, and each distinguishable cube corresponds to exactly $24$ of the $720$ paintings. That gives $\dfrac{720}{24}=30$ cubes for each choice of colours, and
\[
7\cdot30=\dfrac{7!}{24}=\boxed{210}
\]
distinguishable cubes in all. (Direct check of $30$: put the first colour on the bottom; the top can be any of the other $5$ colours; the remaining $4$ colours go around the sides in $(4-1)!=6$ circular orders: $5\cdot6=30$.)
