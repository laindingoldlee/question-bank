Call the left side $F(x)$. Then $F(a)=2(a-b)(a-d)>0$ and $F(b)=(b-a)(b-c)<0$, so a root lies in $(a,b)$ by continuity. Also $F(c)=2(c-b)(c-d)<0$ and $F(d)=(d-a)(d-c)>0$, giving another root in $(c,d)$. These roots are distinct, and a quadratic has at most two.
