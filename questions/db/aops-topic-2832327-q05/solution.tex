$p^3=(b^2-a)(b^2+a)$ with $0<b^2-a<b^2+a$, so either $b^2-a=1,\ b^2+a=p^3$, or $b^2-a=p,\ b^2+a=p^2$.

In the second case $2b^2=p(p+1)$, so $p\mid b$; writing $b=pm$ gives $2pm^2=p+1$, impossible. In the first case $2b^2=p^3+1$. Testing primes: $p=2,3,5,7,11,13,17,19$ give $\dfrac{p^3+1}2=4.5,14,63,172,666,1099,2457,3430$, none a perfect square; $p=23$ gives $\dfrac{12168}2=6084=78^2$. Then $a=b^2-1=6083$, and indeed $6083^2+23^3=78^4$. The answer is $\boxed{23}$.
