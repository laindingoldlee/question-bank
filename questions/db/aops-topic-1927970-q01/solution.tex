Work modulo $7$.

\textbf{Left side.} The cubes modulo $7$ are
\[
0^3\equiv0,\quad 1^3\equiv1,\quad 2^3=8\equiv1,\quad 3^3=27\equiv6,\quad 4^3=64\equiv1,\quad 5^3=125\equiv6,\quad 6^3=216\equiv6 .
\]
The seven consecutive integers $a,a+1,\dots,a+6$ have the seven remainders $0,1,\dots,6$ in some order, so
\[
\text{left side}\equiv0+1+1+6+1+6+6=21\equiv0\pmod7 .
\]

\textbf{Right side.} The fourth powers modulo $7$ are $0^4\equiv0$, $(\pm1)^4\equiv1$, $(\pm2)^4=16\equiv2$ and $(\pm3)^4=81\equiv4$, so for $x\equiv0,1,2,3,4,5,6$ we get $x^4\equiv0,1,2,4,4,2,1$. For $b\equiv0,1,\dots,6\pmod 7$ the sum $b^4+(b+1)^4$ is
\[
0+1=1,\quad 1+2=3,\quad 2+4=6,\quad 4+4=8\equiv1,\quad 4+2=6,\quad 2+1=3,\quad 1+0=1 .
\]
So the right side is $\equiv1$, $3$ or $6\pmod7$, never $0$.

The two sides always have different remainders modulo $7$, so the equation has $\boxed{\text{no integer solutions}}$.
