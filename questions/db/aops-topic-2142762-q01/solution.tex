Number the places around the circle $1$ to $6$.

The even numbers $2,4,6$ have the common factor $2$, so no two of them can be adjacent. Three numbers on a circle of six places with no two adjacent must occupy alternate places: either places $1,3,5$ or places $2,4,6$ ($2$ choices). The evens can be put in their three places in $3!=6$ ways, and the odds $1,3,5$ in the other three places in $6$ ways, giving $2\cdot6\cdot6=72$ arrangements with no two evens adjacent.

Among these, the only other pair that is not relatively prime is $\{3,6\}$ (every other even--odd pair from $1$ to $6$ is coprime). The number $6$ is adjacent to two of the three odd places, and $3$ must be in the third one, opposite $6$. By symmetry this happens in exactly one third of the $72$ arrangements. So the answer is
\[\dfrac{72}{3}=\boxed{24}.\]
