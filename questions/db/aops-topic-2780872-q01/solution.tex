Write $n=qk+r$ with $0\le r\le k-1$. Each friend gets $q$ chocolates and you get $r$.

(a) $(n,k)$ is generous exactly when $r\le q$, that is, when the remainder of $n$ on division by $k$ is at most $\left\lfloor\dfrac nk\right\rfloor$. In particular every $n\ge k(k-1)$ is generous, since then $q\ge k-1\ge r$.

(b) The pair is not generous when $r>q$. Then $q\le k-2$, and for each $q\in\{0,1,\dots,k-2\}$ there are $k-1-q$ choices $r\in\{q+1,\dots,k-1\}$. The number of non-generous $n$ is
\[(k-1)+(k-2)+\dots+1=\boxed{\dfrac{k(k-1)}2},\]
and all of them are less than $k(k-1)$. Hence for $N\ge k(k-1)$, the proportion of generous $n$ among $0,1,\dots,N-1$ is $1-\dfrac{k(k-1)}{2N}$, which tends to $1$.
