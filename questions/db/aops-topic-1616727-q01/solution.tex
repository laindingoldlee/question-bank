Let his usual journey take $T$ minutes. Time is inversely proportional to speed for a fixed distance.

\textbf{First day.} At $\dfrac{4}{3}$ of the usual speed the journey takes $\dfrac{3}{4}T$. He left $S$ minutes late and arrived $S$ minutes early, so the journey was $2S$ minutes shorter than usual:
\[
T-\dfrac{3}{4}T=2S\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad T=8S .
\]
\textbf{Second day.} At $\dfrac{8}{11}$ of the usual speed the journey takes $\dfrac{11}{8}T=11S$, which is $3S$ minutes longer than usual. He left $S$ minutes early, so he arrived $3S-S=2S$ minutes late: $\boxed{\textbf{(b)}\ 2S\text{ minutes late}}$.

(If ``$S$ minutes earlier than the previous day'' is taken literally, he left at his usual time and arrived $3S$ minutes late, which is not among the choices; so the intended meaning is $S$ minutes earlier than usual.)
