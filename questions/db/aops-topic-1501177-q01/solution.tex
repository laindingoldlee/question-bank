Going up one step doubles the value, so $f(n+5)=2^5f(n)=96$. Going down one step halves it, $f(x-1)=\dfrac{1}{2}f(x)$, so $f(n-5)=\dfrac{3}{2^5}=\dfrac{3}{32}$. Hence
\[
f(n-5)\cdot f(n+5)=\dfrac{3}{32}\cdot96=\boxed{9}.
\]
(In general $f(n-k)f(n+k)=f(n)^2$.)
