MATHEMATICS has $11$ letters: the vowels A, A, E, I and the consonants M, M, T, T, H, C, S.

Place the $4$ vowels in a row; they create $5$ gaps (before the first vowel, between vowels, after the last). Let $g_0,g_1,\dots,g_4$ be the numbers of consonants in the gaps, so $g_0+\cdots+g_4=7$. ``Every three consecutive letters contain a vowel'' means no three consonants are consecutive, i.e. each $g_i\le2$. ``Starts or ends with a vowel'' means $g_0=0$ or $g_4=0$.
\begin{itemize}
\item If $g_0=0$: $g_1+g_2+g_3+g_4=7$ with each $g_i\le2$. The maximum is $8$, so exactly one of the four gaps has $1$ and the others have $2$: $4$ ways.
\item If $g_4=0$: likewise $4$ ways.
\item Both $g_0=g_4=0$ is impossible, since $g_1+g_2+g_3\le6<7$.
\end{itemize}
So there are $8$ gap patterns. For each, the vowels can be ordered in $\dfrac{4!}{2!}=12$ ways and the consonants in $\dfrac{7!}{2!\,2!}=1260$ ways. The total is
\[
8\cdot12\cdot1260=\boxed{120960} .
\]
