The four chosen vertices are distinct points on the circumcircle of the decagon. Name them $P_1,P_2,P_3,P_4$ in order around the circle. Given the set of four points, the two pairs form one of three pairings, all equally likely by symmetry (each of the three pairings arises from the same number of ordered choices: $2$, one for each pair chosen first):
\[
\{P_1P_2,P_3P_4\},\qquad\{P_1P_4,P_2P_3\},\qquad\{P_1P_3,P_2P_4\}.
\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair[] V; for(int k=0;k<10;++k) V.push(dir(90+36k));
draw(V[0]--V[1]--V[2]--V[3]--V[4]--V[5]--V[6]--V[7]--V[8]--V[9]--cycle,linewidth(1));
int[] q={0,2,5,7};
draw(V[0]--V[5],blue+linewidth(1));draw(V[2]--V[7],blue+linewidth(1));
draw(V[0]--V[2],gray(0.5)+dashed);draw(V[5]--V[7],gray(0.5)+dashed);
draw(V[0]--V[7],red+dashed);draw(V[2]--V[5],red+dashed);
for(int k=0;k<4;++k) dot(V[q[k]]);
label("$P_1$",V[0],N);label("$P_2$",V[2],dir(90+72));label("$P_3$",V[5],S);label("$P_4$",V[7],dir(90+252));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

In the first two pairings (dashed) the segments join neighbours in the circular order and do not meet; in the third (solid blue) the segments are the diagonals of the convex quadrilateral $P_1P_2P_3P_4$, which cross inside it, hence inside the decagon. So exactly one of the three equally likely pairings gives an intersection, and the probability is $\boxed{\dfrac{1}{3}}$.

(Count: $\dbinom{10}{2}\dbinom82=1260$ ordered choices; $\dbinom{10}{4}=210$ sets of four points, each with exactly $2$ crossing ordered choices, give $420$; and $\dfrac{420}{1260}=\dfrac{1}{3}$.)
