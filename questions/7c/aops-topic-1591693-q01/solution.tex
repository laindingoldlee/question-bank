Every integer is congruent to $0$, $1$ or $2$ modulo $3$, and $x^2\bmod 3$ depends only on $x\bmod3$:
\[
0^2=0,\qquad 1^2=1,\qquad 2^2=4\equiv1\pmod3 .
\]
So the solutions are exactly the integers not divisible by $3$:
\[
\boxed{x\equiv1\text{ or }x\equiv2\pmod3},
\]
that is, $x\equiv\pm1\pmod3$. (Equivalently, $x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)$ is divisible by the prime $3$ exactly when $3\mid x-1$ or $3\mid x+1$.)
