\textbf{Lower bound.} Besides the starting square there are $63$ squares to visit, and each move brings the knight to at most one new square. So at least $63$ moves are needed.

\textbf{$63$ moves suffice.} The four central squares are images of one another under the symmetries of the board, so it is enough to start on d4. The figure shows a \emph{knight's tour} starting there (shaded square, number $0$): the square marked $k$ is the square reached after move $k$. Consecutive numbers are always a knight's move apart, and each of the numbers $0,1,\dots,63$ appears exactly once, so the knight visits all $64$ squares in $63$ moves.

Hence the minimum is $\boxed{63}$. (Such a tour can be found by Warnsdorff's rule: always move to the available square from which the fewest onward moves remain.)
