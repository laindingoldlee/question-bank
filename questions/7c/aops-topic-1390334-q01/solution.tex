\textbf{(a)} This is completing the square followed by a difference of squares. For any $b,c$,
\[
x^2+bx+c=\left(x+\dfrac{b}{2}\right)^2-\left(\dfrac{b^2}{4}-c\right).
\]
If $D=\dfrac{b^2}{4}-c\ge0$, i.e. $b^2\ge4c$, then $D=\left(\sqrt D\right)^2$ and
\[
x^2+bx+c=\left(x+\dfrac{b}{2}+\sqrt D\right)\left(x+\dfrac{b}{2}-\sqrt D\right),
\]
so $x=-\dfrac{b}{2}\pm\sqrt{\dfrac{b^2}{4}-c}=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4c}}{2}$, which is the quadratic formula. If $b^2<4c$, then $\left(x+\dfrac{b}{2}\right)^2=D<0$ has no real solution, and the method (with real square roots) gives nothing. In the example, $D=64-4=60$ and $\sqrt{60}=2\sqrt{15}$, so the answers are correct.

\textbf{(b)} \emph{Not necessary:} $x^2+5x+6=0$ has $b=5<6=c$, yet $b^2=25\ge24=4c$ and the method gives the roots $-2$ and $-3$. \emph{Not sufficient:} $x^2+x+\dfrac{1}{2}=0$ has $b=1>\dfrac{1}{2}=c$, but $\left(x+\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2=-\dfrac{1}{4}$ has no real solution. The correct condition is $b^2\ge4c$; the method is the usual derivation of the quadratic formula.
