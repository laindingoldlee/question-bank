Since $31$ is prime, $(n-3)(n+3)\equiv0\pmod{31}$ implies $n\equiv3$ or $28\pmod{31}$. The first class is $3+31k$ for $0\le k\le32$, giving $33$ numbers. The second is $28+31k$ for $0\le k\le31$, giving $32$. The total is $\boxed{65}$.
