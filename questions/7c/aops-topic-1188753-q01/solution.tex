Let $d=a-c$. Since $b=ad>0$ and $a>0$, $d$ is a positive integer, so $d\ge1$, $c=a-d$ and $b=ad$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair B=(0,0),C=(4,0); real a=4,b=4,c=3;
real ang=acos((a^2+c^2-b^2)/(2*a*c)); pair A=c*dir(degrees(ang));
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
label("$A$",A,N); label("$B$",B,SW); label("$C$",C,SE);
label("$a$",(B+C)/2,S); label("$b=a(a-c)$",(A+C)/2,NE); label("$c$",(A+B)/2,NW);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

By the triangle inequality $a+c>b$, that is,
\[
a+(a-d)>ad\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2a>d(a+1)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d<\dfrac{2a}{a+1}<2 .
\]
Hence $d=1$, so $b=a$ (and $c=a-1$). The triangle has $a=b$, so it is isosceles. $\blacksquare$

(Only the integrality of the sides was used; the integer area is not needed. Heron triangles of this shape do exist: $a=b=17$, $c=16$ satisfies $b=a(a-c)$ and has area $\dfrac{16}{2}\sqrt{17^2-8^2}=8\cdot15=120$.)
