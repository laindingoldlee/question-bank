There is a unique polynomial of degree at most $9$ through these $10$ points, and it is the one of least degree. Let $Q(x)=x(x+1)P(x)-1$, of degree at most $11$, with roots $1,\dots,10$. So
\[Q(x)=(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-10)(ax+b).\]
At $x=0$: $Q(0)=-1=10!\,b$, so $b=-\dfrac1{10!}$. At $x=-1$: $Q(-1)=-1=11!\,(b-a)$, so $a=b+\dfrac1{11!}=-\dfrac{10}{11!}$. Then
\[Q(11)=10!\,(11a+b)=-\dfrac{110}{11}-1=-11,\]
so $132\,P(11)=Q(11)+1=-10$ and $P(11)=\boxed{-\dfrac5{66}}$.
