Yes. By the fundamental theorem of algebra a polynomial of degree $n\ge1$ factors as $c(x-r_1)\cdots(x-r_n)$. It has exactly one distinct zero iff all $r_i$ are equal, i.e. it is $\boxed{c(x-r)^n}$ with $c\ne0$; for example $(x-i)^3$.
