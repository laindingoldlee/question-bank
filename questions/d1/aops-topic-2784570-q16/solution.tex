For $n\ge6$, no value can be divisible by $n^2>25$, so the count is zero. For $n=1$ there are $25$ choices. For $n=2$, $a_2$ is one of six positive multiples of $4$, and $a_1$ has $24$ other choices, giving $144$. For $n=3$, $a_3$ is $9$ or $18$, neither a multiple of $4$, so there are $2\cdot6\cdot23=276$ tuples. For $n=4$, $a_4=16$, removing one choice for $a_2$, and leaving $22$ choices for $a_1$, giving $2\cdot5\cdot22=220$. For $n=5$, $a_5=25$ is another forced distinct value, giving $2\cdot5\cdot21=210$. Thus the counts for lengths $1$ through $5$ are $\boxed{25,144,276,220,210}$, and their total is $\boxed{875}$.
