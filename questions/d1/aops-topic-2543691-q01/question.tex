Call two points of $\mathbb Z^n$ (points with integer coordinates in $n$-dimensional space) \emph{neighbours} if they differ by $1$ in exactly one coordinate. Let $k<n$. Prove that it is impossible to assign to every point of $\mathbb Z^n$ a $k$-tuple of real numbers so that different points get different $k$-tuples and the $k$-tuples of any two neighbours differ by $1$ in exactly one entry (and agree in the other $k-1$ entries).
