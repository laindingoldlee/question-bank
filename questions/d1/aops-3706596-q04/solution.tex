First ignore positions. Enumerating five-element subsets of the ten digits with intersection sizes $2,2,1,1$ against the four guesses leaves exactly $\{0,1,3,8,9\}$ or $\{1,3,6,8,9\}$. This enumeration can be done by choosing a subset and checking the four counts, at most $\binom{10}{5}=252$ cases. In either set the second guess forces digit three in position two and nine in position four; the third forces one in position one. The second set would put six in position three or five, but the fourth and first guesses respectively forbid those positions. Thus the first set is required. Its remaining digits zero and eight occupy positions three and five; the first guess forbids eight in position three. The unique password is $\boxed{13098}$, which directly satisfies all four clues.
