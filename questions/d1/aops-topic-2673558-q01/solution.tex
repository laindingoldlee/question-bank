Mod $5$: $1\cdot1=1$, $2\cdot3=6\equiv1$ and $4\cdot4=16\equiv1$. So the inverses are $\boxed{1^{-1}=1,\ 2^{-1}=3,\ 3^{-1}=2,\ 4^{-1}=4}$.

Mod $6$: $2k$ is even for every integer $k$, and $2k-1$ is odd, so $6\nmid2k-1$; hence $2k\not\equiv1\pmod6$ and $2$ has no inverse. (Write $\mathbb{Z}_n$ for the integers modulo $n$ with this addition and multiplication. This is why $\mathbb{Z}_5$ is a field, while $\mathbb{Z}_6$ is not: $\mathbb{Z}_n$ is a field exactly when $n$ is prime.)
