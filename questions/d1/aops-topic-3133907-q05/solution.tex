(a) For $|r|<1$, differentiating the geometric series gives $\sum_{n\ge1}nr^n=\dfrac{r}{(1-r)^2}$. At $r=\dfrac{1}{3}$, the sum is $\dfrac{3}{4}+\dfrac{1}{2}=\boxed{\dfrac{5}{4}}$. (b) Each $a_n$ is positive and at most $\dfrac{2}{3}$, since $\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=\dfrac{n+2}{3(n+1)}<1$. Thus $0<\prod_{n=1}^Na_n\le(\dfrac{2}{3})^N\to0$, so the product limit is $\boxed0$.
