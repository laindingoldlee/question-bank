(a) $1434=2\cdot3\cdot239$ and $665=5\cdot7\cdot19$, so the ratio is $\dfrac{244+4}{31}=\boxed8$. (b) A prime power $q^e$ has $p(q^e)=q\le q^e$, with equality exactly when $e=1$. If there are at least two distinct prime factors, their product exceeds their sum: for distinct primes $r,s$, $rs>r+s$, starting with $2,3$, and adjoining another prime preserves strict inequality since $uv\ge u+v$ for integers $u,v\ge2$ with equality only when $u=v=2$. Thus $n>p(n)$ for composite $n$, and equality holds exactly for primes. (c) Composite iterates strictly decrease until a prime is reached. Start the reverse search at $2$: its preimages at most $100$ are $2,4,8,16,32,64$. The new preimages are $p^{-1}(8)=\{15,45,75\}$, $p^{-1}(16)=\{39,55,66\}$, $p^{-1}(32)=\{87\}$; then $p^{-1}(15)=\{26,52\}$, $p^{-1}(39)=\{74\}$; then $p^{-1}(26)=\{69\}$ and $p^{-1}(45)=\{86\}$. No other new preimages at most $100$ occur. These lists are obtained by taking sets of distinct primes with the indicated sum and all products of positive powers of those primes at most $100$. In particular a composite at most $100$ has prime-factor sum at most $49$ (the largest two-factor case is $2+47$); this rules out targets above $49$. The resulting set is $\{2,4,8,15,16,26,32,39,45,52,55,64,66,69,74,75,86,87\}$, of size $\boxed{18}$.
