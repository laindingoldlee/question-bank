Since $20^2+21^2=400+441=841=29^2$, there is a right triangle with legs $20$ and $21$ and hypotenuse $29$. Let $\alpha$ be its angle with $\cos\alpha=\dfrac{20}{29}$ and $\sin\alpha=\dfrac{21}{29}$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair O=(0,0), Pp=(20,0), Q=(20,21);
draw(O--Pp--Q--cycle,linewidth(0.8));
real t=1.5; draw(Pp+(-t,0)--Pp+(-t,t)--Pp+(0,t));
draw(arc(O,5,0,degrees(Q)),red); label("$\alpha$",O+6.5*dir(degrees(Q)/2),red);
label("$20$",(O+Pp)/2,S); label("$21$",(Pp+Q)/2,E); label("$29$",(O+Q)/2,NW);
\end{asy}

Then for every $x$,
\[20\sin x+21\cos x=29\left(\sin x\cos\alpha+\cos x\sin\alpha\right)=29\sin(x+\alpha)\le29,\]
with equality when $x+\alpha=90^\circ$, i.e. $x=90^\circ-\alpha$. So the maximum is exactly $\boxed{29}$ ($29.00$ to the nearest hundredth).
