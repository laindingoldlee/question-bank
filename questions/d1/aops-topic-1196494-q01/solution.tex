Look for numbers $\alpha,\beta$ with (second equation) $=\alpha\cdot$(first) $+\beta\cdot$(third) on the left-hand sides:
\[-3\alpha-5\beta=7,\qquad 2\alpha-4\beta=6,\qquad -4\alpha-3\beta=4.\]
The middle equation gives $\alpha=3+2\beta$; then the first gives $-9-11\beta=7$, so $\beta=-\dfrac{16}{11}$ and $\alpha=\dfrac{1}{11}$, and the third equation checks: $-\dfrac{4}{11}+\dfrac{48}{11}=4$. So the left side of the second equation is a combination of the other two, and the system is consistent exactly when $b=6\alpha+(-1)\beta=\dfrac{6}{11}+\dfrac{16}{11}=\boxed{2}$. Then the system reduces to two independent equations in three unknowns, which have infinitely many solutions; for any other $b$ there are none.
