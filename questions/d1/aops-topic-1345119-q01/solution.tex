Each cane costs $\dfrac{0.50}{4}=\dfrac{1}{8}$ dollar and sells for $\dfrac{0.50}{3}=\dfrac{1}{6}$ dollar, a profit of
\[
\dfrac{1}{6}-\dfrac{1}{8}=\dfrac{1}{24}\text{ dollar per cane}.
\]
To make $\$100$ he must sell $100\cdot24=\boxed{2400}$ canes. This is consistent with the bundles: $2400$ canes are $600$ bundles of $4$ (cost $\$300$) and $800$ bundles of $3$ (revenue $\$400$).
