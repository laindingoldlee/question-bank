Write $p=\dfrac{2}{3}$ and $q=\dfrac{1}{3}$.

\textbf{(a)} Classify the outcomes with a streak of at least four wins by where the \emph{first} such streak starts.
\begin{itemize}
\item It starts at game $1$: games $1$--$4$ are wins, probability $p^4$.
\item It starts at game $i\in\{2,3,4,5\}$: game $i-1$ is a loss and games $i$ to $i+3$ are wins. The games before $i-1$ (at most three of them) cannot contain a streak of four, so every such outcome really has its first streak at $i$. Probability $qp^4$ for each $i$.
\end{itemize}
These cases are disjoint, so
\[P=p^4+4qp^4=p^4(1+4q)=\dfrac{16}{81}\cdot\dfrac{7}{3}=\boxed{\dfrac{112}{243}}\left(=\dfrac{3024}{6561}\right).\]

\textbf{(b)} Let $A_i$ be the event that games $i,i+1,i+2,i+3$ are all wins ($i=1,\dots,5$). Inclusion-exclusion needs the probabilities of \emph{all} intersections of these events, not the number of streaks of each length. The intersection of several $A_i$ is the event that all games from the smallest $i$ to the largest $i$ plus $3$ are wins. So:
\begin{itemize}
\item pairs: $4$ pairs give $5$ consecutive wins, $3$ give $6$, $2$ give $7$ and $1$ (namely $A_1\cap A_5$) gives all $8$; that is $10$ pairs, not $4$;
\item triples: $3p^6+4p^7+3p^8$; quadruples: $2p^7+3p^8$; all five: $p^8$.
\end{itemize}
The correct inclusion-exclusion is therefore
\[5p^4-\left(4p^5+3p^6+2p^7+p^8\right)+\left(3p^6+4p^7+3p^8\right)-\left(2p^7+3p^8\right)+p^8=5p^4-4p^5,\]
because all the $p^6$, $p^7$ and $p^8$ terms cancel. This equals $p^4(5-4p)=\dfrac{112}{243}$, agreeing with (a). The student's first two terms happen to be right, but the terms $+3p^6-2p^7+p^8$ come from treating ``win exactly the games of a streak of length $k$'' as if those events were the intersections in inclusion-exclusion; they are not, so the answer $\dfrac{4240}{6561}$ is too large.
