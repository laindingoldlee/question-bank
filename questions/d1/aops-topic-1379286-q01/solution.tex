From the similarity $\triangle PAB\sim\triangle PCA$,
\[
\dfrac{PA}{PC}=\dfrac{PB}{PA}=\dfrac{AB}{CA}=\dfrac{8}{6}=\dfrac{4}{3} .
\]
Let $PC=x$. Then $PA=\dfrac{4}{3}x$ and $PB=\dfrac{4}{3}PA=\dfrac{16}{9}x$. But $PB=PC+CB=x+7$, so
\[
\dfrac{16}{9}x=x+7\iff\dfrac{7}{9}x=7\iff x=9 .
\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(8cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair B=(0,0),C=(7,0),A=(5.5,sqrt(33.75)),P=(16,0);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9)); draw(C--P,linewidth(0.9)); draw(A--P,blue);
dot(P);
label("$A$",A,N); label("$B$",B,SW); label("$C$",C,S); label("$P$",P,SE);
label("$8$",(A+B)/2,NW); label("$6$",(A+C)/2,W); label("$7$",(B+C)/2,S);
label("$9$",(C+P)/2,S,blue); label("$12$",(A+P)/2,NE,blue);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

So $PC=\boxed{9}$ (and $PA=12$, $PB=16$; indeed $PA^2=PB\cdot PC=144$, as the similarity requires).
