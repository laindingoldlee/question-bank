Let the player have had $h$ hits in $a$ at-bats at the end of last week. Then
\[
h=0.256a,\qquad \dfrac{h+12}{a+35}=0.275 .
\]
The second equation gives $h+12=0.275a+9.625$. Substituting $h=0.256a$:
\[
0.256a+12=0.275a+9.625\ \Longrightarrow\ 0.019a=2.375\ \Longrightarrow\ a=125,
\]
and $h=0.256\cdot125=32$. Now the player has $32+12=44$ hits in $125+35=160$ at-bats. So the answer is $\boxed{44\text{ hits and }160\text{ at-bats}}$. Check: $\dfrac{32}{125}=0.256$ and $\dfrac{44}{160}=0.275$.

The student's equation has two errors. The new average is $0.275=\dfrac{11}{40}$, not $\dfrac{3}{8}=0.375$. Also, writing the old totals as $256x$ hits and $1000x$ at-bats is fine (here $x=\dfrac{1}{8}$), but it forces nothing to be an integer, so a non-integer $x$ is not a sign of a mistake. With $\dfrac{11}{40}$ the equation becomes $40(256x+12)=11(1000x+35)$, i.e.\ $10240x+480=11000x+385$, so $x=\dfrac{95}{760}=\dfrac{1}{8}$, giving the same answer.
