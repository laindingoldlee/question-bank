Let the number have digits $d_1d_2\cdots d_{12}$, each $1$ or $2$, and let $k$ be the number of digits equal to $2$. Since $99=9\cdot11$ with $\gcd(9,11)=1$, we need divisibility by $9$ and by $11$.

\textbf{Divisibility by $9$.} The digit sum is $12+k$, with $0\le k\le12$, so $12\le12+k\le24$. The only multiple of $9$ in this range is $18$, so $k=6$: exactly six digits are $2$.

\textbf{Divisibility by $11$.} The alternating sum $(d_1+d_3+\cdots+d_{11})-(d_2+d_4+\cdots+d_{12})$ must be a multiple of $11$. If $a$ of the six odd positions and $b$ of the six even positions hold a $2$, the alternating sum is $(6+a)-(6+b)=a-b$, where $a+b=6$. Then $-6\le a-b\le6$, so $a-b=0$ and $a=b=3$.

So the numbers are exactly those with three $2$'s among the six odd positions and three $2$'s among the six even positions. The number of them is
\[\binom63\binom63=20\cdot20=\boxed{400}.\]
