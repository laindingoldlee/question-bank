We have $x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)$ and $4x-4=4(x-1)$, so
\[
x^2-1=(4x-4)\cdot\dfrac{x+1}{4}.
\]
(a) The quotient $\dfrac{x+1}{4}=\dfrac{1}{4}x+\dfrac{1}{4}$ has rational coefficients, so \textbf{yes}, $4x-4$ divides $x^2-1$ over the rationals (or reals).

(b) If $x^2-1=(4x-4)q(x)$, then comparing degrees and leading coefficients, $q$ is linear with leading coefficient $\dfrac{1}{4}$, which is not an integer. (Equivalently, the quotient is unique and equals $\dfrac{x+1}{4}$.) So \textbf{no}, it does not divide over the integers.

\[
\boxed{\text{(a) Yes; (b) No}}
\]
