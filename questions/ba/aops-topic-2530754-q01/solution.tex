\textbf{(a)} Each half-year pays $0.03\cdot1000=\$30$, and there are $6$ half-years, so the interest is \$180 and the total is $\boxed{\$1180}$.

In general, simple interest is always computed on the original principal $P$. With $m$ payments a year at rate $\dfrac rm$ for $t$ years there are $mt$ payments of $P\cdot\dfrac rm$ each, in total $mt\cdot\dfrac{Pr}m=Prt$. So the amount is $A=P(1+rt)$ whatever $m$ is.

\textbf{(b)} With compounding, each half-year multiplies the balance by $1.03$:
\[1000\cdot1.03^6\approx\boxed{\$1194.05}.\]
This is about \$14.05 more, because interest is also earned on earlier interest.
