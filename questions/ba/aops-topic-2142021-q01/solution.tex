In an isosceles trapezoid the two legs are equal, so the legs are $2.5$ and $2.5$ and the parallel sides are $7$ and $10$. (The equal sides cannot be the parallel sides: then $ABCD$ would be a parallelogram whose other two sides, $7$ and $10$, would have to be equal.) Let $AB=10$ and $DC=7$, and drop perpendiculars from $D$ and $C$ to $AB$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0), B=(10,0), C=(8.5,2), D=(1.5,2);
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(D--(1.5,0),blue+dashed); draw(C--(8.5,0),blue+dashed);
draw((1.5,0.3)--(1.8,0.3)--(1.8,0)); draw((8.5,0.3)--(8.2,0.3)--(8.2,0));
label("$A$",A,SW); label("$B$",B,SE); label("$C$",C,NE); label("$D$",D,NW);
label("$7$",(D+C)/2,N); label("$2.5$",(A+D)/2,W); label("$2.5$",(B+C)/2,E);
label("$1.5$",(0.75,0),S); label("$1.5$",(9.25,0),S); label("$7$",(5,0),S);
label("$2$",(1.5,1),E,blue);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

By symmetry they cut off two segments of length $\dfrac{10-7}{2}=1.5$, so by the Pythagorean theorem the height is
\[\sqrt{2.5^2-1.5^2}=\sqrt{6.25-2.25}=2 .\]
The area is
\[\dfrac{10+7}{2}\cdot2=\boxed{17}.\]
