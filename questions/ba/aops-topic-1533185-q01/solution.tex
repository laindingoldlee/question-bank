Since $12=4\cdot3$ with $\gcd(4,3)=1$, it suffices to find $2^p$ modulo $4$ and modulo $3$.
\begin{itemize}
\item As $p\ge2$, $4\mid2^p$, so $2^p\equiv0\pmod4$.
\item $2\equiv-1\pmod3$ and $p$ is odd, so $2^p\equiv(-1)^p=-1\equiv2\pmod3$.
\end{itemize}
The number $8$ satisfies both ($8\equiv0\pmod4$, $8\equiv2\pmod3$), and by the Chinese remainder theorem the residue modulo $12$ is unique, so $2^p\equiv8\pmod{12}$. Hence $2^p-1\equiv7\pmod{12}$. $\blacksquare$

(Alternatively: $2^3=8$ and $2^{p+2}=4\cdot2^p\equiv4\cdot8=32\equiv8\pmod{12}$, so by induction every odd $p\ge3$ gives $2^p\equiv8$.)
