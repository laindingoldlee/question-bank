If the printed digits form the integer $D$ (allowing initial zeros), the terminating decimal equals $\dfrac{D}{10^{200}}$, so it is rational.
A rational decimal expansion is eventually periodic. If the new infinite tail had a period of length $p$ after some point, it could not contain both arbitrarily late $1$s and arbitrarily long strings of zeros: a run of at least $p$ zeros in the periodic portion would force every position of the repeating block to be zero, so no later $1$ could occur. Our tail has both features, a contradiction. Hence the second number is irrational.
