Use generating functions or casework on the restricted children. Let the oldest get $4+a$, the next $2b$, the third $c\le2$, the youngest $d\le1$, and the last two $u,v\ge0$. Then $a+2b+c+d+u+v=10$.

For fixed $a,b,c,d$ with $r=10-a-2b-c-d\ge0$, the two free children can share $r$ in $r+1$ ways. Fix $c+d=j$: the pairs $(c,d)$ give $j=0$ ($1$ way), $1$ ($2$ ways), $2$ ($2$ ways), $3$ ($1$ way). For a fixed total $m=a+2b$ the number of $(a,b)$ is $\left\lfloor\dfrac {m}{2}\right\rfloor+1$. So the count is
\[
\sum_{j}w_j\sum_{m=0}^{10-j}\left(\left\lfloor\dfrac {m}{2}\right\rfloor+1\right)(11-j-m),
\]
with $w_0=1,w_1=2,w_2=2,w_3=1$. The inner sums are $161$ ($j=0$), $125$ ($j=1$), $95$ ($j=2$) and $70$ ($j=3$), so the total is
\[
161+2\cdot125+2\cdot95+70=\boxed{671}.
\]
