Let $x_k$ be the number of pencils before the $k$th kid's turn, so $x_1$ is the original pile. The $k$th kid needs $x_k\ge1$ and $5\mid x_k-1$, takes $1+\dfrac{x_k-1}{5}$ pencils and leaves
\[x_{k+1}=\dfrac{4}{5}\left(x_k-1\right).\]

\textbf{A substitution.} Put $y_k=x_k+4$. Then
\[y_{k+1}=\dfrac{4}{5}\left(x_k-1\right)+4=\dfrac{4}{5}\left(x_k+4\right)=\dfrac{4}{5}\,y_k,\]
and the condition $5\mid x_k-1$ becomes $5\mid y_k$. Iterating, $y_k=\dfrac{4^{k-1}}{5^{k-1}}\,y_1$.

\textbf{The process works only if $5^n\mid y_1$.} Suppose all $n$ kids can take their pencils. For each $k\le n$, the integer $y_k$ is divisible by $5$, so $5^k$ divides $5^{k-1}y_k=4^{k-1}y_1$. Since $\gcd\left(4^{k-1},5\right)=1$, this gives $5^k\mid y_1$. Taking $k=n$: $5^n\mid y_1=x_1+4$. As $x_1+4\ge5>0$, we get $x_1+4\ge5^n$, so $x_1\ge5^n-4$.

\textbf{$5^n-4$ pencils are enough.} If $x_1=5^n-4$, then $y_1=5^n$ and $y_k=4^{k-1}5^{n-k+1}$ for $k=1,\dots,n+1$. For $k\le n$ this is an integer divisible by $5$, so $x_k-1$ is divisible by $5$, and $x_k=y_k-4\ge5\cdot4^{k-1}-4\ge1$, so the $k$th kid can take $1$ pencil. Thus all $n$ kids can take their pencils (and $4^n-4$ pencils are left at the end).

Therefore the smallest possible original pile is $\boxed{5^n-4}$. $\blacksquare$

(For example, $n=2$: from $21$ pencils the first kid takes $1+4$, leaving $16$; the second takes $1+3$, leaving $12$.)
