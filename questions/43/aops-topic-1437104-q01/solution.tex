Giving out the jerseys means splitting the students into a red, a blue and a green team of $4$ each. Let the teams have $g_1,g_2,g_3$ girls; then they have $4-g_1,4-g_2,4-g_3$ boys. We need $1\le g_i\le3$ and $g_1+g_2+g_3=6$, so $(g_1,g_2,g_3)$ is $(2,2,2)$ or one of the $6$ orderings of $(1,2,3)$.

For given $(g_1,g_2,g_3)$ the girls can be split in $\dbinom{6}{g_1,\,g_2,\,g_3}$ ways and the boys in $\dbinom{6}{4-g_1,\,4-g_2,\,4-g_3}$ ways.
\begin{itemize}
\item $(2,2,2)$: $\dfrac{6!}{2!\,2!\,2!}\cdot\dfrac{6!}{2!\,2!\,2!}=90\cdot90=8100$.
\item Each ordering of $(1,2,3)$ (the boys then form an ordering of $(3,2,1)$): $\dfrac{6!}{1!\,2!\,3!}\cdot\dfrac{6!}{3!\,2!\,1!}=60\cdot60=3600$, and there are $6$ orderings: $21600$.
\end{itemize}
In total $8100+21600=\boxed{29700}$.
