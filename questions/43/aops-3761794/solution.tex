The strongly connected components of a tournament have a unique linear order, with every intercomponent edge pointing forward. If their sizes are $s_i$, exactly $\sum_{i<j}s_is_j$ ordered pairs are unreachable. The desired fraction means there are $\dfrac{42}{7} = 6$ such pairs. Any nontrivial partition other than $6+1$ has at least $5\cdot2=10$ cross pairs; hence there must be a strongly connected six-vertex tournament and a singleton, in either order. Let $S_n$ count strongly connected labelled tournaments. Choosing the unique first component gives $S_n=2^{\binom n2}-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\binom nk S_k2^{\binom{n-k}2}$. Starting with $S_1=1$, this gives $S_2=0,S_3=2,S_4=24,S_5=544,S_6=22320$. Thus the answer is $7\cdot2\cdot22320=\boxed{312480}$.
