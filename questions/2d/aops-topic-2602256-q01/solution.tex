\textbf{Pairwise coprime.} If a prime $p$ divides two of $a,b,c$, then by $a^2+b^2=c^2$ it divides the square of the third, hence the third, contradicting $\gcd(a,b,c)=1$.

\textbf{Exactly one leg is even.} Not both are even, since they are coprime. If both were odd, then $c^2=a^2+b^2\equiv1+1=2\pmod4$, but squares are $0$ or $1\pmod4$. So exactly one leg is even; say $b$. Then $a$ and $c$ are odd.

\textbf{Every primitive triple has the form.} Write $b^2=c^2-a^2=(c-a)(c+a)$. Both factors are even, so put $c+a=2u$ and $c-a=2v$ with integers $u>v>0$. Then $c=u+v$ and $a=u-v$, so a common divisor of $u$ and $v$ divides $a$ and $c$; hence $\gcd(u,v)=1$. Now $\left(\dfrac b2\right)^2=uv$ with $u$, $v$ coprime, so each of $u$, $v$ is a perfect square (each prime appears in $uv$ to an even power and lies in only one of them). Write $u=m^2$, $v=n^2$ with $m>n\ge1$. Then
\[a=m^2-n^2,\qquad c=m^2+n^2,\qquad b=2\sqrt{uv}=2mn.\]
Here $\gcd(m,n)=1$ because $\gcd(u,v)=1$, and $m$, $n$ have opposite parity because $a=m^2-n^2$ is odd.

\textbf{Every such $m,n$ gives a primitive triple.} The identity $(m^2-n^2)^2+(2mn)^2=m^4+2m^2n^2+n^4=(m^2+n^2)^2$ gives a triple, with $b=2mn$ even. If a prime $p$ divided both $a=m^2-n^2$ and $c=m^2+n^2$, it would divide $2m^2$ and $2n^2$. Since $a$ is odd, $p\ne2$, so $p\mid m$ and $p\mid n$, contradicting $\gcd(m,n)=1$. So the triple is primitive. $\blacksquare$

For example, $(m,n)=(2,1),(3,2),(4,1)$ give $(3,4,5)$, $(5,12,13)$ and $(15,8,17)$.
