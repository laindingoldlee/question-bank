Let $O$ be the common centre, let $R$ be the circumradius and $r$ the inradius, and let $M$ be the midpoint of the side $AB$. The incircle touches $AB$ at $M$, so $OM=r$ and $OM\perp AB$, while $OA=R$ and $MA=\dfrac12$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real R=1/(2*Sin(22.5)), r=1/(2*Tan(22.5));
pair[] V; for(int k=0;k<8;++k) V.push(R*dir(22.5+45k));
draw(circle((0,0),R),gray(0.5)); draw(circle((0,0),r),gray(0.5)+dashed);
path p=V[0]; for(int k=1;k<8;++k) p=p--V[k];
draw(p--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
pair O=(0,0), A=V[6], B=V[5], M=(A+B)/2;
draw(O--A,red+linewidth(0.8)); draw(O--M,blue+linewidth(0.8)); draw(M--A,linewidth(1.2));
draw(M+(0.07,0)--M+(0.07,0.07)--M+(0,0.07));
dot(O); dot(M); dot(A); dot(B);
label("$O$",O,N); label("$A$",A,SE); label("$B$",B,SW); label("$M$",M,S);
label("$R$",(O+A)/2,NE,red); label("$r$",(O+M)/2,W,blue);
label("$\dfrac{1}{2}$",(M+A)/2,S,fontsize(8pt));
\end{asy}

By the Pythagorean theorem in the right triangle $OMA$,
\[R^2-r^2=MA^2=\dfrac14.\]
So the difference of the areas is
\[\pi R^2-\pi r^2=\pi\left(R^2-r^2\right)=\boxed{\textbf{(C) }\dfrac{\pi}{4}}.\]
(The same argument shows that the answer is $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ for a regular polygon with side $1$ and any number of sides.)
