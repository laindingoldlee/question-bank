After the first trip she is in Havennew, and $7$ random trips remain. Let $h_n$ be the probability of being in Havennew after $n$ random trips, so $h_0=1$. She is in Havennew after $n+1$ trips exactly when she was elsewhere after $n$ trips and then chose Havennew (probability $\dfrac{1}{3}$):
\[
h_{n+1}=\dfrac{1-h_n}{3}.
\]
Writing $h_n=\dfrac{1}{4}+e_n$ gives $e_{n+1}=-\dfrac{1}{3}e_n$ and $e_0=\dfrac{3}{4}$, so
\[
h_n=\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{3}{4}\left(-\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^n,\qquad q=h_7=\dfrac{1}{4}-\dfrac{3}{4\cdot 2187}=\dfrac{1}{4}-\dfrac{1}{2916}.
\]
Starting from Havennew, the other three cities play symmetric roles, so each has probability $\dfrac{1-h_7}{3}$; in particular $p=\dfrac{1-q}{3}$. Hence
\[
p+q=\dfrac{1+2q}{3}=\dfrac{1}{3}\left(\dfrac{3}{2}-\dfrac{1}{1458}\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{4374}=\dfrac{2186}{4374}=\boxed{\dfrac{1093}{2187}}.
\]
