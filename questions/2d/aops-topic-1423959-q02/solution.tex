Write $T=420^N$, where $N$ is a tower of $419$ copies of $420$; in particular $N$ is a multiple of $20$ (indeed of $420$). Since $420^N=10^N\cdot42^N$ and $42^N$ is not divisible by $5$, the decimal expansion of $T$ is that of $42^N$ followed by $N$ zeros. So we need the last two digits of $42^N$, that is, $42^N\bmod100$.
\begin{itemize}
\item Modulo $4$: $42^N\equiv0$, since $4\mid42^2$ and $N\ge2$.
\item Modulo $25$: $42\equiv17$, and $\gcd(17,25)=1$, so by Euler's theorem $17^{20}\equiv1\pmod{25}$. As $20\mid N$, $42^N\equiv1\pmod{25}$.
\end{itemize}
The number between $0$ and $99$ that is $0\bmod4$ and $1\bmod25$ is $76$. So $42^N$ ends in $76$; both digits are nonzero, and their product is $7\cdot6=\boxed{42}$.
