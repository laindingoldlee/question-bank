Every interior crossing comes from exactly one set of four vertices: the four vertices form a convex quadrilateral whose two diagonals cross once, and conversely two crossing diagonals have four distinct endpoints. Since no three diagonals are concurrent, distinct sets of four vertices give distinct points. So an $n$-gon has $\dbinom n4$ interior crossing points:
\[
\dbinom64=\boxed{15},\qquad\dbinom84=\boxed{70},\qquad\dbinom{10}4=\boxed{210}.
\]
(For \emph{regular} polygons some diagonals are concurrent, so there are fewer points; for instance in the regular hexagon the three long diagonals meet at the centre, giving $15-2=13$ points.)
