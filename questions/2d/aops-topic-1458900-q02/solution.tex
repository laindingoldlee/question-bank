Since $y\ne0$ (it is a denominator) we have $y^2>0$, so $x=\dfrac{10^8}{y^2}>0$; then $\dfrac{x^3}{y}>0$ forces $y>0$.

Multiply the first equation by the square of the second:
\[
xy^2\cdot\dfrac{x^6}{y^2}=x^7=10^8\cdot10^{20}=10^{28},
\]
so $x=10^4$ (the only real seventh root). Then $y^2=\dfrac{10^8}{10^4}=10^4$ and $y>0$ give $y=10^2$. Check: $\dfrac{(10^4)^3}{10^2}=10^{10}$. The unique solution is
\[
\boxed{x=10000,\ y=100}.
\]
