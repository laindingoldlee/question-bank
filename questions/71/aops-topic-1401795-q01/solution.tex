The total of all six cards is $31$. If a set of cards has sum $s<31$, the complementary (nonempty) set has sum $31-s$; so $s$ is obtainable exactly when $31-s$ is (for $1\le s\le30$), and $31$ is obtainable (all cards).

\textbf{Small sums.} With only $2$s we get $2$ and $4$; any set containing a card $\ge5$ has sum at least $5$, and the sums $5,7$ come from $5$, $5+2$. So $1$ and $3$ are impossible, and $6$ is impossible too: $6$ would need cards from $\{2,2,5\}$ (larger cards are too big), and their subset sums are $2,4,5,7,9$. The sums $7,\dots,15$ are all obtainable:
\[
7=5+2,\ 8,\ 9,\ 10=8+2,\ 11=9+2,\ 12=5+5+2,\ 13=8+5,\ 14=9+5,\ 15=8+5+2 .
\]
\textbf{Large sums.} By the complement rule, $16,\dots,24$ (from $15,\dots,7$), $26=31-5$, $27=31-4$, $29=31-2$ and $31$ are obtainable, while $25=31-6$, $28=31-3$ and $30=31-1$ are not.

So exactly the six integers $1,3,6,25,28,30$ cannot be obtained, and the answer is $\boxed{6}$.
