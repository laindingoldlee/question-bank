Divide by $a-2$, which must be nonzero, to make the polynomial monic. Both roots lie strictly between the endpoints exactly when its discriminant is nonnegative, its vertex is inside the interval, and its values at both endpoints are positive. Here
\[
\Delta=4(6-a),\quad x_{\mathrm{vertex}}=-\dfrac{a}{a-2},\quad
f(-2)=a-5,\quad f(1)=4a+1.
\]
For $a<2$, positivity after division requires $a-5<0$ and $4a+1<0$, so $a<-\dfrac{1}{4}$. The vertex and discriminant conditions then hold automatically.

For $a>2$, endpoint positivity requires $a>5$. The vertex is then in $(-2,1)$, and the discriminant imposes $a\le6$. Hence the answer is
\[
\boxed{(-\infty,-\dfrac{1}{4})\ \cup\ (5,6]}.
\]
At $a=6$, the repeated root is $-\dfrac{3}{2}$, so that endpoint is included.
