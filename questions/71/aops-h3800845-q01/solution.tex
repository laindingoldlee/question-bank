No entry is zero. Since $2016=2^5\cdot3^2\cdot7$, the absolute values of nonunit entries consume a total of eight prime factors, counted with multiplicity. At most five distinct signed prime entries can coexist: $2,-2,3,-3$, and at most one of $7,-7$, because the exponent of seven is one. If there were seven distinct nonunit entries, at least two would be composite in absolute value, consuming at least $5+2+2=9$ prime factors (or more if fewer than five are prime). This is impossible. Thus at most six distinct nonunits and the two units $1,-1$ can occur, for at most eight distinct values.
The bound is attained by $2008$ copies of $1$, one copy of $-1$, and the seven entries
\[2,-2,3,-3,4,7,-2.\]
There are $2016$ entries. The seven displayed entries have sum $9$ and product $-2016$, so the total sum is $2008-1+9=2016$ and the total product is $2016$. Their distinct values are $-3,-2,-1,1,2,3,4,7$. Hence the answer is $\boxed8$.
