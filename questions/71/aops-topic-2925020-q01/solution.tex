(a) $n^n=9n$, so $n^{n-1}=9$. Then $n=3$ ($n=1,2$ give $1,2$, and $n\ge4$ gives at least $64$). $\boxed{n=3}$.

(b) Checking small values: $n=1$: $2\ne110$; $n=2$: $6<10(2^{\sqrt2}+10)$; $n=3$: $30<10(3^{\sqrt3}+10)\approx167$; $n=4$: $256+4=260=10(4^2+10)$. \checkmark. For $n\ge5$, $n^n=n^{\sqrt n}\cdot n^{n-\sqrt n}\ge85\,n^{\sqrt n}$ (as $n^{n-\sqrt n}\ge5^{5-\sqrt5}>85$), and $n^{\sqrt n}\ge5^{\sqrt5}>36$. So $n^n+n>10n^{\sqrt n}+75\cdot36>10n^{\sqrt n}+100$, and there are no solutions with $n\ge5$. Hence $\boxed{n=4}$.
