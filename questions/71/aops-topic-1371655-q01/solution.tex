Seen from above, the centres of the three big balls are at height $6$ above $P$ and form an equilateral triangle of side $12$ (the balls are mutually tangent). By symmetry the centre of the fourth ball, of radius $r$, lies above the centre of this triangle, at horizontal distance $\dfrac{12}{\sqrt3}=4\sqrt3$ from each big centre, and at height $r$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real h=4*sqrt(3);
pair O1=h*dir(90),O2=h*dir(210),O3=h*dir(330);
draw(circle(O1,6),blue); draw(circle(O2,6),blue); draw(circle(O3,6),blue);
draw(O1--O2--O3--cycle,gray+dashed);
draw(circle((0,0),2),red);
draw((0,0)--O3,dashed); dot((0,0)); dot(O1);dot(O2);dot(O3);
label("$12$",(O2+O3)/2+(0,-3.7),gray+fontsize(9pt));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

In the vertical plane through a big centre and the small centre, the two centres are $6+r$ apart (the balls are tangent), the horizontal distance is $4\sqrt3$ and the vertical distance is $6-r$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real r=2, R=6, h=4*sqrt(3);
draw((-7,0)--(h+3.5,0),linewidth(0.9));
draw(circle((0,R),R),blue); draw(circle((h,r),r),red);
pair Ob=(0,R), Os=(h,r);
draw(Ob--Os,dashed); draw(Ob--(h,R),gray+dashed); draw((h,R)--Os,gray+dashed);
dot(Ob); dot(Os);
label("$6+r$",(Ob+Os)/2,SW,fontsize(9pt));
label("$4\sqrt3$",(h/2,R),N,gray+fontsize(9pt)); label("$6-r$",(h,5),E,gray+fontsize(9pt));
label("plane $P$",(-6.5,0),S,fontsize(9pt));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

By the Pythagorean theorem,
\[
(6+r)^2=(4\sqrt3)^2+(6-r)^2\iff 24r=48\iff r=\boxed{2}.
\]
(The same equation holds if the fourth ball is larger than the others, with vertical distance $r-6$, so $r=2$ is the only possibility.)
