\textbf{The statement is false:} $7=2+5$.

\textbf{All such numbers.} Let $n$ have last digit $7$ and suppose $n=p+q$ with primes $p\le q$. Since $n$ is odd, $p$ and $q$ have different parity (two odd primes have an even sum, and $2+2=4$). So $p=2$ and $q=n-2$, whose last digit is $5$. A number ending in $5$ is divisible by $5$, and the only such prime is $5$. Hence $q=5$ and $n=7$.

So $\boxed{7}$ is the only number ending in $7$ that is a sum of two primes; for every other number ending in $7$ the statement is true.
