Place the apex at the center of a cube of side $2$, and take one face as the pyramid's base. Joining the center to all six faces partitions the cube into six congruent pyramids of the required dimensions. The unit sphere is inside the cube and is centered at that common apex. Cube symmetries permute the six pyramids and preserve the sphere, so their intersections with the sphere have equal volumes and partition it up to boundaries. Each volume is $\dfrac{1}{6}\cdot\dfrac{4}{3}\pi=\dfrac{2\pi}{9}$. Therefore $\boxed{k=\dfrac{2}{9}}$.
