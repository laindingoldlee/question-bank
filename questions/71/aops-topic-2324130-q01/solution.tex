A three-digit number $\overline{abc}$ is a time $a{:}bc$ exactly when $1\le a\le9$ and $\overline{bc}\le59$. (Times $10{:}00$ to $12{:}59$ give four-digit numbers.) The three-digit squares are $10^2,\dots,31^2$:
\[100,121,144,169,196,225,256,289,324,361,400,441,484,529,576,625,676,729,784,841,900,961.\]
Those whose last two digits are at most $59$ are
\[100,\ 121,\ 144,\ 225,\ 256,\ 324,\ 400,\ 441,\ 529,\ 625,\ 729,\ 841,\ 900,\]
thirteen numbers in all.

\textbf{(a)} $\boxed{100,121,144,225,256,324,400,441,529,625}$.

\textbf{(b)} The squares $1^2,\dots,9^2$ have fewer than three digits, and $31^2$ is not among $1^2,\dots,30^2$ (and is not clock-perfect anyway). So the answer is all $\boxed{13}$ clock-perfect numbers.

\textbf{(c)} Each of the $13$ times appears twice in $24$ hours (am and pm), for one minute each time: $\boxed{26}$ minutes.
