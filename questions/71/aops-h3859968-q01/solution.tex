The repeating decimal is defined as the limit of its finite truncations:
\[
0.\overline9=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{9}{10^k}
=\dfrac{\dfrac{9}{10}}{1-\dfrac{1}{10}}=1.
\]
Equivalently, its first $N$ digits give $1-10^{-N}$, whose limit is $1$.
A decimal expansion has a digit at each positive integer position; there is no position after all of those positions. Thus infinitely many zeros followed by a final one does not define a nonzero real decimal. The actual difference $0.\overline9-1$ is zero, and division by it is undefined.
