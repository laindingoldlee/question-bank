Let $R_n$ be the greatest number of regions for $n$ circles. One circle gives $2$ regions. When the $k$th circle is added, it meets each of the $k-1$ earlier circles in at most $2$ points, so in at most $2(k-1)$ points. If there are $m\ge1$ such points, they cut the new circle into $m$ arcs; each arc runs through one existing region and splits it into at most two parts, so at most $m\le2(k-1)$ regions are added. (If $m=0$, exactly $1$ region is added.) Hence
\[R_n\le2+\sum_{k=2}^{n}2(k-1)=2+n(n-1)=n^2-n+2.\]
Equality holds when every two circles meet in two points and no three circles pass through a common point (for example, equal circles with centres close together): then every arc really does split a region into two. For three circles this gives $8$ regions:

\end{asy}

For $n=100$: $100^2-100+2=\boxed{9902}$.
