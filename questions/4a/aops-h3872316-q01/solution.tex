Encode clockwise and counterclockwise by two binary symbols. A person receives exactly one point precisely when their two neighbours have the same symbol. The comparisons therefore form two separate four-cycles, one on the odd-numbered suspects and one on the even-numbered suspects.

In a binary four-cycle, the number $r$ of changes is $0,2$, or $4$, with counts $2,12,2$, respectively. Stability requires four equalities in total, equivalently $r_{\rm odd}+r_{\rm even}=4$. Thus there are
\[
2\cdot2+12\cdot12+2\cdot2=152
\]
stable arrangements.

Suspects $1,5$ are opposite vertices of the odd four-cycle. Their symbols agree in both strings with $r=0$ and both strings with $r=4$. Among the twelve strings with $r=2$, exactly four have equal opposite symbols: choose their common value in two ways, then choose which of the other two positions differs in two ways. Hence the favourable count is
\[
2\cdot2+4\cdot12+2\cdot2=56.
\]
The probability is $\dfrac{56}{152}=\dfrac{7}{19}$, and $\boxed{m+n=26}$.
