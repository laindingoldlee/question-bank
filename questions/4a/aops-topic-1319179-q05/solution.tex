Let the sides be $a,b,c$ and the medians to them $m_a,m_b,m_c$. By the previous result (a median is shorter than the average of the two sides at its vertex),
\[
m_a<\dfrac{b+c}{2},\qquad m_b<\dfrac{c+a}{2},\qquad m_c<\dfrac{a+b}{2}.
\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(1,3),B=(0,0),C=(4,0); draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9)); draw(A--(B+C)/2,red+dashed); draw(B--(A+C)/2,red+dashed); draw(C--(A+B)/2,red+dashed);
label("$A$",A,N); label("$B$",B,W); label("$C$",C,E);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Adding, $m_a+m_b+m_c<a+b+c$. $\blacksquare$ (Proof of the lemma: extend the median $AM$ to $D$ with $MD=AM$; then $ABDC$ is a parallelogram, $BD=AC$, and $2AM=AD<AB+BD=AB+AC$.)
