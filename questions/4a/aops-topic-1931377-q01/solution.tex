Both $x$ and $y$ divide their lcm $36$, so they are among the divisors $1,2,3,4,6,9,12,18,36$. As $x+y=26$, both are at most $25$, so $x\in\{1,2,3,4,6,9,12,18\}$ and then
\[
y=26-x\in\{25,24,23,22,20,17,14,8\}.
\]
None of these is a divisor of $36$. Hence no such $x,y$ exist, and the greatest common factor asked for in the original problem does not exist. $\blacksquare$

(Another way: if $g=\gcd(x,y)$, $x=ga$, $y=gb$ with $\gcd(a,b)=1$, then $gab=36$ and $g(a+b)=26$, so $g\mid\gcd(36,26)=2$. For $g=1$: $a+b=26$, $ab=36$, and $t^2-26t+36$ has discriminant $532$, not a square. For $g=2$: $a+b=13$, $ab=18$, and $t^2-13t+18$ has discriminant $97$, not a square.)
