There are $\dfrac{6!}{3!2!}=60$ arrangements. First arrange the non-red balls: $WWB,WBW,BWW$. Insert the three reds into three of the four gaps, at most one per gap. For $WWB$ or $BWW$, the gap between whites must be chosen, giving $\binom32=3$ choices each. For $WBW$, all $\binom43=4$ choices work. Thus there are $3+4+3=10$ favourable arrangements, and probability $\boxed{\dfrac{1}{6}}$.
