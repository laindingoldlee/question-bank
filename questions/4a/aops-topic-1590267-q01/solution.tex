The slope of the line is $\dfrac{-1-2}{7-(-3)}=-\dfrac{3}{10}$. Starting at $(-3,2)$, the line drops $3$ units for every $10$ units to the right, so it drops the $2$ units needed to reach the $x$-axis after $\dfrac{10}{3}\cdot2=\dfrac{20}{3}$ units to the right. Hence
\[
a=-3+\dfrac{20}{3}=\boxed{\dfrac{11}{3}}.
\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(7cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
import graph;
pair P=(-3,2),Q=(7,-1),X=(11/3,0);
draw((-4.5,0)--(8.5,0),gray,Arrow(TeXHead));draw((0,-2.2)--(0,3.2),gray,Arrow(TeXHead));
label("$x$",(8.5,0),E,gray);label("$y$",(0,3.2),N,gray);
pair u=unit(Q-P);
draw((P-0.9*u)--(Q+0.9*u),blue+linewidth(0.9));
dot(P);dot(Q);dot(X,red);
label("$(-3,2)$",P,NE);label("$(7,-1)$",Q,SW);
label("$\left(\dfrac{11}{3},0\right)$",X,NE,red);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Check with the equation of the line, $y-2=-\dfrac{3}{10}(x+3)$: at $x=\dfrac{11}{3}$ we get $y=2-\dfrac{3}{10}\cdot\dfrac{20}{3}=0$, and at $x=7$ we get $y=2-3=-1$. (The poster's $\dfrac{9}{2}$ is not correct; it would give $y=2-\dfrac{3}{10}\cdot\dfrac{15}{2}=-\dfrac{1}{4}$.)
