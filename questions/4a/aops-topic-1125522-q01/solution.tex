Subtracting and adding the two equations,
\[
A^3-B^3=5(A-B),\qquad A^3+B^3=19(A+B).
\]
Since $A\ne B$ and $A\ne -B$ we may divide by $A-B$ and by $A+B$:
\[
A^2+AB+B^2=5,\qquad A^2-AB+B^2=19 .
\]
Adding gives $2(A^2+B^2)=24$, so $A^2+B^2=\boxed{12}$ (and $AB=-7$).

Such numbers exist: conversely, these two equations give back $A^3-B^3=5(A-B)$ and $A^3+B^3=19(A+B)$, and half their sum and difference are the original equations. With $AB=-7$ we get $(A+B)^2=12-14=-2$ and $(A-B)^2=12+14=26$, so for example
\[
A=\dfrac{\sqrt{26}+i\sqrt2}{2},\qquad B=\dfrac{-\sqrt{26}+i\sqrt2}{2}.
\]
Since $(A+B)^2<0$, there are no real solutions, so the problem must be read over the complex numbers. Also $|A|=|B|=\sqrt7$ for every solution, so the post's condition ``$|A|\ne|B|$'' cannot be meant literally; the intended condition is $A^2\ne B^2$, which excludes the solutions with $A=\pm B$.
