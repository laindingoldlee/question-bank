Equal opposite chords $AB=CD$ make the cyclic quadrilateral an isosceles trapezoid with other sides $x,y$ as bases; the diagonals have equal integer length $d$. Ptolemy gives $d^2=xy+4$, and the positive altitude requires $|x-y|<4$. Assume $y=x+\delta$, with $\delta\in\{0,1,2,3\}$. Then $(2d-2x-\delta)(2d+2x+\delta)=16-\delta^2$. Checking positive factor pairs of $16,15,12,7$, respectively, with the required parity leaves only $(x,y,d)=(3,4,4)$. This trapezoid has height $\dfrac{\sqrt{15}}{2}$ and exists. Its perimeter is $2+3+2+4=\boxed{11}$, the only possible perimeter.

texpreamble("\usepackage{amsmath,amssymb}");

\end{asy}
