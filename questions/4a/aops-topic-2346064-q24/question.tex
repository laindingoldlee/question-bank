A large square region is paved with $n^2$ gray square tiles, each measuring $s$ inches on a side. A border $d$ inches wide surrounds each tile. The figure below shows the case for $n=3$. When $n=24$, the $576$ gray tiles cover $64\%$ of the area of the large square region. What is the ratio $\dfrac{d}{s}$ for this larger value of $n$?

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real s=1, d=0.25; int n=3;
real L=n*s+(n+1)*d;
draw((0,0)--(L,0)--(L,L)--(0,L)--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
for(int i=0;i<n;++i) for(int j=0;j<n;++j){ real x=d+i*(s+d), y=d+j*(s+d); filldraw((x,y)--(x+s,y)--(x+s,y+s)--(x,y+s)--cycle,gray(0.6),black); }
draw((d,-0.18)--(d+s,-0.18),Arrows(3)); label("$s$",(d+s/2,-0.18),S);
draw((d+s,L+0.18)--(d+s+d,L+0.18),Arrows(3)); label("$d$",(d+s+d/2,L+0.18),N);
\end{asy}

$\textbf{(A) }\dfrac{6}{25} \qquad \textbf{(B) }\dfrac14 \qquad \textbf{(C) }\dfrac{9}{25} \qquad \textbf{(D) }\dfrac{7}{16} \qquad \textbf{(E) }\dfrac{9}{16}$
