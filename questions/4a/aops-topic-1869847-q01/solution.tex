\textbf{(a)} For a binomial distribution with $n=120$ and $p=0.2$,
\[
\mu=np=24,\qquad \sigma=\sqrt{np(1-p)}=\sqrt{120\cdot0.2\cdot0.8}=\sqrt{19.2}\approx4.38 .
\]
\textbf{(b)} The range is from $\mu-2\sigma\approx24-8.76=15.24$ to $\mu+2\sigma\approx32.76$, so about $15$ to $33$ yellow candies. Its width is $4\sigma\approx17.5$, i.e. about $\boxed{18}$ (also $33-15=18$).

The student's $2\sigma\approx8.76$ is only the distance from the mean to \emph{one} end of the range. Going two standard deviations in \emph{both} directions gives a range of width $2\cdot2\sigma=4\sigma$, so the book's answer $18$ is correct.
