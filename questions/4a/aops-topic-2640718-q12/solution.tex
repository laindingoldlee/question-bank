Let the numbers be $a$ (Ashley), $b$ (Bethany), $c$ (Caitlin). Bethany refers to $a+c$, Caitlin to $a+b$, Ashley to $b+c$, and
\[a+c<b+c<a+b\]
(earlier date $<$ today $<$ later date). So $a<b$ and $c<a$, i.e. $c<a<b$. All three sums are dates, so at most $31$. The two-digit primes are $11,13,17,19,23,\dots$, and the largest sum $a+b\le31$ with $c<a<b$ forces $\{c,a,b\}=\{11,13,17\}$ (the next option, $c=11$, $a=13$, $b=19$, gives $a+b=32$). Hence $c=\boxed{11}$, with sums $24$, $28$, $30$.
