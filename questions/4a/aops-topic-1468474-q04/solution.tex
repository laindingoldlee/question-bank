There are $4\cdot4\cdot3=48$ such numbers. A number is divisible by $3$ exactly when its digit sum is. The residues of $0,2,4,6,8$ modulo $3$ are $0,2,1,0,2$. Three different digits have sum divisible by $3$ when their residues are $\{0,0,0\}$ (impossible, only two digits $0,6$ have residue $0$) or $\{0,1,2\}$: one of $\{0,6\}$, the digit $4$, and one of $\{2,8\}$. That gives the digit sets $\{0,4,2\},\{0,4,8\},\{6,4,2\},\{6,4,8\}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item Sets with $0$: $2$ sets, each with $3!-2!=4$ valid numbers: $8$.
\item Sets without $0$: $2$ sets, each with $3!=6$ numbers: $12$.
\end{itemize}
So $20$ numbers work, and the probability is $\dfrac{20}{48}=\boxed{\dfrac{5}{12}}$.
