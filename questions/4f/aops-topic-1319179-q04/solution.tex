Let $AM$ be the median of triangle $ABC$ to side $BC$. Extend it past $M$ to $D$ with $MD=AM$. Then $ABDC$ is a parallelogram (its diagonals bisect each other), so $BD=AC$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(1,3),B=(0,0),C=(4,0),M=(B+C)/2,D=2M-A; draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9)); draw(A--D,red+dashed); draw(B--D--C,gray+dashed);
label("$A$",A,N); label("$B$",B,W); label("$C$",C,E); label("$M$",M,NE); label("$D$",D,S);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

By the triangle inequality in triangle $ABD$ (non-degenerate, as $A$, $B$, $D$ are not collinear), $AD<AB+BD=AB+AC$. Since $AD=2AM$, we get $AM<\dfrac{AB+AC}{2}$. $\blacksquare$
