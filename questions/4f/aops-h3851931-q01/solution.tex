For a linear homogeneous recurrence, substituting $a_n=r^n$ gives its characteristic polynomial. Distinct characteristic roots give independent exponential solutions; the initial values determine their coefficients uniquely.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The polynomial is
\[
r^4-r^3-7r^2+r+6=(r-3)(r-1)(r+1)(r+2).
\]
Writing $a_n=A3^n+B+C(-1)^n+D(-2)^n$ and substituting $n=0,1,2,3$ gives
\[
\boxed{a_n=\dfrac{13}{40}3^n+\dfrac{1}{12}-\dfrac{7}{8}(-1)^n+\dfrac{7}{15}(-2)^n.}
\]
For example the four equations have right sides $0,1,4,6$ and coefficient rows $(1,1,1,1)$, $(3,1,-1,-2)$, $(9,1,1,4)$, $(27,1,-1,-8)$.
\item The characteristic polynomial factors as
\[
r^5-\dfrac{9}{2}r^4-\dfrac{5}{2}r^3+20r^2-6r-8
=(r-4)(r-2)(r-1)(r+2)(r+\dfrac{1}{2}).
\]
Solving the five initial-value equations in the corresponding exponential basis gives
\[
\boxed{a_n=-\dfrac{1}{27}4^n+\dfrac{11}{10}2^n-1+\dfrac{1}{18}(-2)^n-\dfrac{16}{135}(-\dfrac{1}{2})^n.}
\]
Direct substitution at $n=0,1,2,3,4$ gives $0,1,3,5,8$; each exponential satisfies the recurrence because its base is a characteristic root. This also verifies the formula for every later term.
\end{enumerate}
