There are $\dbinom93=84$ triangles in all. Count the isosceles ones (including equilateral).

Choose the apex: $9$ ways. The other two vertices must be symmetric about the line through the apex and the centre; the remaining $8$ vertices form $4$ symmetric pairs. That gives $9\cdot4=36$ (apex, triangle) pairs. An equilateral triangle is counted three times (once for each vertex as apex), and every other isosceles triangle exactly once. The nonagon has $3$ equilateral triangles (vertices $3$ apart), so the number of isosceles triangles is $36-2\cdot3=30$.

Hence the number of scalene triangles is $84-30=\boxed{54}$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair[] V; for(int k=0;k<9;++k) V[k]=dir(90+40*k);
draw(V[0]--V[1]--V[2]--V[3]--V[4]--V[5]--V[6]--V[7]--V[8]--cycle);
draw(V[0]--V[3]--V[6]--cycle,red); draw(V[0]--V[2]--V[7]--cycle,blue+dashed);
draw((0,1.15)--(0,-1.15),gray+dotted); for(int k=0;k<9;++k) dot(V[k]);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

(The red triangle is one of the $3$ equilateral triangles; the blue one is isosceles with apex at the top vertex, its other two vertices symmetric about the dotted axis.)

(The nonagon has only $3$ equilateral triangles, not $9$; the poster's count of $66$ before correcting was off for this reason.)
