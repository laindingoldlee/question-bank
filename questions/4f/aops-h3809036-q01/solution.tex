For the unit square, Pythagoras gives diagonal $\boxed{\sqrt2}$. Bisecting the equilateral triangle gives a right triangle with hypotenuse $1$ and short leg $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the altitude is $\boxed{\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}}$.
For the regular pentagon let its diagonal be $d$. Four consecutive vertices form a cyclic quadrilateral with sides $1,1,1,d$ and diagonals both $d$. Ptolemy gives $d^2=d+1$, hence the positive solution is $\boxed{d=\dfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2}}$.

There is no universal rule assigning one irrational number to every shape. For instance a rectangle of sides $3,4$ has diagonal $5$, area $12$, and perimeter $14$, all rational. Even for a fixed shape, changing the scale changes its lengths; different quantities can involve different constants.
