Take
\[f(x)=\dfrac{2x-1}{x(1-x)}.\]
It is defined on $(0,1)$. Its derivative is
\[f'(x)=\dfrac{2x(1-x)-(2x-1)(1-2x)}{x^2(1-x)^2}=\dfrac{2x^2-2x+1}{x^2(1-x)^2}>0\]
(the numerator has negative discriminant), so $f$ is strictly increasing, hence one-to-one. As $x\to0^+$, $f(x)\to-\infty$, and as $x\to1^-$, $f(x)\to+\infty$; being continuous, $f$ takes every real value. (One cannot use the closed interval $[0,1]$: a rational function defined on all of it is continuous there and hence bounded.)
