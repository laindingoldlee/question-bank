By AM-GM, $x^2+1\ge2x$, and similarly for $y$ and $z$. The denominators are positive, so
\[
\dfrac{x^2+1}{y+z}+\dfrac{y^2+1}{z+x}+\dfrac{z^2+1}{x+y}\ge2\left(\dfrac{x}{y+z}+\dfrac{y}{z+x}+\dfrac{z}{x+y}\right).
\]
It remains to prove Nesbitt's inequality $\dfrac{x}{y+z}+\dfrac{y}{z+x}+\dfrac{z}{x+y}\ge\dfrac{3}{2}$. Let $s=x+y+z$. Then
\[
\dfrac{x}{y+z}+1=\dfrac{s}{y+z},\quad\text{so}\quad\sum\dfrac{x}{y+z}+3=s\left(\dfrac{1}{y+z}+\dfrac{1}{z+x}+\dfrac{1}{x+y}\right).
\]
By the Cauchy--Schwarz (or AM-HM) inequality,
\[
\bigl((y+z)+(z+x)+(x+y)\bigr)\left(\dfrac{1}{y+z}+\dfrac{1}{z+x}+\dfrac{1}{x+y}\right)\ge9,
\]
that is, $2s\left(\sum\dfrac{1}{y+z}\right)\ge9$. Hence $\sum\dfrac{x}{y+z}+3\ge\dfrac{9}{2}$, so $\sum\dfrac {x}{y+z}\ge\dfrac{3}{2}$, and the original sum is at least $2\cdot\dfrac{3}{2}=3$. Equality holds for $x=y=z=1$. $\blacksquare$
