Write $t_i=x_iy_{i+1}-x_{i+1}y_i$, the term for the edge from $P_i$ to $P_{i+1}$; indices are taken cyclically, so $t_n$ is the term for the edge from $P_n$ to $P_1$.

\textbf{(a)} The sum $S$ has exactly one term for each edge of the polygon, traversed in the given direction. Starting at $P_k$ instead of $P_1$ lists the same edges, in the same direction, in the cyclic order $t_k,t_{k+1},\dots,t_n,t_1,\dots,t_{k-1}$. A sum does not depend on the order of its terms, so $S$ is unchanged. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{(b)} Listing the vertices in the opposite direction traverses each edge backwards, from $P_{i+1}$ to $P_i$, and the term for that is
\[
x_{i+1}y_i-x_iy_{i+1}=-t_i .
\]
So every term changes sign, and the new sum is $-S$. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{(c)}

Counterclockwise, $P_1=(0,0)$, $P_2=(4,0)$, $P_3=(1,3)$:
\[
S=(0\cdot0-4\cdot0)+(4\cdot3-1\cdot0)+(1\cdot0-0\cdot3)=12 .
\]
Clockwise, $(0,0)$, $(1,3)$, $(4,0)$:
\[
S=(0\cdot3-1\cdot0)+(1\cdot0-4\cdot3)+(4\cdot0-0\cdot0)=-12 .
\]
In both cases the area is $\dfrac{1}{2}|S|=\boxed{6}$ (check: base $4$, height $3$).

So the direction and the starting vertex do not matter as long as the vertices are taken in order around the boundary and the absolute value is used at the end: the sign of $S$ only tells the orientation (positive for counterclockwise, negative for clockwise). Different answers usually mean that the vertices were not listed in boundary order, which describes a different, self-crossing polygon.
