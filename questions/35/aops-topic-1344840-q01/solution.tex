By Vieta's formulas, $a$ and $b$ are the two roots of
\[
t^2-(a+b)t+ab=t^2+t+1=0 .
\]
Conversely, if $\{a,b\}$ are the two roots of $t^2+t+1=0$ (in either order), then $a+b=-1$ and $ab=1$. (If $a$ is one root, then $b=-1-a$ is the other.)

The roots are $t=\dfrac{-1\pm i\sqrt3}{2}$, so the solutions are exactly
\[
\boxed{(a,b)=\left(\dfrac{-1+i\sqrt3}{2},\dfrac{-1-i\sqrt3}{2}\right)\ \text{or}\ \left(\dfrac{-1-i\sqrt3}{2},\dfrac{-1+i\sqrt3}{2}\right)} .
\]
So the claim is \textbf{wrong}: there are exactly $2$ ordered pairs, and \textbf{no} real ones (the discriminant $1-4=-3$ is negative; also, real $a,b$ with $ab=1$ have the same sign, so $|a+b|\ge2\sqrt{ab}=2>1$).
