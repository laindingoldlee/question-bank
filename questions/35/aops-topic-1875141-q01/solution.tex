Let the four strings be $w_1,w_2,w_3,w_4$, and fix a position $i$ ($1\le i\le8$). The four digits in position $i$ take at most three values, so by the pigeonhole principle two of the strings have the same digit there. Choose one such pair of strings and call it $p_i$.

This assigns to each of the $8$ positions one of the $\dbinom42=6$ pairs of strings. Since $8>6$, by the pigeonhole principle some pair is assigned to two different positions $i\ne j$. Those two strings have the same digit in position $i$ and in position $j$, so they coincide in at least $2$ places.

(If two of the chosen elements are equal, they coincide in all $8$ places, and the argument still applies.)
