In a random order, the positions of Anna and Bob form a uniformly random pair of distinct positions among $1,2,\dots,n$; there are $\dbinom n2$ such pairs. The distance is the difference of the positions. The number of pairs at distance $d$ is $n-d$ (the smaller position can be $1,\dots,n-d$). So the expected distance is
\[
\dfrac{1}{\dbinom n2}\sum_{d=1}^{n-1}d(n-d).
\]
Now
\[
\sum_{d=1}^{n-1}d(n-d)=n\cdot\dfrac{(n-1)n}{2}-\dfrac{(n-1)n(2n-1)}{6}=\dfrac{n(n-1)\bigl(3n-(2n-1)\bigr)}{6}=\dfrac{(n-1)n(n+1)}{6}.
\]
Dividing by $\dbinom n2=\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}$ gives the expected distance
\[
\boxed{\dfrac{n+1}{3}\text{ metres}}.
\]
Check: for $n=3$ the distances $1,2,1$ of the three pairs average $\dfrac{4}{3}$.
