Let three consecutive terms be $a-d$, $a$, $a+d$ (all positive). Their reciprocals are consecutive terms of a geometric sequence, so the middle one squared equals the product of its neighbours:
\[
\dfrac{1}{a^2}=\dfrac{1}{(a-d)(a+d)}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad a^2=a^2-d^2\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad d=0 .
\]
So the arithmetic sequence is constant, every term equals the third term, and the seventh term is $\boxed{12}$ (the only possible value, hence also the greatest).
