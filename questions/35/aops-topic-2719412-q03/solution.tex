As a polynomial in $a$, the expression vanishes at $a=b$ (it becomes $b^2(b-c)+b^2(c-b)=0$), so $a-b$ is a factor; by symmetry so are $b-c$ and $c-a$. The expression has degree $3$, so it equals $k(a-b)(b-c)(c-a)$. Comparing the coefficient of $a^2b$ ($1$ on the left, $-k$ on the right) gives $k=-1$:
\[a^2(b-c)+b^2(c-a)+c^2(a-b)=\boxed{-(a-b)(b-c)(c-a)}=(a-b)(b-c)(a-c).\]
