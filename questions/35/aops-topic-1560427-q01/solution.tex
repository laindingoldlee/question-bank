For any two sets $P$, $Q$ of people in a town of $100$, inclusion--exclusion gives
\[
|P\cap Q|=|P|+|Q|-|P\cup Q|\ge|P|+|Q|-100 ,
\]
since $P\cup Q$ has at most $100$ people.

\textbf{(a)} True: $|\text{Loaps}\cap\text{Ikas}|\ge99+99-100=98\ge1$.

\textbf{(b)} True for the same reason: $|\text{Anas}\cap\text{Enas}|\ge98\ge1$.

\textbf{(c)} True: the same bound gives at least $98\ge2$ people who are both. In fact $98$ is the best possible bound: if the one person outside the Anas differs from the one person outside the Enas, exactly $98$ people are in both groups.

(The other groups, and the condition that everyone is in some group, play no role.) $\blacksquare$
