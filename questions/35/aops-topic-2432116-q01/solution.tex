Yes. First, conjugation is multiplicative: for $u=a+bi$ and $v=c+di$,
\[uv=(ac-bd)+(ad+bc)i,\qquad\overline{u}\,\overline{v}=(a-bi)(c-di)=(ac-bd)-(ad+bc)i=\overline{uv}.\]
Now let $z=\dfrac{x}{y}$, so $x=zy$. Then $\overline{x}=\overline{z}\,\overline{y}$, and $\overline{y}\ne0$ (because $y\ne0$), so
\[\dfrac{\overline{x}}{\overline{y}}=\overline{z}=\overline{\left(\dfrac{x}{y}\right)}.\]
So $\boxed{\dfrac{\overline{x}}{\overline{y}}=\overline{\left(\dfrac{x}{y}\right)}}$ always holds. (Example: $x=1+i$, $y=i$: $\dfrac{x}{y}=1-i$ with conjugate $1+i$, and $\dfrac{1-i}{-i}=1+i$.)
