Since $\left(\sqrt2+\sqrt3\right)^2=5+2\sqrt6$, the number is $\left\lfloor x^{1008}\right\rfloor$ with $x=5+2\sqrt6$. Let $y=5-2\sqrt6$. Then $x+y=10$, $xy=25-24=1$, and $0<y<1$ (as $2\sqrt6=\sqrt{24}$ is between $4$ and $5$).

\textbf{The integers $S_k$.} Put $S_k=x^k+y^k$. Then $S_0=2$, $S_1=10$, and since $x$, $y$ are the roots of $t^2-10t+1=0$,
\[
S_k=10S_{k-1}-S_{k-2}\qquad(k\ge2).
\]
So every $S_k$ is an integer.

\textbf{The floor.} As $0<y^{1008}<1$, we have $S_{1008}-1<x^{1008}<S_{1008}$, so $\left\lfloor x^{1008}\right\rfloor=S_{1008}-1$.

\textbf{Last digit.} Modulo $10$ the recurrence is $S_k\equiv-S_{k-2}$. So $S_{2j}\equiv(-1)^jS_0=2(-1)^j\pmod{10}$, and with $j=504$ (even), $S_{1008}\equiv2\pmod{10}$. Hence $S_{1008}-1$ ends in the digit $\boxed{1}$.
