Let $n=p_1^{e_1}\cdots p_k^{e_k}$ have $d(n)=(e_1+1)\cdots(e_k+1)$ divisors. The divisors that are not composite are $1$ and the $k$ primes $p_1,\dots,p_k$, so the number of composite divisors is $d(n)-1-k$. We need
\[
d(n)=11+k .
\]
\begin{itemize}
\item $k=1$: $d(n)=12$, so $n=p^{11}$; the least is $2^{11}=2048$.
\item $k=2$: $d(n)=13$ is prime, but a number with two prime factors has $d(n)=(e_1+1)(e_2+1)$, a product of two factors each at least $2$. Impossible.
\item $k=3$: $d(n)=14=2\cdot7$ cannot be a product of three factors each at least $2$. Impossible.
\item $k\ge4$: $d(n)\ge2^k$, but $2^k>11+k$ for $k\ge4$. Impossible.
\end{itemize}
So the answer is $\boxed{2048}$, whose composite divisors are $2^2,2^3,\dots,2^{11}$.
