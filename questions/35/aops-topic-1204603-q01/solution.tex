\emph{Maximum.} At most $50$ people hop, so at most $50$ do all four; $50$ is possible if the $50$ hoppers are among the swimmers, jumpers and shooters.

\emph{Minimum.} The numbers who \emph{miss} each activity are $20,30,50,40$, totalling $140$. A person misses at least one activity unless he does all four, so the number doing all four is at least $100-140<0$; this gives no restriction. Indeed $0$ is possible: give each of the $100$ people at least one ``miss'' using the $140$ misses (for example the $50$ non-hoppers, then $40$ non-shooters among the other $50$, then the remaining $10$ people miss jumping, and the leftover misses are spread arbitrarily).

So the minimum is $\boxed{0}$ and the maximum is $\boxed{50}$.
