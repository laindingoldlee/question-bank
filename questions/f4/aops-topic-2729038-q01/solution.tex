Let $d=\gcd(m+2n,n+2m)$. Then $d$ divides $2(m+2n)-(n+2m)=3n$ and $2(n+2m)-(m+2n)=3m$, so $d\mid\gcd(3m,3n)=3$. Thus $d\in\{1,3\}$. Since $m+2n\equiv m-n$ and $n+2m\equiv n-m\pmod3$, we get $d=3$ exactly when $m\equiv n\pmod3$. Both occur: $(m,n)=(1,1)$ gives $3$, and $(1,2)$ gives $\gcd(5,4)=1$. Answer: $\boxed{1\text{ or }3}$.
