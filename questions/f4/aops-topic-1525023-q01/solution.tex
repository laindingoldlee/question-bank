The $j$-th term is $2j(3j+1)=6j^2+2j$. Using $\sum_{j=1}^k j^2=\dfrac{k(k+1)(2k+1)}{6}$ and $\sum_{j=1}^k j=\dfrac{k(k+1)}{2}$,
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{k}2j(3j+1)=k(k+1)(2k+1)+k(k+1)=k(k+1)(2k+2)=\boxed{2k(k+1)^2}.
\]
Check: $k=1$ gives $8$ and $k=2$ gives $8+28=36=2\cdot2\cdot9$.
