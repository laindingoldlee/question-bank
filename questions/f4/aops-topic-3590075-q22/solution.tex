Put $A=x^{1960}-x^{1919}$ and $B=x^{2001}-x^{1960}$. If $A=0$, then $x^{1919}(x^{41}-1)=0$, giving $x=0$ or $1$. Otherwise $x^{41}=\dfrac{B}{A}$ is rational, and $x^{1919}=\dfrac{A}{x^{41}-1}$ is rational and nonzero. Since $\gcd(41,1919)=1$, Bezout's identity expresses $x$ as an integer-power product of these two nonzero rational numbers, so $x$ is rational. Write $x=\dfrac{p}{q}$ in lowest terms with $q>0$. Then $A=\dfrac{p^{1919}(p^{41}-q^{41})}{q^{1960}}$. Its numerator is coprime to $q$, so integrality forces $q=1$. Thus $x$ is an integer.
