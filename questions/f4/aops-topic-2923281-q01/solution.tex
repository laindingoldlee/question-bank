Construct the perpendicular bisector of $AC$, meeting it at its midpoint $M$. Draw the circle with centre $M$ through $A$; it meets the bisector at $B$ and $D$. Then $ABCD$ is the square.
\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),C=(4,0),M=(2,0),B=(2,2),D=(2,-2);draw(A--C);draw((2,-2.6)--(2,2.6),dashed);draw(circle(M,2),dotted);
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle);label("$A$",A,W);label("$C$",C,E);label("$B$",B,N);label("$D$",D,S);label("$M$",M,SE);
\end{asy}

\emph{Why.} The diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ bisect each other, so $ABCD$ is a parallelogram. They are equal, so it is a rectangle, and perpendicular, so it is a rhombus. Hence it is a square. Constructing $45^\circ$ angles at $A$ and $C$ also works, but the perpendicular bisector method is shorter.
