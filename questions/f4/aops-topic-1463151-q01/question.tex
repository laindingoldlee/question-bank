There are $3$ different balls, and we want to know how many ways there are to choose $2$ of them. A student argues: ``There are $3$ choices for the first ball and $2$ for the second, so there are $3\cdot2=6$ ways.'' But the answer is $\dbinom{3}{2}=3$. Explain the error, and say what the number $6$ does count.
