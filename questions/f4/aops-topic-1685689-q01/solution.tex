Condition on the second flip; each outcome has probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

\textbf{Bryan won flip 2.} After three flips Bryan has $3$ wins and Carl $0$. Carl wins the game only by winning the next four flips: probability $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^4=\dfrac{1}{16}$.

\textbf{Carl won flip 2.} After three flips Bryan has $2$ and Carl $1$. Carl needs $3$ more wins before Bryan gets $2$. Either Carl wins the next three flips (probability $\dfrac{1}{8}$), or Bryan wins exactly one of the next three flips and Carl wins the fourth (there are $3$ positions for Bryan's win, probability $\dfrac{3}{16}$). Total $\dfrac{1}{8}+\dfrac{3}{16}=\dfrac{5}{16}$.

By Bayes' theorem,
\[
P(\text{Bryan won flip 2}\mid\text{Carl won})=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{16}}{\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{16}+\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{5}{16}}=\dfrac{1}{1+5}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{6}}.
\]
