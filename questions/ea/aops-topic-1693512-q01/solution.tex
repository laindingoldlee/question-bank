We show that $P$ is the $\boxed{\text{centroid}}$ (the intersection of the medians).

The three triangles fill triangle $ABC$, so each has area $\dfrac{1}{3}[ABC]$. Triangles $PBC$ and $ABC$ have the same base $BC$, so the height of $P$ above $BC$ is $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of the height of $A$: $PK=\dfrac{1}{3}AH$, where $H$ and $K$ are the feet of the perpendiculars from $A$ and $P$ to $BC$. Hence $P$ lies on the line parallel to $BC$ at one third of the way from $BC$ to $A$. In the same way $P$ lies on the line parallel to $CA$ one third of the way from $CA$ to $B$. These two lines meet in exactly one point, so there is at most one such point $P$.

The centroid $G$ has this property: it lies on the median $AM$ ($M$ the midpoint of $BC$) with $GM=\dfrac{1}{3}AM$, so its distance from $BC$ is $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of the distance of $A$, i.e.\ $[GBC]=\dfrac{1}{3}[ABC]$; similarly $[GCA]=[GAB]=\dfrac{1}{3}[ABC]$. Hence $P=G$. $\blacksquare$

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(1,4),B=(0,0),C=(6,0);pair G=(A+B+C)/3;
fill(A--B--G--cycle,paleblue);fill(B--C--G--cycle,palegreen);fill(C--A--G--cycle,paleyellow);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));draw(A--G,gray);draw(B--G,gray);draw(C--G,gray);
pair Ma=(B+C)/2;draw(G--Ma,dashed);
pair Hf=B+dot(G-B,unit(C-B))*unit(C-B),Ha=B+dot(A-B,unit(C-B))*unit(C-B);
draw(G--Hf,red+dashed);draw(A--Ha,red+dashed);
dot(G);dot(Ma);
label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,SW);label("$C$",C,SE);label("$P$",G,dir(80));label("$M$",Ma,S);
label("$H$",Ha,S,red);label("$K$",Hf,S,red);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}
