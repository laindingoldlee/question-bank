Let the campus distance be $D$. At the first meeting the cyclists have traveled a total of $D$. At their next meeting after turning, their total distance is $3D$, so elapsed time and each rider's travel are triple those at the first meeting. The Berkeley rider therefore travels $3(17.5)=52.5$ miles, consisting of $D$ to Stanford and $10$ back. Thus $D=\boxed{42.5}$ miles. The resulting first-leg ratio $17.5:25$ ensures both have turned before the second meeting, as required.
