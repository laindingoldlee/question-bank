The sum or product of two functions is defined at $x$ exactly when \emph{both} functions are defined at $x$, so its domain is the intersection of the two domains. Here $f$ is defined for $x\ge0$ and $g$ for $x\ge4$.

\textbf{(a)} The domain of $s$ is $\{x\ge0\}\cap\{x\ge4\}=[4,\infty)$.

\textbf{(b)} At $x=-4$, $x^2-4x=16+16=32\ge0$, so $\sqrt{x^2-4x}=\sqrt{32}=4\sqrt2$ is defined. But $p(-4)=f(-4)\,g(-4)$ requires $\sqrt{-4}$ and $\sqrt{-8}$, which are not real numbers, so $-4$ is \emph{not} in the domain of $p$. The formula $\sqrt{x^2-4x}$ agrees with $p$ only where $p$ is defined; simplifying the formula does not enlarge the domain.

\textbf{(c)} As in (a), the domain of $p$ is the intersection of the domains of $f$ and $g$:
\[
\boxed{[4,\infty)}\ \text{for both } s \text{ and } p.
\]
