For a positive integer $b$ that is not a square, $a+\sqrt b$ is a root of $m(x)=(x-a)^2-b$, which has no rational roots. If a polynomial $P$ with rational coefficients has the root $a+\sqrt b$, divide: $P(x)=m(x)Q(x)+ux+v$ with $u,v$ rational. Then $u\left(a+\sqrt b\right)+v=0$, and since $\sqrt b$ is irrational, $u=0$ and then $v=0$. So $m$ divides $P$.

The six quadratics
\[x^2-4x+1,\quad x^2-6x+4,\quad x^2-10x+18,\quad x^2-14x+38,\quad x^2-22x+108,\quad x^2-26x+152\]
are distinct, irreducible over the rationals, and monic, so they are pairwise coprime, and each divides any such $P$. Hence their product divides $P$, so $\deg P\ge12$, and the product itself works. The answer is
\[\boxed{\left(x^2-4x+1\right)\left(x^2-6x+4\right)\left(x^2-10x+18\right)\left(x^2-14x+38\right)\left(x^2-22x+108\right)\left(x^2-26x+152\right)}.\]
