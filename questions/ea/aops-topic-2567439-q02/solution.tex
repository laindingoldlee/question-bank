The parabola $y=(x+p)(x+q)$ has its vertex midway between the roots $-p$ and $-q$:
\[x_v=-\dfrac{p+q}2,\qquad y_v=\left(\dfrac{q-p}2\right)\left(\dfrac{p-q}2\right)=-\dfrac{(p-q)^2}4.\]
For $p=9$ and $p=7$ (with the same $q=b$), the vertices differ by $1$ horizontally and by
\[\dfrac{(9-b)^2-(7-b)^2}4=\dfrac{2(16-2b)}4=8-b\]
vertically. By the distance formula, $1+(8-b)^2=122$, so $8-b=\pm11$ and $b=-3$ or $b=19$.

The roots of $(x+9)(x+b)$ are $-9$ and $-b$, with sum $-9-b$:
\[\boxed{-6\ \text{ or }\ -28}.\]
For example, with $b=-3$ the vertices are $V=(-3,-36)$ and $V'=(-2,-25)$, at distance $\sqrt{1+121}=\sqrt{122}$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
import graph;real s=0.2;
real f(real x){return s*(x+9)*(x-3);} real g(real x){return s*(x+7)*(x-3);}
draw((-10.5,0)--(4.5,0),Arrow(4));draw((0,-7.8)--(0,2),Arrow(4));
draw(graph(f,-9.6,3.6),linewidth(0.9));draw(graph(g,-7.6,3.6),dashed+linewidth(0.9));
pair V1=(-3,s*(-36)),V2=(-2,s*(-25));dot(V1);dot(V2);draw(V1--V2,red+linewidth(0.9));
label("$V$",V1,S,fontsize(9pt));label("$V'$",V2,N,fontsize(9pt));
label("solid: $y=(x+9)(x-3)$, dashed: $y=(x+7)(x-3)$",(-3,-8.3),S,fontsize(8pt));label("$x$",(4.5,0),E);label("$y$",(0,2),N);
\end{asy}
