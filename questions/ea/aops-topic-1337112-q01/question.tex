For positive integers $n$ and $k\ge0$ let $S_k=\dfrac{1}{1^k}+\dfrac{1}{2^k}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{n^k}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Prove that for every positive integer $x$,
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{x}S_k=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{x}S_k\right)+n .
\]
\item[(b)] The post states the same identity with $x\to\infty$ on both sides. Explain why that version is not an equation between real numbers.
\end{enumerate}
