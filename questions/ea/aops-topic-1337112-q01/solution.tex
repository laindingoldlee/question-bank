\textbf{(a)} The left sum contains exactly one more term than the right sum, namely $S_0$. Since $j^0=1$ for every $j$,
\[
S_0=\underbrace{1+1+\cdots+1}_{n}=n ,
\]
so $\sum_{k=0}^xS_k=S_0+\sum_{k=1}^xS_k=\left(\sum_{k=1}^xS_k\right)+n$.

\textbf{(b)} Every $S_k$ contains the term $\dfrac{1}{1^k}=1$, so $S_k\ge1$ and $\sum_{k=1}^xS_k\ge x$. Hence both sums grow without bound as $x\to\infty$: the limits do not exist as real numbers, and ``$\infty=\infty+n$'' says nothing. The identity is true and meaningful for each finite $x$, as shown in (a). $\boxed{\text{(a) holds because }S_0=n\text{; in (b) both sides diverge}}$
