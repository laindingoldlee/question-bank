On this circle $x\ge3-\sqrt6>0$, so $\dfrac {y}{x}$ is the slope of the line from the origin to $(x,y)$. The largest slope is attained when the line $y=kx$ is tangent to the circle.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
import graph; real k=3+2*sqrt(2); draw(circle((3,3),sqrt(6)),blue);
draw((0,0)--(1.25,1.25*k),red); draw((-0.5,0)--(6.2,0),gray,Arrow); draw((0,-0.5)--(0,7.6),gray,Arrow);
pair T=(3*(1+k)/(1+k^2),3*(1+k)*k/(1+k^2)); dot(T); dot((3,3)); label("$(3,3)$",(3,3),SE);
label("$y=(3+2\sqrt2)x$",(1.25,1.25*k),E,red); label("$x$",(6.2,0),S); label("$y$",(0,7.6),W);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The line $kx-y=0$ is tangent when its distance from the centre $(3,3)$ equals the radius $\sqrt6$:
\[
\dfrac{|3k-3|}{\sqrt{k^2+1}}=\sqrt6\iff9(k-1)^2=6(k^2+1)\iff k^2-6k+1=0,
\]
so $k=3\pm2\sqrt2$. The maximum of $\dfrac {y}{x}$ is $\boxed{3+2\sqrt2}$ (and the minimum is $3-2\sqrt2$).
