If a cube of edge $L$ is cut into equal cubes of edge $d$, then the cubes along one edge of the big cube fill that edge, so $L$ is a multiple of $d$; conversely, if $d\mid L$ the cut is possible, in $\left(\dfrac {L}{d}\right)^3$ cubes. ``Smaller'' means $d<L$.

So we need integers $d$ and $n$ with
\[
n\mid d,\quad n<d,\qquad d\mid120,\quad d<120 .
\]
Such a $d$ exists exactly when $n\mid120$ and $\dfrac{120}{n}$ has a divisor $k=\dfrac {d}{n}$ with $1<k<\dfrac{120}{n}$, that is, when $\dfrac{120}{n}$ is neither $1$ nor a prime.

The divisors of $120$ are $1,2,3,4,5,6,8,10,12,15,20,24,30,40,60,120$. Exactly four of them, $1,2,3,5$, are $1$ or prime, so $\dfrac{120}{n}$ can be any of the other $12$ divisors. The possible values of $n$ are
\[
1,2,3,4,5,6,8,10,12,15,20,30,
\]
which is $\boxed{12}$ values. (The excluded ones are $n=120,60,40,24$.)
