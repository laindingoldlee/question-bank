The diagonals cut the rhombus into four congruent right triangles whose legs are the half-diagonals and whose hypotenuse is a side. The diagonals differ by $6$, so the half-diagonals differ by $3$; call them $x$ and $x+3$.

draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(0.9));draw(A--C,dashed);draw(B--D,dashed);
real q=0.6;draw((q,0)--(q,q)--(0,q),linewidth(0.6));

label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

By Pythagoras' theorem in triangle $OBC$,
\[
x^2+(x+3)^2=89\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2x^2+6x-80=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (x-5)(x+8)=0 ,
\]
so $x=5$. The diagonals are $2x=10$ and $2(x+3)=16$, and the area of a rhombus is half the product of its diagonals:
\[
\dfrac{10\cdot16}{2}=\boxed{80}\text{ square units}.
\]
