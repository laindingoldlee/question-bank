\textbf{Positions.} The vertices are in the order $A,B,C,D,E$ around the circle. Seen from $B$, the rays to $C,D,E,A$ are in this angular order, so going along $AC$ from $A$ we cross $BE$ before $BD$: the order on $AC$ is $A,X,Y,C$. Seen from $C$, the rays to $D,E,A,B$ are in order, so along $BD$ from $B$ we cross $CA$ before $CE$: the order on $BD$ is $B,Y,Z,D$. Also $X$ lies on segment $BE$ and $Z$ on segment $CE$.

\textbf{(a)} Let the arcs $AB$, $BC$, $CD$ (those not containing the other vertices) measure $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$. By the inscribed angle theorem $\angle AEB=\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$, $\angle DEC=\dfrac{\gamma}{2}$ and $\angle BEC=\dfrac{\beta}{2}$, so the hypothesis says $\alpha+\gamma=\beta$.
\begin{itemize}
\item Since $X$ is on segment $EB$ and $Z$ on segment $EC$, $\angle XEZ=\angle BEC=\dfrac{\beta}{2}$.
\item The chords $AC$ and $BD$ cross at $Y$, so $\angle AYB=\dfrac{\alpha+\gamma}{2}=\dfrac{\beta}{2}$. The ray $YX$ points towards $A$ and the ray $YZ$ towards $D$, so $\angle XYZ=\angle AYD=180^\circ-\angle AYB=180^\circ-\dfrac{\beta}{2}$.
\end{itemize}
Thus $\angle XYZ+\angle XEZ=180^\circ$. Moreover $Y$ and $E$ are on opposite sides of the line $XZ$: the segments $XC$ and $BZ$ are the diagonals of the quadrilateral $XBCZ$ (cut off from triangle $EBC$ by $XZ$), and $Y$ is their intersection, inside $XBCZ$. So $XYZE$ is a convex quadrilateral with opposite angles adding to $180^\circ$, hence cyclic. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{(b)} By Ptolemy's theorem for the cyclic quadrilateral $XYZE$ (vertices in this order),
\[
XZ\cdot YE=XY\cdot ZE+YZ\cdot EX .
\]
If $XY=YZ$, the right side is $XY\,(EX+EZ)$, which gives $EY\cdot XZ=XY\cdot(EX+EZ)$. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{(c)} Suppose all three conditions hold. In triangle $XYZ$, by the law of cosines,
\[
\cos\angle XYZ=\dfrac{1+1-\dfrac{9}{4}}{2}=-\dfrac{1}{8},\qquad\sin\angle XYZ=\dfrac{\sqrt{63}}{8} ,
\]
so its circumcircle, which by (a) passes through $E$, has diameter $\dfrac{XZ}{\sin\angle XYZ}=\dfrac{3}{2}\cdot\dfrac{8}{\sqrt{63}}=\dfrac{4}{\sqrt7}<1.52$. Every chord of this circle is at most its diameter, so
\[
XY+YZ+ZE+EX\le2+2\cdot\dfrac{4}{\sqrt7}<5.1<10 ,
\]
a contradiction. (By (b), a perimeter of $10$ would also give $EY=\dfrac{8}{\dfrac{3}{2}}=\dfrac{16}{3}$, longer than the diameter.) $\blacksquare$
