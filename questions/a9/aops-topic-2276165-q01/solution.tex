\textbf{(a)} \textbf{No.} The variable $x$ appears to the power $n$ in one term and to the power $1$ in the other, and $n\ne1$. For example, $3x^2+5x$ cannot be simplified to one term, whereas $3x^2+5x^2=8x^2$ can.

\textbf{(b)} Suppose $x^2+x=cx^k$ for every real $x$.
\begin{itemize}
\item Putting $x=1$: $1+1=c\cdot1$, so $c=2$.
\item Putting $x=-1$: $1-1=c(-1)^k$, so $0=2(-1)^k=\pm2$.
\end{itemize}
This is impossible, so no such $c$ and $k$ exist: $x^2+x$ is a sum of two unlike terms and is already in simplest form. $\blacksquare$
