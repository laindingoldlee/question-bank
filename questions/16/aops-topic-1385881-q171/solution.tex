Each number on the board is processed independently, so let $f(n)$ be the number of $1$'s that a single $n$ eventually produces. Then $f(1)=1$ and
\[
f(n)=\sum_{d\mid n,\ d<n}f(d)\qquad(n>1),
\]
whatever order Becky chooses. The value depends only on the exponents in the prime factorisation, so a table indexed by exponents saves work. For $72=2^3\cdot3^2$, let $g(a,b)=f(2^a3^b)$; each entry is the sum of all entries in the rectangle up to it, except itself:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 & b=0 & b=1 & b=2\\ \hline
a=0 & 1 & 1 & 2\\
a=1 & 1 & 3 & 8\\
a=2 & 2 & 8 & 26\\
a=3 & 4 & 20 & ?
\end{array}
\]
For instance $g(1,1)=f(6)=f(1)+f(2)+f(3)=3$ and $g(2,2)=f(36)=1+1+2+1+3+8+2+8=26$. Finally
\[
f(72)=1+1+2+1+3+8+2+8+26+4+20=\boxed{76}.
\]
