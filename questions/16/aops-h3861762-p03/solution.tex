If $k$ is odd, colour even integers white and odd integers red. Red integers are never adjacent, and two white integers have even difference, so this works.

Suppose $k$ is even. There must be a red integer $r$, since an all-white colouring fails. Its neighbours $r-1,r+1$ are white, so the integers at distance $k$ from these neighbours must be red. Thus from any red position, translations by each of $\pm(k-1),\pm(k+1)$ lead to red positions. Combining these translations gives a translation by $2$, and since $k-1$ is odd, combinations of $2$ and $k-1$ also give a translation by $1$. Hence a red integer would have a red neighbour, a contradiction.

Exactly odd $k$ work. Among the five listed values, only $33$ is odd, so the answer is $\boxed1$.
