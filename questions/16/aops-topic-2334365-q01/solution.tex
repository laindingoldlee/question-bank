\textbf{(a)} \textbf{Not a function}: $6$ has the two prime factors $2$ and $3$, so the rule does not give a single output for $6$. (Matching $n$ with the \emph{set} of its prime factors, or with its \emph{smallest} prime factor, would give a function.)

\textbf{(b)} \textbf{A function}: the greatest divisor of a natural number $n$ is $n$ itself, so the rule is $n\mapsto n$.

\textbf{(c)} \textbf{Not a function on the real numbers}: $-1$ has no real fourth root (no real number has a negative fourth power), and $16$ has two real fourth roots, $2$ and $-2$. Restricted to $x\ge0$ and taking the nonnegative root, the rule $x\mapsto\sqrt[4]{x}$ is a function.
