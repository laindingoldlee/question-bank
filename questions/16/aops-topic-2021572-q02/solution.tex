Let $T=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{n}{3^{n}}$; it converges because the terms decrease faster than a geometric series with ratio $\dfrac{1}{2}$ from some point on (for instance by the ratio test, the ratio of consecutive terms tends to $\dfrac{1}{3}$). Then
\[
T-\dfrac{T}{3}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{n}{3^n}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{n-1}{3^{n}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{3^n}=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{3}}{1-\dfrac{1}{3}}=\dfrac{1}{2},
\]
so $\dfrac{2T}{3}=\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $T=\dfrac{3}{4}$. The required sum is
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{2n}{3^{n+1}}=\dfrac{2}{3}\,T=\dfrac{2}{3}\cdot\dfrac{3}{4}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}}.
\]
