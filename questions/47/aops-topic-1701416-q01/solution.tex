We need $b\,\#\,e=2be+b+e=b$ for every real $b$, that is,
\[
e(2b+1)=0\quad\text{for all }b .
\]
Taking $b=0$ gives $e=0$. Conversely $e=0$ works: $b\,\#\,0=0+b+0=b$, and $0\,\#\,b=b$ in the same way (the operation is commutative). So the identity exists and it is $\boxed{e=0}$.

(A tempting mistake is to solve $e(2b+1)=0$ as ``$e=0$ or $b=-\dfrac{1}{2}$''; but $e$ must work for \emph{every} $b$ at once, so $e=0$ is the only candidate.)
