\textbf{Interpret as distances.} Complete the squares:
\[
x^2-2x+2=(x-1)^2+(0-1)^2,\qquad x^2-4x+8=(x-2)^2+(0-2)^2 .
\]
So $f(x)=XA+XB$, where $X=(x,0)$ is a point on the $x$-axis, $A=(1,1)$ and $B=(2,2)$.

\textbf{Reflect.} Let $A'=(1,-1)$ be the reflection of $A$ in the $x$-axis. For every $X$ on the axis, $XA=XA'$, so by the triangle inequality
\[
f(x)=XA'+XB\ge A'B=\sqrt{(2-1)^2+(2-(-1))^2}=\sqrt{10},
\]
with equality when $X$ lies on segment $A'B$. That segment does cross the axis, because $A'$ and $B$ are on opposite sides: the line $A'B$ is $y=3x-4$, which meets the axis at $P=\left(\dfrac{4}{3},0\right)$.

\textbf{Check.} At $x=\dfrac{4}{3}$: $\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{9}+1}+\sqrt{\dfrac{4}{9}+4}=\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{3}+\dfrac{2\sqrt{10}}{3}=\sqrt{10}$.

The smallest value is $\boxed{\sqrt{10}}$.
