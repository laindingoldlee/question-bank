We show the stronger statements $x<1$ and $y>3$, which give $y-x>2>1.3\overline1$.

\textbf{$x<1$.} Since $3^5=243<256=2^8$,
\[
3^{48}=3^3\cdot\left(3^5\right)^9<27\cdot\left(2^8\right)^9=27\cdot2^{72}<32\cdot2^{72}=2^{77}.
\]
Hence $3^{48}+8<2^{77}+9$, i.e.\ $x<1$.

\textbf{$y>3$.} Since $5^3=125$ and $11^2=121$,
\[
5^{74}=25\cdot\left(5^3\right)^{24}=25\cdot\left(\dfrac{125}{121}\right)^{24}\cdot11^{48}.
\]
By Bernoulli's inequality ($(1+h)^n\ge1+nh$ for $h>0$), $\left(\dfrac{125}{121}\right)^{24}=\left(1+\dfrac{4}{121}\right)^{24}\ge1+\dfrac{96}{121}=\dfrac{217}{121}$. So
\[
5^{74}\ge\dfrac{25\cdot217}{121}\cdot11^{48}=\dfrac{5425}{121}\cdot11^{48}>\dfrac{5324}{121}\cdot11^{48}=44\cdot11^{48}=4\cdot11^{49}.
\]
Then $5^{74}+11>4\cdot11^{49}>3\cdot11^{49}+15=3\left(11^{49}+5\right)$, i.e.\ $y>3$.

Therefore $y-x>3-1=2>1.3\overline1$. $\blacksquare$

(Numerically $x\approx0.53$ and $y\approx4.99$.)
