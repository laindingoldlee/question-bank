Let $g=\gcd(a,c)$ and $h=\gcd(b,c)$. Dividing by $c$ gives $\dfrac{a}{g}+\dfrac{b}{h}=\dfrac{26}{27}(a+b)$. Both gcds cannot be $1$, nor can both be at least $2$. By symmetry suppose $g=1$, $h\ge2$. Write $b=hk$; then $a=(26h-27)k$. The bound $a\le50$ forces $h=2$, $k=1$ or $2$. If $k=2$, then $a=50$, and even $c$ contradicts $g=1$. Thus $(a,b)=(25,2)$ or $(2,25)$, with $c$ even and not divisible by $5$. There are $25-5=20$ choices of $c$, hence $\boxed{40}$ triples.
