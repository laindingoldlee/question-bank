Using $k(k+1)(k+2)(k+3)=24\binom{k+3}{4}$ and the hockey-stick identity, the sum is $24\binom{29}{5}$. The exponent of $2$ in $\binom{29}{5}=\dfrac{25\cdot26\cdot27\cdot28\cdot29}{120}$ is $(1+2)-3=0$. Thus this binomial coefficient is odd, while $24$ has exactly three factors of $2$. Hence $\boxed{n=3}$.
