Choose two distinct candidates uniformly. A fixed resident has at most 70 nonfriends, including themself, so the probability neither candidate is a friend is at most $\dfrac{\binom{70}{2}}{\binom{100}{2}}$. By linearity of expectation, the expected number of voters is at least $100\left(1-\dfrac{2415}{4950}\right)=\dfrac{1690}{33}>51$. Some candidate pair therefore has at least 52 voters, which is stronger than required.
