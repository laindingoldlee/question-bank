Let $h_j$ be the probability of eventually winning with $j$ consecutive heads currently recorded, for $j=1,2,3$, and $t$ the probability with one tail currently recorded. Then $t=\dfrac{h_1}{2}$, $h_3=\dfrac{1+t}{2}$, $h_2=\dfrac{h_3+t}{2}$, and $h_1=\dfrac{h_2+t}{2}$. Substitution yields $h_1=\dfrac{1}{8}+\dfrac{7}{8}t=\dfrac{1}{8}+\dfrac{7}{16}h_1$, hence $h_1=\dfrac{2}{9}$, $t=\dfrac{1}{9}$, and from a fresh start $q=\dfrac{h_1+t}{2}=\dfrac{1}{6}$. The positive squares are $k^2$ for $1\le k\le216$. Checking residues modulo $14$, $k^2\equiv11$ exactly when $k\equiv5,9$. The first $210$ integers contain $30$ such values and $211$ through $216$ contain one more. Thus $r=\dfrac{31}{216}$ and $\boxed{\dfrac{q}{r}=\dfrac{36}{31}}$.
