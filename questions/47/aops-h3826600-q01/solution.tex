Let $u=(1,a,1)$, $v=(a,1,1)$, and $b=(1,1,a)$. Their cross product and scalar triple product are
\[
u\times v=(a-1)(1,1,-a-1),\qquad b\cdot(u\times v)=-(a-1)^2(a+2).
\]
At $a=1$ the lines coincide, since $b=u=v$. For $a\ne1$ they are not parallel; they intersect exactly when $a=-2$. In that case $t=-1,s=1$ gives the intersection $(-1,2,-1)$. Thus they are skew exactly for $\boxed{a\notin\{1,-2\}}$.

Write the perpendicular's endpoints as $P=tu$, $Q=b+sv$. Orthogonality requires
\[
(b+sv-tu)\cdot u=(b+sv-tu)\cdot v=0.
\]
Here $u^2=v^2=a^2+2$, $u\cdot v=2a+1$, and $b\cdot u=b\cdot v=2a+1$. Subtracting the two equations gives $(a-1)^2(t+s)=0$. Solving gives
\[
t=\dfrac{2a+1}{a^2+2a+3},\qquad s=-t.
\]
Consequently the required line is
\[
\boxed{(x,y,z)=\dfrac{2a+1}{a^2+2a+3}(1,a,1)+\lambda(1,1,-a-1),\quad\lambda\in\mathbb R.}
\]
The constructed endpoints lie on the given lines and their difference is orthogonal to both directions, proving the assertion.
