\textbf{One-digit $k$:} $g(k)=k\ne0$.

\textbf{Numbers with a digit $0$:} $10,20,\dots,90,100$ have digit product $0$, so $g=0$: $10$ numbers.

\textbf{Two-digit numbers with nonzero digits $a,b$:} $f(k)=ab$ is a product of two digits, at most $81$. Checking the possible products, $g(ab)=0$ exactly for
\[
ab\in\{10,20,30,40\}\ (\text{a zero digit}),\qquad 25\to10,\quad 45\to20,\quad 54\to20,\quad 56\to30,
\]
while every other product of two digits leads to a nonzero digit (for example $35\to15\to5$, $48\to32\to6$, $49\to36\to18\to8$, $63\to18\to8$, $64\to24\to8$, $72\to14\to4$). Counting ordered digit pairs:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccccc}
ab & 10 & 20 & 25 & 30 & 40 & 45 & 54 & 56\\\hline
(a,b) & 25,52 & 45,54 & 55 & 56,65 & 58,85 & 59,95 & 69,96 & 78,87
\end{array}
\]
That gives $15$ numbers.

The total is
\[10+15=\boxed{25}.\]
