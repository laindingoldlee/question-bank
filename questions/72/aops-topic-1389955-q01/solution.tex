Although balls of the same colour look alike, they are physically different balls, and each ball independently chooses a container with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$. The event requires a specific choice for every ball: both red balls in the red container, and both blue balls and the yellow ball in the green container. Its probability is
\[
\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^5=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{32}}.
\]
Counting colour patterns instead (how many balls of each colour are in the red container: $3\cdot3\cdot2=18$ patterns) would be wrong, because those patterns are not equally likely.
