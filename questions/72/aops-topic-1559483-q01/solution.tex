There are $25$ points, so $\binom{25}{3}=2300$ ways to choose three of them. Subtract the collinear triples. Every line through at least three of the points has one of the slopes $0$, $\infty$, $\pm1$, $\pm2$, $\pm\dfrac{1}{2}$ (examples in the figure):

draw((1,2)--(5,2),blue+linewidth(0.9));

label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\begin{itemize}
\item Horizontal lines: $5$ lines with $5$ points each, $5\binom53=50$ triples. Vertical lines: also $50$.
\item Slope $1$: diagonals with $5,4,4,3,3$ points give $10+4+4+1+1=20$ triples; slope $-1$: also $20$.
\item Slopes $\pm2$ and $\pm\dfrac{1}{2}$: such a line contains at most $3$ points (a step of $(1,2)$ or $(2,1)$ can be taken at most twice inside the grid). For slope $\dfrac{1}{2}$ the triples are $(x,y),(x+2,y+1),(x+4,y+2)$ with $x=1$ and $y\in\{1,2,3\}$: $3$ triples. The same holds for each of the four slopes: $12$ triples.
\item Any other slope $\dfrac {p}{q}$ in lowest terms needs a step $(q,p)$ with $\max(|p|,|q|)\ge3$, and two such steps do not fit in the grid.
\end{itemize}
Collinear triples: $50+50+20+20+12=152$. Triangles: $2300-152=\boxed{2148}$, answer $\textbf{(B)}$.
