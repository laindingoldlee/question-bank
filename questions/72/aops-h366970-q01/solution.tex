Let the first two radicands be $A,B$ and the last be $C$. Squaring once gives
\[
\sqrt{AB}=(x+1)(x+2),
\]
so $(x+1)(x+2)\ge0$. Squaring again and factoring gives
\[
(p-x^2-x)(2p+x^2+8x+9)=0.
\]
If $2p+x^2+8x+9=0$, then $A=-2(x+2)^2$ and $B=-\dfrac{1}{2}(x+1)^2$; they cannot both be nonnegative. Thus $p=x^2+x$.

In this branch $A=(x+1)^2,B=(x+2)^2,C=(2x+3)^2$, and the original equation is
$|x+1|+|x+2|=|2x+3|$. It holds precisely when $x\le-2$ or $x\ge-1$.

The quadratic $x^2+x=p$ has two distinct roots for $p>-\dfrac{1}{4}$. For $-\dfrac{1}{4}<p\le0$, both roots lie in $[-1,\infty)$. For $0<p<2$, the negative root lies strictly between $-2$ and $-1$, and is excluded. For $p\ge2$, it lies at or below $-2$ and both roots qualify. Hence
\[
\boxed{p\in\left(-\dfrac{1}{4},0\right]\cup[2,\infty)}.
\]
