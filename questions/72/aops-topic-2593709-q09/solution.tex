Since $63=3^2\cdot7$, $\gcd(63,n+120)=21$ means $21\mid n+120$ but $9\nmid n+120$. Since $120=2^3\cdot3\cdot5$, $\gcd(n+63,120)=60$ means $60\mid n+63$ but $8\nmid n+63$.

The divisibilities give $n\equiv-120\equiv6\pmod{21}$ and $n\equiv-63\equiv57\pmod{60}$. Among $57,117,177,237$ (the numbers $\equiv57\pmod{60}$), $237$ is the first that is $\equiv6\pmod{21}$, so $n\equiv237\pmod{420}$.

The candidates above $1000$ are $1077,1497,1917,\dots$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $1077+120=1197=9\cdot133$, which is divisible by $9$: rejected.
\item $1497+63=1560=8\cdot195$, which is divisible by $8$: rejected.
\item $1917+120=2037$ has digit sum $12$, so it is not divisible by $9$; $1917+63=1980$ is not divisible by $8$. So $n=1917$ works.
\end{itemize}
The digit sum is $1+9+1+7=\boxed{\textbf{(C) }18}$.
