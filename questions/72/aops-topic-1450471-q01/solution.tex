Regard the equation as a quadratic in $x$:
\[
x^2-(y+2)x+(y^2-2y)=0 .
\]
For an integer (even a real) solution the discriminant must be a nonnegative perfect square:
\[
\Delta=(y+2)^2-4(y^2-2y)=-3y^2+12y+4=16-3(y-2)^2 .
\]
$\Delta\ge0$ forces $(y-2)^2\le5$, so $y\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}$, with $\Delta=4,13,16,13,4$. Only $y=0,2,4$ give squares.
\begin{itemize}
\item $y=0$: $x=\dfrac{2\pm2}{2}$, so $x=0$ or $2$.
\item $y=2$: $x=\dfrac{4\pm4}{2}$, so $x=0$ or $4$.
\item $y=4$: $x=\dfrac{6\pm2}{2}$, so $x=2$ or $4$.
\end{itemize}
The solutions are
\[
\boxed{(x,y)\in\{(0,0),(2,0),(0,2),(4,2),(2,4),(4,4)\}}.
\]
