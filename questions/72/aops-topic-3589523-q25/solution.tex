For $n=5$, a row with $b$ black points creates $\binom b2+\binom{5-b}{2}\ge4$ same-colour pairs of columns. Across five rows there are at least $20$ such pairs. There are only $2\binom52=20$ colour-and-column-pair types, and no type may repeat without forming a forbidden rectangle. Equality must therefore hold everywhere, so each row has two or three black points, and exactly ten black pairs occur. If $r$ rows have three black points, the number of black pairs is $3r+(5-r)=5+2r$, which cannot equal ten. Hence $n=5$ is impossible, and any larger grid also contains a forbidden $5\times5$ subgrid. For $n=4$, the rows $0011,0101,1010,1100$ (where $1$ means black) work: any two rows share at most one black column and at most one white column. Thus $\boxed{n=4}$.
