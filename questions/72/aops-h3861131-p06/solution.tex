The area of a quadrilateral is at most half the product of its diagonals. Each diagonal is at most the sum of its endpoint distances from $P$. The three pairings give bounds $\dfrac{1}{2}(5+6)(7+9)=88$, $\dfrac{1}{2}(5+7)(6+9)=90$, and $\dfrac{1}{2}(5+9)(6+7)=91$. The largest is attained with $A=(5,0),D=(-9,0),B=(0,6),C=(0,-7)$ and $P=(0,0)$, whose perpendicular diagonals are $AD,BC$. Thus $\boxed{91}$. 
\begin{asy}
texpreamble("\usepackage{amsmath,amssymb}");
size(220);
defaultpen(fontsize(9pt));
pair A=(5,0),B=(0,6),C=(0,-7),D=(-9,0),P=(0,0);draw(A--B--D--C--cycle);draw(A--D);draw(B--C);label("$A$",A,(1,0));label("$B$",B,(0,1));label("$C$",C,(0,-1));label("$D$",D,(-1,0));label("$P$",P,(1,1));
\end{asy}
