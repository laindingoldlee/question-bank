Treat ounces as measures of volume that simply add.

Deb pours $\dfrac{16}{3}$ ounces of cocoa into Harold's mug, keeping $\dfrac{32}{3}$. Harold's mug then holds $\dfrac{16}{3}$ ounces of cocoa and $\dfrac{32}{3}$ of milk, $16$ ounces in all, so the well-stirred mixture is $\dfrac{1}{3}$ cocoa.

Deb's mug needs $16-\dfrac{32}{3}=\dfrac{16}{3}$ ounces to be full. That much mixture contains $\dfrac{1}{3}\cdot\dfrac{16}{3}=\dfrac{16}{9}$ ounces of cocoa. Deb's mug now holds
\[
\dfrac{32}{3}+\dfrac{16}{9}=\dfrac{112}{9}=\boxed{12\dfrac{4}{9}}\text{ ounces}
\]
of cocoa powder.
