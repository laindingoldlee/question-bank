Squaring gives $x+y+2\sqrt{xy}=3+\sqrt5$. Set rational $u=3-x-y$. Then $2\sqrt{xy}=u+\sqrt5$; another squaring shows $4xy=u^2+5+2u\sqrt5$. Rationality forces $u=0$, so $x+y=3$ and $xy=\dfrac{5}{4}$. The roots are $\dfrac{1}{2},\dfrac{5}{2}$, both nonnegative and valid. Their sum is $\boxed{3}$, choice A.
