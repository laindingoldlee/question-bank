\textbf{(a)} Completing the square,
\[x^2+bx+c=\left(x+\dfrac b2\right)^2+\left(c-\dfrac{b^2}{4}\right).\]
If $c>\dfrac{b^2}{4}$, the right side is at least $c-\dfrac{b^2}{4}>0$ for every real $x$, so there is no real root. If $c\le\dfrac{b^2}{4}$, then $x=-\dfrac b2+\sqrt{\dfrac{b^2}{4}-c}$ is real and makes the right side $0$, so there is a real root. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{(b)} The roots of $ax^2+bx+c$ are the roots of $x^2+\dfrac bax+\dfrac ca$. By (a) there are none iff $\dfrac ca>\dfrac{b^2}{4a^2}$; multiplying by $4a^2>0$, iff $4ac>b^2$, i.e. $\boxed{b^2-4ac<0}$. $\blacksquare$
