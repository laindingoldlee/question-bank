A row is either a single $3$ or a $1$ and a $2$. The same is true of columns. Replacing each $3$ by a superimposed $1$ and $2$ uniquely splits the matrix into $P+2Q$, where $P,Q$ are permutation matrices. Conversely any pair of permutation matrices gives a valid matrix, with coincident entries making $3$. This bijection with ordered pairs of permutations gives $\boxed{(N!)^2}$.
