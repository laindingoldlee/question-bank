(a) Writing the sum forwards and backwards, $2S=n(n+1)$, so $\boxed{1+\cdots+n=\dfrac{n(n+1)}2}$.

(b) Sum the identity $(k+1)^3-k^3=3k^2+3k+1$ for $k=1,\dots,n$. The left side telescopes to $(n+1)^3-1$, so
\[3\sum k^2=(n+1)^3-1-\dfrac{3n(n+1)}2-n=\dfrac{(n+1)(2n^2+n)}2,\]
giving $\boxed{\sum_{k=1}^nk^2=\dfrac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}6}$.

(c) Similarly, $(k+1)^4-k^4=4k^3+6k^2+4k+1$. Summing,
\[4\sum k^3=(n+1)^4-1-n(n+1)(2n+1)-2n(n+1)-n=n^2(n+1)^2,\]
so $\boxed{\sum_{k=1}^nk^3=\left(\dfrac{n(n+1)}2\right)^2}$. (Expanding confirms the middle step: $(n+1)^4-(n+1)-n(n+1)(2n+1)-2n(n+1)=(n+1)\big[(n+1)^3-1-n(2n+1)-2n\big]=(n+1)(n^3+n^2)$.)
