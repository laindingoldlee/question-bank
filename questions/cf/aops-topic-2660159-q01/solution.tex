\emph{Size.} Together $x^2$ and $x^3$ must have $10$ digits. For $x\le46$, $x^2\le2116$ has at most $4$ digits and $x^3\le97336$ at most $5$; for $x\ge100$ they have at least $5+7=12$ digits. For $47\le x\le99$, $x^2$ (from $2209$ to $9801$) has $4$ digits and $x^3$ (from $103823$ to $970299$) has $6$. So $47\le x\le99$.

\emph{Mod $9$.} The ten digits add up to $45$, so $x^2+x^3=x^2(x+1)\equiv0\pmod9$. If $3\nmid x$ this needs $9\mid x+1$. So $3\mid x$ or $x\equiv8\pmod9$.

\emph{Last digit.} If $x$ ends in $0,1,5$ or $6$, then $x^2$ and $x^3$ end in the same digit, a repetition.

\emph{Check.} The remaining candidates are $48,54,57,63,69,72,78,84,87,93,99$ and $53,62,89,98$. All but one show a repeated digit:

For instance $93$ fails because $8649$ and $804357$ share $4$ and $8$. Only $x=69$ works: $69^2=4761$ and $69^3=328509$ use each digit once. So $x=\boxed{69}$, and it is the only such number.
