Dividing by $3$, $x+\dfrac{1}{x}=\dfrac{5}{3}$. Squaring,
\[
x^2+2+\dfrac{1}{x^2}=\dfrac{25}{9},\qquad\text{so}\qquad x^2+\dfrac{1}{x^2}=\dfrac{25}{9}-2=\dfrac{7}{9}.
\]
Therefore
\[
3x^2+\dfrac{3}{x^2}=3\cdot\dfrac{7}{9}=\boxed{\dfrac{7}{3}}.
\]
A common mistake is to square $3x+\dfrac{3}{x}$ directly and forget both the middle term and the factor: $\left(3x+\dfrac{3}{x}\right)^2=9x^2+18+\dfrac{9}{x^2}$, which equals $25$, so $9\left(x^2+\dfrac{1}{x^2}\right)=7$ and again $3x^2+\dfrac{3}{x^2}=\dfrac{7}{3}$. (Here $x$ is not real, since $x+\dfrac{1}{x}=\dfrac{5}{3}$ has negative discriminant; the identity holds for the complex roots all the same.)
