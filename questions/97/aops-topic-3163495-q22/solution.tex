Negative $x$ give nonpositive products, and $0\le x<1$ gives zero. At positive integer $x=m$, the product is $m^3$, supplying ten values for $1\le m\le10$. For $n<x<n+1$, $n\ge1$, the product ranges over the open interval $(n^2(n+1),n(n+1)^2)$. These intervals lie strictly between consecutive cubes and are disjoint. For $n=1,\ldots,9$, each contributes $n(n+1)-1$ integers, all at most $900$. The next interval starts at $1100$. Thus the count is $10+\sum_{n=1}^9(n^2+n-1)=10+285+45-9=\boxed{331}$.
