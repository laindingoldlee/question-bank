With width $w$ the length is $w+4$:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
draw(box((0,0),(16,12)),linewidth(0.9));
label("$w+4$",(8,0),S);label("$w+4$",(8,12),N);label("$w$",(0,6),W);label("$w$",(16,6),E);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The perimeter is the sum of the four sides,
\[
w+(w+4)+w+(w+4)=4w+8 .
\]
The student's left side $4+w+4+w+w+w$ is the same expression (the two lengths written as $4+w$, then the two widths), so \textbf{the equation is correct}; the mistake must have come later, for example in solving it or in answering with the width or the length instead of the area.

Solving, $4w+8=56$ gives $4w=48$, so $w=12$ and the length is $16$. The area is
\[
12\cdot16=\boxed{192}\text{ square inches}.
\]
(Check: $2(12+16)=56$.)
