\textbf{(a)} Let the lines make angles $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ with the positive $x$-axis, measured counterclockwise and chosen in the interval $(-90^\circ,90^\circ)$, so that $m_1=\tan\alpha_1$ and $m_2=\tan\alpha_2$. The lines intersect, so they are not parallel and $\alpha_1\ne\alpha_2$; say $\alpha_1<\alpha_2$. The lines form the angles $\varphi=\alpha_2-\alpha_1$ and $180^\circ-\varphi$, where $0^\circ<\varphi<180^\circ$, and $\theta$ is the smaller of them.

Since $\cos\alpha_1>0$ and $\cos\alpha_2>0$,
\[\cos\varphi=\cos\alpha_2\cos\alpha_1+\sin\alpha_2\sin\alpha_1=\cos\alpha_1\cos\alpha_2\left(1+m_1m_2\right),\]
which is $0$ exactly when $m_1m_2=-1$. So the lines are perpendicular exactly when $m_1m_2=-1$.

Otherwise, by the subtraction formula for tangents,
\[\tan\varphi=\dfrac{\tan\alpha_2-\tan\alpha_1}{1+\tan\alpha_1\tan\alpha_2}=\dfrac{m_2-m_1}{1+m_1m_2}.\]
The angle $\theta$ is $\varphi$ or $180^\circ-\varphi$, and $\tan\left(180^\circ-\varphi\right)=-\tan\varphi$. As $\theta<90^\circ$, $\tan\theta>0$, so $\tan\theta=\left|\tan\varphi\right|=\left|\dfrac{m_1-m_2}{1+m_1m_2}\right|$. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{(b)} \textbf{No.} Fix any $\theta$ with $0^\circ<\theta<90^\circ$.
\begin{itemize}
\item The lines through the origin at angles $0^\circ$ and $\theta$ to the $x$-axis meet at angle $\theta$, and their slopes $0$ and $\tan\theta$ have product $0$.
\item The lines at angles $\beta=45^\circ-\dfrac{\theta}{2}$ and $45^\circ+\dfrac{\theta}{2}=90^\circ-\beta$ also meet at angle $\theta$ (both angles lie between $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$), and their slopes $\tan\beta$ and $\tan\left(90^\circ-\beta\right)=\dfrac{1}{\tan\beta}$ have product $1$.
\end{itemize}
So the same angle $\theta$ allows the products $0$ and $1$, and $m_1m_2$ is \textbf{not} determined by $\theta$. For example, with $\theta=45^\circ$, the slopes $0,1$ and the slopes $\sqrt2-1,\sqrt2+1$ both give lines meeting at $45^\circ$. What $\theta$ does determine is the combination $\left|\dfrac{m_1-m_2}{1+m_1m_2}\right|$ from (a); only for $\theta=90^\circ$ does it pin down the product.
