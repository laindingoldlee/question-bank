Assume $c=\min(a,b,c)$ and set $x=a+\dfrac c2$, $y=b+\dfrac c2$, so $x+y=a+b+c\le3$. Since $a\ge c$, $x^2=a^2+ac+\dfrac{c^2}4\ge a^2+c^2$, and similarly $y^2\ge b^2+c^2$. Also $x^2+y^2\ge a^2+b^2$. So the left side is at least
\[g(x,y)=\dfrac1{x^2+y^2}+\dfrac1{x^2}+\dfrac1{y^2}.\]
$g$ is homogeneous of degree $-2$, so scaling $x+y$ up to $3$ only decreases it; assume $x+y=3$. Let $t=x^2+y^2\in\left[\dfrac92,9\right)$. Then $xy=\dfrac{9-t}2$ and
\[g=\dfrac1t+\dfrac{t}{(xy)^2}=\dfrac1t+\dfrac{4t}{(9-t)^2}.\]
Its derivative is $-\dfrac1{t^2}+\dfrac{4(9+t)}{(9-t)^3}$. For $t\ge\dfrac92$ this is at least $-\dfrac4{81}+\dfrac{4\cdot13.5}{4.5^3}>0$, so $g$ is increasing and $g\ge g\left(\dfrac92\right)=\dfrac29+\dfrac89=\dfrac{10}9$. Equality holds at $(a,b,c)=\left(\dfrac32,\dfrac32,0\right)$ and permutations. $\blacksquare$
