The integers are countably infinite. Rational numbers are countable as well: list reduced fractions with positive denominators in increasing order of $|m|+n$, listing the finitely many pairs $(m,n)$ of each size. Since the rationals contain the integers, the two sets have equal countably infinite cardinality.

The real numbers are uncountable. If all numbers in $[0,1)$ were listed in decimal form, choosing the $n$th digit of a new decimal to be $1$ when the $n$th listed decimal's $n$th digit is not $1$, and $2$ otherwise, would create a number different from every listed one. Use decimal expansions not ending in recurring nines; the new decimal with only digits $1,2$ has no expansion ambiguity.
If the irrationals were countable, adjoining the countable rationals would make all reals countable, a contradiction. Therefore
\[
\boxed{|\mathbb Q|<|\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q|,\qquad |\mathbb Z|=|\mathbb Q|}.
\]
