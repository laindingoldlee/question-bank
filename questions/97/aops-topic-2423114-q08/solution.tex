Since $4000=2^5\cdot5^3$,
\[4000\cdot\left(\dfrac25\right)^n=2^{5+n}\cdot5^{3-n}.\]
This is an integer exactly when both exponents are nonnegative (a negative exponent of $2$ or $5$ leaves that prime in the denominator, and the other prime cannot cancel it), i.e. when $-5\le n\le3$. There are $\boxed{9}$ such integers.
