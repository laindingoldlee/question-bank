\textbf{No.}

For a regular $n$-gon with side $s$ and apothem $a$ (the distance from the centre $O$ to a side), cutting it into $n$ triangles from the centre gives
\[
\text{Area}=n\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}sa=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\text{perimeter}\cdot a .
\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair ft(pair P,pair U,pair V){return U+dot(P-U,V-U)/dot(V-U,V-U)*(V-U);}
path ra(pair P,pair u,pair v,real s=0.2){return P+s*unit(u)--P+s*unit(u)+s*unit(v)--P+s*unit(v);}
int n=6; real R=2; pair O=(0,0); pair[] V; for(int i=0;i<n;++i) V.push(R*dir(-90-180/n+360*i/n));
draw(V[0]--V[1]--V[2]--V[3]--V[4]--V[5]--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
pair M=(V[0]+V[1])/2;
draw(O--V[0],gray+dashed); draw(O--V[1],gray+dashed); draw(O--M,red+linewidth(0.8));
draw(ra(M,V[1]-M,O-M,0.15));
dot(O); label("$O$",O,N); label("$a$",(O+M)/2+(0,-0.25),W,red); label("$s$",M,S);
draw(arc(O,0.5,-90,-90+180/n)); label("$\theta$",O+0.68*dir(-75));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Here $\theta=\dfrac{180^\circ}{n}$ is half the central angle. So we would need $160=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot48\cdot a$, that is $a=\dfrac{20}{3}$. Each central triangle is isosceles with apex angle $\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}$, so $\dfrac{s}{2}=a\tan\dfrac{180^\circ}{n}$, and with $s=\dfrac{48}{n}$:
\[
\dfrac{24}{n}=\dfrac{20}{3}\tan\dfrac{180^\circ}{n}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n\tan\dfrac{180^\circ}{n}=\dfrac{18}{5}=3.6 .
\]
Let $g(n)=n\tan\dfrac{180^\circ}{n}$ for $n\ge3$. Writing $x=\dfrac{\pi}{n}$ (in radians), $g(n)=\pi\cdot\dfrac{\tan x}{x}$, and $\dfrac{\tan x}{x}$ increases on $\left(0,\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$; so $g$ decreases as $n$ grows. Now
\[
g(6)=6\tan30^\circ=2\sqrt3\approx3.464<3.6,
\]
\[
g(5)=5\tan36^\circ=5\sqrt{5-2\sqrt5}>3.6,
\]
because $25(5-2\sqrt5)>12.96\iff 2\sqrt5<4.4816\iff\sqrt5<2.2408$, which is true. Hence $g(n)>3.6$ for $n\le5$ and $g(n)<3.6$ for $n\ge6$, so $g(n)=3.6$ has no solution: no regular polygon has area $160$ and perimeter $48$.

(For comparison, even a circle of circumference $48$ has area only $\dfrac{48^2}{4\pi}\approx183$, and a regular $n$-gon with perimeter $48$ has area $\dfrac{576}{n\tan\dfrac{180^\circ}{n}}$: about $158.5$ for $n=5$ and $166.3$ for $n=6$. The value $160$ falls in between.)
