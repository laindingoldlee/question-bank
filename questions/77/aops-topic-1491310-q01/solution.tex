\textbf{When is an inscribed triangle obtuse?} Let $O$ be the centre. An inscribed angle is obtuse exactly when it subtends an arc greater than a semicircle. So a triangle with vertices on the circle is obtuse exactly when its three vertices lie in an open semicircle, that is, when $O$ lies outside the triangle. (A right triangle needs a side to be a diameter, which has probability $0$.)

\textbf{Case 1: all four points lie in a semicircle.} Then every three of them do, so all four triangles are obtuse.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
draw(unitcircle,linewidth(0.8)); pair O=(0,0);
pair[] P={dir(15),dir(65),dir(115),dir(160)};
draw((-1.15,0)--(1.15,0),gray+dashed);
draw(P[0]--P[1]--P[2]--P[3]--cycle,blue+linewidth(0.8)); draw(P[0]--P[2]^^P[1]--P[3],blue+dashed);
dot(O); label("$O$",O,S);
for(int k=0;k<4;++k){dot(P[k]); label("$P_{"+(string)(k+1)+"}$",P[k],P[k]);}
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\textbf{Case 2: they do not.} Then $O$ is inside the quadrilateral $P_1P_2P_3P_4$; otherwise a line through $O$ would have all four points on one side, in one semicircle. The diagonal $P_1P_3$ splits the quadrilateral into triangles $P_1P_2P_3$ and $P_1P_3P_4$, and (with probability $1$) $O$ lies inside one of them, which is then acute. So not every triangle is obtuse. (In fact the other diagonal gives a second acute triangle, so exactly two of the four triangles are acute.)

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
draw(unitcircle,linewidth(0.8)); pair O=(0,0);
pair[] P={dir(-10),dir(80),dir(160),dir(250)};
draw(P[0]--P[1]--P[2]--P[3]--cycle,blue+linewidth(0.8)); draw(P[0]--P[2]^^P[1]--P[3],blue+dashed);
dot(O,red); label("$O$",O,dir(-45),red);
for(int k=0;k<4;++k){dot(P[k]); label("$P_{"+(string)(k+1)+"}$",P[k],P[k]);}
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\textbf{Probability of Case 1.} For each point $P_i$, let $E_i$ be the event that the other three points lie in the semicircle that starts at $P_i$ and runs counterclockwise. Then $P(E_i)=\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^3=\dfrac{1}{8}$. All four points lie in some semicircle exactly when one of the $E_i$ occurs (take $P_i$ to be the first point of the semicircle, going counterclockwise), and with probability $1$ at most one $E_i$ occurs. Hence
\[
P(\text{all four triangles obtuse})=4\cdot\dfrac{1}{8}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}}.
\]
(Under the other reading, ``some triangle is obtuse'', the probability is $1$: in Case 2 exactly two triangles are obtuse.)
