The available letters are S, I, Y and two copies of L.

\textbf{Three different letters.} Choose them from the four distinct letters S, I, L, Y and order them: $4\cdot3\cdot2=24$ strings.

\textbf{Two L's and one other letter.} The other letter is S, I or Y ($3$ choices) and it can be in any of the $3$ positions, the L's filling the rest: $3\cdot3=9$ strings.

A string cannot use three L's. In total there are $24+9=\boxed{33}$ strings.

Dividing $5\cdot4\cdot3=60$ by $2$ gives $30$, which is wrong: only the $18$ ordered selections that use both L's are counted twice (they give the $9$ strings with two L's), while the other $42$ ordered selections give $42$ different strings. So the count is $42+\dfrac{18}{2}=33$.
