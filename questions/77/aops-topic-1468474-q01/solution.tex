Each student can shake hands with at most $10$ others (everyone except themselves and their partner). The other eleven students have different counts from $\{0,1,\dots,10\}$, so they have exactly the counts $0,1,\dots,10$.

The student with $10$ shook hands with everyone except their partner; in particular with everyone who is not their partner. So the student with $0$, who shook no hands, must be that partner. Remove this pair: every remaining student shook exactly one hand with the $10$-student and none with the $0$-student, so the remaining counts all drop by $1$, and the same argument pairs $9$ with $1$, then $8$ with $2$, $7$ with $3$ and $6$ with $4$. The only one of the eleven left without a partner among them is the student with $5$ handshakes, who must therefore be Hannah's partner.

So Hannah's partner shook $\boxed{5}$ hands. (Hannah herself also shook $5$.)
