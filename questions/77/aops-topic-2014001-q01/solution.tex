Statements I and III cannot both be true, since the digit cannot be both $1$ and $3$. As only one statement is false, exactly one of I and III is false, and therefore II and IV are both true.
\begin{itemize}
\item If I is the false one, then III is true and the digit is $3$; II and IV hold. Consistent.
\item If III is the false one, then I is true and the digit is $1$; II and IV hold. Consistent.
\end{itemize}
So the digit is $1$ or $3$, and the statements that must necessarily be true are
\[
\boxed{\text{II and IV}}
\]
(I and III may each be true or false).
