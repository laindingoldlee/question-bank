The vector space of polynomials $Ax^2+Bxy+Cy^2+Dx+Ey+F$ has dimension six. Evaluation at the five points defines a linear map into $\mathbb R^5$.
This map has rank five: for each point $P_i$, partition the other four points into two pairs and multiply the equations of the two joining lines. The resulting quadratic vanishes at the other four points but not at $P_i$, because no joining line contains a third point. Its evaluation vector is a nonzero multiple of the $i$th coordinate vector.

The kernel therefore has dimension one. A nonzero polynomial in it gives a conic through the five points, unique up to scalar. It cannot have degree at most one, since five such points cannot lie on a line. Nor can it factor into two real lines, whose union could contain at most four of the points. An imaginary or isolated-point degenerate conic also cannot contain five real points. Thus the conic is nondegenerate.
