Let $L=\dfrac{1+\sqrt{29}}{2}\approx3.19$, the positive root of $x^2=x+7$.

\textbf{The limit exists.} By induction $0<a_n<L$: indeed $a_1=\sqrt7<L$, and if $a_n<L$ then $a_{n+1}=\sqrt{7+a_n}<\sqrt{7+L}=\sqrt{L^2}=L$. Also the sequence increases: $a_{n+1}^2-a_n^2=7+a_n-a_n^2=-(a_n-L)(a_n-L')>0$, where $L'=\dfrac{1-\sqrt{29}}{2}<0$, because $a_n<L$ and $a_n>L'$. An increasing sequence bounded above converges.

\textbf{The value.} If $a_n\to x$, then passing to the limit in $a_{n+1}^2=7+a_n$ gives $x^2=7+x$, so $x=\dfrac{1\pm\sqrt{29}}{2}$. Since all $a_n>0$, $x\ge0$, hence
\[
\sqrt{7+\sqrt{7+\sqrt{7+\cdots}}}=\boxed{\dfrac{1+\sqrt{29}}{2}} .
\]
