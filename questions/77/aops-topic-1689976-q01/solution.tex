Interpret each term as a distance. Let $O=(0,0)$, $P=(a,3)$, $Q=(b,5)$ and $T=(8,9)$, as in the figure (drawn for one particular choice of $a$ and $b$). Then
\[
OP=\sqrt{a^2+3^2},\qquad PQ=\sqrt{(b-a)^2+2^2},\qquad QT=\sqrt{(8-b)^2+4^2},
\]
so the expression is the length of the broken line $O\to P\to Q\to T$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");

draw((-0.3,0)--(8.8,0),gray);draw((0,-0.3)--(0,9.6),gray);
draw((0,3)--(8.8,3),gray+dotted);draw((0,5)--(8.8,5),gray+dotted);
draw(O--P--Q--T,blue+linewidth(0.9));
draw(O--T,red+dashed+linewidth(0.8));
dot(O);dot(P);dot(Q);dot(T);
label("$O$",O,SW);label("$P$",P,NW);label("$Q$",Q,SE);label("$T(8,9)$",T,NE);
label("$3$",(0,3),W);label("$5$",(0,5),W);label("$9$",(0,9),W);label("$8$",(8,0),S);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

By the triangle inequality, $OP+PQ+QT\ge OT=\sqrt{8^2+9^2}=\sqrt{145}$. Equality holds when $P$ and $Q$ lie on segment $OT$, i.e.\ at the points of $OT$ with heights $3$ and $5$: $a=\dfrac{3}{9}\cdot8=\dfrac{8}{3}$ and $b=\dfrac{5}{9}\cdot8=\dfrac{40}{9}$. Then the three terms are $\dfrac{\sqrt{145}}{3}$, $\dfrac{2\sqrt{145}}{9}$ and $\dfrac{4\sqrt{145}}{9}$, which add up to $\sqrt{145}$. The least value is $\boxed{\sqrt{145}}$.
