Walking once around a convex polygon, at each vertex we turn through the exterior angle there, and in total we turn through one full turn. So the exterior angles of any convex polygon add up to $360^\circ$. In a regular polygon with $s$ sides all $s$ exterior angles are equal, so
\[
7.5s=360,\qquad s=\boxed{48}.
\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair U=(-3,0), V=(0,0), Wv=5*dir(20);
draw(U--V--Wv,linewidth(1));
draw(V--(5.2,0),dashed);
draw(arc(V,2.2,0,20),red);
label("$7.5^\circ$",V+2.3*dir(10),E,red);
dot(U); dot(V); dot(Wv);
label("$V$",V,S); label("$U$",U,S); label("$W$",Wv,N);
label("(angle not to scale)",(1,-0.9),fontsize(8pt));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

(The figure shows two consecutive sides $UV$ and $VW$ of the $48$-gon, enlarged: side $UV$ is extended beyond $V$ (dashed), and the red angle between the extension and side $VW$ is the $7.5^\circ$ exterior angle at $V$, drawn larger than true size so that it is readable.) Equivalently, each interior angle is $180^\circ-7.5^\circ=172.5^\circ$, and $\dfrac{180(s-2)}{s}=172.5$ also gives $s=48$, as in the post.
