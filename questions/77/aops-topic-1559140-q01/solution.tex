Since $k(k-1)=2\dbinom k2$ and, by the hockey-stick identity, $\dbinom22+\dbinom32+\cdots+\dbinom n2=\dbinom{n+1}{3}$,
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{n}k(k-1)=2\binom{n+1}{3}=\boxed{\dfrac{(n-1)n(n+1)}{3}}.
\]
Alternatively, $\sum k^2-\sum k=\dfrac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}-\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}=\dfrac{n(n+1)(2n-2)}{6}$, the same. Check: $n=5$ gives $\dfrac{4\cdot5\cdot6}{3}=40=0+2+6+12+20$.
