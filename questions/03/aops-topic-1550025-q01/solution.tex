Only remainders matter, since the remainder of a sum depends only on the remainders of the summands. Reducing modulo $4$, starting from $F_1=F_2=1$:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccccc}
n & 1&2&3&4&5&6&7&8\\\hline
F_n \bmod 4 & 1&1&2&3&1&0&1&1
\end{array}
\]
Since $F_7\equiv F_1$ and $F_8\equiv F_2$, the remainders repeat with period $6$. As $100=6\cdot16+4$, we get $F_{100}\equiv F_4\equiv\boxed{3}\pmod 4$.

(A pattern written as $1,0,1,1,2,3,\ldots$ starts at $F_5$, not $F_1$; using it as if it started at $F_1$ gives the wrong remainder $1$.)
