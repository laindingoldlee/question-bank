Since $9\equiv1\pmod8$, a number written in base $9$ is congruent modulo $8$ to the sum of its digits. So each digit-summing step keeps the number's remainder modulo $8$, and the $M$-root of $n$ is a single nonzero base-$9$ digit ($1$ to $8$) congruent to $n$ modulo $8$. Hence the $M$-root is $1$ exactly when $n\equiv1\pmod8$.

In base $10$ the range is $100_9=81$ to $1000_9=729$. Both ends are $\equiv1\pmod8$ ($81=8\cdot10+1$, $729=8\cdot91+1$), so the numbers are $81,89,\dots,729$, and there are
\[\dfrac{729-81}{8}+1=\boxed{82}\]
of them.
