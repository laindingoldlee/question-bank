Clearing denominators in the second equation gives $a(xy+yz+zx)=xyz$. Now use the identity
\[(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)=(x+y+z)(xy+yz+zx)-xyz,\]
which follows by expanding both sides (each equals $\sum x^2y+2xyz$ summed over the six terms $x^2y,x^2z,y^2x,y^2z,z^2x,z^2y$). Then
\[(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)=a(xy+yz+zx)-xyz=xyz-xyz=\boxed{0}.\]
So one of $x+y$, $y+z$, $z+x$ is $0$: two of the numbers are opposites and the third equals $a$.
