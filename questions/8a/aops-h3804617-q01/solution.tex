Put $\angle A=3\alpha$ and $\angle C=3\beta$, so $\alpha+\beta=30^\circ$. In triangle $ARC$, $\angle ARC=150^\circ$, and the sine rule gives $AR=2AC\sin\beta$. In triangle $AQC$, $\angle ACQ=2\beta$, hence
\[
AQ=AC\,\dfrac{\sin2\beta}{\sin(60^\circ+\alpha)}
=2AC\sin\beta=AR,
\]
using $\sin(60^\circ+\alpha)=\cos\beta$. Thus $AQR$ is isosceles with vertex angle $2\alpha$, and $\angle ARQ=90^\circ-\alpha$. The three angles around $R$ give
\[
\angle QRC=360^\circ-150^\circ-(90^\circ-\alpha)=120^\circ+\alpha.
\]
Since $0<\alpha<30^\circ$, its least integer value is $\boxed{121^\circ}$, attained when $\angle A=3^\circ$.

\end{asy}
