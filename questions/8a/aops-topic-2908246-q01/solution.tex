Let $S=a^3+b^3+c^3$, $Q=\sum_{\text{sym}}a^2b$ (six terms) and $P=abc$. Then $\sum a(2b^2+2c^2-a^2)=2Q-S$ and $(a+b+c)^3=S+3Q+6P$. The claim is $6Q-3S\le S+3Q+6P$, i.e.
\[4S+6P-3Q=2(S+3P-Q)+(2S-Q)\ge0.\]
Schur's inequality gives $S+3P\ge Q$. And $2S\ge Q$ since $a^3+a^3+b^3\ge3a^2b$ by AM-GM, summed over the six terms. $\blacksquare$ Equality holds at $a=b=c$.
