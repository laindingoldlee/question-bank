The first marble is red with probability $\dfrac{5}{10}=\dfrac{1}{2}$, not $\dfrac{1}{5}$: five of the ten marbles are red. (The student seems to have divided $1$ by $5$ instead of dividing $5$ by $10$.) Given that the first is red, $4$ of the remaining $9$ marbles are red, so
\[
P(\text{both red})=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{4}{9}=\dfrac{2}{9} .
\]
Equivalently, $\dfrac{\binom52}{\binom{10}2}=\dfrac{10}{45}=\dfrac{2}{9}$. The answer is $\boxed{\text{(a) }\dfrac{2}{9}}$.
