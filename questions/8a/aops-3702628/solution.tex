Let $p_0=0$ and $p_n$ be the current player's optimal win probability before rolling. After rolling $r$, choose a move leaving the opponent the smallest $p$ among legal positions. Thus $p_n=1-\dfrac{1}{3}\sum_{r=1}^3\min_{1\le j\le\min(r,n)}p_{n-j}$. This gives $p_1=1,p_2=\dfrac{2}{3},p_3=\dfrac{5}{9},p_4=\dfrac{4}{9}$. More generally, direct induction in blocks of four gives, for $k\ge0$, $p_{4k+1}=\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2\cdot9^k}$, $p_{4k+2}=\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{6\cdot9^k}$, $p_{4k+3}=\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{18\cdot9^k}$, and for $k\ge1$, $p_{4k}=\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{2\cdot9^k}$. To check the induction, the value at each multiple of four is below one half; the next term is its complement, and the following two updates average that minimum with the successive decreasing values above one half. The final update complements the smallest preceding value and gives the next below-half term. Substitution in the displayed recurrence verifies each formula. Therefore (a) $\boxed{\dfrac{2}{3}}$, (b) $\boxed4$, and (c) exactly the positive multiples of four favour Bob.
