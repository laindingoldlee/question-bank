Prices per carrot: John $\dfrac{1}{3}$ dollar, Jihn $\dfrac{1}{2}$ dollar, Jahn $\dfrac{3}{5}$ dollar. Track the change in each person's money:
\[
\begin{array}{l|c|c|c}
 & \text{John} & \text{Jihn} & \text{Jahn}\\ \hline
\text{hour 1: sales} & 6 & 8 & 3\\
\text{Jahn takes 5 from each} & -5 & -5 & +10\\
\text{hour 2: sales} & 7 & 2 & 6\\
\text{Jihn takes 4 from each} & -4 & +8 & -4\\
\text{hour 3: sales} & 1 & 4 & 9\\
\text{John takes 3 from each} & +6 & -3 & -3\\ \hline
\text{one cycle} & 11 & 14 & 21
\end{array}
\]
(Nobody ever runs short: for example John has $6$ dollars when Jahn takes $5$, and $8$ when Jihn takes $4$; later cycles start with more money.) After three cycles they have $33$, $42$ and $63$ dollars. In the last hour they earn $18\cdot\dfrac{1}{3}=6$, $18\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}=9$ and $18\cdot\dfrac{3}{5}=10.8$ dollars, giving $39$, $51$ and $73.80$. The burglar takes $5$ from each. At the end:
\[
\boxed{\text{John }\$34,\quad \text{Jihn }\$46,\quad \text{Jahn }\$68.80,\quad \text{burglar }\$15.}
\]
