Every $O$ has exactly $4$ neighbouring $P$s (above, below, left and right), and there are $4$ $O$s. A copy is determined by its $O$, its first $P$ ($4$ choices among the neighbours) and its last $P$ ($4$ choices, possibly the same). So there are $4\cdot4\cdot4=\boxed{64}$ copies.

The poster's alternative counts $20$ per $O$ by treating a ``same-letter'' copy $P_1OP_1$ as having two orders; that double-counts a single sequence of positions. Under the stated convention a same-letter copy is one sequence, and each two-letter copy $P_iOP_j$ ($i\ne j$) and its reverse $P_jOP_i$ are two sequences: $4+12=16$ per $O$. If instead reverses were identified, there would be $4+6=10$ per $O$, $40$ in all; the answer depends only on stating the convention, which the original problem did not do.
