Expanding and summing gives $p=2n^2+14n+35$. Since $n(n+7)$ is even, $p\equiv3\pmod4$. Modulo $3$, $p\equiv2(n^2+n+1)$. If $n\equiv1\pmod3$, this is zero, impossible because $p\ge51>3$ is prime. In the other two residue classes $p\equiv2\pmod3$. Hence $p+1$ is divisible by both $4$ and $3$, and therefore by $12$.
