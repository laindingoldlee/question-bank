Let him buy $n$ balls, at $\dfrac{6}{n}$ dollars each. Then
\[
\left(\dfrac{6}{n}-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)(n+6)=6 .
\]
Multiply by $2n$: $(12-n)(n+6)=12n$, i.e.\ $-n^2+6n+72=12n$, so $n^2+6n-72=0$ and $(n-6)(n+12)=0$. Hence $n=\boxed{6}$.

Check: $6$ balls at \$1.00 each; at \$0.50 each, \$6 buys $12=6+6$ balls.
