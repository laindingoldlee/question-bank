If the tower converges to $2$, then $2=x^2$ (the exponent of the bottom $x$ is again the tower), so $x=\sqrt2$.

Conversely, for $x=\sqrt2$: $a_1=\sqrt2<2$, and if $a_k<2$ then $a_{k+1}=\sqrt2^{a_k}<\sqrt2^2=2$. Also $a_2=\sqrt2^{\sqrt2}>\sqrt2=a_1$, and since $t\mapsto\sqrt2^t$ is increasing, $a_{k+1}>a_k$ implies $a_{k+2}>a_{k+1}$. So the sequence increases and is bounded by $2$, hence converges to some $L\le2$ with $\sqrt2^L=L$. The equation $2^{\dfrac L2}=L$ has the roots $2$ and $4$ on $(0,4]$, and $L\le2$, so $L=2$. The answer is $\boxed{x=\sqrt2}$.

(The same reasoning would give $x=\sqrt2$ for ``tower $=4$'', but the tower at $\sqrt2$ converges to $2$, not $4$; so ``$=4$'' has no solution.)
