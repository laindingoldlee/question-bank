Two triangles $ABC$ and $DEF$ are called similar when $\angle A=\angle D$, $\angle B=\angle E$, $\angle C=\angle F$ and $\dfrac{DE}{AB}=\dfrac{EF}{BC}=\dfrac{FD}{CA}$. The three similarity criteria say that each of the following conditions is enough:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(SSS)] $\dfrac{DE}{AB}=\dfrac{EF}{BC}=\dfrac{FD}{CA}$;
\item[(SAS)] $\dfrac{DE}{AB}=\dfrac{FD}{CA}$ and $\angle A=\angle D$;
\item[(AA)] $\angle A=\angle D$ and $\angle B=\angle E$.
\end{itemize}
Prove directly, using the laws of sines and cosines, that if two triangles satisfy any one of these conditions, then they satisfy all three (so it does not matter which criterion is used to show that two triangles are similar).
