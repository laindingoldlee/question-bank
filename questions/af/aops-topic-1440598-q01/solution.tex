\textbf{Step 1: take $y=2$.} The variable $y$ appears only in the numerators, and both numerators increase with $y$ (all quantities are positive). So for fixed $x,z,t$, $F$ is largest at $y=2$.

\textbf{Step 2: the bound with $y=2$.} We show
\[
\dfrac{(x+2)(2+t)}{(x+z)(z+t)}+\dfrac{(2+t)(t+z)}{(z+x)(x+t)}\le4,
\]
that is, after multiplying by $(x+z)(z+t)(x+t)>0$,
\[
4(x+z)(z+t)(x+t)-(x+2)(2+t)(x+t)-(2+t)(t+z)^2\ \ge\ 0 .
\]
Put $x=1+p$, $z=1+q$, $t=1+r$ with $p,q,r\in[0,1]$. Expanding, the left side equals
\[
\begin{aligned}
&4p^2q+3p^2r+5p^2+4pq^2+8pqr+24pq+3pr^2+16pr+17p\\
&\quad+3q^2r+5q^2+2qr^2+14qr+20q+\left(2+r\right)\left(1-r\right)\left(1+r\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Every term is $\ge0$ for $p,q,r\in[0,1]$ (the last because $r\le1$). So $F\le4$ always.

\textbf{Equality.} All terms vanish exactly when $p=q=0$ and $r=1$, i.e. $x=z=1$, $t=2$, together with $y=2$. Then
\[
F=\dfrac{3\cdot4}{2\cdot3}+\dfrac{4\cdot3}{2\cdot3}=2+2=4 .
\]
The maximum value is $\boxed{4}$.
