\textbf{No.} The right side equals $b+c$, which is $\log_a\left(a^b\cdot a^c\right)$: the logarithm turns a \emph{product} into a sum, not a sum into a sum. For a counterexample take $a=10$, $b=c=1$:
\[
\log_{10}(10+10)=\log_{10}20\approx1.301,\qquad \log_{10}10+\log_{10}10=2 .
\]

The correct rules are
\[
\log_a\left(a^b\cdot a^c\right)=b+c,\qquad \log_a\left(a^b+a^c\right)=b+\log_a\left(1+a^{c-b}\right).
\]
The proposed identity holds only in the special cases where $a^b+a^c=a^{b+c}$ (for example $a=2$, $b=c=1$, since $2+2=4$).
