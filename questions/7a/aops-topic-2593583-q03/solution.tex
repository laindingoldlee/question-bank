Each of the six marbles is equally likely to be the one drawn (each bag has probability $\dfrac13$ and each of its marbles $\dfrac12$). Given that the marble is white, it is one of the three white marbles, each equally likely: the two in bag A and the one in bag C. The other marble is white in the first two cases, so the probability is $\boxed{\dfrac23}$.

Equivalently, by conditional probability,
\[P(\text{bag A}\mid\text{white})=\dfrac{\dfrac13\cdot1}{\dfrac13\cdot1+\dfrac13\cdot0+\dfrac13\cdot\dfrac12}=\dfrac23.\]
The tempting answer $\dfrac12$ (``it is bag A or bag C'') is wrong because a white draw is twice as likely from bag A as from bag C.
