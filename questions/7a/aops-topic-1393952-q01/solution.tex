Reduce modulo $14$: $15\equiv1$, so $15y+1\equiv y+1\equiv0\pmod{14}$, that is $y\equiv-1\equiv13\pmod{14}$. Write $y=14t-1$ with $t$ a positive integer (so $y\ge13$). Then
\[
14x=15(14t-1)+1=210t-14,\qquad x=15t-1 .
\]
Conversely every such pair works: $15(14t-1)+1=210t-14=14(15t-1)$. The solutions are
\[
\boxed{(x,y)=(15t-1,\ 14t-1),\quad t=1,2,3,\dots}
\]
that is $(14,13),(29,27),(44,41),\dots$ (Including $0$ in $\mathbb{N}$ adds nothing, since $y=14t-1\ge0$ needs $t\ge1$.)
