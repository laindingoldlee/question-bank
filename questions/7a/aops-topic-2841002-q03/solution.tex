A player facing $1$ stick must take it and lose. More generally, the positions with $n\equiv1\pmod6$ sticks are losing for the player to move: from such a position every move leaves $n-k$ with $n-k\not\equiv1$, while from any other position one can move to a number $\equiv1\pmod6$.

Since $98\equiv2\pmod6$, the first player takes $\boxed{1}$ stick, leaving $97$, and afterwards always takes $6-k$ when the opponent takes $k$. The pile then passes through $91,85,\dots,7,1$, leaving the opponent the last stick.
