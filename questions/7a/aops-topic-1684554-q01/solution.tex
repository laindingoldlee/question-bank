The side of the octagon is $\dfrac{64}{8}=8$ cm, so the square has side $8$ cm and area $64$ cm$^2$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real s=8,t=s/sqrt(2);
pair[] P={(0,0),(s,0),(s+t,t),(s+t,s+t),(s,s+2t),(0,s+2t),(-t,s+t),(-t,t)};
fill((0,0)--(s,0)--(s,s)--(0,s)--cycle,lightgray);
draw(P[0]--P[1]--P[2]--P[3]--P[4]--P[5]--P[6]--P[7]--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw((0,s)--(s,s),linewidth(0.7));draw((0,0)--(0,s),linewidth(0.7));draw((s,0)--(s,s),linewidth(0.7));
label("$8$",(s/2,0),S);label("$8$",(s/2,s),N);
label("removed",(s/2,s/2+0.6),fontsize(8pt));label("square",(s/2,s/2-0.6),fontsize(8pt));
label("$8$",(P[1]+P[2])/2,SE);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

A regular octagon of side $s$ is a square of side $s+2\cdot\dfrac{s}{\sqrt2}=s(1+\sqrt2)$ with four isosceles right triangles of legs $\dfrac{s}{\sqrt2}$ cut from its corners, so its area is
\[
s^2(1+\sqrt2)^2-4\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{s^2}{2}=s^2\left(3+2\sqrt2-1\right)=2(1+\sqrt2)s^2 .
\]
With $s=8$ this is $128+128\sqrt2$ cm$^2$. The square fits inside, because the distance between opposite sides of the octagon is $8(1+\sqrt2)>8$. The remaining area is
\[
128+128\sqrt2-64=\boxed{64+128\sqrt2\text{ cm}^2}.
\]
