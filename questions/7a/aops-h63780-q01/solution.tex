The disk centered at $O$ contributes equal area to each side. For equal-radius disks, the excess area on one side is an odd, strictly increasing function of the signed center-to-line distance whenever the line cuts the disk's interior. Therefore the other two signed distances must be negatives of each other. Their midpoint $M=(\dfrac{33}{2},88)$ lies on the line. Its slope through $O$ is
\[
\dfrac{88-76}{\dfrac{33}{2}-17}=-24.
\]
The line $24x+y=484$ has distances $\dfrac{56}{\sqrt{577}}<3$ from both $U$ and $V$, so it satisfies the intersection condition and the required area equality. The answer is $\boxed{24}$.
