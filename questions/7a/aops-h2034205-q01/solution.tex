Choose $A=(0,0)$ and $AD$ as the horizontal axis. Write
\[
B=(c\cos\theta,c\sin\theta),\qquad
C=(b\cos\theta,-b\sin\theta),
\]
where $b=AC,c=AB$ and $2\theta=\angle A$. The angle-bisector theorem gives $D=(d,0)$ with $d=\dfrac{2bc\cos\theta}{b+c}$.

Circle $ABD$ has equation $x^2+y^2-dx+vy=0$, where $v=\dfrac{d\cos\theta-c}{\sin\theta}$. On the vertical line $x=c\cos\theta$, the two intersection ordinates sum to $-v$. Subtracting the ordinate of $B$ gives
\[
E=\left(c\cos\theta,\ \dfrac{\cos\theta(c\cos\theta-d)}{\sin\theta}\right).
\]
The circumcenter equations $2O\cdot B=c^2$ and $2O\cdot C=b^2$ give
\[
O=\left(\dfrac{b+c}{4\cos\theta},\ \dfrac{c-b}{4\sin\theta}\right).
\]
Both $E$ and $O$ have the same slope from $A$:
\[
\dfrac{E_y}{E_x}=\dfrac{(c-b)\cos\theta}{(b+c)\sin\theta}
=\dfrac{O_y}{O_x}.
\]
Thus $A,O,E$ are collinear, including the case $b=c$, when both ordinates are zero.
