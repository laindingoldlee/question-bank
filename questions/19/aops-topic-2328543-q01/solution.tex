The triple $(0,0,0)$ works. We show it is the only one.

\textbf{All three are even.} The right side is a multiple of $4$, so $x^2+y^2+z^2\equiv0\pmod4$. The square of an even number is $\equiv0\pmod4$ and the square of an odd number is $\equiv1\pmod4$, so $x^2+y^2+z^2$ is congruent to the number of odd ones among $x,y,z$, which is $0,1,2$ or $3$. Hence none of them is odd.

\textbf{Descent.} So $x=2x_1$, $y=2y_1$, $z=2z_1$, and dividing the equation by $4$ shows that $(x_1,y_1,z_1)$ is again a solution. Suppose there were a solution other than $(0,0,0)$, and choose one with $|x|+|y|+|z|>0$ as small as possible. Then $(x_1,y_1,z_1)$ is a solution with $0<|x_1|+|y_1|+|z_1|=\dfrac12\left(|x|+|y|+|z|\right)$, smaller, a contradiction.

So the only solution is $\boxed{(x,y,z)=(0,0,0)}$.
