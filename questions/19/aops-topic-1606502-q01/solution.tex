\textbf{Necessary condition.} If $k$ divides both numbers, it divides their difference $(n+1)^2+1-(n^2+1)=2n+1$. Then it also divides
\[
4\left(n^2+1\right)-(2n+1)(2n-1)=4n^2+4-\left(4n^2-1\right)=5 .
\]
So $k=1$ or $k=5$.

\textbf{Both occur.} $k=1$ divides everything. For $k=5$, take $n=2$: $n^2+1=5$ and $(n+1)^2+1=10$ are both divisible by $5$.

Hence $k\in\boxed{\{1,5\}}$.
