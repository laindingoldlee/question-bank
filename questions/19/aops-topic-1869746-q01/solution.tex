No. Every rational number $\dfrac {p}{q}$ is a real number, and the square of every real number is at least $0$: if $x\ge0$ then $x^2=x\cdot x\ge0$, and if $x<0$ then $x^2=(-x)^2\ge0$. Suppose $i$ were rational. Then $i$ would be real, so $i^2\ge0$, contradicting $i^2=-1<0$. Hence $\boxed{i\text{ is not rational}}$ (it is not even real).

Consequently, when a quadratic with rational coefficients has a negative discriminant, its roots are non-real and so certainly not rational. Its roots are rational exactly when the discriminant is the square of a rational number, which in particular is at least $0$.
