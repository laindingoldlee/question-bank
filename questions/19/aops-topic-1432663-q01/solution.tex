Let $n\ge5$. We count the primes that are at most $2n$. Apart from $2$, every prime is odd, and the odd numbers greater than $1$ and at most $2n$ are
\[
3,5,7,9,\dots,2n-1,
\]
which is $n-1$ numbers. Since $2n-1\ge9$, the list contains $9=3^2$, which is not prime. So at most $n-2$ of them are prime, and together with $2$ there are at most $n-1$ primes not exceeding $2n$.

Hence the $n$-th prime cannot be $\le2n$, that is, $p_n>2n$. $\blacksquare$

(For example $p_5=11>10$, $p_6=13>12$, $p_7=17>14$. The bound fails for $n=4$: $p_4=7<8$.)
