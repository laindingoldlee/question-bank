Unique prime factorisation shows each positive divisor has a unique form $ p_1^{b_1}\cdots p_k^{b_k}$ with $ 0\le b_i\le a_i $. There are $ a_i+1 $ independent choices for $ b_i $, giving $\boxed{\prod_{i=1}^k(a_i+1)}$ divisors. The example has $(2+1)(1+1)(1+1)=\boxed{12}$ divisors.
