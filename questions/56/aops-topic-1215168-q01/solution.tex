A circle of radius $1$ tangent to $O$ from outside has its centre at distance $2$ from $O$. Two such circles do not overlap exactly when their centres are at least $2$ apart. If two centres $P,Q$ are at distance $2$ from $O$ with $\angle POQ=\theta$, then $PQ=4\sin\dfrac{\theta}{2}\ge2$ requires $\theta\ge60^\circ$. So at most $\dfrac{360^\circ}{60^\circ}=6$ circles fit, and $6$ is achieved with the centres at the vertices of a regular hexagon:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
draw(circle((0,0),1),blue+linewidth(0.9)); for(int k=0;k<6;++k) draw(circle(2*dir(60*k),1)); label("$O$",(0,0));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The answer is $\boxed{6}$.
