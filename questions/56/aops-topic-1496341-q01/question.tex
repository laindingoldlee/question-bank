The zero polynomial $0$ (all coefficients equal to $0$) is a polynomial, but it has no nonzero leading coefficient. Prove that no real number $d$ can be chosen as the degree of the zero polynomial so that the rule
\[
\deg(pq)=\deg p+\deg q
\]
holds for all polynomials $p$, $q$ (including the zero polynomial). (This is why the degree of the zero polynomial is left undefined, or set to $-\infty$ by convention.)
