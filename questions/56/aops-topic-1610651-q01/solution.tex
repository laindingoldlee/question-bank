The other marble is blue exactly when the chosen bag is A. Each bag is chosen with probability $\dfrac{1}{3}$, and the probability of drawing a blue marble from A, B, C is $1$, $\dfrac{1}{2}$, $0$. So
\[
P(\text{blue})=\dfrac{1}{3}\left(1+\dfrac{1}{2}+0\right)=\dfrac{1}{2},\qquad P(\text{bag A and blue})=\dfrac{1}{3},
\]
and
\[
P(\text{other is blue}\mid\text{blue drawn})=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{3}}{\dfrac{1}{2}}=\boxed{\dfrac{2}{3}}.
\]
The guess $\dfrac{1}{2}$ (``it is bag A or bag B'') is wrong: the two bags are not equally likely once a blue marble is seen. Of the three blue marbles that could have been drawn, equally likely, two are in bag A.
