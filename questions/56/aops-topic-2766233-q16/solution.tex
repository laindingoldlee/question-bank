Put $u=x+\sqrt6$, so $u>\sqrt6$ and the radical domain gives $u\le3$. The equation is $u=\sqrt{6+2u}-\sqrt{6-2u}$. Squaring gives $u^2=12-2\sqrt{36-4u^2}$; squaring again after isolating the nonnegative radical gives $u^2(u^2-8)=0$. The only candidate in the domain is $u=2\sqrt2$. It works because $\sqrt{6\pm4\sqrt2}=2\pm\sqrt2$. Hence $\boxed{x=2\sqrt2-\sqrt6}$.
