\textbf{Area of one patch.} Let the sphere have centre $O$ and radius $R$, let the patch have centre $P$, and let $Q$ be a point on its rim, so $PQ=\rho$. The patch is a spherical cap; let $h$ be its height, measured along $OP$ from $P$ to the plane of the rim. In the cross-section through $O$, $P$ and $Q$, the point $Q$ is at distance $R-h$ from $O$ along $OP$ and at distance $\sqrt{R^2-(R-h)^2}$ from line $OP$, so
\[\rho^2=h^2+R^2-(R-h)^2=2Rh,\qquad h=\dfrac{\rho^2}{2R}.\]
By Archimedes' formula, a cap of height $h$ on a sphere of radius $R$ has area $2\pi Rh$, so the patch has area
\[2\pi R\cdot\dfrac{\rho^2}{2R}=\pi\rho^2.\]
(The cap has exactly the area of a flat disc of radius $\rho$.)

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real R=3, rho=1.8, h=rho^2/(2R);
pair O=(0,0), P=(0,R), Q=(sqrt(R^2-(R-h)^2),R-h), Qp=(-Q.x,Q.y), M=(0,R-h);
draw(circle(O,R),linewidth(0.8));
real t=degrees(atan2(Q.y,Q.x));
draw(arc(O,R,t,180-t),blue+linewidth(2.5));
draw(Qp--Q,dashed);
draw(O--Q,gray); draw(O--P,gray);
draw(P--Q,red+linewidth(0.9));
dot(O);dot(P);dot(Q);dot(M);
label("$O$",O,S); label("$P$",P,N); label("$Q$",Q,E);
label("$R$",(O+Q)/2,SE,gray);
label("$\rho$",3.45*unit((P+Q)/2),red);
label("$h$",(0,R-h/2),W);
label("$R-h$",(0,(R-h)/2),W);
label("cap",(-1.2,R+0.15),NW,blue);
\end{asy}

\textbf{The probability.} With $\rho=2$, each patch has area $4\pi$, and the $12$ patches have total area $48\pi$. The sphere has area $4\pi\cdot42^2=7056\pi$. So the probability is
\[\dfrac{48\pi}{7056\pi}=\dfrac{1}{147},\]
and $a+b=1+147=\boxed{148}$.
