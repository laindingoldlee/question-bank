We prove a stronger statement by induction: with any odd number of positive-valued cards in piles of height at most two, the first player can secure a strictly larger sum. The one-card case is immediate. Choose a largest-valued card M. If M is exposed, the first player takes it. The opponent's reply has value at most M, so the first player is not behind after those two moves; the remaining position has fewer odd-many cards and is winning by induction. If M lies below a top card t, temporarily ignore that two-card pile. The remaining position has a smaller odd number of cards, so follow its winning strategy. If the opponent ever takes t, immediately take M, gaining a nonnegative amount relative to the opponent, and resume the strategy at the same virtual turn. If the pile is untouched until the reduced game ends, the opponent must take t and the first player takes M last. In every case the strict advantage from the reduced game is preserved. The original 21-card position satisfies this theorem, so Alice always wins.
