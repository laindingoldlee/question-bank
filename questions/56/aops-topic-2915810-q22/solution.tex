At least two rectangle vertices lie on one triangle side. They must be adjacent rectangle vertices, since a rectangle’s opposite corners cannot lie on a supporting line with its other corners on the same side. Use that rectangle side as base, on a triangle side of length $b$ and altitude $h$. If rectangle height is $y$, its width is $b(1-\dfrac{y}{h})$ by similarity. Thus its area is $by(1-\dfrac{y}{h})\le \dfrac{bh}{4}$, with equality at $y=\dfrac{h}{2}$. This is half the triangle area, independent of the chosen side. Heron gives triangle area $\sqrt{21\cdot8\cdot7\cdot6}=84$, so the maximum is $\boxed{42}$.
