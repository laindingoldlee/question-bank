\textbf{What is wrong.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Stars and bars treats the remaining prizes as identical.} It counts how many of the $4$ prizes each winner gets, not which prizes. Since the prizes are different, each remaining prize independently goes to one of $3$ winners, in $3^4=81$ ways; and for $4$ identical items among $3$ people the correct stars-and-bars count would be $\dbinom62=15$, not $\dbinom92$.
\item \emph{Overcounting.} Even with $7\cdot6\cdot5\cdot3^4$, each distribution is counted several times: a winner who ends with prizes $P$ and $Q$ is counted once with $P$ as the ``first'' prize and once with $Q$. So the method cannot be repaired by a simple fix.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Correct count (inclusion--exclusion).} Without the condition there are $3^7$ ways. Subtract those in which a given winner gets nothing ($2^7$ for each of the $3$ winners) and add back those in which two given winners get nothing ($1$ for each of the $3$ pairs):
\[
3^7-3\cdot2^7+3\cdot1=2187-384+3=\boxed{1806}.
\]
