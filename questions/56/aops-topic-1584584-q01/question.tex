Seven different prizes are to be distributed among three contest winners so that each winner receives at least one prize and each prize goes to one of the three winners. A student argues: ``First give every winner one prize: $7\cdot6\cdot5$ ways. Then distribute the remaining $4$ prizes with stars and bars: $\dbinom92=36$ ways. So there are $7\cdot6\cdot5\cdot36=7560$ ways.'' Explain what is wrong with this argument, and find the correct number of distributions.
