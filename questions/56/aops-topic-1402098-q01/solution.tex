If the endpoints differ by $(a,b)$, then $a^2+b^2=9$ with integers $a,b$. Since $9-a^2\in\{9,8,5,0\}$ for $|a|\le3$, and $8$ and $5$ are not squares, the only possibilities are $(a,b)=(\pm3,0)$ and $(0,\pm3)$. So every such segment is horizontal or vertical and passes through four consecutive points of a row or column of the array.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3.6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
for(int i=0;i<5;++i) for(int j=0;j<5;++j) dot((i,j),gray(0.4)+3);
draw((0,4)--(3,4),blue+linewidth(1.4)); draw((1,2)--(4,2),blue+linewidth(1.4));
draw((0,0)--(0,3),red+linewidth(1.4));
label("$3$",(1.5,4),N,blue); label("$3$",(0,1.5),W,red); label("$3$",(2.5,2),S,blue);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

In a row of $5$ points, a horizontal segment of length $3$ can start at the first or second point: $2$ segments per row, $5\cdot2=10$ horizontal segments. Likewise there are $10$ vertical segments. The total is $\boxed{20}$.
