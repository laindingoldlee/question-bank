Since $1\text{ m}^3=10^6\text{ cm}^3$, gold has mass $\dfrac{19320}{10^6}=0.01932$ kg per cubic centimetre.

\textbf{(a)} The case holds $25\cdot75\cdot50=93750\text{ cm}^3$. Filled with gold this is $93750\cdot0.01932=1811.25$ kg of gold, far more than $75$ kg. So \textbf{no}, he cannot lift it.

\textbf{(b)} One bar has volume $3\cdot12.5\cdot5=187.5\text{ cm}^3$ and mass $187.5\cdot0.01932=3.6225$ kg. With $n$ bars the total mass is $2.5+3.6225n$, and we need
\[
2.5+3.6225n\le75\iff n\le\dfrac{72.5}{3.6225}\approx20.01 .
\]
So $n\le20$. Twenty bars weigh $72.45$ kg, giving a total of $74.95$ kg, and they fit easily (for example, a single layer of $2$ bars across the $25$ cm side, with their $12.5$ cm edges along it, and $10$ bars along the $75$ cm side, with their $5$ cm edges along it, uses only a $3$ cm high layer). The answer is $\boxed{20}$ bars.
