Every such integer is $2pq$ for primes $p\le q$. Count those with $2pq\le286$: for $p=2,3,5,7,11$, the counts of possible $q$ are respectively $20,14,7,5,2$, totalling $48$. For example, the last two with $p=11$ use $q=11,13$. No $p\ge13$ is possible because $2p^2>286$. Since $286=2\cdot11\cdot13$ qualifies, exactly $47$ qualifying numbers are smaller, and the answer is $\boxed{286}$.
