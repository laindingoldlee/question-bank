Exactly seven draws are needed if and only if the first six draws have all six different colours. There are $6!$ possible colour orders and $4\cdot4\cdot2=32$ ways to select their distinct labelled balls. All ordered samples of six labelled balls are equally likely, so
\[
P=\dfrac{6!\cdot32}{13\cdot12\cdot11\cdot10\cdot9\cdot8}
=\dfrac{8}{429}.
\]
The seventh ball must repeat one of the six colours. Thus $m+n=\boxed{437}$.
