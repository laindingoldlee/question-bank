Let $g(t)=t+\sqrt{1+t^2}>0$. Then $g(t)\,g(-t)=(1+t^2)-t^2=1$, so $\dfrac1{g(x)}=g(-x)$. The condition says $g(y)=g(-x)$. Since $g$ is strictly increasing, $y=-x$ and $x+y=\boxed{0}$.
