From $\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3=\dfrac{9}{2}\pi$ we get $r^3=\dfrac{27}{8}$, so $r=\dfrac{3}{2}$ inches. The can has radius $\dfrac{3}{2}$ and height $3\cdot2r=9$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real r=1.5;
draw((-r,0)--(r,0)--(r,6r)--(-r,6r)--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
for(int i=0;i<3;++i) draw(circle((0,r+2r*i),r),blue);
draw((r+0.5,0)--(r+0.5,6r),Arrows(5)); label("$9$",(r+0.5,3r),E);
dot((0,5r)); draw((0,5r)--(r,5r),red);
label("$1.5$",(r/2,5r),N,red+fontsize(8pt));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Its surface area is two discs plus the curved side:
\[
2\pi r^2+2\pi rh=2\pi\cdot\dfrac{9}{4}+2\pi\cdot\dfrac{3}{2}\cdot9=\dfrac{9\pi}{2}+27\pi=\boxed{\dfrac{63\pi}{2}}\text{ square inches}.
\]
