Four teams have $4!=24$ possible strict rankings; four binary results distinguish at most 16, so at least five games are needed. Compare two disjoint pairs, then their winners (three games). Rename so $a<b<d$ and $c<d$. Insert $c$ into the chain $a<b<d$ with at most two more comparisons, giving five. For five teams, $5!=120>2^6$ requires seven games. After the same three comparisons on four teams, insert the fifth team $e$ into $a<b<d$ with two comparisons. Now insert $c$ among the positions before $d$; there are at most four such positions, distinguishable with two comparisons. Thus seven suffice.
