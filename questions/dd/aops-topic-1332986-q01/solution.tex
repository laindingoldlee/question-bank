No. Write $f(x)=ax^3+bx^2+cx+d$ with $a\ne0$. The critical points are the roots of $f'(x)=3ax^2+2bx+c$, so $f'(x)=3a(x-p)(x-q)$, which gives
\[
b=-\dfrac{3a(p+q)}{2},\qquad c=3apq .
\]
The $y$-intercept gives $d$. Finally
\[
f(1)-d=a+b+c=a\left(1-\dfrac{3(p+q)}{2}+3pq\right)=\dfrac{a}{2}\bigl(2-3(p+q)+6pq\bigr).
\]
If $2-3(p+q)+6pq\ne0$, this determines $a$, and hence $f$. But if $2-3(p+q)+6pq=0$, the data force $f(1)=d$ and leave $a$ free. For example, with $p=0$ and $q=\dfrac{2}{3}$ every cubic
\[
f(x)=a\left(x^3-x^2\right)+d\qquad(a\ne0)
\]
has critical points at $x=0$ and $x=\dfrac{2}{3}$, $y$-intercept $d$ and $f(1)=d$, so infinitely many cubics fit the same consistent data. Hence $f$ is determined exactly when $2-3(p+q)+6pq\ne0$, and not always.
