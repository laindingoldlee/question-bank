Record the list as a set. Let $\mathcal P$ be the $25$ primes, $\mathcal Q=\{1,4,8,9,16,25,27,32,36,49,64,81,100\}$ the $13$ perfect powers, $\mathcal M$ the $74$ multiples of $2$, $3$ or $5$, and $\mathcal Q_2=\{1,4,9,\dots,100\}$ the $10$ squares. The six rolls act as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item $1$: erase $\mathcal P$ and $\mathcal Q$;\quad $2$: erase $\mathcal M$;\quad $3$ and $5$: erase $\mathcal Q$ and $\mathcal M$;
\item $4$: stop;\quad $6$: add $\mathcal Q_2$.
\end{itemize}
Since erasing and adding sets are simple operations, the final list after any sequence of rolls is easy to describe. For example:
\begin{itemize}
\item rolls that never include $6$ leave $\{1,\dots,100\}$ minus the union of the erased sets (a $4$ ends the process early);
\item a $6$ restores the squares, and only rolls after it can erase them again.
\end{itemize}
Going through all $6^3=216$ equally likely roll sequences (stopping at the first $4$) and averaging the final sizes gives the exact expectation
\[
E=\dfrac{4063}{108}\approx37.62 .
\]
Some sample values: the list keeps all $100$ numbers if the first roll is $4$; it has $62$ numbers after $1,4$ (the $25$ primes and $13$ perfect powers are gone); $24$ after $3,4$ (only the numbers coprime to $30$ that are not perfect powers remain); $26$ after $2,2,2$; $34$ after $2,6,4$; and $2$ after $1,2,1$ (only $77$ and $91$ survive).

So the expected number of integers is $\boxed{38}$ (to the nearest whole number).
