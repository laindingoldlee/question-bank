Split the sum into two geometric series:
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^k+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^k=\dfrac{1}{1-\dfrac{1}{3}}+\dfrac{1}{1-\dfrac{2}{3}}=\dfrac{3}{2}+3=\boxed{\dfrac{9}{2}}.
\]
The student's terms are wrong from the third term on. The terms are $\dfrac{1+1}{1}=2$, $\dfrac{3+6}{9}=1$, $\dfrac{9+36}{81}=\dfrac{5}{9}$, $\dfrac{27+216}{729}=\dfrac{1}{3}$, $\dots$; the third term is $\dfrac{5}{9}$, not $\dfrac{1}{3}$.
