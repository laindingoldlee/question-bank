By symmetry assume $x\ge y$. If $x\ge y+2$, put $Q=\dfrac{x!}{y!}$, an even integer. If $w\le y$, the equation makes $2^z=(\dfrac{y!}{w!})(Q+1)$, impossible because $Q+1>1$ is odd. If $w>y\ge1$, the factor $y+1>1$ divides both $Q$ and $Q+1$, impossible. If $y=0$, $w=0,1$ cannot absorb the odd value $x!+1>1$, and $w\ge2$ makes the right side even, also impossible. Thus only equal or adjacent indices remain.
For $x=y=m$, the standard solution is $(z,w)=(1,m)$. For $m=0,1,2$ there are three choices each: when $m=0,1$, $(z,w)=(1,0),(1,1),(0,2)$; when $m=2$, $(2,0),(2,1),(1,2)$. For $m\ge3$, a smaller factorial can differ by a power of two only when $m$ itself is a power of two and $w=m-1$, since two consecutive integers beyond two include an odd factor. These eight extra cases are $m=4,8,\ldots,512$. Equal indices contribute $1001+6+8=1015$.
For $x=y+1$, divide by $y!$ to get the factor $y+2$. For $y=0$ there are three $(z,w)$ choices; for $y=2$ there are also three. For $y\ge3$, necessarily $w=y$ and $y+2$ is a power of two, giving $y=6,14,30,62,126,254,510$, seven choices. This is thirteen cases before reversing $x,y$. Therefore the total is $\boxed{1015+2\cdot13=1041}$.
