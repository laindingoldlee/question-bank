Since $x>0$ and $y>0$, the number $\sqrt x\,\sqrt y$ is positive, so we may divide both sides by it:
\[\dfrac{x\sqrt y}{\sqrt x\,\sqrt y}=\dfrac{y\sqrt x}{\sqrt x\,\sqrt y}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\dfrac{x}{\sqrt x}=\dfrac{y}{\sqrt y}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\sqrt x=\sqrt y .\]
Squaring the nonnegative numbers $\sqrt x$ and $\sqrt y$ gives $\boxed{x=y}$.

(Another way: write $x=a^2$ and $y=b^2$ with $a,b>0$. The equation becomes $a^2b=b^2a$, that is, $ab(a-b)=0$, and since $ab>0$ we get $a=b$, so $x=y$.)

Positivity is needed: the post says ``nonzero numbers'', but $\sqrt x$ and $\sqrt y$ are real only when $x,y\ge0$.
