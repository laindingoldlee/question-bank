A sphere of radius $r$ has surface area $4\pi r^2$, so the three kinds of doughnut holes have surface areas
\[
4\pi\cdot36,\qquad 4\pi\cdot64,\qquad 4\pi\cdot100 ,
\]
in the ratio $36:64:100=9:16:25$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(9cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real[] r={6,8,10}; real[] x={6,6+6+3+8,6+6+3+8+8+3+10};
string[] nm={"Niraek","Theo","Akshaj"};
for(int i=0;i<3;++i){pair O=(x[i],0);
 draw(circle(O,r[i]),linewidth(0.9)); dot(O);
 draw(O--O+r[i]*dir(30),dashed); label("$"+string(r[i])+"$",O+0.5*r[i]*dir(30),NW);
 label(nm[i],O-(0,r[i]),S);}
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Since everyone coats surface at the same rate, choose the unit of time so that Niraek finishes one doughnut hole every $9$ units; then Theo finishes one every $16$ units and Akshaj one every $25$ units. All three finish at the same moment exactly at the common multiples of $9$, $16$ and $25$; these are pairwise coprime, so the first such moment is
\[
\operatorname{lcm}(9,16,25)=9\cdot16\cdot25=3600 .
\]
By then Niraek has covered $\dfrac{3600}{9}=\boxed{400}$ doughnut holes.
