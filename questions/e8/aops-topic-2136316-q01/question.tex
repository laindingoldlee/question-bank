The harmonic series $H=1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{4}+\cdots$ is usually shown to diverge by comparing it with
\[T=1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{1}{8}+\dfrac{1}{8}+\dfrac{1}{8}+\dfrac{1}{8}+\dfrac{1}{16}+\cdots,\]
whose $n$th term is $\dfrac{1}{2^k}$, where $2^k$ is the least power of $2$ that is at least $n$. Each term of $T$ is at most the corresponding term of $H$, so $T\le H$, and grouping the terms of $T$ gives
\[T=1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\left(\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{1}{4}\right)+\left(\dfrac{1}{8}+\dfrac{1}{8}+\dfrac{1}{8}+\dfrac{1}{8}\right)+\cdots=1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}+\cdots,\]
which diverges.

A student objects: the series $G=1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}+\cdots$ has each term at least the corresponding term of $H$ (since $\dfrac{1}{2}\ge\dfrac{1}{3}$, $\dfrac{1}{2}\ge\dfrac{1}{4}$, and so on), so $G\ge H$. Since $G=T$, the series $T$ is both at most and at least $H$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Explain why there is no contradiction.
\item[(b)] Show that if $H$ were a finite number, the student's two comparisons \emph{would} lead to a contradiction. Conclude that the student's argument is itself another proof that $H$ diverges.
\end{enumerate}
