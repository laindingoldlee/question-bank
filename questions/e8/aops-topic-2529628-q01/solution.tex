Let the penny show $H_p$ heads and $T_p$ tails, and the nickel $H_n$ heads and $T_n$ tails, so $H_p+T_p=n+1$ and $H_n+T_n=n$. Hence
\[(H_p-H_n)+(T_p-T_n)=1.\]
Two integers adding to $1$ cannot both be at least $1$, and cannot both be at most $0$. So exactly one of the events
\[E:\ H_p>H_n\qquad\text{and}\qquad F:\ T_p>T_n\]
occurs, i.e. $P(E)+P(F)=1$.

Exchanging heads and tails on every coin is a bijection between the outcomes in $E$ and those in $F$. All $2^{2n+1}$ outcomes are equally likely, so $P(E)=P(F)=\dfrac12$. $\blacksquare$
