\textbf{(a)} There are $\dbinom{10}{4}=210$ choices in all. A choice with no complete pair takes gloves from $4$ different pairs ($\dbinom54=5$ ways) and one glove, left or right, from each ($2^4=16$ ways): $80$ choices. So $210-80=\boxed{130}$ choices contain at least one pair.

\textbf{(b)} Choose the complete pair ($5$ ways). The other two gloves must not form a pair: there are $\dbinom82=28$ ways to choose two of the remaining $8$ gloves, of which $4$ are pairs, leaving $24$. So there are $5\cdot24=\boxed{120}$ choices with exactly one pair.

Check: the choices with two complete pairs number $\dbinom52=10$, and $120+10=130$.
