Let the differences in the numbers of $7$, $12$ and $23$-cent coins be $x,y,z$ (not all zero). Then $x+y+z=0$ and $7x+12y+23z=0$; substituting $z=-x-y$ gives $16x+11y=0$, so $(x,y,z)=t(11,-16,5)$ with $t\ne0$.

Taking $t=1$ (swap the roles otherwise), one collection has at least $11$ sevens and $5$ twenty-threes and the other at least $16$ twelves. The minimum is $(11,0,5)$ against $(0,16,0)$, both worth $77+115=192=16\cdot12$. So the smallest value is $\boxed{192}$ cents.
