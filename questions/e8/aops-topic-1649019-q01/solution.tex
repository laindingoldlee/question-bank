Let $p_n$ be the probability of at least three heads in $n$ flips. Clearly $p_n$ increases with $n$ (extra flips cannot remove heads), and $p_n=0$ for $n\le2$.

For $n=4$: at least three heads means $3$ or $4$ heads, so $p_4=\dfrac{\dbinom43+\dbinom44}{2^4}=\dfrac{5}{16}<\dfrac{1}{2}$.

For $n=5$: by symmetry between heads and tails, ``at least $3$ heads'' and ``at least $3$ tails'' (that is, at most $2$ heads) are equally likely, and exactly one of them happens. So $p_5=\dfrac{1}{2}$ exactly, which meets the requirement ``at least $50\%$''. (Directly: $\dfrac{10+5+1}{32}=\dfrac{16}{32}$.)

The answer is $\boxed{5}$ flips.
