Let the still-water speeds be $u,v$, current speed $c$, and use minutes consistently. The downstream catch gives $\dfrac{v+c}{u+c}=\dfrac{50}{40}=\dfrac{5}{4}$. The upstream catch gives $\dfrac{v-c}{u-c}=\dfrac{30}{20}=\dfrac{3}{2}$. Solving yields $u=3c,v=4c$. If the first downstream journey takes $T$, the second takes $\dfrac{4}{5}T$ but starts ten minutes later; its twenty-minute lead gives $T-(10+\dfrac{4}{5}T)=20$, so $T=150$. The distance is $600c$. Upstream times are therefore 300 and 200 minutes. Allowing for the ten-minute later start, the second arrives $300-(10+200)=\boxed{90\text{ minutes}}$ earlier.
