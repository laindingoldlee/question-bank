Put $u=\dfrac{t}{k}$. Then $\log_2(k+t)=\log_2k+\dfrac{\log(1+u)}{\log2}$. For $u\ge0$,
\[
0\le u-\log(1+u)=\int_0^u\dfrac{v}{1+v}\,dv
\le\int_0^u v\,dv=\dfrac{u^2}{2}.
\]
Consequently the linear approximation and error bound are
\[
\boxed{\log_2(k+t)\approx\log_2k+\dfrac{t}{k\log2}},
\]
\[
0\le\log_2k+\dfrac{t}{k\log2}-\log_2(k+t)
\le\dfrac{t^2}{2k^2\log2}.
\]
The factor $\dfrac{1}{\log2}$ is required by the derivative of a base-two logarithm. The source-style coefficient $1$ is a rough secant-based estimate, not the first-order Taylor coefficient.
