\textbf{Counting.} Both sides count the subsets of an $n$-element set: $\dbinom nk$ subsets have $k$ elements, and summing over $k$ counts all subsets; on the other hand each element is either in or out, giving $2^n$ subsets.

\textbf{Binomial theorem.} Setting $a=b=1$ in $(a+b)^n=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dbinom nk a^{n-k}b^k$ gives $2^n=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dbinom nk$. $\blacksquare$
