Suppose such integers exist. Since $\gcd(b,c)=4$, the number $4$ divides $c$. Since $\gcd(d,e)=16$, the number $16$ divides $d$, so $4$ divides $d$. Hence $4$ is a common divisor of $c$ and $d$, so $4\mid\gcd(c,d)=9$, which is false. This contradiction shows that no such integers exist. $\blacksquare$

(The same argument works at every odd $k$ with $3\le k\le23$: the $k$-th number is divisible by $(k-1)^2$ and the $(k+1)$-th by $(k+1)^2$, and both of these are multiples of $4$, while their greatest common divisor $k^2$ is odd.)
