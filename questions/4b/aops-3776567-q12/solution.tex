Substitution gives $g(x)=x^4+4x^3+5x^2-4=0$. Its derivative is $g^{\prime}(x)=2x(2x^2+6x+5)$, and $2x^2+6x+5=2(x+\dfrac{3}{2})^2+\dfrac{1}{2}>0$. Thus $g$ decreases to $g(0)=-4$ then increases, tending to infinity at both ends. There is exactly one root on each side of zero. Each determines exactly one $y$, so there are $\boxed2$ solutions.
