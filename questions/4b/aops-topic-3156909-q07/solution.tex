Write $ N=6\cdot10^k+r $ with $ 0\le r<10^k $. The moved number is $ 10r+6 $, so $ 39r=6\cdot10^k-24 $ and $ r=\dfrac{2\cdot10^k-8}{13}$. Integrality requires $ 10^k\equiv4\pmod{13}$. For $ k=1,2,3,4,5 $ the residues are 10, 9, 12, 3, 4, so the first possible $ k $ is 5. It gives $ r=15384 $ and $\boxed{N=615384}$. Indeed moving the 6 yields 153846, one quarter of $ N $. A one-digit number 6 fails directly.
