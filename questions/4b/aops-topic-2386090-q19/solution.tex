The number $\operatorname{cis}\theta$ is the point of the unit circle at angle $\theta$ from the positive real axis. Turning through $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$ (three quarter turns) ends at the bottom of the circle:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
draw((-1.5,0)--(1.5,0),Arrow(6)); draw((0,-1.5)--(0,1.5),Arrow(6));
label("Re",(1.5,0),E); label("Im",(0,1.5),N);
draw(unitcircle,gray(0.5));
draw(arc((0,0),0.35,0,270),red,Arrow(5));
label("$\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$",0.35*dir(135),NW,red);
draw((0,0)--(0,-1),blue+linewidth(0.9)); dot((0,-1),blue); dot((1,0));
label("$-i$",(0,-1),SE,blue); label("$1$",(1,0),SE);
\end{asy}

Indeed $\cos\dfrac{3\pi}{2}=0$ and $\sin\dfrac{3\pi}{2}=-1$, so $\operatorname{cis}\dfrac{3\pi}{2}=\boxed{-i}$.
