Suppose $2^n$ ends in the four digits $dddd$. Then $2^n\ge1000$, so $n\ge10$, and the number formed by the last four digits is $2^n\bmod 10000=d\cdot1111$.

The digit $d$ is not $0$: otherwise $10000\mid2^n$, and $5$ would divide a power of $2$. Since $n\ge4$, $16\mid2^n$, and since $16\mid10000$, also $16\mid d\cdot1111$. But $1111$ is odd, so $16\mid d$, which is impossible for $1\le d\le9$. Hence no power of $2$ ends in four equal digits. $\blacksquare$
