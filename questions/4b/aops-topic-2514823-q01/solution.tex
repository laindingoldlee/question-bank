Evaluating from left to right, the number $1$ is always a factor, and each $k\in\{2,\dots,14\}$ is multiplied or divided. Let $D$ be the product of the numbers preceded by $\div$. All the numbers $1,\dots,14$ together have product $14!$, so the multiplied ones have product $\dfrac{14!}{D}$, and the value is
\[V=\dfrac{14!}{D}\cdot\dfrac1D=\dfrac{14!}{D^2},\qquad\text{where}\qquad14!=2^{11}\cdot3^5\cdot5^2\cdot7^2\cdot11\cdot13.\]
\textbf{Every integer value is a multiple of $858$.} If $V$ is an integer, then $D^2\mid14!$. Since $11^2$ and $13^2$ do not divide $14!$, neither $11$ nor $13$ divides $D$, so $V$ contains $11^1\cdot13^1$. The exponent of $2$ in $V$ is $11-2e$ for an integer $e\ge0$, which is odd, hence at least $1$. Similarly the exponent of $3$ is $5-2f$, odd, hence at least $1$. So $2\cdot3\cdot11\cdot13=858$ divides every integer value.

\textbf{$858$ itself occurs.} Divide by $2,3,10,12,14$ and multiply by everything else: then $D=2\cdot3\cdot10\cdot12\cdot14=10080=2^5\cdot3^2\cdot5\cdot7$ and
\[V=\dfrac{2^{11}\cdot3^5\cdot5^2\cdot7^2\cdot11\cdot13}{2^{10}\cdot3^4\cdot5^2\cdot7^2}=2\cdot3\cdot11\cdot13=858.\]
Hence the greatest common divisor of all the integer values is $\boxed{858}$.
