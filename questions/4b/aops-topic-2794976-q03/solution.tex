A two-digit palindrome is $\overline{aa}=11a$ with $1\le a\le9$. A four-digit palindrome $\overline{pqqp}=1001p+110q$. Let the sum be $\overline{rssr}=1001r+110s$. Then
\[11a=1001(r-p)+110(s-q),\qquad\text{so}\qquad a=91(r-p)+10(s-q).\]
If $r=p$, then $a=10(s-q)$ is a multiple of $10$, impossible for $1\le a\le9$. Since $a\le9$ we need $r=p+1$ and $a=91+10(s-q)$, forcing $s-q=-9$, i.e. $q=9$, $s=0$, and $a=1$.

So the equalities are $11+\overline{p99p}=\overline{(p+1)00(p+1)}$ for $p=1,\dots,8$:
\[11+1991=2002,\ 11+2992=3003,\ \dots,\ 11+8998=9009\quad(\boxed{8}\text{ equalities}).\]
