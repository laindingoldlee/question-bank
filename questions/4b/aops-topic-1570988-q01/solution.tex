Draw the diagonal $AC$; it splits $ABCE$ into triangles $ABC$ and $ACE$.

\begin{asy}

fill(A--B--C--Ep--cycle,paleyellow);

\textbf{Triangle $ABC$.} Each interior angle of a regular hexagon is $120^\circ$, so
\[
[ABC]=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot1\cdot1\cdot\sin120^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}.
\]
\textbf{Triangle $ACE$.} The short diagonals $AC$, $CE$, $EA$ are equal, so $ACE$ is equilateral. In isosceles triangle $ABC$ with apex angle $120^\circ$, the base is $AC=2\cdot1\cdot\sin60^\circ=\sqrt3$. Hence
\[
[ACE]=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}\left(\sqrt3\right)^2=\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{4}.
\]
Therefore
\[
[ABCE]=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}+\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{4}=\boxed{\sqrt3}\text{ square inches}.
\]
(Check: the whole hexagon has area $\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{2}$, and the part cut off, triangle $CDE$ together with triangle $EFA$, has area $2\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$.)
