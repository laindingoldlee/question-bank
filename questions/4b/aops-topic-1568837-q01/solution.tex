Let $x$ copies be sold.

\textbf{Money in.} For each copy the customer pays $\$30$ and Alex keeps $30-0.3\cdot30=\$21$; so he receives $21x$ dollars.

\textbf{Money out.} His costs are $20000+15x$ dollars.

A profit of at least $10\%$ means that what he receives is at least $110\%$ of his costs:
\[
21x\ge1.1\,(20000+15x)=22000+16.5x\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad 4.5x\ge22000\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad x\ge4888.\overline{8}.
\]
So at least $\boxed{4889}$ books were sold.

This is why the book's equation $1.1(20000+15x)=30x-9x$ works: Alex does spend money on the author and the printing, but every sale brings in $\$30$, of which $\$9$ goes to the bookstore. Selling enough copies makes the income $21x$ larger than the spending, and the equation asks when it is $10\%$ larger.
