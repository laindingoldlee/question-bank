We need $k\le b\le c\le9$, so $b$ and $c$ come from the $m=10-k$ integers $k,k+1,\dots,9$. Pairs with $b<c$ correspond to $2$-element subsets: $\dbinom m2$ of them; pairs with $b=c$: $m$ of them. In total
\[
\dbinom m2+m=\dfrac{m(m+1)}{2}=\boxed{\dfrac{(10-k)(11-k)}{2}}.
\]
For example $k=9$ gives $1$ pair, $(9,9)$, and $k=1$ gives $45$.
