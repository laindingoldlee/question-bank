The radius is $r=10$.

\begin{asy}

\textbf{(a)} $V_{\text{sphere}}=\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3=\dfrac{4}{3}\pi\cdot1000=\boxed{\dfrac{4000\pi}{3}}\approx4188.8$.

\textbf{(b)} The cylinder has radius $10$ and height $20$, so $V_{\text{cyl}}=\pi r^2\cdot 2r=2\pi r^3=2000\pi$. The cylinder is larger (it contains the sphere), and
\[
\dfrac{V_{\text{sphere}}}{V_{\text{cyl}}}=\dfrac{\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3}{2\pi r^3}=\boxed{\dfrac{2}{3}} .
\]
(This is Archimedes' result. A sphere and an arbitrary cylinder cannot be compared without knowing their sizes; the comparison makes sense for the cylinder that just fits around the sphere.)
