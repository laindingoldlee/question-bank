The can holds $\pi r^2h=\pi\cdot3^2\cdot10=90\pi$ cubic inches, and each hemispherical bowl holds half of a sphere of radius $3$:
\[\dfrac12\cdot\dfrac43\pi\cdot3^3=18\pi\text{ cubic inches}.\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real r=3, h=10, k=0.3;
draw(ellipse((0,h),r,k*r));
draw((-r,0)--(-r,h)); draw((r,0)--(r,h));
draw(shift(0,0)*xscale(r)*yscale(k*r)*arc((0,0),1,180,360));
draw(shift(0,0)*xscale(r)*yscale(k*r)*arc((0,0),1,0,180),dashed);
draw((0,h)--(r,h),red); label("$3$",(r,h),E,red);
draw((-r-0.6,0)--(-r-0.6,h),Arrows(4)); label("$10$",(-r-0.6,h/2),W);
pair c=(12,5);
draw(shift(c)*xscale(r)*yscale(k*r)*unitcircle);
draw(arc(c,r,180,360));
draw(c--c+(r,0),red); label("$3$",c+(r,0),E,red);
label("can",(0,-1.6)); label("bowl",c+(0,-r-0.9));
\end{asy}

So the can fills exactly $\dfrac{90\pi}{18\pi}=\boxed{5}$ bowls.
