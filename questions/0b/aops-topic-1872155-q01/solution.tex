Add and subtract terms so that pieces divisible by $a^3-1$ appear:
\[
a^{10}+a^5+1=(a^{10}-a)+(a^5-a^2)+(a^2+a+1)=a(a^9-1)+a^2(a^3-1)+(a^2+a+1).
\]
Since $a^3-1=(a-1)(a^2+a+1)$ and $a^9-1=(a^3-1)(a^6+a^3+1)$, every piece is divisible by $a^2+a+1$:
\[
a^{10}+a^5+1=(a^2+a+1)\bigl[a(a-1)(a^6+a^3+1)+a^2(a-1)+1\bigr].
\]
Expanding the bracket, $(a^2-a)(a^6+a^3+1)+a^3-a^2+1=a^8-a^7+a^5-a^4+a^2-a+a^3-a^2+1$, so
\[
\boxed{a^{10}+a^5+1=(a^2+a+1)(a^8-a^7+a^5-a^4+a^3-a+1)}.
\]
(Why $a^2+a+1$ is a factor: its roots $\omega$ satisfy $\omega^3=1$, so $\omega^{10}+\omega^5+1=\omega+\omega^2+1=0$. The degree-$8$ factor cannot be factored further over the integers.)
