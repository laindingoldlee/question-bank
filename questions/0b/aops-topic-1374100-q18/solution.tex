Let the planet start at $P_0$ and meet the UFO at $P_1$, one third of the way round, so $\angle P_0OP_1=120^\circ$ where $O$ is the star. The UFO starts at $U$, the midpoint of $OP_0$, with $OU=10000$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair O=(0,0),P0=(2,0),U=(1,0),P1=2*dir(120);
draw(circle(O,2),gray);
draw(O--P0,linewidth(0.8)); draw(O--P1,gray+dashed);
draw(U--P1,red+linewidth(0.9),Arrow(TeXHead));
draw(arc(O,2.25,8,118),blue,Arrow(TeXHead));
draw(arc(O,0.25,0,120));
dot(O);dot(P0);dot(U,red);dot(P1);
label("star $O$",O,W); label("$P_0$",P0,E); label("UFO",U,NE,red); label("$P_1$",P1,NW);
label("$120^\circ$",(0.14,0.42),fontsize(8pt));
label("$10000$",(0.5,0),S); label("$10000$",(1.5,0),S);
label("$20000$",P1/2,SW);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

By the law of cosines in triangle $UOP_1$,
\[
UP_1^2=10000^2+20000^2-2\cdot10000\cdot20000\cos120^\circ=10^8(1+4+2)=7\cdot10^8,
\]
so the UFO travels $UP_1=10000\sqrt7$ miles. In the same time the planet travels $\dfrac{1}{3}\cdot2\pi\cdot20000=\dfrac{40000\pi}{3}$ miles. The ratio of speeds is
\[
\dfrac{10000\sqrt7}{\dfrac{40000\pi}{3}}=\dfrac{3\sqrt7}{4\pi},
\]
so $a=3$, $b=7$, $c=4$ and $a+b+c=\boxed{14}$.
