The statement is true. Let $m=n^2-3n=n(n-3)$. Then $m^2=n^4-6n^3+9n^2$ and $14m=14n^2-42n$, so
\[n^4-6n^3+23n^2-42n+48=m^2+14m+48=(m+6)(m+8).\]
\emph{Divisibility by $8$.} The integers $n$ and $n-3$ have opposite parity, so $m$ is even, say $m=2t$. Then $(m+6)(m+8)=4(t+3)(t+4)$, and $(t+3)(t+4)$ is a product of two consecutive integers, hence even. So $8$ divides the expression.

\emph{Divisibility by $3$.} $m=n(n-3)\equiv n^2\pmod3$, so $m\equiv0$ or $1\pmod3$, and $(m+6)(m+8)\equiv m(m+2)\pmod3$. If $m\equiv0$ the first factor is divisible by $3$; if $m\equiv1$ the second is.

Since $\gcd(3,8)=1$, the expression is divisible by $24$ for every integer $n$ (including negative $n$, where the value may be a negative multiple of $24$). $\blacksquare$
