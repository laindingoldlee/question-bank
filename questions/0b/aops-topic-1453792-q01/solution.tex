First consider one guest. A guest who likes the movie says ``yes'' when telling the truth, with probability
\[
\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{100}+\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{51}{200}
\]
(averaging over the two cities). A guest who dislikes it says ``yes'' when lying, with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{99}{100}+\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{149}{200}$. By Bayes' theorem,
\[
P(\text{likes}\mid\text{yes})=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{51}{200}}{\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{51}{200}+\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{149}{200}}=\dfrac{51}{200}.
\]
The two guests are independent, so the probability that both like the movie, given that both said yes, is
\[
\left(\dfrac{51}{200}\right)^2=\boxed{\dfrac{2601}{40000}}\approx0.065 .
\]
