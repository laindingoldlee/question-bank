All angles of the three equilateral triangles are $60^\circ$. Going around $A$, the rays $AD$, $AB$, $AC$, $AF$ come in this order, because $D$ and $C$ are on opposite sides of $AB$, and $F$ and $B$ are on opposite sides of $AC$. So
\[
\angle DAF=\angle DAB+\angle BAC+\angle CAF=60^\circ+60^\circ+60^\circ=180^\circ ,
\]
which means $D$, $A$, $F$ lie on one line. $\blacksquare$

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=2*dir(120),C=2*dir(60),D=2*dir(180),F=2*dir(0);
draw(D--F,red+dashed);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9)); draw(A--D--B,linewidth(0.9)); draw(A--F--C,linewidth(0.9));
draw(arc(A,0.45,0,60),blue); draw(arc(A,0.55,60,120),blue); draw(arc(A,0.45,120,180),blue);
label("$60^\circ$",A+0.95*dir(30),blue); label("$60^\circ$",A+1.0*dir(90),blue); label("$60^\circ$",A+0.95*dir(150),blue);
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);dot(D);dot(F);
label("$A$",A,S); label("$B$",B,N); label("$C$",C,N); label("$D$",D,W); label("$F$",F,E);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}
