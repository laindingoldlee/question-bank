Call the identical balls $I,I$, the two balls that must be apart $X$ and $Y$, the ball that must be at an end $Z$, and the other two balls $U$ and $V$.

Place $Z$: $2$ choices (left or right end). The other $6$ balls fill the remaining $6$ consecutive places in $\dfrac{6!}{2!}=360$ ways (the two $I$'s are identical). Among these, $X$ and $Y$ are next to each other in $5$ positions of the pair $\times\ 2$ orders $\times\ \dfrac{4!}{2!}=12$ arrangements of the rest, that is $120$ ways. So for each position of $Z$ there are $360-120=240$ good arrangements, and the total is
\[2\cdot240=\boxed{480}.\]
