Fix $r\ge0$ and use induction on $n\ge r$, with Pascal's rule $\dbinom{m}{j}+\dbinom{m}{j+1}=\dbinom{m+1}{j+1}$.

\textbf{Base case $n=r$.} The left side is $\dbinom rr=1$ and the right side is $\dbinom{r+1}{r+1}=1$.

\textbf{Inductive step.} Suppose the identity holds for some $n\ge r$. Then
\[\dbinom rr+\cdots+\dbinom nr+\dbinom{n+1}{r}=\dbinom{n+1}{r+1}+\dbinom{n+1}{r}=\dbinom{n+2}{r+1}\]
by Pascal's rule with $m=n+1$, $j=r$. This is the identity for $n+1$.

By induction the identity holds for all $n\ge r$. $\blacksquare$ (In Pascal's triangle the terms on the left run down a diagonal, and their sum is the entry just below and to the side of the last one, which is why this is called the hockey-stick identity.)
