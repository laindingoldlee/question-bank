Count a path by its penultimate integer. For $n\ge2$, $f(n)=f(n-1)+\sum_{d\mid n,\ d<n}f(d)-\mathbf1_{n=2}$, with $f(1)=1$. The subtraction removes the duplicate description of the single move $1\to2$. The series converge: paths correspond to increasing subsets of intermediate integers, so $f(n)\le2^{n-2}$ alone is insufficient, but the recurrence gives an exponential bound $f(n)\le Cc^n$ for any fixed $1<c<2$ and sufficiently large $C$, because every proper divisor is at most $\dfrac{n}{2}$ and $\dfrac{n c^{\dfrac{n}{2}}}{c^{n-1}} \to 0$. Summing after division by $2^n$ gives $M - \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{M}{2} + (N-M) - \dfrac{1}{4}$. Hence $\boxed{N = \dfrac{3 M}{2} - \dfrac{1}{4}}$. The resulting polynomial is $P (t) = \dfrac{3 t}{2} - \dfrac{1}{4}$, so $\boxed{P (45) = \dfrac{269}{4}}$.
