Let $n$ have $k$ digits and digit sum $S$. Then $n\ge10^{k-1}$ and $S\le9k$, so $10^{k-1}\le n=4S\le36k$. For $k=3$ this would need $100\le108$, possible in principle, but then $n=4S\le4\cdot27=108$ and the three-digit numbers $100,\dots,108$ have digit sums at most $9$, so $4S\le36$, a contradiction. For $k\ge4$, $10^{k-1}>36k$. A one-digit $n$ would satisfy $n=4n$, impossible for $n>0$.

So $n=10a+b$ with $a\ge1$: $10a+b=4(a+b)$ gives $6a=3b$, so $b=2a$, with $b\le9$, so $a\le4$. The numbers are
\[
\boxed{12,\ 24,\ 36,\ 48}.
\]
