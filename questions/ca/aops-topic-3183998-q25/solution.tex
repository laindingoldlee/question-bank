Let $E$ mean that the three middle departures are increasing in age, and let $Y$ mean youngest leaves before oldest. We have $\Pr(E)=\dfrac{1}{6}$ and $\Pr(Y)=\dfrac{1}{2}$. Conditional on $E$, the set of three middle people is uniform. Both extreme ages are in it with probability $\dfrac{\binom51}{\binom73}=\dfrac{1}{7}$; then $Y$ certainly holds. In every other case, $Y$ has probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$: if one extreme is among the middle three, exactly two of the four outside slots are before it and two after; if neither is, their relative order is fair. Hence $\Pr(Y\mid E)=\dfrac{1}{7}+\dfrac{6}{7}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{4}{7}$. Bayes gives $\Pr(E\mid Y)=\dfrac{(\dfrac{1}{6})(\dfrac{4}{7})}{\dfrac{1}{2}}=\dfrac{4}{21}$, so $m+n=\boxed{25}$.
