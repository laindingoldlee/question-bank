Work modulo $5$. By Fermat's little theorem $t^5\equiv t\pmod5$ for every integer $t$, so $8(n-2)^5\equiv3(n-2)$. Also $14\equiv-1$ and $-24\equiv1$. Hence
\[
8(n-2)^5-n^2+14n-24\equiv3n-6-n^2-n+1=-n^2+2n-5\equiv-n(n-2)\pmod5 .
\]
This is divisible by $5$ exactly when $n\equiv0$ or $n\equiv2\pmod5$. Going down from $99999$: $99999\equiv4$, $99998\equiv3$, $99997\equiv2$. So the answer is $\boxed{99997}$.
