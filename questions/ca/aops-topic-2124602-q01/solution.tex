\textbf{(a)} The car covers $40+50=90$ miles in $2$ hours, so its average speed is $\boxed{45}$ mph.

\textbf{(b)} The two parts take $\dfrac{100}{40}=2.5$ hours and $\dfrac{100}{50}=2$ hours, so the average speed is
\[\dfrac{200}{4.5}=\boxed{\dfrac{400}{9}}\approx44.4\text{ mph}.\]

\textbf{(c)} If each part takes time $t$, the distance is $at+bt$ and the time is $2t$, so the average speed is $\dfrac{(a+b)t}{2t}=\dfrac{a+b}{2}$, the arithmetic mean. If each part has length $d$, the times are $\dfrac {d}{a}$ and $\dfrac {d}{b}$, so the average speed is
\[\dfrac{2d}{\dfrac {d}{a}+\dfrac {d}{b}}=\dfrac{2}{\dfrac{a+b}{ab}}=\dfrac{2ab}{a+b},\]
the harmonic mean. So $\dfrac{2ab}{a+b}$ applies only when the distances are equal, as in (b); in (a) the times are equal. In general, the only formula that always works is total distance divided by total time. (Since the car spends more time at the lower speed when the distances are equal, the harmonic mean is smaller than the arithmetic mean.)
