Since $n=(n+1)-1$, we have $n\equiv-1\pmod{n+1}$. Raising this congruence to the power $2k+1$,
\[n^{2k+1}\equiv(-1)^{2k+1}=-1\equiv n\pmod{n+1},\]
because $2k+1$ is odd. $\blacksquare$

(Without congruences: for $k\ge1$, $n^{2k+1}-n=n\left(n^{2k}-1\right)=n\left(n^2-1\right)\left(n^{2k-2}+n^{2k-4}+\cdots+1\right)$, and $n^2-1=(n-1)(n+1)$ is a multiple of $n+1$; for $k=0$ the difference is $0$.)
