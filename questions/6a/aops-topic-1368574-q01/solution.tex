First denest $\sqrt{2+\sqrt3}$. We have
\[\left(\sqrt6+\sqrt2\right)^2=6+2\sqrt{12}+2=8+4\sqrt3=4\left(2+\sqrt3\right).\]
Since $\sqrt6+\sqrt2>0$, taking square roots gives
\[\sqrt{2+\sqrt3}=\dfrac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{2}.\]
Therefore
\[\dfrac{2\left(\sqrt2+\sqrt6\right)}{3\sqrt{2+\sqrt3}}=\dfrac{2\left(\sqrt2+\sqrt6\right)}{3\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt2+\sqrt6}{2}}=\dfrac{4}{3},\]
which is rational. $\blacksquare$

(As printed in the post, with denominator $3\left(\sqrt2+\sqrt3\right)$, the value is about $0.819$ and is irrational; the square root over $2+\sqrt3$ was lost.)
