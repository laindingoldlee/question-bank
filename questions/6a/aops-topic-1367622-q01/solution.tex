There are $6^6=46656$ equally likely sequences. We need: no $0$, exactly one $1$, exactly two $2$'s, and $3$ not appearing exactly three times ($4$ and $5$ cannot win anyway, since only three places remain).

Choose the place of the $1$ ($6$ ways) and the places of the two $2$'s ($\dbinom52=10$ ways). The other $3$ places must be filled from $\{3,4,5\}$ (no $0$, no more $1$'s or $2$'s): $3^3=27$ ways, minus the one filling $333$, in which $3$ would win. That gives $6\cdot10\cdot26=1560$ sequences, and the probability is
\[\dfrac{1560}{46656}=\boxed{\dfrac{65}{1944}} .\]
