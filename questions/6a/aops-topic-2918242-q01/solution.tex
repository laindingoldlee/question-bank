$\angle ADC=180^\circ-70^\circ=110^\circ$, so in triangle $ADC$, $\angle ACD=180^\circ-110^\circ-28^\circ=42^\circ$. Then $\angle DCE=180^\circ-42^\circ=138^\circ$, and the isosceles triangle $DCE$ has $\angle CDE=\dfrac{180^\circ-138^\circ}2=21^\circ$.
\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair B=(0,0),C=(5,0),D=(2,0);pair A=extension(D,D+dir(110),C,C+dir(180-42));pair E=C+length(C-D)*unit(C-A);
draw(A--B--C--cycle);draw(A--D);draw(C--E,dashed);draw(D--E);label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,SW);label("$C$",C,S);label("$D$",D,S);label("$E$",E,NE);
\end{asy}

Since $B,D,C$ are collinear, $\angle BDE=180^\circ-21^\circ=\boxed{159^\circ}$.
