If the median is $100$, strictly more than half the observations must equal $100$: this is immediate for odd sample size; for even size both middle entries must be $100$. If the minimum is $m$, the mean is therefore greater than $\dfrac{m+100}{2}$ unless all grades are $100$. Consequently $91>\dfrac{m+100}{2}$ and $m<82$. The integer $81$ is attainable: take nine grades of $81$ and ten of $100$, with average $\dfrac{9\cdot81+10\cdot100}{19}=91$ and median $100$. Thus $\boxed{81}$.
