\textbf{(a)} Recording for $t$ hours uses $\dfrac {t}{2}$ of the battery, and playing back for $t$ hours uses $\dfrac {t}{3}$. So
\[\dfrac {t}{2}+\dfrac {t}{3}=1\iff\dfrac{5t}{6}=1\iff t=\dfrac{6}{5}\text{ hours}=\boxed{72}\text{ minutes}.\]

\textbf{(b)} The average rate of battery use over the whole job is the total battery used divided by the total time:
\[\dfrac{\dfrac {t}{2}+\dfrac {t}{3}}{2t}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}\right)=\dfrac{5}{12}\text{ battery per hour}.\]
This is the \emph{arithmetic} mean of the two rates, because the two phases last equally long. The harmonic mean of two rates is the right average when equal amounts of the quantity in the numerator (here, battery) are used in the two parts. The student's $\dfrac{2}{5}$ describes using half a battery for recording ($1$ hour) and half for playing back ($1.5$ hours), which are not the same length. With the correct average $\dfrac{5}{12}$ battery per hour, one battery lasts $\dfrac{12}{5}$ hours, half of which is $\dfrac{6}{5}$ hours $=72$ minutes.

\textbf{(c)} Measured in hours per battery, the rates are $2$ and $3$, and now the numerator quantity is time. Recording and playing back take equal times, so equal amounts of the numerator are used in the two parts, and the overall rate is the harmonic mean
\[\dfrac{2\cdot2\cdot3}{2+3}=\dfrac{12}{5}\text{ hours per battery},\]
giving again $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{12}{5}=\dfrac{6}{5}$ hours of recording. There is no convention involved: either unit works, provided one uses the arithmetic mean when the denominator quantity is split equally and the harmonic mean when the numerator quantity is split equally.
