\emph{Bounded by $2$.} $a_1<2$, and if $a_n<2$ then $a_{n+1}=\sqrt2^{\,a_n}<\sqrt2^{\,2}=2$.

\emph{Increasing.} $a_2=\sqrt2^{\,\sqrt2}>\sqrt2^{\,1}=a_1$, and if $a_n>a_{n-1}$ then $a_{n+1}=\sqrt2^{\,a_n}>\sqrt2^{\,a_{n-1}}=a_n$, because $\sqrt2^{\,x}$ is increasing.

So $(a_n)$ is increasing and bounded, hence converges to some $L$ with $\sqrt2<L\le2$, and by continuity $L=\sqrt2^{\,L}$, i.e. $2^{\dfrac L2}=L$.

This equation has the two solutions $L=2$ and $L=4$, and no others: $f(L)=2^{\dfrac L2}-L$ is convex, so it has at most two zeros. Since $L\le2$, the tower equals $\boxed{2}$.

(The trap: writing $y=\sqrt2^{\,y}$ and solving gives both $2$ and $4$, but $4$ is impossible because every $a_n$ is below $2$.)
