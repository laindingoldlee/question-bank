All terms lie in $[1,2]$: this holds initially and $1+\dfrac{1}{s}\in[1,2]$ whenever $s\in[1,2]$. Let $\phi=\dfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2}$, the positive solution of $\phi=1+\dfrac{1}{\phi}$. Then
\[|s_{n+1}-\phi|=\dfrac{|s_n-\phi|}{s_n\phi}\le\dfrac{1}{\phi}|s_n-\phi|.\]
Iterating gives $|s_n-\phi|\le\phi^{-n}|s_0-\phi|\to0$ because $\phi>1$. Therefore the continued fraction converges to $\boxed{\dfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2}}$. The negative root cannot be a limit of terms at least $1$.
