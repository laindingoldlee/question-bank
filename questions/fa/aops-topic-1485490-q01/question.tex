A student states the Chinese remainder theorem as: ``if $x\equiv y\pmod a$ and $x\equiv y\pmod b$, then $x\equiv y\pmod{ab}$.''
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Show by a counterexample that this statement is false in general.
\item[(b)] Prove that it is true when $\gcd(a,b)=1$.
\item[(c)] The usual Chinese remainder theorem says: if $\gcd(a,b)=1$, then for any integers $r$ and $s$ there is an integer $x$ with $x\equiv r\pmod a$ and $x\equiv s\pmod b$, and all such $x$ are congruent modulo $ab$. Prove it.
\end{enumerate}
