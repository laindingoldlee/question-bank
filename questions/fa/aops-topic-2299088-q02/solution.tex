Compare with the standard form $(y-k)^2=4p(x-h)$, a parabola with a horizontal axis, vertex $(h,k)$, focus $(h+p,k)$ and directrix $x=h-p$. Here $h=0$, $k=2$ and $4p=4$, so $p=1$:
\begin{itemize}
\item vertex $(0,2)$ and axis of symmetry $y=2$;
\item since $x=\dfrac{(y-2)^2}{4}\ge0$, the parabola opens to the right;
\item focus $(1,2)$ and directrix $x=-1$;
\item $x$-axis: $y=0$ gives $x=\dfrac{4}{4}=1$, the point $(1,0)$; $y$-axis: $x=0$ gives $y=2$ only, so the parabola touches the $y$-axis at its vertex $(0,2)$.
\end{itemize}
A few more points: $y=4$ gives $(1,4)$, and $y=6$, $y=-2$ give $(4,6)$ and $(4,-2)$. The sketch:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
import graph;
real f(real y){return (y-2)^2/4;}
path p=graph(new pair(real y){return (f(y),y);},-2.6,6.6);
draw((-2,0)--(6.2,0),Arrow(5)); draw((0,-3)--(0,7.2),Arrow(5));
label("$x$",(6.2,0),E); label("$y$",(0,7.2),N);
draw(p,blue+linewidth(1));
draw((-1,-3)--(-1,7),red+dashed); label("$x=-1$",(-1,-3),S,red+fontsize(8pt));
draw((-0.6,2)--(6,2),gray+dotted); label("$y=2$",(6,2),E,gray+fontsize(8pt));
dot((0,2)); label("$(0,2)$",(0,2),W,fontsize(8pt));
dot((1,2),red); label("$F(1,2)$",(1,2),NE,red+fontsize(8pt));
dot((1,0)); label("$(1,0)$",(1,0),NE,fontsize(8pt));
dot((1,4)); label("$(1,4)$",(1,4),NW,fontsize(8pt));
dot((4,6)); label("$(4,6)$",(4,6),W,fontsize(8pt)); dot((4,-2)); label("$(4,-2)$",(4,-2),W,fontsize(8pt));
\end{asy}
