For positive absolute exponents $u,v,w$ with $u+v+w\le16$, the eight sign patterns contribute $4(u+v+w)$ to $N$. Since $s\binom{s-1}{2}=3\binom s3$,
\[
N=4\sum_{s=3}^{16}s\binom{s-1}{2}
=12\binom{17}{4}=28560.
\]
The replacements preserve the product but cancel numerator factors. A particular prime cancels exactly when its exponents in $X,Y$ have opposite signs, and then the loss in $\Omega$ is the smaller absolute exponent. All three primes must cancel, while the total loss is $28560-28557=3$. Therefore the signs are opposite in all three coordinates, and each pair of absolute exponents has minimum $1$.

Write the absolute exponents as $1+x_i$ and $1+y_i$ for $i=1,2,3$. Then $\sum x_i,\sum y_i\le13$, all are nonnegative integers, and $x_iy_i=0$. If $r$ coordinates have positive $x_i$ and $s$ have positive $y_i$, choose their disjoint supports and positive values in
\[
\dfrac{3!}{r!\,s!\,(3-r-s)!}\binom{13}{r}\binom{13}{s}
\]
ways. Here the binomial counts positive entries with sum at most $13$, using one slack variable. Summing over $r+s\le3$ gives
\[
1+78+468+1014+572+6084=8217.
\]
For each absolute-exponent pair there are $8$ sign choices for $X$, with $Y$ then forced opposite. They are automatically distinct. Hence the answer is $\boxed{8(8217)=65736}$.
