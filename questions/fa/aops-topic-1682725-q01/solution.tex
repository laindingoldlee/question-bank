The base angles are $\angle CAB=\angle CBA=\dfrac{180^\circ-96^\circ}{2}=42^\circ$, so $\angle DAC=42^\circ-18^\circ=24^\circ$ and $\angle DBC=42^\circ-30^\circ=12^\circ$. Let $x=\angle ACD$, so $\angle DCB=96^\circ-x$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(6,0),C=(3,3*Tan(42));
pair D=extension(A,A+dir(18),B,B+dir(150));
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(A--D--B,linewidth(0.7));draw(C--D,blue+linewidth(0.7));
draw(arc(A,1.3,0,18),linewidth(0.5));label("$18^\circ$",A+1.85*dir(9),fontsize(8pt));
draw(arc(B,1.0,150,180),linewidth(0.5));label("$30^\circ$",B+1.55*dir(165),fontsize(8pt));
draw(arc(C,0.8,-138,-42),linewidth(0.5));label("$96^\circ$",C+1.25*dir(-112),fontsize(8pt));
label("$A$",A,SW);label("$B$",B,SE);label("$C$",C,N);label("$D$",D,dir(25));

The cevians $AD$, $BD$, $CD$ meet at $D$, so by the trigonometric form of Ceva's theorem
\[
\dfrac{\sin\angle BAD}{\sin\angle DAC}\cdot\dfrac{\sin\angle ACD}{\sin\angle DCB}\cdot\dfrac{\sin\angle CBD}{\sin\angle DBA}=1,
\quad\text{i.e.}\quad
\dfrac{\sin x}{\sin(96^\circ-x)}=\dfrac{\sin24^\circ\sin30^\circ}{\sin18^\circ\sin12^\circ}=\dfrac{\sin24^\circ}{2\sin18^\circ\sin12^\circ}.
\]
Try $x=78^\circ$: then $\dfrac{\sin78^\circ}{\sin18^\circ}$, and
\[
\dfrac{\sin78^\circ}{\sin18^\circ}=\dfrac{\sin24^\circ}{2\sin18^\circ\sin12^\circ}\iff2\sin12^\circ\cos12^\circ=\sin24^\circ,
\]
which is the double-angle formula (using $\sin78^\circ=\cos12^\circ$). The left side $\dfrac{\sin x}{\sin(96^\circ-x)}$ is strictly increasing for $0^\circ<x<96^\circ$ (the numerator increases and the denominator decreases while both are positive), so $x=78^\circ$ is the only solution. Hence $\angle ACD=\boxed{78^\circ}$.
