Such a permutation has exactly one ascent. It splits uniquely at that ascent into two nonempty decreasing runs. Choose the set of entries in the first run: there are $2^9-2$ nonempty proper subsets, and both runs then have forced decreasing order. A chosen boundary fails to be an ascent precisely when the entire concatenation is the decreasing order $9,8,\ldots,1$. Its eight possible cut positions give eight invalid subsets. Hence the count is $\boxed{2^9-2-8=502}$.
