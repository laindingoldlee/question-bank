Divisors of $n$ come in pairs $d$ and $\dfrac {n}{d}$. The two members of a pair are different unless $d=\dfrac {n}{d}$, i.e.\ $n=d^2$. So $n$ has an odd number of divisors exactly when $n$ is a perfect square, and an even number otherwise.

There are $10$ perfect squares from $1$ to $100$ ($1^2,\dots,10^2$), so $100-10=\boxed{90}$ numbers have an even number of divisors. (Counting primes and listing numbers like $8$ is unnecessary: the pairing argument does everything at once.)
