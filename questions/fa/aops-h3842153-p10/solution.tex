Every button has four neighbors. After choosing the first two buttons, the third has three available choices. One of these shares the line containing the first two buttons, leaving two choices for the fourth; the other two lie off that line and each leaves three choices. Therefore the total is $9\cdot4(1\cdot2+2\cdot3)=\boxed{288}$. Row and column permutations make this count the same for every initial button; corner/edge distinctions are unnecessary.
