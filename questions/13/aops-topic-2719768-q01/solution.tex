Let the numbers greater than $1$ be $a_1,\dots,a_k$; the other $100-k$ numbers are $1$. The condition is
\[a_1a_2\cdots a_k-(a_1+\cdots+a_k)=100-k.\]
$k=0$ and $k=1$ are impossible ($1\ne100$; $a\ne a+99$). Increasing any $a_i\ge2$ increases the left side, so it is at least $2^k-2k$; then $2^k-2k\le100-k$ forces $k\le6$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $k=2$: $ab-a-b=98$, i.e. $(a-1)(b-1)=99$, giving $\{a,b\}=\{2,100\},\{4,34\},\{10,12\}$: products $200,136,120$.
\item $k=3,\dots,6$: a finite search (each $a_i$ is bounded by the condition) gives only $\{4,4,7\}$ (product $112=15+97$) for $k=3$ and $\{2,2,3,3,3\}$ (product $108=13+95$) for $k=5$.
\end{itemize}
The possible products are $\boxed{108,\ 112,\ 120,\ 136,\ 200}$.
