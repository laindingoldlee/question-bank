Measure time in hours after 3 p.m., so the arrival times $X,Y,Z$ are independent and uniform in $[0,1]$. Each friend stays from arrival until $30$ minutes later (or 4 p.m.). All three are together at some moment exactly when the last arrival comes at most half an hour after the first:
\[\max(X,Y,Z)-\min(X,Y,Z)\le\dfrac12.\]
Indeed, if this holds, at the moment of the last arrival the other two are still waiting (no one has waited more than half an hour, and that moment is before 4 p.m.); if it fails, the first friend has left before the last one arrives.

\textbf{As a volume.} The point $(X,Y,Z)$ is uniform in the unit cube, so the probability is the volume of the region. By symmetry, each of the three friends is the first to arrive with probability $\dfrac13$, and ties have probability $0$; so the answer is $3V$, where $V$ is the volume of the region where $X$ is the minimum and $Y,Z\in\left[X,X+\dfrac12\right]$.

For a fixed $X=x$, the cross-section is the square $\left[x,\min\left(x+\dfrac12,1\right)\right]^2$ in the $(y,z)$-plane:
\begin{itemize}
\item for $0\le x\le\dfrac12$ it is a square of side $\dfrac12$, sliding diagonally: a slanted prism (parallelepiped) with base $\dfrac14$ and length $\dfrac12$ along the $x$-axis, of volume $\dfrac14\cdot\dfrac12=\dfrac18$;
\item for $\dfrac12\le x\le1$ the side $1-x$ shrinks linearly to $0$: a square pyramid with base $\dfrac12\times\dfrac12$, apex $(1,1,1)$ and height $\dfrac12$, of volume $\dfrac13\cdot\dfrac14\cdot\dfrac12=\dfrac1{24}$.
\end{itemize}

So $V=\dfrac18+\dfrac1{24}=\dfrac16$, and the probability is $3\cdot\dfrac16=\boxed{\dfrac12}$.

(In general, the probability that three uniform times lie within $t$ of each other is $3t^2-2t^3$.)
