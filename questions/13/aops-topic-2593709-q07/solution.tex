For integers $m$ and $n\ge1$, $\left\lfloor\dfrac{m+1}{n}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\dfrac mn\right\rfloor+1$ if $n\mid m+1$, and the two floors are equal otherwise.

If $n$ divides neither $999$ nor $1000$, the three floors are equal, so the sum $3q$ is divisible by $3$.

Since $\gcd(999,1000)=1$, only $n=1$ divides both, and then the sum is $998+999+1000=2997=3\cdot999$.

If $n>1$ divides $1000$ (but not $999$), the floors are $q-1,q-1,q$ with $q=\dfrac{1000}{n}$, and the sum $3q-2$ is not divisible by $3$. If $n>1$ divides $999$ (but not $1000$), they are $q-1,q,q$ and the sum $3q-1$ is not divisible by $3$.

So we count the divisors greater than $1$ of $1000=2^35^3$ (there are $16-1=15$) and of $999=3^3\cdot37$ (there are $8-1=7$). The answer is $15+7=\boxed{\textbf{(A) }22}$.
