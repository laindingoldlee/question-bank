Let the original group sizes be $s,t$, and the switching player\textquotesingle s age be $k$. Then $15s+k=16(s+1)$, so $k=s+16$. Also $25t-k=26(t-1)$, so $k=26-t$. Hence $s+t=\boxed{10}$. This is attainable, for example with one swimmer aged $15$, one tennis player aged $17$, and eight tennis players aged $26$. The original tennis average is $25$ and the new averages are $16,26$. No other total satisfies the two equations.
