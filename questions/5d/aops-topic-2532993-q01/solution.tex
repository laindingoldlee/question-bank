Let the street have length $L$ metres. Because both speeds are constant, the distances Allen and Ben have walked are always in the same ratio as their speeds.

\textbf{First meeting.} Together they have walked $L$, and Allen has walked $300$. So in any time interval, Allen walks $\dfrac{300}{L}$ of the combined distance.

\textbf{Second meeting.} Before either of them turns, they meet only once. While exactly one of them has turned, both walk towards the same end of the street, so they are not walking towards each other. Hence at the second meeting both have already turned. Allen has walked to $B$ and $400$ m back, $L+400$ in total, and Ben has walked to $A$ and $L-400$ m back, $2L-400$ in total. Together they have walked $3L$, three times as much as at the first meeting, so Allen has walked $3\cdot300=900$ m:
\[L+400=900,\qquad L=500.\]

At the first meeting Allen had walked $300$ m and Ben $500-300=200$ m in the same time, so Allen's speed is $\dfrac32$ of Ben's.

\emph{Check.} Take speeds $3$ and $2$ (metres per unit time). Allen reaches $B$ at time $\dfrac{500}{3}$ and Ben reaches $A$ at time $250$; at that moment Allen is $500-3\left(250-\dfrac{500}{3}\right)=250$ m from $A$, walking towards $A$. They meet when $250-3s=2s$, i.e. $s=50$, at $100$ m from $A$, which is $400$ m from $B$, walking towards each other.

The street is $\boxed{500\text{ m}}$ long, and \textbf{Allen} walks faster.
