Let $X=9^{9^{9^{9^{9}}}}$ and $Y=9^{9^{9^{9^{8.9999999}}}}$, and let $D=X-Y$. A tower gets larger when its top exponent increases, so the five choices satisfy $(A)<(B)<(C)<(D)=Y<(E)=X$.

The ratio $\dfrac XY$ is $9$ raised to the difference of two huge exponents ($9^{9^{9^9}}-9^{9^{9^{8.9999999}}}$, itself a difference of numbers whose ratio is enormous), so certainly $X>3Y$.
\begin{itemize}
\item Distance from $D$ to choice (E): $X-D=Y$.
\item Distance from $D$ to any of (A)--(D), each at most $Y$: at least $D-Y=X-2Y>Y$.
\end{itemize}
So the closest choice is $\boxed{\textbf{(E) }9^{9^{9^{9^{9}}}}}$: subtracting the (relatively tiny) number $Y$ hardly changes $X$, even though the two towers look almost the same.
