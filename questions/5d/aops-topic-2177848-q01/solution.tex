Each team played $10+10=20$ games, one against each other team, so there are $21$ teams and $\dbinom{21}{3}=1330$ sets of three teams.

\textbf{Two kinds of triples.} In a set of three teams, the three games among them produce $3$ wins in total, and each team wins $0$, $1$ or $2$ of its two games inside the triple. So the numbers of wins inside the triple are either $1,1,1$ (each team beats exactly one other: the cyclic triples we want) or $2,1,0$. In the second case exactly one team wins both of its games, that is, one team beats both of the others. (If two teams both beat the third team, they also played each other, and the winner of that game beat both others.)

\textbf{Counting the non-cyclic triples.} Each non-cyclic triple has exactly one team that beat both others, so we can count these triples by that team. A team beat $10$ teams, and any $2$ of them form a triple in which it beat both others: $\dbinom{10}{2}=45$ triples per team, and $21\cdot45=945$ in total.

Hence the number of cyclic triples is
\[1330-945=\boxed{385}.\]
