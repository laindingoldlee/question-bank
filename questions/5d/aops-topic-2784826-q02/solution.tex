Let $N=(k+1)(k+3)(3k^2+2k+16)$. It suffices to show $3\mid N$ and $8\mid N$.

\textbf{Modulo 3.} $3k^2+2k+16\equiv2k+1\pmod3$ and $k+3\equiv k$, so $N\equiv k(k+1)(2k+1)\pmod3$. If $k\equiv0$ or $k\equiv2$, one of $k$, $k+1$ is divisible by $3$; if $k\equiv1$, then $2k+1\equiv0$. So $3\mid N$.

\textbf{Modulo 8.} If $k$ is odd, $k+1$ and $k+3$ are consecutive even numbers, one of which is divisible by $4$, so $8\mid(k+1)(k+3)$. If $k=2m$ is even, then
\[3k^2+2k+16=12m^2+4m+16=4\bigl(m(3m+1)+4\bigr),\]
and $m(3m+1)$ is always even ($m$ and $3m+1$ have opposite parity), so $8\mid3k^2+2k+16$.

Hence $24\mid N$ for every $k\ge0$. $\blacksquare$
