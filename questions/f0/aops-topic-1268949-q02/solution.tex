Both lines are parallel to the $y$-axis. Let $P_1=(4,s,4)$, $P_2=(u,v,0)$ and $P_3=(9,t,9)$. Changing only $y$-coordinates, the distances
\[P_1P_2=\sqrt{(u-4)^2+(v-s)^2+16},\qquad P_2P_3=\sqrt{(u-9)^2+(v-t)^2+81}\]
are smallest when $s=v=t$; so we may assume all three points have the same $y$-coordinate and work in the $xz$-plane with points $(4,4)$ and $(9,9)$ and a point $(u,0)$ on the $x$-axis.

Reflect $(9,9)$ in the $x$-axis to $(9,-9)$. For any point $Q=(u,0)$, the distance from $Q$ to $(9,9)$ equals its distance to $(9,-9)$, so
\[P_1P_2+P_2P_3\ge \text{dist}\bigl((4,4),(9,-9)\bigr)=\sqrt{5^2+13^2}=\sqrt{194},\]
with equality when $Q$ is where the segment from $(4,4)$ to $(9,-9)$ meets the axis, at $u=4+\dfrac{4}{13}\cdot 5=\dfrac{72}{13}$. The minimum is $\boxed{\sqrt{194}}$.
