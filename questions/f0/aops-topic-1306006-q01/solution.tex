By the binomial theorem,
\[
11^n=(10+1)^n=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dbinom nk10^k .
\]
So $11^n$ is the number whose ``digits'' are the entries of row $n$ of Pascal's triangle, provided every entry is less than $10$, so that no carrying occurs. This holds for $n\le4$. Row $5$ is $1,5,10,10,5,1$: the entries $10$ carry, and $11^5=161051$ instead of ``$15101051$''. (Writing $11^n$ in a base larger than every entry of the row restores the pattern.)
