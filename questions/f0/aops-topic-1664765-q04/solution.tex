Call a pair of side-sharing squares with different colours a \emph{boundary pair}.

\textbf{Lower bound.} Let the red squares occupy $R$ rows and $C$ columns. In each such row, the leftmost red square has a white left neighbour and the rightmost red square has a white right neighbour: two boundary pairs in that row. In the same way each such column contains two vertical boundary pairs. These pairs are all different, so there are at least $2(R+C)$ boundary pairs. All red squares lie in the $R\times C$ intersection of these rows and columns, so $RC\ge2015$, and by AM-GM
\[
R+C\ge2\sqrt{RC}\ge2\sqrt{2015}>2\cdot44.8=89.6 .
\]
As $R+C$ is an integer, $R+C\ge90$, and there are at least $180$ boundary pairs.

\textbf{$180$ is possible.} Take $44$ full rows of $45$ squares ($1980$ squares) and put the remaining $35$ red squares at the left end of the next row. Each of the $45$ rows of this shape is one block of red squares, which gives exactly $2$ horizontal boundary pairs per row, and each of the $45$ columns is one block, which gives exactly $2$ vertical ones. That is $2(45+45)=180$ pairs.

The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(B)}\ 180}$.
