Three hours are sufficient: carry the first son 24 km in 1.2 hours, ride back alone 15 km in 0.6 hour to meet the second son at kilometre 9, then carry him the remaining 24 km in 1.2 hours. The first son walks his remaining 9 km in 1.8 hours and also arrives at hour 3.
For optimality, let $T$ be the elapsed time and $r$ the total time a son is riding (at most one rides at a time). The sons\textquotesingle s combined displacement is 66 km and cannot exceed $20r+5(2T-r)=10T+15r$, so $r\ge\dfrac{66-10T}{15}$. Their combined displacement while riding is at least $66-5(2T-r)=66-10T+5r$. This is also the father\textquotesingle s displacement while carrying a passenger. His displacement during the other $T-r$ hours is at least $-25(T-r)$. Since his net displacement is 33, we obtain $33\ge66-35T+30r\ge198-55T$, hence $T\ge3$. This allows backward travel and waiting as well, so no omitted route improves the bound. The minimum is $\boxed{3\text{ hours}}$.
