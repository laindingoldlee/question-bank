We construct $g$ odd about $0$ and $h=f-g$ odd about $1$. Set $g(0)=0$, $g(1)=f(1)$, and $g(x)=0$ for $0<x<1$. For negative arguments impose $g(-x)=-g(x)$. For $x>1$, define recursively
\[
g(x)=f(x)+f(2-x)-g(2-x).
\]
This is well-defined: $|2-x|<x$ for $x>1$, and repeated reduction, together with oddness, reaches $[-1,1]$ in finitely many steps (for $x>2$ the positive argument drops by $2$).

By definition, $g(x)+g(-x)=0$. Also $h(x)+h(2-x)=0$ for $x>1$ by the recursion, for $x<1$ by replacing $x$ with $2-x$, and for $x=1$ because $h(1)=0$. Hence the graph of $g$ is centrally symmetric about $(0,0)$ and that of $h$ about $(1,0)$, with $f=g+h$. No regularity assumptions on $f$ are required.
