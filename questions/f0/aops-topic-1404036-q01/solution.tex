Charlie wins on his $k$th turn when the first $3k-1$ rolls are not $6$ and the next one is $6$; this has probability $\left(\dfrac{5}{6}\right)^{3k-1}\dfrac{1}{6}$. So
\[
P(\text{Charlie})=\dfrac{1}{6}\left(\dfrac{5}{6}\right)^{2}\left(1+r+r^2+\cdots\right),\qquad r=\left(\dfrac{5}{6}\right)^3=\dfrac{125}{216}.
\]
An infinite geometric series with $|r|<1$ has a finite sum: the partial sums are $S_n=1+r+\dots+r^{n-1}=\dfrac{1-r^n}{1-r}$, and since $r^n\to0$ they approach $\dfrac{1}{1-r}$. Hence
\[
P(\text{Charlie})=\dfrac{25}{216}\cdot\dfrac{1}{1-\dfrac{125}{216}}=\dfrac{25}{216}\cdot\dfrac{216}{91}=\boxed{\dfrac{25}{91}} .
\]
Infinitely many terms can add to a finite number because they shrink fast enough. Likewise, $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}+\dots+\dfrac{1}{2^n}=1-\dfrac{1}{2^n}$, which gets as close to $1$ as we like, so the infinite sum is exactly $1$ (and $0.\overline{9}=1$ for the same reason). As a check, Alex and Bob win with probabilities $\dfrac{36}{91}$ and $\dfrac{30}{91}$, and $\dfrac{36+30+25}{91}=1$.
