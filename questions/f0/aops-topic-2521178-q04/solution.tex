Since the triangles are equilateral and $ABCD$ is a square,
\[CE=CB=CD=CF,\]
so triangle $CEF$ is isosceles with $CE=CF$.

The angles around $C$ are $\angle BCE=60^\circ$, $\angle BCD=90^\circ$ and $\angle DCF=60^\circ$, and $E$ and $F$ are outside the square, so going around $C$ from $CE$ through $CB$ and $CD$ to $CF$ covers $60^\circ+90^\circ+60^\circ=210^\circ$. The other way round,
\[\angle ECF=360^\circ-210^\circ=150^\circ.\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0), B=(2,0), C=(2,2), D=(0,2);
pair Ep=(2+sqrt(3),1), F=(1,2+sqrt(3));
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(0.8));
draw(B--Ep--C,linewidth(0.8)); draw(D--F--C,linewidth(0.8));
draw(Ep--F,blue+linewidth(0.9));
draw(arc(C,0.3,-30,120),red);
draw(C+0.3*dir(45)--(3.05,3.05),red+linewidth(0.4));
label("$150^\circ$",(3.05,3.05),NE,red+fontsize(8pt));
draw(arc(Ep,0.75,degrees(F-Ep),degrees(C-Ep)),red);
draw(Ep+0.75*dir(142.5)--(3.6,2.3),red+linewidth(0.4));
label("$15^\circ$",(3.6,2.3),NE,red+fontsize(8pt));
label("$A$",A,SW); label("$B$",B,SE); label("$C$",C,SW); label("$D$",D,W); label("$E$",Ep,E); label("$F$",F,N);
\end{asy}

In the isosceles triangle $CEF$ the base angles are equal, so
\[\angle CEF=\dfrac{180^\circ-150^\circ}{2}=\boxed{15^\circ}.\]
