The number cannot be determined without the side length of the equilateral triangle. If the side is $s$, the nearest corner is at distance $\dfrac{s}{\sqrt3}$ from its centre. For $s>16\sqrt3$, no particle can reach any corner in eight seconds, since its total path length is at most 16. For $s\le16\sqrt3$, the three direct corner directions already qualify. Reflection paths can be counted by unfolding the triangular tiling, but that count also depends on $s$. Thus the omitted size is essential.
