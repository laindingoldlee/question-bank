\textbf{The error.} Every step is correct except the last. From $a+b=13$ and $a-b=-\dfrac{119}{13}$,
\[
a=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(13-\dfrac{119}{13}\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{169-119}{13}=\dfrac{25}{13},\qquad b=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(13+\dfrac{119}{13}\right)=\dfrac{144}{13}.
\]
The student's pair does not even satisfy $a+b=13$, since $-\dfrac{40}{13}+\dfrac{79}{13}=3$. (A complete solution must also check the conditions $a^2\le25$ and $b^2\le144$, which are needed for the square roots to exist; squaring alone does not guarantee them.)

\textbf{Check.} $25-\left(\dfrac{25}{13}\right)^2=\dfrac{4225-625}{169}=\dfrac{3600}{169}$ and $144-\left(\dfrac{144}{13}\right)^2=\dfrac{24336-20736}{169}=\dfrac{3600}{169}$, so both square roots equal $\dfrac{60}{13}$. Since squaring both sides is reversible here (both sides are nonnegative), this is the only solution:
\[
\boxed{(a,b)=\left(\dfrac{25}{13},\dfrac{144}{13}\right)}.
\]
(Geometrically: in a right triangle with legs $5$ and $12$ and hypotenuse $13$, the altitude $\dfrac{60}{13}$ cuts the hypotenuse into pieces $\dfrac{25}{13}$ and $\dfrac{144}{13}$.)
