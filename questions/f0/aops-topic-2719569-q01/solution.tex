Let the ship's length be $L$ steps and let the ship move $r$ steps' worth of distance for each step Emily takes. Walking with the ship, Emily's $210$ steps cover the ship's length plus the ship's motion: $210=L+210r$. Walking against it: $42=L-42r$. Subtracting, $168=252r$, so $r=\dfrac23$, and $L=210-140=\boxed{\textbf{(A) }70}$.

Only the ratio $r$ of the two speeds enters the equations, and it is determined by the data. So choosing any particular speeds with that ratio, or leaving them as unknowns, gives the same answer.
