List the possible series (in the order of the games) in which $B$ wins game $2$ and $A$ wins the series:
\[
ABAA,\qquad ABABA,\qquad ABBAA,\qquad BBAAA .
\]
(If $A$ wins game $1$, then $A$ needs two more wins before $B$ gets two more. If $B$ wins game $1$, then $A$ must win the next three games.)

These series are not equally likely. A series of $4$ games has probability $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^4=\dfrac{1}{16}$, and a series of $5$ games has probability $\dfrac{1}{32}$. One way to see this is to imagine that all five games are always played. Then all $32$ results are equally likely, and the series $ABAA$ corresponds to the two results $ABAAA$ and $ABAAB$, which is why it is counted twice. So
\[
P(B\text{ wins game }2\text{ and }A\text{ wins the series})=\dfrac{1}{16}+\dfrac{3}{32}=\dfrac{5}{32},
\]
and the only series in which $B$ also wins game $1$ is $BBAAA$, of probability $\dfrac{1}{32}$. Therefore the conditional probability is
\[
\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{32}}{\dfrac{5}{32}}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{5}}.
\]
