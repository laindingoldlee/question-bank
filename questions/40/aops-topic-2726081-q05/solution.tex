The first swim stays within distance $3<5$ of $O$. Let $Q$ be its endpoint ($OQ=3$) and let $\varphi$ be the angle between the two swimming directions. Along the second swim, the squared distance from $O$ is $9+6t\cos\varphi+t^2$ for $0\le t\le4$, which is convex in $t$, so its maximum is at an endpoint: $\max\left(9,\ 25+24\cos\varphi\right)$.

\end{asy}

So the fish is safe exactly when $25+24\cos\varphi<25$, i.e. $\cos\varphi<0$. For two independent uniform directions in space, the angle between them is obtuse with probability $\dfrac12$, by the symmetry $v\mapsto-v$ of the second direction (the case $\cos\varphi=0$ has probability $0$). The answer is $\boxed{\dfrac12}$.
