Look at the four horizontal layers from above (shaded cubes touch the box):

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(7cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
void grid(pair o,bool all){
 for(int i=0;i<4;++i) for(int j=0;j<4;++j){
  bool inner=(i==1||i==2)&&(j==1||j==2);
  path sq=shift(o+(i,j))*unitsquare;
  if(all||!inner) fill(sq,paleblue);
  draw(sq,gray(0.3));
 }}
grid((0,0),true);grid((5.5,0),false);
label("bottom layer",(2,-0.5));label("each of the 3 upper layers",(7.5,-0.5));
label("$16$",(2,4.45));label("$12$",(7.5,4.45));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\begin{itemize}
\item All $16$ cubes of the bottom layer touch the bottom.
\item In each of the other $3$ layers, the $12$ cubes on the border touch a side; the central $2\times2$ block does not (and the top is open).
\end{itemize}
So $16+3\cdot12=52$ cubes touch a side or the bottom. Equivalently, the cubes that touch nothing form a $2\times2\times3$ block of $12$, and $64-12=52$. The answer is $\boxed{\text{(B)}\ 52}$.
