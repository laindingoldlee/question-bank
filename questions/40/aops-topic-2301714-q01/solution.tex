The equation says $\dfrac{1}{y^2+5}=\dfrac{3}{y^4-39}$ with both denominators nonzero, so $y^4-39=3\left(y^2+5\right)$. Substitute $x=y^2\ge0$:
\[x^2-39=3x+15\iff x^2-3x-54=0\iff(x-9)(x+6)=0.\]
Since $x=y^2\ge0$, $x=9$, and since $y\ge0$, $y=\boxed{3}$. (Check: $y^2+5=14$ and $y^4-39=42=3\cdot14$, so $\dfrac1{14}-\dfrac3{42}=0$.)
