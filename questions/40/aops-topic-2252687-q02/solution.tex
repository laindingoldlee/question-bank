Given that the four numbers are different, the set of numbers shown is equally likely to be any of the $\dbinom64=15$ four-element subsets of $\{1,\dots,6\}$ (each subset arises from $4!$ equally likely ordered outcomes). The four numbers have sum $21-(\text{sum of the two missing numbers})$, so the sum exceeds $15$ exactly when the two missing numbers have sum less than $6$: $\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{1,4\},\{2,3\}$. That is $4$ subsets, and the probability is $\boxed{\dfrac{4}{15}}$.
