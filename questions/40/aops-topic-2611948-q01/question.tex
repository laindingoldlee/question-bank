Let $f$ be a function and let $g(x)=f(2x-4)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Show that the point $(p,q)$ lies on the graph of $y=f(x)$ exactly when the point $\left(\dfrac{p+4}{2},\,q\right)$ lies on the graph of $y=g(x)$.
\item[(b)] Deduce that the graph of $g$ is obtained from the graph of $f$ by shifting $4$ units to the right and then compressing horizontally towards the $y$-axis by a factor of $2$, and also by compressing first and then shifting $2$ units to the right.
\item[(c)] Which function has the graph obtained by shifting the graph of $f$ $2$ units to the right first and then compressing by a factor of $2$?
\item[(d)] Give an order of the four transformations (horizontal shift, horizontal compression, vertical stretch, vertical shift) that turns the graph of $y=f(x)$ into the graph of $y=3f(2x-4)+1$.
\end{enumerate}
