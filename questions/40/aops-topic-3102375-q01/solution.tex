For $n=1$ or prime $n$, no suitable $k$ exists; for $n=4$, the only noncoprime choices are 2 and 4, both divisors. Now let $n$ be composite and $n\ne4$. Its least prime factor $p$ satisfies $p<\dfrac{n}{2}$: equality would force $n=2p$ and $p=2$, hence $n=4$. Set $k=n-p$. Then $\dfrac{n}{2}<k<n$ and $\gcd(k,n)=p>1$. A proper positive divisor of $n$ is at most $\dfrac{n}{2}$, so $k$ is not a divisor. Thus the answer is $\boxed{\text{all composite }n\ne4}$.
