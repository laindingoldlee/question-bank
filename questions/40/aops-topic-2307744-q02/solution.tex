The triangle inequality requires each side to be shorter than the sum of the other two:
\begin{itemize}
\item $x+(x+2)>2x$, i.e. $2>0$: always true;
\item $x+2x>x+2$, i.e. $x>1$;
\item $(x+2)+2x>x$: always true for $x>0$.
\end{itemize}
So $x\ge2$. The triangle is scalene, so its three sides are different; $x\ne x+2$ and $x\ne2x$ always, while $x+2=2x$ exactly when $x=2$. Hence $x\in\{3,4,\dots,19\}$, which gives $\boxed{17}$ triangles. For example, $x=3$ gives the sides $3$, $5$, $6$:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair B=(0,0), C=(6,0), A=(5/3,sqrt(56)/3);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(1));
label("$A$",A,N); label("$B$",B,SW); label("$C$",C,SE);
label("$x=3$",(A+B)/2,NW,blue); label("$x+2=5$",(A+C)/2,NE,blue); label("$2x=6$",(B+C)/2,S,blue);
\end{asy}
