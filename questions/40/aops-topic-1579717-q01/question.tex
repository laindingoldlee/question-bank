The digital root of a positive integer is found by adding its digits, then adding the digits of the result, and so on, until a single digit remains. (For example, $109\to10\to1$.)

Suppose a positive integer has a digit $9$ that is not its leading digit. Prove or disprove: deleting that $9$ (that is, leaving it out of the digit sum) does not change the digital root. For example, for $109$ the digits $1$ and $0$ alone give digital root $1$, the same as for $109$.
