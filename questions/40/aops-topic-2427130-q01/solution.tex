\textbf{(a)} Write $S$ as a row of $S$ ones. Choosing $n-1$ of the $S-1$ gaps between them to cut gives exactly one ordered sum of $n$ positive parts, and every ordered sum arises once. So there are $\boxed{\dbinom{S-1}{n-1}}$ ordered sums (stars and bars).

\textbf{(b)} Count by the smallest part $a$. Given $a$, the middle part $b$ runs from $a$ to $\left\lfloor\dfrac{S-a}{2}\right\rfloor$ (then $c=S-a-b\ge b$).
\begin{itemize}
\item $S=17$: $a=1,2,3,4,5$ give $8,6,5,3,2$ triples, in total $\boxed{24}$.
\item $S=18$: $a=1,2,3,4,5,6$ give $8,7,5,4,2,1$ triples, in total $\boxed{27}$.
\end{itemize}
(For comparison, there are $\dbinom{16}{2}=120$ and $\dbinom{17}{2}=136$ ordered sums. In general the number of unordered sums of $S$ into three positive parts is the integer nearest to $\dfrac{S^2}{12}$. If the three numbers must also be different, there are $16$ ways for $17$ and $19$ ways for $18$.)
