Every element is $3$ times an element of $\{1,2,\dots,10\}$, so it is enough to count the distinct sums $x+y+z$ of three different numbers from $1$ to $10$, and multiply them by $3$.

The smallest such sum is $1+2+3=6$ and the largest is $8+9+10=27$. Every integer in between occurs: starting from $\{1,2,3\}$, we can repeatedly increase one number by $1$ while keeping the three numbers different (raise the largest until it is $10$, then the middle one until it is $9$, then the smallest until it is $8$), and each step raises the sum by exactly $1$, passing through every value from $6$ to $27$.

So there are $27-6+1=\boxed{22}$ distinct sums (namely $18,21,\dots,81$).
