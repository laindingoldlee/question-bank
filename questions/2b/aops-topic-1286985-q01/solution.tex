Let the entries be $\dbinom nk,\dbinom n{k+1},\dbinom n{k+2}$. Using $\dfrac{\dbinom n{j+1}}{\dbinom nj}=\dfrac{n-j}{j+1}$, written as equations:
\[
\dfrac{n-k}{k+1}=\dfrac{4}{3}\iff3n=7k+4,\qquad\dfrac{n-k-1}{k+2}=\dfrac{5}{4}\iff4n=9k+14 .
\]
Eliminating $n$: $4(7k+4)=3(9k+14)$, so $k=26$ and $n=62$. Since the two ratios determine $n$ and $k$ uniquely, the only (hence smallest) row is $n=\boxed{62}$, with $\dbinom{62}{26}:\dbinom{62}{27}:\dbinom{62}{28}=3:4:5$.
