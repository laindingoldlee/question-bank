A purely repeating base-nine expansion with block $d_1d_2\dots d_m$ equals $\dfrac{(d_1d_2\dots d_m)_9}{9^m-1}$, so we need a power of $9$ that is $1$ more than a multiple of $7$. Since $9\equiv2\pmod 7$, we get $9^3\equiv8\equiv1\pmod7$, and indeed $9^3-1=728=7\cdot104$. Hence
\[
\dfrac{1}{7}=\dfrac{104}{728}=\dfrac{104}{9^3-1}.
\]
In base nine, $104=1\cdot81+2\cdot9+5=125_9$, so
\[
\dfrac{1}{7}=\boxed{0.\overline{125}_9}.
\]
Check by long division in base nine: $9\cdot1=9=7+2$, $9\cdot2=18=2\cdot7+4$, $9\cdot4=36=5\cdot7+1$, giving digits $1,2,5$ and remainder $1$ again.
