The claim is false: $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is a rational number between $0$ and $1$.

All the individual steps are correct ($a^2<a$, $a^2>0$, $a^2$ is rational). The error is the sentence ``let $a$ be the smallest of them''. A set of numbers need not have a smallest element, and this one does not. The argument assumes that \emph{if} there are rational numbers in $(0,1)$, \emph{then} there is a smallest one; that assumption is never justified.

What the argument really proves is: \textbf{there is no smallest rational number between $0$ and $1$}. Indeed, for any rational $a$ in $(0,1)$, the rational number $a^2$ (or simply $\dfrac {a}{2}$) is smaller and still in $(0,1)$. The contradiction comes from the unjustified assumption, not from the existence of rational numbers in $(0,1)$.
