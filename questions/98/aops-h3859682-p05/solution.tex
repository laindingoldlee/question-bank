Scale lengths by two and use $A=(0,0),B=(2,0),C=(1,\sqrt3),D=(1,-\sqrt3)$. Since $AP=AC=2$ and $\angle APC=45^\circ$, the angle at $A$ is right, giving $P=\varepsilon(-\sqrt3,1)$ for $\varepsilon=\pm1$. Substitution of line $PC$ into the circle centered at $B$ gives its other intersection $Q=(2,2\varepsilon)$. Thus $QB$ is vertical, while $PA$ always has slope $-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$. Their intersection is $E=(2,-\dfrac{2}{\sqrt3})$. Hence in the scaled figure $DE^2=1+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{4}{3}$. Restoring the scale gives $DE^2=\dfrac{16}{3}$, so $\boxed{19}$. Both choices of $P$ give the same answer.
