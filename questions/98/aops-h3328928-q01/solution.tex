Let two roots be $r,s$, whose absolute values are primes, and the third be $t$. The missing linear coefficient gives $rs+t(r+s)=0$. We cannot have $r+s=0$, because $rs\ne0$. Nor can $|r|=|s|$: distinctness would force $r=-s$. Thus $r,s$ are coprime.

Since $t=-\dfrac{rs}{(r+s)}$ is an integer and $\gcd(r+s,rs)=1$, we must have $r+s=\pm1$. Their signs are opposite, and their distinct prime magnitudes differ by $1$. The only such primes are $2,3$. This yields
\[
\boxed{\{2,-3,-6\}\quad\text{or}\quad\{-2,3,6\}}.
\]
Both triples have zero pairwise-product sum and nonzero product, so they satisfy the required form.
