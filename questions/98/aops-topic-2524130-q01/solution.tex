Let $P_n=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{2k-1}{2k}$. A direct induction on the given inequality does not work, because from $P_n<\dfrac1{\sqrt{3n}}$ one only gets $P_{n+1}<\dfrac{2n+1}{(2n+2)\sqrt{3n}}$, which is larger than $\dfrac1{\sqrt{3n+3}}$. So we prove the stronger statement
\[P_n\le\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3n+1}}\qquad(n\ge1)\]
by induction.

\textbf{Base case.} $P_1=\dfrac12=\dfrac1{\sqrt4}$.

\textbf{Inductive step.} Suppose $P_n\le\dfrac1{\sqrt{3n+1}}$. Then
\[P_{n+1}=P_n\cdot\dfrac{2n+1}{2n+2}\le\dfrac{2n+1}{(2n+2)\sqrt{3n+1}},\]
and it suffices to show $\dfrac{2n+1}{(2n+2)\sqrt{3n+1}}\le\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3n+4}}$, i.e. (squaring positive quantities)
\[(2n+1)^2(3n+4)\le(2n+2)^2(3n+1).\]
Expanding, the left side is $12n^3+28n^2+19n+4$ and the right side is $12n^3+28n^2+20n+4$; their difference is $n\ge0$. So $P_{n+1}\le\dfrac1{\sqrt{3(n+1)+1}}$.

Hence for all $n\ge1$,
\[P_n\le\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3n+1}}<\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3n}}.\ \blacksquare\]
