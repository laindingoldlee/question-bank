Here $a,b\ne0$. Let $g=\gcd(|a|,|b|)$ and write $|a|=gx$, $|b|=gy$ with $\gcd(x,y)=1$. Clearing denominators,
\[
a^2b^2=p\,(a^2+b^2)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad g^2x^2y^2=p\,(x^2+y^2).
\]
Since $\gcd(x,y)=1$, the number $x^2+y^2$ shares no prime factor with $x$ (a prime dividing $x$ and $x^2+y^2$ would divide $y$) or with $y$. So $\gcd(x^2y^2,x^2+y^2)=1$, and $x^2y^2$ divides $p$. A square dividing a prime must be $1$, so $x=y=1$. Then $g^2=2p$, so $2\mid g$, $4\mid2p$, and $p=2$, $g=2$.

Hence $|a|=|b|=2$ and $p=2$: the solutions are $\boxed{p=2,\ a=\pm2,\ b=\pm2}$ (four sign choices). Check: $\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{1}{2}$.
