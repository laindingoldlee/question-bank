\textbf{i. False.} If the two points coincide, infinitely many lines pass through them.

\textbf{ii. True.} The centroid $G$ lies on each median, two thirds of the way from the vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side. A median joins a vertex to a point of the opposite side, so all its points except its endpoints are interior to the triangle; hence $G$ is inside.

\textbf{iii. False.} A plane perpendicular to the axis cuts the cone in a circle (or in the single point at the apex), never a triangle. (A triangle appears when the plane contains the axis.)

\textbf{iv. False.} Three distinct collinear points lie on infinitely many planes.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(9cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(5,0),C=(1.5,3.5);pair G=(A+B+C)/3;
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(A--(B+C)/2,dashed);draw(B--(A+C)/2,dashed);draw(C--(A+B)/2,dashed);
dot(G);label("$G$",G,1.6*dir(65));
label("$A$",A,SW);label("$B$",B,SE);label("$C$",C,N);
pair V=(9,3.6);real R=1.6;
draw(V--(9-R,0));draw(V--(9+R,0));draw(ellipse((9,0),R,0.35));
real hh=0.45;draw(ellipse((9,3.6*(1-hh)),R*hh,0.35*hh),blue);
label("circle",(9+R*hh+0.1,3.6*(1-hh)),E,blue);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Exactly $\boxed{1}$ statement (ii) is always true.
