\textbf{(a)} Let $x=\sqrt2^{\sqrt2}$. There are two cases.
\begin{itemize}
\item If $x$ is rational, take $a=b=\sqrt2$: both are irrational and $a^b=x$ is rational.
\item If $x$ is irrational, take $a=x$ and $b=\sqrt2$: then
\[
a^b=\left(\sqrt2^{\sqrt2}\right)^{\sqrt2}=\sqrt2^{\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt2}=\sqrt2^{\,2}=2,
\]
which is rational.
\end{itemize}
In either case suitable irrational $a,b$ exist. $\blacksquare$ (The proof does not tell us which case occurs; by the Gelfond--Schneider theorem it is the second.)

\textbf{(b)} Suppose $\log_2 9=\dfrac {p}{q}$ with positive integers $p,q$. Then $2^{\dfrac {p}{q}}=9$, so $2^p=9^q$; the left side is even and the right side is odd, a contradiction. So $\log_2 9$ is irrational, and $\sqrt2$ is irrational. But
\[
\sqrt2^{\log_29}=\left(2^{\dfrac{1}{2}}\right)^{\log_29}=\left(2^{\log_29}\right)^{\dfrac{1}{2}}=9^{\dfrac{1}{2}}=3,
\]
which is rational. $\blacksquare$
