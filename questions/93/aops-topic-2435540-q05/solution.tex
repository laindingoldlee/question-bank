Let $p$ be the probability that Al wins. Consider the first two flips:
\begin{itemize}
\item Al fails with probability $\dfrac15$, and then he loses;
\item Al lands and Bob fails with probability $\dfrac45\cdot\dfrac14=\dfrac15$, and then Al wins;
\item both land with probability $\dfrac45\cdot\dfrac34=\dfrac35$, and then the game starts afresh.
\end{itemize}
So $p=\dfrac15+\dfrac35p$, giving $\dfrac25p=\dfrac15$ and $p=\boxed{\dfrac12}$. (The game ends with probability $1$, since the chance that it lasts more than $2k$ flips is $\left(\dfrac35\right)^k$.)
