Track squared values instead: the operation becomes absolute difference on $ 1,4,9,16,25 $. Each final square is the absolute value of a signed sum of these five numbers. Their sum is 55, so it is odd. It cannot equal 1, since no subset sums to 27 or 28: a subset containing 25 needs an additional 2 or 3, impossible; without 25, the remaining total 30 would require omitting 3 or 2, also impossible. The value 3 is attainable via $ 25-16=9 $, then $ 9-9=0 $, $ 4-1=3 $, and $ 3-0=3 $. For the maximum, absolute difference never exceeds its larger operand, so it is at most 25. Equality 25 would require the other four numbers to reduce to 0 before combining with 25, but no subset of $ 1,4,9,16 $ sums to 15. Hence the maximum odd value is at most 23. It is attained by reducing $ 16,9,4,1 $ successively to $ 7,3,2 $, then taking $ 25-2=23 $. Returning to square roots, $\boxed{(M,m)=(\sqrt{23},\sqrt3)}$.
