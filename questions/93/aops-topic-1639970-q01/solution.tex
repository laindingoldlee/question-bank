Let $P(x)=ax^m+bx^n+cx^k+dx^l+ex^f$. Every exponent is even, so $(-x)^j=x^j$ for each of them and
\[
P(-x)=P(x)\qquad\text{for all }x .
\]
Hence $r$ is a solution exactly when $-r$ is. After collecting equal powers, $P$ is a nonzero polynomial or the zero polynomial; in the second case every real number is a solution, so we assume the coefficients do not cancel completely (for example, the exponents are distinct). Then $P$ has finitely many real roots.

Group the real solutions: $0$ (if it is a solution) contributes $0$, and every nonzero solution $r$ pairs with the different solution $-r$, contributing $r+(-r)=0$. So the sum of all real solutions is $0$. $\blacksquare$

No casework on the coefficients is needed. (The same holds for the complex roots counted with multiplicity: $P$ is a polynomial in $x^2$, so the coefficient of the second-highest power of $x$ is $0$, and by Vieta's formulas the roots add up to $0$.)
