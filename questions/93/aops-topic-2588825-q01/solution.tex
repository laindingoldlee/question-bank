Let $t=\sqrt{x^2+7}$, so $t>0$ and $t^2=x^2+7$. The right side is $t^2+4x$, so the equation becomes
\[(x+4)t=t^2+4x\iff t^2-(x+4)t+4x=0\iff(t-4)(t-x)=0.\]
If $t=x$, then $x^2+7=x^2$, which is impossible. So $t=4$, i.e. $x^2+7=16$ and $x=\pm3$.

Check: for $x=3$, $7\cdot4=28=9+12+7$; for $x=-3$, $1\cdot4=4=9-12+7$. The solutions are $\boxed{x=3\text{ and }x=-3}$.
