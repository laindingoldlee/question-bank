An infinite series $\sum_{i\ge0}t_i$ has a value only if its partial sums $t_0+t_1+\cdots+t_n$ approach a finite limit.

For $\sum_{i\ge0}i$ the partial sums are $0+1+\cdots+n=\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}$, which grow without bound; similarly the partial sums of $\sum_{i\ge0}(-i)$ are $-\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}$, which decrease without bound. Neither series converges, so the expression is a sum of two undefined quantities (``$\infty+(-\infty)$'') and $\boxed{\text{has no value}}$.

The student's cancellation replaces the expression by the different series $\sum_{i\ge0}\bigl(i+(-i)\bigr)=\sum0=0$. Adding two series term by term is only justified when both converge. That pairing is one arbitrary way of interleaving the terms; another interleaving, $0+1-1+2-2+3-3+\cdots$, has partial sums $0,1,0,2,0,3,\dots$ and does not converge at all, and taking two positive terms for each negative one makes the partial sums tend to $+\infty$. So no single value can be assigned.
