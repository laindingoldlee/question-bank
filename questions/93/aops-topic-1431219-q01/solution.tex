We need the factorisation $2009=7^2\cdot41$ (try small primes: $2009=7\cdot287=7\cdot7\cdot41$, and $41$ is prime). Since $b$ is prime, $b\ge2$, so $a^b$ is a prime power with exponent at least $2$ dividing $2009$; the only one is $7^2$. Hence $a=7$, $b=2$, $c=41$, and
\[
\sqrt{\dfrac{7+2+41}{2}}=\sqrt{25}=\boxed{5}.
\]
There is no real shortcut: the uniqueness of the prime factorisation is what makes $a,b,c$ unique, so one has to find it.
