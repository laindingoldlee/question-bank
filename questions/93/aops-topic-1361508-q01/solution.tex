The map $x\mapsto7-x$ on each die is a bijection between outcomes with sum $s$ and outcomes with sum $21-s$. So the sums $3,\dots,10$ and the sums $11,\dots,18$ are equally likely, each group having probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

The sum is less than $12$ when it is at most $10$ or exactly $11$. The number of outcomes with sum $11$ is the number of solutions of $a+b+c=11$ with $1\le a,b,c\le6$: by stars and bars there are $\dbinom{10}{2}=45$ solutions in positive integers, and those with some value at least $7$ number $3\dbinom{4}{2}=18$ (at most one value can exceed $6$), leaving $27$. So
\[
P=\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{27}{216}=\dfrac{108+27}{216}=\dfrac{135}{216}=\boxed{\dfrac{5}{8}} .
\]
