Let the common differences be $d$ and $e$, integers with $1\le d\le e$ (since $1<a_2\le b_2$). Put $k=n-1\ge1$ (for $n=1$ the product is $1$). Then
\[a_n=1+kd,\qquad b_n=1+ke,\qquad(1+kd)(1+ke)=2010.\]
So $2010=uv$ with $u=1+kd$, $v=1+ke$, $2\le u\le v$, and both $u-1$ and $v-1$ divisible by $k$.

Since $2010=2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot67$, the factor pairs $(u,v)$ with $2\le u\le v$ are
\[(2,1005),\ (3,670),\ (5,402),\ (6,335),\ (10,201),\ (15,134),\ (30,67),\]
and $\gcd(u-1,v-1)$ is $1,1,1,1,1,7,1$ respectively. Only $(15,134)$ allows $k>1$, with $k$ dividing $7$. The largest choice is $k=7$: $d=\dfrac{15-1}{7}=2$, $e=\dfrac{134-1}{7}=19$, giving $a_8=15$, $b_8=134$ and $15\cdot134=2010$.

The largest possible value is $n=\boxed{8}$.
