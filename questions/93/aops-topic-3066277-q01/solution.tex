Let $v=\dfrac{20}{21}$ be the one-year discount factor. Untreated ties incur an initial 11 and thereafter a net 10 every 10 years, so $C_u=11+\dfrac{10v^{10}}{1-v^{10}}=\dfrac{11-v^{10}}{1-v^{10}}$. Treated ties incur 14 every 15 years starting immediately, giving $C_t=\dfrac{14}{1-v^{15}}$. Exact rational comparison gives $C_u<C_t$; numerically they are approximately $26.90$ and $26.98$, respectively. Thus $\boxed{\text{the untreated option}}$ has the lower cost in this model. The result includes both the different replacement schedules and the scrap receipt.
\begin{verbatim}
from fractions import Fraction
v=Fraction(20,21)
u=(11-v**10)/(1-v**10)
t=14/(1-v**15)
assert u<t
print(float(u),float(t))
\end{verbatim}
