(a) No: $\dfrac{123}{246}=\dfrac{246}{492}=\dfrac{1}{2}$, and both pairs consist of three-digit integers. Independent information about an unrelated irrational number does not remove this ambiguity. (b) Two distinct reduced fractions with denominators at most $999$ differ by at least $\dfrac{1}{999^2}>10^{-9}$, since their cross-product difference is a nonzero integer. A fixed nine-place truncated decimal specifies an interval of width $10^{-9}$, so it contains at most one such reduced fraction. To recover it, enumerate $100\le a<b\le999$, retain pairs with $\gcd(a,b)=1$, and test $d\le\dfrac{a}{b}<d+10^{-9}$, where $d$ is the displayed decimal. The original pair guarantees existence, and the separation bound proves uniqueness.
