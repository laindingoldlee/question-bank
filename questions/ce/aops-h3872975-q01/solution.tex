Let the similar small triangle be $AIB$, where $I$ is the incenter and the opposite original angle is $C$. Its angles are $\dfrac{A}{2},\dfrac{B}{2},90^\circ+\dfrac{C}{2}$. The last is largest and cannot equal $C$, since that would require $C=180^\circ$. Interchange $A,B$ if needed so it equals $A$. The remaining angles must match $B,C$. The assignment $B=\dfrac{B}{2}$ is impossible, so $B=\dfrac{A}{2}$ and $C=\dfrac{B}{2}$. Thus $A:B:C=4:2:1$, and the smallest angle is $\boxed{\dfrac{180}{7}^\circ}$. Conversely these angles give exactly the required similarity, so the condition is attainable.

texpreamble("\usepackage{amsmath,amssymb}");

defaultpen(fontsize(9pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(1,0),C=(-.4009689,1.7567594); draw(A--B--C--cycle); label("$A$",A,(-1,-1)); label("$B$",B,(1,-1)); label("$C$",C,(0,1)); pair I=(1.8019377*B+C)/(2.2469796+1.8019377+1);draw(A--I--B);draw(C--I);label("$I$",I,(1,0));
\end{asy}
