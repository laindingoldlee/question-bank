A plane with intercepts $2$, $3$, $5$ on the axes has equation
\[
\dfrac {x}{2}+\dfrac {y}{3}+\dfrac {z}{5}=1
\]
(the intercept form: this is a linear equation, and it is satisfied by $(2,0,0)$, $(0,3,0)$ and $(0,0,5)$, which determine the plane). At $(4,-6,0)$ the left side is
\[
\dfrac{4}{2}+\dfrac{-6}{3}+\dfrac{0}{5}=2-2+0=0\ne1 .
\]
So \textbf{no}, the point does not lie on the plane. In fact it lies in the $xy$-plane on the line $\dfrac {x}{2}+\dfrac {y}{3}=0$ through the origin (dashed red), which is parallel to the plane's trace $\dfrac {x}{2}+\dfrac {y}{3}=1$ and does not meet it.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(7cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair ox=(-0.55,-0.45),oy=(1,0),oz=(0,1);
pair P(real x,real y,real z){return x*ox+y*oy+z*oz;}
draw(P(0,0,0)--P(5.5,0,0),gray,Arrow(TeXHead));draw(P(0,-7,0)--P(0,4.2,0),gray,Arrow(TeXHead));draw(P(0,0,0)--P(0,0,6),gray,Arrow(TeXHead));
label("$x$",P(5.5,0,0),SW,gray);label("$-6$",P(0,-6,0),N);label("$4$",P(4,0,0),SE);label("$y$",P(0,4.2,0),E,gray);label("$z$",P(0,0,6),N,gray);
pair X=P(2,0,0),Y=P(0,3,0),Z=P(0,0,5);
fill(X--Y--Z--cycle,paleblue);draw(X--Y--Z--cycle,blue+linewidth(0.9));
dot(X);dot(Y);dot(Z);
label("$2$",X,(-1.2,0.3));label("$3$",Y,S);label("$5$",Z,W);
pair Q=P(4,-6,0);
draw(P(0,0,0)--Q,red+dashed);draw(P(4,0,0)--Q,dotted);draw(P(0,-6,0)--Q,dotted);
dot(Q,red);label("$(4,-6,0)$",Q,S,red);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}
