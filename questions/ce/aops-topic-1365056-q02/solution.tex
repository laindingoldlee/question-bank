The points of $y=\pm\sqrt x$ are exactly the points $(y^2,y)$. Substituting $x=y^2$ gives $|a|y^3=|b|$.

\textbf{Case $a\ne0$.} Take $y=\sqrt[3]{\dfrac{|b|}{|a|}}$ (a real cube root always exists) and $x=y^2$. Then $|a|xy=|a|y^3=|b|$, so $(x,y)$ lies on both graphs. (If $b=0$ this is the origin.)

\textbf{Case $a=0$, $b=0$.} The first graph is the whole plane, so it meets the parabola.

\textbf{Case $a=0$, $b\ne0$.} The first equation $0=|b|$ has no solutions.

So again the statement is \textbf{false} (counterexample $a=0$, $b=1$), and the graphs intersect exactly when $\boxed{a\ne0\text{ or }b=0}$.
