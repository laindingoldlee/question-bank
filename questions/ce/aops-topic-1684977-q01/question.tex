The number of ways of selecting $6$ objects from $n$ distinct objects is the same as the number of ways of selecting $3$ objects from $n$ distinct objects. An answer key says $n=9$ is the only solution, but a student claims that $n=0,1,2$ also work. Find all nonnegative integers $n$ with $\dbinom n6=\dbinom n3$, and decide who is right.
