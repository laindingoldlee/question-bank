The ball first falls $15$ feet. After that, each bounce consists of a rise and an equal fall, of heights
\[
15\cdot\dfrac{2}{3}=10,\quad 10\cdot\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{20}{3},\quad\dots,
\]
a geometric series with first term $10$ and ratio $\dfrac{2}{3}$, whose sum is $\dfrac{10}{1-\dfrac{2}{3}}=30$. Each of these heights is travelled twice (up and down), so the total distance is
\[
15+2\cdot30=\boxed{75\text{ feet}}.
\]
So if the ball bounces forever, the teacher is wrong. (After finitely many bounces the distance is less than $75$ feet; for example after $n$ complete bounces it is $75-60\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^n$ feet.)
