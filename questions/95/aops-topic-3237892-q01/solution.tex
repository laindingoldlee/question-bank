Write $F(x)=\dfrac{6}{3+30x}$. For $n\ge1$, $0<x_n\le2$. Its second iterate is $G(x)=F(F(x))=\dfrac{2+20x}{21+10x}$, with $G\prime(x)=\dfrac{400}{(21+10x)^2}\le\dfrac{400}{441}<1$ for $x\ge0$. Since $G(\dfrac{2}{5})=\dfrac{2}{5}$, the mean value theorem gives $|x_{n+2}-\dfrac{2}{5}|\le\dfrac{400}{441}|x_n-\dfrac{2}{5}|$. Both parity subsequences converge to $\boxed{\dfrac{2}{5}}$. This also justifies the formal equation $x=F(x)$ rather than assuming the infinite fraction converges.
