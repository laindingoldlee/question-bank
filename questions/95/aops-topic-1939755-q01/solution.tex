In a $7$-digit palindrome the digits in positions $1$ and $7$, $2$ and $6$, $3$ and $5$ are equal, so every digit except the middle one is used an even number of times. The digit $5$ appears an odd number of times, so the middle digit is $5$. The remaining digits $2,2,3,3,5,5$ form the pairs $\{2,2\},\{3,3\},\{5,5\}$, and positions $1,2,3$ get the digits $2,3,5$ in some order. That gives $3!=\boxed{6}$ palindromes.
