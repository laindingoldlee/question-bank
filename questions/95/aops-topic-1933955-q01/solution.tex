Let $g$ be the gcd. Since
\[
(n+1)!+1-(n+1)\left(n!+1\right)=1-(n+1)=-n,
\]
$g$ divides $n$, and so $g=\gcd(n!+1,n)$. If a prime $p$ divided both $n$ and $n!+1$, then $p\le n$, so $p\mid n!$, and $p$ would divide $(n!+1)-n!=1$, which is impossible. Hence $g$ has no prime factors, and
\[
\gcd\bigl(n!+1,(n+1)!+1\bigr)=\boxed{1}.
\]
