The fraction is reducible exactly when $d=\gcd(n-13,\,5n+6)>1$. By the Euclidean algorithm,
\[
\gcd(n-13,\,5n+6)=\gcd\big(n-13,\,(5n+6)-5(n-13)\big)=\gcd(n-13,\,71).
\]
Since $71$ is prime, the fraction is reducible exactly when $71\mid n-13$. The fraction is non-zero when $n\ne13$. The least positive $n$ with $n-13$ a non-zero multiple of $71$ is $n=13+71=84$ (the choice $n=13-71<0$ is not positive). Then $\dfrac{71}{426}=\dfrac{1}{6}$, which is indeed reduced by $71$.

For example $n=1$ gives $\dfrac{-12}{11}$, which is already in lowest terms because $\gcd(12,11)=1$; that is why $n=1$ does not work. The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(E)}\ 84}$.
