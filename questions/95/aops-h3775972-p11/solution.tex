The joint probabilities for a draw in the lower half and continuation, upper half and continuation, lower half and termination, and upper half and termination are respectively $\int_0^{\dfrac{1}{2}}p\,dp=\dfrac{1}{8}$, $\int_{\dfrac{1}{2}}^1p\,dp=\dfrac{3}{8}$, $\dfrac{3}{8}$, and $\dfrac{1}{8}$. Let $q$ be the requested odd-parity probability from a fresh start. A terminating upper-half draw contributes $\dfrac{1}{8}$; a continuing lower-half draw contributes $\dfrac{1}{8}q$; a continuing upper-half draw reverses parity and contributes $\dfrac{3}{8}(1-q)$. Thus $q=\dfrac{1}{8}+\dfrac{1}{8}q+\dfrac{3}{8}(1-q)$, giving $\boxed{q=\dfrac{2}{5}}$. The process terminates almost surely because each independent step continues with unconditional probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
