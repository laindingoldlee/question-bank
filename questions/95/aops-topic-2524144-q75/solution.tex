A point $(p,e)$ lies on $y=2x$ exactly when $e=2p$. We need at least four primes $p$ dividing $n$ to the exact power $p^{2p}$. To make $n$ as small as possible, use the four smallest primes and no other prime factors:
\[n=2^4\cdot3^6\cdot5^{10}\cdot7^{14}.\]
Any other admissible $n$ contains four factors $p_i^{2p_i}$ with distinct primes $p_1<p_2<p_3<p_4$, and $p_i$ is at least the $i$th prime, so each factor is at least the corresponding factor above; hence $n$ is at least this number. The answer is $\boxed{2^4\cdot3^6\cdot5^{10}\cdot7^{14}}$.
