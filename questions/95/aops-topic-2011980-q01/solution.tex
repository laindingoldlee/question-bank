At an intersection point, $x>0$ and $y>0$ (the logarithms must be defined). For $a=0$ the first equation gives $y=1$ while the second gives $y=0$, so there is no intersection; hence $a>0$.

From $y=ax$ we get $a=\dfrac{y}{x}$. Substituting into $\ln y=a\ln x$:
\[\ln y=\dfrac{y}{x}\ln x\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\ln y=y\ln x\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \ln\left(y^x\right)=\ln\left(x^y\right)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y^x=x^y.\]
So every intersection point lies on the curve $x^y=y^x$, and the answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(B)}}$.

(For example, $a=1$ gives the points of the line $y=x$, and $a=2$ gives $\ln y=2\ln x$ and $y=2x$, so $x^2=2x$, $x=2$, $y=4$; indeed $2^4=4^2$.)
