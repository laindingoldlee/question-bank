From $x^3+y^3=(x+y)^3-3xy(x+y)$, we obtain $xy=\dfrac{209}{18}$. For real $x,y$, $(x-y)^2=36-4xy=-\dfrac{94}{9}<0$, so there are no real solutions. Over $\mathbb C$, the quadratic $t^2-6t+\dfrac{209}{18}=0$ has two roots satisfying the data. Then $x^2+y^2=36-\dfrac{209}{9}=\dfrac{115}{9}$, and $x^4+y^4=(x^2+y^2)^2-2x^2y^2=\dfrac{13225}{81}-\dfrac{43681}{162}=\boxed{-\dfrac{17231}{162}}$.
