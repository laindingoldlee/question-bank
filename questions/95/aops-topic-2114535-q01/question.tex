A student offers the following ``proof'' that $0=\infty$. Draw regular polygons with $4$, $50$, $360$ and $9000$ sides inscribed in a circle. As the number of sides grows, the polygons look more and more like the circle, and with $9000$ sides there is no visible difference at all. A circle has no straight sides, that is, it has $0$ sides. Therefore $0=\infty$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Explain what is wrong with this argument.
\item[(b)] Show that a regular $n$-gon inscribed in a circle of radius $1$ has area $\dfrac {n}{2}\sin\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}$, and find this area exactly for $n=4$, $6$ and $12$. How do these areas compare with the area of the circle?
\end{enumerate}
