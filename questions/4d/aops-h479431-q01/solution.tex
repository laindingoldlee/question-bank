Write $u=\tan\dfrac{X}{2},v=\tan\dfrac{Y}{2},w=\tan\dfrac{Z}{2}$, all positive. Since $\dfrac{X}{2}+\dfrac{Y}{2}+\dfrac{Z}{2}=90^\circ$, expanding the cosine of this sum and dividing by the positive product of the three cosines gives $1-uv-vw-wu=0$. This proves the identity.

AM--GM now gives $1=uv+vw+wu\ge3(uvw)^{\dfrac{2}{3}}$, so $uvw\le\dfrac{1}{3\sqrt3}$. Equality holds precisely when $uv=vw=wu$, hence $u=v=w$, or $X=Y=Z=60^\circ$.
