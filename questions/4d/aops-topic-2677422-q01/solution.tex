Let $a=\sqrt{2501}+50$ and $b=\sqrt{2501}-50$. Then $ab=2501-2500=1$, so $b=\dfrac1a<\dfrac1{100}$.

By the binomial theorem, $\left(50+\sqrt{2501}\right)^n+\left(50-\sqrt{2501}\right)^n$ is an integer, since the odd powers of $\sqrt{2501}$ cancel. For $n=2013$ (odd), $\left(50-\sqrt{2501}\right)^{2013}=-b^{2013}$, so
\[a^{2013}=N+b^{2013}\]
for an integer $N$, with $0<b^{2013}<10^{-4026}$. Hence the decimal expansion of $a^{2013}$ has at least $4026$ zeros right after the decimal point, and the first $1000$ digits after the point are $\boxed{\text{all }0}$.
