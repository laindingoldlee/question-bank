The case $b=1$ is impossible. For $b\ge2$, the equation forces $c>b$. Put $d=c-b$. Then $(\dfrac{a}{b})^b=b^d-1$ is an integer; a rational number whose positive integer power is integral is itself integral. Thus $a=mb$ and $m^b+1=b^d$.
If $m=1$, then $b^d=2$, giving $(a,b,c)=(2,2,3)$. If $m\ge2$, $d=1$ is impossible since $m^b\ge2^b>b-1$. Catalan's theorem then forces $m^b=8,b^d=9$, giving $m=2,b=3,d=2$ and $(a,b,c)=(6,3,5)$. The distinct sums are $7$ and $14$, totaling $\boxed{21}$.
