The equation has a solution exactly when the right side $R$ lies in $[-1,1]$, the range of $\sin x$:

Two standard facts:
\[
a^2+b^2+c^2-(ab+bc+ca)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left[(a-b)^2+(b-c)^2+(c-a)^2\right]\ge0,
\]
\[
a^2+b^2+c^2+(ab+bc+ca)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left[(a+b)^2+(b+c)^2+(c+a)^2\right]\ge0 .
\]
\textbf{Case $ab+bc+ca>0$.} The first fact gives $R\ge1$, so we need $R=1$, i.e.\ $(a-b)^2+(b-c)^2+(c-a)^2=0$, i.e.\ $a=b=c$. Conversely if $a=b=c\ne0$ then $ab+bc+ca=3a^2>0$ and $R=1$.

\textbf{Case $ab+bc+ca<0$.} Then $R<0$, and $R\ge-1$ means $a^2+b^2+c^2\le-(ab+bc+ca)$, i.e.\ the second sum of squares is $\le0$. That forces $a+b=b+c=c+a=0$, so $a=b=c=0$, contradicting $ab+bc+ca\ne0$. So this case never works.

Hence the equation is solvable exactly when $\boxed{a=b=c\ne0}$, and then $\sin x=1$, i.e.\ $x=\dfrac{\pi}{2}+2k\pi$ for integers $k$.
