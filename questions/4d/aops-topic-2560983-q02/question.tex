Unit square $MATH$ (vertices in this order) has an incircle and a circumcircle. A point $I$ is chosen on the incircle so that the sum of the distances from $I$ to the two sides of the square nearest to $I$ is as small as possible, and $I$ is closer to $H$ than to any other vertex. A point $Z$ is chosen on the circumcircle so that its distance to the nearest side of the square is as large as possible, and the pentagon $MATHZ$ is not self-intersecting. If $[MATHI]+[MATHZ]=\dfrac{a+b\sqrt c}{d}$, where $a,b,c,d$ are positive integers, $\gcd(a,b,d)=1$ and $c$ is not divisible by the square of any prime, find $a+b+c+d$.
