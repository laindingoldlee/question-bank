Let $O$ be the centre of $ABC$. The figure has $120^\circ$ rotational symmetry about $O$. Its convex hull is the hexagon $DEHIFG$: the outer sides $DE$, $HI$, $FG$ of the squares (length $2$) alternate with the segments $GD$, $EH$, $IF$ joining neighbouring squares. For example, at $B$ the angle between $BD$ and $BG$ is $360^\circ-60^\circ-90^\circ-90^\circ=120^\circ$, so $GD=2\cdot2\sin60^\circ=2\sqrt3$.

\textbf{Distances from $O$ to the hexagon's side lines.} The line $DE$ is parallel to $AB$ at distance $2$ beyond it, so its distance from $O$ is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}+2\approx2.58$. The line $GD$ is perpendicular to $OB$ (by symmetry in line $OB$), and $D$ projects onto line $OB$ at distance $OB+BD\cos60^\circ=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt3}+1\approx2.15$ from $O$. So the nearest side lines are $GD$, $EH$, $IF$, at distance
\[
r_0=1+\dfrac{2}{\sqrt3}.
\]
Hence the disc of radius $r_0$ about $O$ lies inside the hexagon (the hexagon is the intersection of six half-planes whose boundary lines are all at distance at least $r_0$ from $O$).

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");

pair nAB=(0,-1), nBC=(s3/2,1/2), nCA=(-s3/2,1/2);
pair D=B+2*nAB, Ep=A+2*nAB, F=C+2*nBC, G=B+2*nBC, H=A+2*nCA, I=C+2*nCA;
pair X=extension(D,G,F,I), Y=extension(F,I,H,Ep), Z=extension(H,Ep,D,G);
draw(X--Y--Z--cycle,red+linewidth(0.9));
filldraw(A--B--D--Ep--cycle,palegray,black); filldraw(B--C--F--G--cycle,palegray,black); filldraw(C--A--H--I--cycle,palegray,black);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
real r0=1+2/s3;
draw(circle(O,r0),blue+dashed);

draw(O--TB,blue); dot(O);

\textbf{Lower bound.} Let $T$ be an equilateral triangle containing the squares. It contains the hexagon and hence the disc of radius $r_0$ about $O$, so $O$ is inside $T$ at distance at least $r_0$ from each side line of $T$. For any interior point of an equilateral triangle, the three distances to the sides add up to the height (split $T$ into three triangles with apex at the point). So the height of $T$ is at least $3r_0$, and its side is at least
\[
\dfrac{2}{\sqrt3}\cdot3r_0=2\sqrt3\left(1+\dfrac{2}{\sqrt3}\right)=4+2\sqrt3 .
\]

\textbf{Equality.} The lines $GD$, $EH$, $IF$ are at distance $r_0$ from $O$ and make $120^\circ$ angles with each other, so they bound an equilateral triangle (red) with inradius $r_0$ and side $4+2\sqrt3$; it contains the hexagon because the hexagon lies on the inner side of each of its side lines.

The minimum area is therefore
\[
\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}\left(4+2\sqrt3\right)^2=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}\left(28+16\sqrt3\right)=\boxed{12+7\sqrt3}\approx24.12 .
\]
(Extending the outer sides $DE$, $FG$, $HI$ instead gives a larger equilateral triangle, of side $2+4\sqrt3$. A numerical search over all triangles bounded by three supporting lines of the hexagon finds no triangle of any shape with smaller area than $12+7\sqrt3$.)
