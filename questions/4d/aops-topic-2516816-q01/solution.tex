\textbf{(a)} Expanding, $(m+1)(n+1)=mn+m+n+1$.

\textbf{(b)} Adding $1$ to both sides and using (a):
\[mn+m+n+1=60\iff(m+1)(n+1)=60.\]
For positive $m,n$ both factors are integers at least $2$, so $m+1$ is a divisor of $60$ other than $1$ and $60$, and $n+1=\dfrac{60}{m+1}$. The divisors of $60$ are $1,2,3,4,5,6,10,12,15,20,30,60$, so $m+1\in\{2,3,4,5,6,10,12,15,20,30\}$, giving $10$ ordered pairs:
\[\boxed{(m,n)=(1,29),(2,19),(3,14),(4,11),(5,9),(9,5),(11,4),(14,3),(19,2),(29,1)}.\]
