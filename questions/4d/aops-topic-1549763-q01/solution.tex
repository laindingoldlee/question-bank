A positive integer that is both a perfect square and a perfect cube is a perfect sixth power: in its prime factorisation every exponent is divisible by $2$ and by $3$, hence by $6$. So we need $k^6$ to be a multiple of $\operatorname{lcm}(18,30)=90=2\cdot3^2\cdot5$.

A prime divides $k^6$ only if it divides $k$, so $2$, $3$ and $5$ must all divide $k$, i.e.\ $30\mid k$. Conversely, if $30\mid k$ then $90\mid30^6\mid k^6$. So the numbers are $(30m)^6$ for $m=1,2,3,\dots$, and the five smallest are
\[
30^6,\ 60^6,\ 90^6,\ 120^6,\ 150^6 ,
\]
that is,
\[
\boxed{729{,}000{,}000,\ 46{,}656{,}000{,}000,\ 531{,}441{,}000{,}000,\ 2{,}985{,}984{,}000{,}000,\ 11{,}390{,}625{,}000{,}000}.
\]
