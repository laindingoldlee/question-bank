\textbf{Lower bound.} There are $5\cdot2=10$ possibilities (which coin, heavier or lighter), while two weighings have at most $3^2=9$ outcomes. So $2$ weighings cannot always suffice.

\textbf{Three weighings suffice.} Weigh coin $1$ against coin $2$.
\begin{itemize}
\item If they balance, both are genuine. Weigh $3$ against $1$: if unbalanced, $3$ is fake and the tilt shows heavy or light. If balanced, weigh $4$ against $1$: if unbalanced, $4$ is fake (with its type); if balanced, $5$ is fake, and a third weighing ($5$ against $1$) gives its type. That is at most $3$ weighings.
\item If they do not balance, say $1$ is heavier, then $3,4,5$ are genuine. Weigh $1$ against $3$: if $1$ is heavier, $1$ is the heavy fake; if they balance, $2$ is the light fake. That is $2$ weighings.
\end{itemize}
The least number is $\boxed{3}$.
