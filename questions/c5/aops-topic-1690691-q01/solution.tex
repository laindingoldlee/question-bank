\textbf{Method.} If $x$ has a block of $k$ repeating digits, then $10^kx$ has the same digits after the decimal point, shifted by one whole block, so $10^kx-x$ is a terminating number. Solving the resulting linear equation gives $x$ as a fraction.

\textbf{(a)} Let $x=0.777\ldots$. Then $10x=7.777\ldots$, and subtracting, $9x=7$, so $x=\boxed{\dfrac{7}{9}}$.

\textbf{(b)} Let $x=0.3636\ldots$. The block has two digits, so $100x=36.3636\ldots$ and $99x=36$. Hence $x=\dfrac{36}{99}=\boxed{\dfrac{4}{11}}$.

\textbf{(c)} Let $x=0.1666\ldots$. Here only the $6$ repeats, and it starts one place after the point. Then $10x=1.666\ldots$ and $100x=16.666\ldots$, so $100x-10x=15$, $90x=15$, and $x=\dfrac{15}{90}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{6}}$.

\textbf{(d)} Let $x=2.405405\ldots$. The block has three digits, so $1000x=2405.405405\ldots$ and $999x=2403$. Hence $x=\dfrac{2403}{999}=\boxed{\dfrac{89}{37}}$ (divide top and bottom by $27$).

Check: $7\div9=0.777\ldots$, $4\div11=0.3636\ldots$, $1\div6=0.1666\ldots$ and $89\div37=2.405405\ldots$.

In general a purely repeating decimal $0.\overline{d_1d_2\ldots d_k}$ equals $\dfrac{d_1d_2\ldots d_k}{99\ldots9}$ with $k$ nines in the denominator.
