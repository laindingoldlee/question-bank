Suppose $f(x,y)=(u,v)$, i.e.\ $x+y=u$ and $x-y=v$. Adding and subtracting,
\[
x=\dfrac{u+v}{2},\qquad y=\dfrac{u-v}{2}.
\]
So every $(u,v)$ comes from exactly one $(x,y)$, which means $f$ is a bijection, with inverse
\[
\boxed{f^{-1}(u,v)=\left(\dfrac{u+v}{2},\ \dfrac{u-v}{2}\right)}.
\]
Check: $f^{-1}(f(x,y))=\left(\dfrac{(x+y)+(x-y)}{2},\dfrac{(x+y)-(x-y)}{2}\right)=(x,y)$, and $f(f^{-1}(u,v))=\left(\dfrac{u+v}{2}+\dfrac{u-v}{2},\ \dfrac{u+v}{2}-\dfrac{u-v}{2}\right)=(u,v)$.

(Using the same letters $x,y$ for the inputs of $f^{-1}$ gives the textbook form $g(x,y)=\left(\dfrac{x+y}{2},\dfrac{x-y}{2}\right)$; the letters are only placeholders.)
