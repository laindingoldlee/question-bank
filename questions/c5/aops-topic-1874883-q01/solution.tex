The volume of a sphere of radius $r$ is $\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(7cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
import graph;
void sph(pair c,real r,string s){
 draw(circle(c,r),linewidth(1));
 draw(shift(c)*xscale(r)*yscale(0.3*r)*arc((0,0),1,180,360));
 draw(shift(c)*xscale(r)*yscale(0.3*r)*arc((0,0),1,0,180),dashed);
 dot(c);draw(c--c+(0,r),blue+linewidth(1));label(s,c+(0,0.68*r),E,blue);}
sph((0,0),2,"$2$");sph((10,0),6,"$6$");
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

So the ratio is
\[
\dfrac{\dfrac{4}{3}\pi\cdot6^3}{\dfrac{4}{3}\pi\cdot2^3}=\left(\dfrac{6}{2}\right)^3=3^3=\boxed{27},
\]
i.e. $27:1$. In general, when lengths are scaled by a factor $k$, volumes are scaled by $k^3$.
