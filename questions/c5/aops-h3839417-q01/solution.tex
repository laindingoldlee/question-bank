The $50$ opposite pairs use each number once, so their common sum is $\dfrac{1+\cdots+100}{50}=101$. Hence the opposite pairs must be $\{1,100\},\{2,99\},\ldots,\{50,51\}$.

Fix the position of $1$ to remove rotational symmetry; $100$ is then fixed opposite it. Assign the other $49$ pairs to the remaining $49$ pairs of opposite positions in $49!$ ways. Each pair has two orientations, chosen independently. Thus the answer is $\boxed{2^{49}49!}$.
