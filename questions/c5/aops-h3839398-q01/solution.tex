After the first move the tourist is on the outer cycle. Every outer city has exactly three neighbors, one of which is $P$. Thus each subsequent move returns to $P$ with probability $\dfrac{1}{3}$, and stays on the cycle with probability $\dfrac{2}{3}$, regardless of the previous outer cities.

The answers are (a) $\boxed{\dfrac{1}{3}}$, (b) $\boxed{\dfrac{2}{9}}$, and in general
\[
\boxed{\Pr(T=n)=
\begin{cases}
0,&n=1,\\
\dfrac{1}{3}\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{n-2},&n\ge2.
\end{cases}}
\]
The probabilities sum to $1$ by the geometric-series formula.
