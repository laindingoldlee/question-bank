Each variable is at most $3$, so all denominators are positive.

\textbf{Step 1 (Cauchy--Schwarz).} Writing each term as $\sqrt a\cdot\sqrt{\dfrac{a}{4-b}}$,
\[
\left(\sum_{\text{cyc}}\dfrac{a}{\sqrt{4-b}}\right)^2\le\left(\sum_{\text{cyc}} a\right)\left(\sum_{\text{cyc}}\dfrac{a}{4-b}\right)=3\sum_{\text{cyc}}\dfrac{a}{4-b}.
\]
So it suffices to prove $\dfrac{a}{4-b}+\dfrac{b}{4-c}+\dfrac{c}{4-a}\le1$.

\textbf{Step 2 (clearing denominators).} Multiplying by the positive number $(4-a)(4-b)(4-c)$, we need
\[
a(4-a)(4-c)+b(4-a)(4-b)+c(4-b)(4-c)\le(4-a)(4-b)(4-c).
\]
Let $q=ab+bc+ca$ and $r=abc$; then $a^2+b^2+c^2=9-2q$. The right side is $64-16\cdot3+4q-r=16+4q-r$. The left side is
\[
\sum_{\text{cyc}}a\,(16-4a-4c+ac)=48-4(9-2q)-4q+\left(a^2c+b^2a+c^2b\right)=12+4q+a^2c+b^2a+c^2b .
\]
So the inequality is equivalent to
\[
a^2c+c^2b+b^2a+abc\le4. \qquad (\ast)
\]

\textbf{Step 3 (the cyclic lemma).} Put $(x,y,z)=(a,c,b)$, so $(\ast)$ reads $F=x^2y+y^2z+z^2x+xyz\le4$ with $x+y+z=3$. $F$ is unchanged by the cyclic shift $x\to y\to z\to x$, so we may assume $y$ lies between $x$ and $z$. Then
\[
y(x+z)^2-F=xyz+yz^2-y^2z-z^2x=z(x-y)(y-z)\ge0,
\]
and by AM-GM,
\[
y(x+z)^2=4\cdot y\cdot\dfrac{x+z}{2}\cdot\dfrac{x+z}{2}\le4\left(\dfrac{y+(x+z)}{3}\right)^3=4 .
\]
Hence $F\le4$, which proves $(\ast)$ and therefore the inequality.

\textbf{Equality.} We need equality in AM-GM ($y=1$, $x+z=2$) and $z(x-y)(y-z)=0$, giving $(x,y,z)=(1,1,1)$ or a cyclic shift of $(2,1,0)$, together with equality in Cauchy--Schwarz. The cases from $(2,1,0)$ are $(a,b,c)=(2,0,1),(1,2,0),(0,1,2)$, where the left side equals $1+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}<\sqrt3$. At $a=b=c=1$ the left side is $3\cdot\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}=\sqrt3$. So equality holds exactly when $\boxed{a=b=c=1}$.
