Sheet $k$ carries pages $2k-1$ and $2k$, whose sum is $4k-1$. Suppose the roommate tore out $m$ sheets with numbers $k_1<k_2<\cdots<k_m$, all at least $2$. Then
\[\sum_{i=1}^{m}(4k_i-1)=101\iff4(k_1+\cdots+k_m)=101+m.\]
So $101+m$ is divisible by $4$, i.e. $m\equiv3\pmod4$.

If $m\ge7$, then $k_1+\cdots+k_m\ge2+3+\cdots+(m+1)=\dfrac{m(m+3)}{2}$, while the equation needs $k_1+\cdots+k_m=\dfrac{101+m}{4}$; but $\dfrac{m(m+3)}{2}\ge35>\dfrac{101+m}{4}$ for $m=7$, and the left side grows faster. So $m=3$ and
\[k_1+k_2+k_3=26,\qquad2\le k_1<k_2<k_3.\]
(All such sheets exist, since $k_3\le21$ and there are at least $50$ sheets.) Count by the smallest sheet $a=k_1$; then $b=k_2$ satisfies $a<b<26-a-b$, i.e. $a<b<\dfrac{26-a}{2}$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|cccccc}
$a$ & $2$ & $3$ & $4$ & $5$ & $6$ & $7$\\\hline
values of $b$ & $3$--$11$ & $4$--$11$ & $5$--$10$ & $6$--$10$ & $7$--$9$ & $8$--$9$\\
number & $9$ & $8$ & $6$ & $5$ & $3$ & $2$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
($a\ge8$ is impossible, since then $a+b+c\ge8+9+10>26$.) The number of possible sets is $9+8+6+5+3+2=\boxed{33}$.
