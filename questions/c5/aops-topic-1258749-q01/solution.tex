For $\ell$: setting $y=0$ gives $x=18$, and setting $x=0$ gives $y=-8$. So the new line has $x$-intercept $-8$ and $y$-intercept $18$, and its intercept form is
\[
\dfrac{x}{-8}+\dfrac{y}{18}=1 .
\]
Multiplying by $72$ gives $-9x+4y=72$, and multiplying by $-1$ gives the standard form with positive leading coefficient:
\[
\boxed{9x-4y=-72}.
\]

The equations $72=-9x+4y$ and $-72=9x-4y$ describe the same line, because one is the other multiplied by $-1$; they have the same solutions and the same intercepts. Books multiply by $-1$ only to follow the convention that $A>0$ in standard form, which makes the answer unique.
