Call the two smaller piles the small piles. The first player removes from the pile of $1005$ and both large piles, leaving small piles $(1004,1004)$ and large piles $(2008,2009)$.
Thereafter the second player changes either one small pile or both. The first player removes from precisely those small piles that are now odd, and fills out the move with large piles. Thus after every first-player turn both small piles are even. If the second player changes one small pile, the response uses that pile and both large piles; if both, it uses both small piles and one large pile.
These responses are legal: after the initial move the large piles each contain at least the total number $2008$ of remaining small stones, and every subsequent move consumes at least one small stone and at most one stone from any given large pile. Consequently both large piles remain nonempty before every move made while small stones remain. A second-player move cannot empty all small piles, since a positive even small pile has at least two stones. The first player eventually empties the small piles, leaving at most two nonempty piles. The second player then cannot move. Hence $\boxed{\text{the first player wins}}$.
