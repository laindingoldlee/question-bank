Each entry is $2^a3^b$ with $a,b\in\{0,1,2\}$. A product of such numbers is a cube exactly when the sums of the exponents of $2$ and of $3$ are both multiples of $3$. So the exponents of $2$ form a $3\times3$ array with entries in $\{0,1,2\}$ (that is, residues mod $3$) whose row and column sums are $\equiv0\pmod3$, and the same holds independently for the exponents of $3$.

For one such array, the top-left $2\times2$ block can be chosen freely ($3^4$ ways). This determines the last entry of each of the first two rows and the first two columns, and the bottom-right entry is then forced by the third row; it also makes the third column's sum $\equiv0$, since the total of all entries is $\equiv0$ by rows. So there are $81$ arrays for each prime, and $81^2=\boxed{6561}$ grids.
