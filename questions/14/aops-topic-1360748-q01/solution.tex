(a) If $r=\dfrac{p}{q}$ and $s=\dfrac{u}{v}$ with integers $p,q,u,v$ and $q,v\ne0$, then $rs=\dfrac{pu}{qv}$, where $pu$ and $qv$ are integers and $qv\ne0$. So $rs$ is rational.

(b) Every rational $r$ equals $r\cdot1$, a product of two rationals (and in infinitely many ways, e.g.\ $r=(2r)\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}$). A product of two irrational numbers need not be irrational, so the answer to the last question is $\boxed{\text{no}}$: for example $\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt2=2$ and $\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt8=4$ are rational.
