\textbf{Lemma (inverses are unique).} If $a+b=0$ and $a+c=0$, then $b=c$. Indeed,
\[b=b+0=b+(a+c)=(b+a)+c=(a+b)+c=0+c=c+0=c,\]
using the laws listed (commutativity in the fourth and sixth steps).

\textbf{Proof.} The number $-(-x)$ is, by definition, an inverse of $-x$: $(-x)+\bigl(-(-x)\bigr)=0$. But $x$ is also an inverse of $-x$, because $(-x)+x=x+(-x)=0$ by commutativity. By the lemma with $a=-x$, the two inverses are equal: $-(-x)=x$. $\blacksquare$
