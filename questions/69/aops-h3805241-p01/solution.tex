Let $d_i$ be the difference of two configurations, with $d_0=d_{n+1}=0$. Equal displayed sums require $d_{i-1}+d_i+d_{i+1}=0$. Starting with $d_1=t$ gives the repeating pattern $t,-t,0$. A nonzero solution meets the final boundary condition precisely when $n+1$ is divisible by three. Taking $t=1$ produces two valid binary configurations by putting mines at the positive positions in one and negative positions in the other. The eligible lengths are $2,5,8,11,14,17$, whose sum is $\boxed{57}$.
