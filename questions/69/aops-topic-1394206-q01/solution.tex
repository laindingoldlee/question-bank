The perfect squares less than $100$ are $1,4,\dots,81$, that is $9$ of the $99$ numbers. Choosing two different numbers, all $\binom{99}{2}=4851$ pairs are equally likely and $\binom{9}{2}=36$ of them consist of two squares, so the probability is
\[
\dfrac{36}{4851}=\boxed{\dfrac{4}{539}} .
\]
Equivalently, $\dfrac{9}{99}\cdot\dfrac{8}{98}=\dfrac{4}{539}$.

The student's $\dfrac{9}{99}=\dfrac{1}{11}$ is the probability that \emph{one} randomly chosen number is a square. Both numbers must be squares, so this has to be multiplied by the probability $\dfrac{8}{98}$ that the second number is a square given that the first one is. (If the two numbers could be equal, chosen independently, the answer would be $\left(\dfrac{1}{11}\right)^2=\dfrac{1}{121}$.)
