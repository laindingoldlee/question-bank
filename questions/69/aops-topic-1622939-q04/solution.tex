Sort the numbers by remainder modulo $3$: four have remainder $0$ $(3,6,9,12)$, four have remainder $1$ $(1,4,7,10)$ and four have remainder $2$ $(2,5,8,11)$. Three remainders add up to a multiple of $3$ exactly when they are all equal or all different, so each group is of type $000$, $111$, $222$ or $012$.

Let $k$ groups be of type $012$. Each class then has $4-k$ numbers left for groups of a single type, so $4-k$ must be $0$ or $3$; hence $k=4$ or $k=1$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $k=4$: each group has one number from each class. Put the four multiples of $3$ in different groups; then match the four numbers $\equiv1$ to them in $4!$ ways and the four numbers $\equiv2$ in $4!$ ways: $24\cdot24=576$ ways.
\item $k=1$: choose the mixed group in $4\cdot4\cdot4=64$ ways; the remaining three numbers of each class form the other three groups.
\end{itemize}
The total is $576+64=\boxed{640}$.
