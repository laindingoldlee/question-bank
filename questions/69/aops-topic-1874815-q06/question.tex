Since $\dfrac{1}{7}=0.\overline{142857}$, multiplying $142857$ by any of $1,2,\dots,6$ only rotates its digits (for example $142857\times3=428571$). Here is a similar trick based on $17$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Find the repeating block of the decimal expansion of $\dfrac{1}{17}$.
\item[(b)] Let $N=\dfrac{10^{16}-1}{17}$, and write $N$ with $16$ digits (with a leading zero). Prove that for every $k=1,2,\dots,16$, the number $kN$, written with $16$ digits, is a cyclic shift of the digits of $N$.
\item[(c)] What is $17N$?
\end{enumerate}
