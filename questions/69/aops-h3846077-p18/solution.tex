Put $x=a-d$, $y=b-d$, $z=c-d$, and $t=d-1$. The equations become
\[
x+y+z=0,\qquad x^2+y^2+z^2=t^2.
\]
Eliminating $z$ gives $t^2=2(x^2+xy+y^2)$. Modulo $3$, this is $t^2\equiv2(x-y)^2$. A square is $0$ or $1$, so both sides must be zero: $3\mid t$ and $x\equiv y\pmod3$.

Write $x=y+3k$. Then $2(x^2+xy+y^2)\equiv6y^2\pmod9$. Because $3\mid t$, this is zero modulo $9$, forcing $3\mid y$. Hence $x,y,z,t$ are all divisible by $3$. Dividing them by $3$ gives another solution of the same two equations. If any were nonzero, indefinite repetition would make a nonzero integer divisible by arbitrarily large powers of $3$, impossible. Therefore all are zero. Thus $d=1$ and $a=b=c=1$, which indeed satisfy the original system.
