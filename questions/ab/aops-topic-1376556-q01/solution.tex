Let $c_n$ be the number of such colourings of the $n$ sides of a polygon with $3$ colours, adjacent sides different (the sides form a cycle).

Colour the sides $1,2,\dots,n$ in order, each different from the previous one: $3\cdot2^{n-1}$ ways. These are exactly the valid colourings, except that sides $n$ and $1$ may still have the same colour. In a ``bad'' colouring (side $n$ equal to side $1$), merging sides $n$ and $1$ gives a valid colouring of a cycle of $n-1$ sides, and this correspondence is one-to-one. Hence
\[
c_n=3\cdot2^{n-1}-c_{n-1}.
\]
For a triangle all three sides must differ: $c_3=3!=6$. Then
\[
c_4=3\cdot8-6=18,\qquad c_5=3\cdot16-18=\boxed{30}.
\]
(In general $c_n=2^n+2(-1)^n$, which gives $32-2=30$ for $n=5$.)
