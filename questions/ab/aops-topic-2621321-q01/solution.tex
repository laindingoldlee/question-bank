No. The polynomial $f(x)=x$ takes the value $p$ at $x=p$ for every prime $p$, and there are infinitely many primes.

Proof of the last fact (Euclid): if $p_1,\dots,p_k$ were all the primes, then $N=p_1p_2\cdots p_k+1$ leaves remainder $1$ on division by each $p_i$. But $N>1$ has some prime factor, and it is none of the $p_i$, a contradiction. $\blacksquare$

(Any $f(x)=ax+b$ with $a>0$ and $\gcd(a,b)=1$ also takes infinitely many prime values, by Dirichlet's theorem. For irreducible polynomials of degree at least $2$, such as $x^2+1$, it is a famous open problem whether infinitely many prime values occur.)
