Put $t=x^3$. Then $t^3+t^2+t-3=(t-1)(t^2+2t+3)$, so one factor is $(x-1)(x^2+x+1)$. For the remaining sextic, let $r=3^{\dfrac{1}{6}}$ and choose $\theta\in(0,\pi)$ with $\cos\theta=-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$ and $\sin\theta=\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{3}}$. The roots of $x^3=-1+i\sqrt2$ are $r e^{\dfrac{i(\theta+2\pi j)}{3}}$ for $j=0,1,2$; the other three roots are their conjugates. Pairing conjugates yields the real factorisation $\boxed{(x-1)(x^2+x+1)\prod_{j=0}^2\left(x^2-2r\cos\left(\dfrac{\theta+2\pi j}{3}\right)x+r^2\right)}$. None of the three listed angles is a multiple of $\pi$, so each quadratic has negative discriminant. Also $x^2+x+1$ has discriminant $-3$. Thus this factorisation is complete over the reals.
