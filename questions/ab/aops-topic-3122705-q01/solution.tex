The first equation, including its positivity conditions, gives $(2x+y)^2=x^2+xy+7y^2$, hence $3(x+2y)(x-y)=0$. If $x=y$, positivity requires $y>0$. If $x=-2y$, it requires $y<0$. In both cases $y\ne0$, and $3x+y>0$. The second equation gives $(3x+y)^2=3x^2+4xy+Ky^2$, so $K=6\left(\dfrac{x}{y}\right)^2+2\dfrac{x}{y}+1$. Substituting ratios $1,-2$ yields $9,21$, both attainable. Their product is $\boxed{189}$.
