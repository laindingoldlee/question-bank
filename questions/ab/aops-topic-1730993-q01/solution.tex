Let $E$ be the expected number of throws. The first throw is a $6$ with probability $\dfrac{1}{6}$, and then one throw was needed. With probability $\dfrac{5}{6}$ it is not a $6$; then one throw has been used and the situation starts afresh, so on average $E$ more throws are needed. Hence
\[
E=1+\dfrac{5}{6}E\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\dfrac{1}{6}E=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad E=\boxed{6}.
\]
(Directly: $E=\sum_{k\ge1}k\left(\dfrac{5}{6}\right)^{k-1}\dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{1}{6}\cdot\dfrac{1}{\left(1-\dfrac{5}{6}\right)^2}=6$.)
