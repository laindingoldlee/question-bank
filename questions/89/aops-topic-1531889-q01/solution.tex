The expression $\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{x}}$ requires $x>0$. Let $s=\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{x}}>0$. Then $x=3+s$ and $xs^2=2$, so
\[
(3+s)s^2=2\iff s^3+3s^2-2=0\iff(s+1)\left(s^2+2s-2\right)=0 .
\]
The roots are $s=-1$ and $s=-1\pm\sqrt3$; the only positive one is $s=\sqrt3-1$. Hence $x=3+s=2+\sqrt3$.

Now $\sqrt{2x}=x\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{x}}=xs$ (as $x>0$), so
\[
x-\sqrt{2x}=x(1-s)=\left(2+\sqrt3\right)\left(2-\sqrt3\right)=4-3=\boxed{1}.
\]
Check: $\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{2+\sqrt3}}=\sqrt{2\left(2-\sqrt3\right)}=\sqrt{4-2\sqrt3}=\sqrt3-1$, and $2+\sqrt3-\left(\sqrt3-1\right)=3$.
