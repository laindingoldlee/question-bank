Put $s=x+y$ and $p=xy$. Then $sp=\dfrac{1}{8}$ and $s=s^3-3 sp+\dfrac{3}{4}=s^3+\dfrac{3}{8}$. Hence\[8 s^3-8 s+3=(2 s-1)(4 s^2+2 s-3)=0,\]so $s=\dfrac{1}{2}$ or $s=\dfrac{-1\pm\sqrt{13}}{4}$. For real x,y their quadratic $z^2-sz+p=0$ must have nonnegative discriminant $s^2-\dfrac{1}{2 s}$. For positive s, this requires $2 s^3\ge 1$. Using $s^3=s-\dfrac{3}{8}$, it requires $s\ge\dfrac{7}{8}$. Both positive candidates are smaller than $\dfrac{7}{8}$, so neither works. The negative candidate gives p negative and therefore positive discriminant; real x,y do exist. Thus $\boxed{x+y=-\dfrac{1+\sqrt{13}}{4}}$.
