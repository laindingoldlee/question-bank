Work modulo each prime $\ell=11,17$. For a prime $p\equiv1\pmod\ell$, $\sigma(p^r)\equiv r+1$, forcing $\ell\mid r$; use $p=23$ for 11 and $p=103$ for 17. For $p\not\equiv0,1$, the geometric sum equals 1 precisely when $p^r\equiv1$. The prime 2 has order 10 modulo 11 ($2^5=-1$, $2^2\ne1$), and 3 has order 16 modulo 17 ($3^8=-1$), forcing $10\mid r$ and $16\mid r$. Hence $\operatorname{lcm}(11,17,10,16)=14960$ divides $r$. Conversely, if $r=14960$, every prime power $p^{er}$ has divisor sum 1 modulo both 11 and 17: if $p=\ell$ this is immediate; if $p\equiv1$ use $\ell\mid r$; otherwise use Fermat and $(\ell-1)\mid r$. Multiplicativity of $\sigma$ then proves the condition for every $a$. Thus $r=14960=2^4\cdot5\cdot11\cdot17$, and the requested sum is $\boxed{35}$.
