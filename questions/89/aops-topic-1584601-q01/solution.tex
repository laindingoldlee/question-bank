Let $N$ be the midpoint of $AD$.

\textbf{$NM=\dfrac{AD}{2}$.} Since $\angle AMD=90^\circ$, $M$ lies on the circle with diameter $AD$, whose centre is $N$. So $NM=NA=ND=\dfrac{AD}{2}=\dfrac{AB+CD}{2}$.

\textbf{$AB\parallel DC$.} In vectors,
\[
\overrightarrow{NM}=\dfrac{B+C}{2}-\dfrac{A+D}{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\overrightarrow{AB}+\overrightarrow{DC}\right).
\]
By the triangle inequality, $\left|\overrightarrow{AB}+\overrightarrow{DC}\right|\le AB+DC$, with equality only if $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{DC}$ point in the same direction. We have just seen that equality holds. Hence $AB\parallel DC$ (so $ABCD$ is a trapezoid), and $\overrightarrow{NM}$ points in the same direction as $\overrightarrow{AB}$.

\textbf{The angle.} Triangle $NMD$ is isosceles with $NM=ND$, so $\angle NMD=\angle NDM=15^\circ$ and
\[
\angle MND=180^\circ-15^\circ-15^\circ=150^\circ .
\]
This is the angle between the directions $\overrightarrow{NM}$ and $\overrightarrow{ND}$, which are the directions of $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AD}$. Therefore
\[
\angle BAD=\boxed{150^\circ}.
\]
(Such quadrilaterals exist: the figure, with $AD=6$, $AB=2$, $DC=4$, $\angle BAD=150^\circ$ and $\angle ADC=30^\circ$, satisfies all the conditions.)
