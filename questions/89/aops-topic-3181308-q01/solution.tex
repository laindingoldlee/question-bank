Put $w=\ln x$, so $x=e^w$ and $we^w=\ln y$. Thus each real inverse value $w=W(\ln y)$ gives $x=\boxed{e^{W(\ln y)}}$. To count them without assuming properties of $W$, differentiate $x\ln x$: its derivative is $1+\ln x$, negative before $e^{-1}$ and positive afterward. Its minimum is $-e^{-1}$, its limit at $0^+$ is $0$ from below, and its value at $1$ is $0$. Consequently there are no solutions for $0<y<e^{-\dfrac{1}{e}}$, one at $y=e^{-\dfrac{1}{e}}$, two for $e^{-\dfrac{1}{e}}<y<1$, and one for $y\ge1$. At $y=1$ the sole positive solution is $x=1$.
