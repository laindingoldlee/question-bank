Manhole covers are usually round because a round cover cannot fall through its own hole.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] A square cover of side $s$ rests on a rim around a square hole of side $h$, where $h<s$. Show that if $s<h\sqrt2$, the cover can be dropped through the hole.
\item[(b)] Take an equilateral triangle $ABC$ of side $w$ and replace each side by the arc of radius $w$ centred at the opposite vertex (the \emph{Reuleaux triangle}). Prove that the distance between any two parallel lines that touch the Reuleaux triangle on opposite sides (its width in that direction) is $w$, whatever the direction. (So, like a circle, it has constant width, and a cover of this shape cannot be turned to slip through a slightly smaller hole of the same shape.)
\item[(c)] Find the perimeter and the area of the Reuleaux triangle of width $w$.
\end{enumerate}
