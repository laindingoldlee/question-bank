Since $1+2+\cdots+36=\dfrac{36\cdot37}{2}=666$, she has completed exactly $36$ legs, of lengths $1,2,\dots,36$, in the directions N, E, S, W repeating.

Group the legs in fours: legs $4j+1,4j+2,4j+3,4j+4$ (for $j=0,1,\dots,8$) go north $4j+1$, east $4j+2$, south $4j+3$, west $4j+4$. The net effect of each group is $2$ blocks south and $2$ blocks west. Nine groups give $18$ blocks south and $18$ blocks west, so she ends at $(-18,-18)$ relative to home.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair p=(0,0); pair[] d={(0,1),(1,0),(0,-1),(-1,0)};
path g=p;
for(int k=1;k<=36;++k){p=p+k*d[(k-1)%4]; g=g--p;}
draw(g,blue+linewidth(0.5));
draw((0,0)--p,red+linewidth(1.1));
dot((0,0)); dot(p);
label("home",(0,0)+(0.6,0.6),NE,Fill(white)); label("$(-18,-18)$",p,SW);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

By the Pythagorean theorem her distance from home is
\[
\sqrt{18^2+18^2}=\boxed{18\sqrt2}\text{ blocks}.
\]
