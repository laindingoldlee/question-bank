The power-reduction identity is
\[
\cos^6\theta=\dfrac{10+15\cos2\theta+6\cos4\theta+\cos6\theta}{32}.
\]
For $k=1,2,3$, the sum $\sum_{j=0}^{179}e^{2\pi i k \dfrac{j}{180}}$ vanishes by the geometric-series formula. Taking real parts shows that all three nonconstant cosine sums vanish. Thus
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{179}\cos^6(j^\circ)
=\dfrac{180\cdot10}{32}-1=\dfrac{221}{4}=55.25.
\]
The largest integer strictly below it is $\boxed{55}$, choice B.
