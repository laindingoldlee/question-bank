Let $DC=DB=x$ and $AD=y$. The cosine rule in triangles $ADC,ADB$ gives
\[
AC^2=x^2+y^2-xy=64,\qquad AB^2=x^2+y^2+xy=100.
\]
Subtracting yields $2xy=36$, so $xy=18$. The altitude of $A$ above $BC$ is $y\sin60^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt3 y}{2}$. Hence
\[
[ABC]=\dfrac{1}{2}(2x)\dfrac{\sqrt3 y}{2}=9\sqrt3.
\]
Therefore $\boxed{u+v=9+3=12}$. The equations allow two positive choices interchanging $x,y$, but both have the same product and area.
\begin{asy}
texpreamble("\usepackage{amsmath,amssymb}");
size(240);
defaultpen(fontsize(9pt));
real u=(sqrt(118)+1*sqrt(46))/2,v=18/u;pair D=(0,0),B=(-u,0),C=(u,0),A=(v/2,sqrt(3)*v/2);draw(A--B--C--cycle);draw(A--D,blue);label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,SW);label("$C$",C,SE);label("$D$",D,S);label("$10$",(A+B)/2,NW);label("$8$",(A+C)/2,NE);label("$60^\circ$",D+(.45,.2),NE);
\end{asy}
