Subtract $2$: $3x\equiv2\pmod7$. To divide by $3$, multiply by an inverse of $3$ modulo $7$, a number $u$ with $3u\equiv1\pmod 7$. Since $3\cdot5=15=2\cdot7+1$, the inverse is $5$. Multiplying both sides by $5$:
\[
x\equiv5\cdot2=10\equiv3\pmod7 .
\]
So the solutions are $\boxed{x\equiv3\pmod7}$, i.e.\ $x=\dots,-4,3,10,17,\dots$. Check: $3\cdot3+2=11\equiv4\pmod7$.

(In general, $ax\equiv b\pmod m$ with $\gcd(a,m)=1$ is solved by multiplying by the inverse of $a$ modulo $m$, which can be found by trial for small $m$ or by the Euclidean algorithm.)
