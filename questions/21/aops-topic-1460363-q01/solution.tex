The quantity $y-\lfloor y\rfloor$ is the fractional part of $y$. Split $[0,100)$ into the $100$ intervals $[k,k+1)$, $k=0,\dots,99$ (the single point $y=100$ has probability $0$). On $[k,k+1)$ the fractional part is $y-k$, and $y-k\ge\dfrac{1}{3}$ exactly for $y\in\left[k+\dfrac{1}{3},k+1\right)$, an interval of length $\dfrac{2}{3}$.

So the favourable set has total length $100\cdot\dfrac{2}{3}$, and the probability is $\dfrac{100\cdot\dfrac{2}{3}}{100}=\boxed{\dfrac{2}{3}}$. Every unit interval looks the same, which is why the book's solution only works with $0\le y<1$.
