Note $a\ne0$. Divide the numerator and the denominator by $a^2$:
\[
\dfrac{a^2}{a^4+a^2+1}=\dfrac{1}{a^2+1+\dfrac{1}{a^2}} .
\]
Squaring the given equation, $a^2+2+\dfrac{1}{a^2}=3$, so $a^2+\dfrac{1}{a^2}=1$. Hence the value is $\dfrac{1}{1+1}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}}$.

(Remark: for real $a\ne0$, $\left|a+\dfrac{1}{a}\right|\ge2>\sqrt3$, so $a$ is not real; the solutions are the complex numbers $a=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\pm\dfrac{1}{2}i$. The algebra above is valid for them, and the value is $\dfrac{1}{2}$ for both.)
