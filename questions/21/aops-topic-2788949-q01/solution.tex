Put the light exactly at a corner $V$ of the cube. The rays from $V$ that are blocked by the cube are exactly those passing through its interior, and these fill the solid angle at $V$ bounded by the three edges of the cube at $V$ (a trihedral angle with three right angles). Place the surface beyond the cube, perpendicular to the cube's long diagonal through $V$, for example the plane $x+y+z=3$ when the cube is $[0,1]^3$ and $V$ is the origin. It cuts the three edge rays at $(3,0,0)$, $(0,3,0)$, $(0,0,3)$, and the shadow is the equilateral triangle with these vertices.
\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair P(real x,real y,real z){return x*(-0.75,-0.45)+y*(1,-0.3)+z*(0,1);}
pair V=P(0,0,0);fill(P(3,0,0)--P(0,3,0)--P(0,0,3)--cycle,gray(0.85));draw(P(3,0,0)--P(0,3,0)--P(0,0,3)--cycle);
draw(P(1,0,0)--P(1,1,0)--P(0,1,0));draw(P(1,0,0)--P(1,0,1)--P(0,0,1)--P(0,1,1)--P(0,1,0));draw(P(1,1,0)--P(1,1,1)--P(1,0,1));draw(P(1,1,1)--P(0,1,1));
draw(P(1,0,0)--P(3,0,0),dashed);draw(P(0,1,0)--P(0,3,0),dashed);draw(P(0,0,1)--P(0,0,3),dashed);draw(V--P(1,0,0));draw(V--P(0,1,0));draw(V--P(0,0,1));
dot(V);label("light",V,W);label("shadow",P(0.2,2.3,0.8));
\end{asy}

(If the light is strictly outside the cube, the blocked rays form a cone over the cube's outline as seen from the light, which has $4$ or $6$ sides, so a bounded shadow is a quadrilateral or a hexagon.)
