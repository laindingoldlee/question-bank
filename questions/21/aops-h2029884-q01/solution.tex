Scale the square to $A=(0,0),B=(1,0),C=(1,1),D=(0,1)$, and write $P=(0,p),N=(n,0),Q=(q,0)$. Thus $0<p<1$ and $0<q<n<1$. The length condition gives
\[
n^2+p^2=(1-n)^2+1,\qquad n=1-\dfrac{p^2}{2}.
\]
The tangents of the two equal acute angles give
\[
\dfrac{p(n-q)}{p^2+nq}=1-n.
\]
Substituting for $n$ and solving gives $q=\dfrac{2(1-p)}{2-p}$. Put $\theta=\angle BCQ$, so $\tan\theta=1-q$ and $0<2\theta<90^\circ$. Then
\[
\tan(2\theta)=\dfrac{2(1-q)}{1-(1-q)^2}
=\dfrac{2(1-q)}{q(2-q)}
=\dfrac{p}{q}
=\tan\angle AQP.
\]
Both angles lie in $(0,90^\circ)$, where tangent is injective. Therefore $\angle AQP=2\theta$, as required.
