The order in which the colours come out is equally likely to be any of the $\dbinom73=35$ arrangements of $4$ B's and $3$ Y's. The fifth marble is yellow when the other two Y's are in two of the remaining $6$ positions: $\dbinom62=15$ arrangements. So the probability is $\dfrac{15}{35}=\boxed{\dfrac{3}{7}}$.

(By symmetry: the fifth marble removed is equally likely to be any of the $7$ marbles, and $3$ of them are yellow.)

The student's $11$ arrangements split the cases by the number of yellows among the first four draws: none ($1$ way), one or two. With exactly two yellows before the fifth draw there are $\dbinom42=6$ arrangements, but with exactly one yellow there are $4$ choices for its position \emph{and} $2$ choices (sixth or seventh) for the last yellow, i.e.\ $8$ arrangements, not $4$. The correct total is $1+8+6=15$.
