Each word has three copies of $A$. To avoid matching $A$ positions, the three $A$ positions in the second word must be exactly the complement of the three in the first.

Choose the $A$ positions in BANANA in $\binom63=20$ ways. Fill its remaining positions with $B,N,N$ in $3$ ways. PANAMA has its $A$ positions forced; its remaining $P,N,M$ can be arranged in $3!=6$ ways. Its $N$ is placed where BANANA has an $A$, so there is no additional matching-letter restriction. The total is $\boxed{20\cdot3\cdot6=360}$.
