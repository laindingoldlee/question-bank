For numbers $u$ and $v\ne0$, the quotient $\dfrac uv$ is by definition the unique number $w$ with $w\cdot v=u$. Take $u=\dfrac ab$, $v=\dfrac cd$ and try $w=\dfrac ab\cdot\dfrac dc$. Since $\dfrac dc\cdot\dfrac cd=\dfrac{dc}{cd}=1$,
\[w\cdot\dfrac cd=\dfrac ab\cdot\left(\dfrac dc\cdot\dfrac cd\right)=\dfrac ab.\]
So $w$ is the quotient: $\dfrac{\dfrac ab}{\dfrac cd}=\dfrac ab\cdot\dfrac dc=\dfrac{ad}{bc}$. In words: multiplying by $\dfrac dc$ exactly undoes multiplying by $\dfrac cd$, which is what division must do. $\blacksquare$
