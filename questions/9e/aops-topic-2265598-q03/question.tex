In right triangle $ACG$, $\angle C=90^\circ$ and $AC=CG=6$. Let $B$, $D$, $J$ be the midpoints of $AC$, $CG$, $GA$, and shade triangle $BCD$. Next, in triangle $JDG$ (which has its right angle at $D$), let $K$, $E$, $I$ be the midpoints of $JD$, $DG$, $GJ$, and shade triangle $KDE$. Continue in the same way: at each step, take the midpoints of the sides of the triangle at the corner $G$ left by the previous step, and shade the small triangle at its right angle. If this dividing and shading process is done $100$ times, then the total area of the shaded triangles is nearest to

$\textbf{(A)}\ 6\qquad\textbf{(B)}\ 7\qquad\textbf{(C)}\ 8\qquad\textbf{(D)}\ 9\qquad\textbf{(E)}\ 10$
