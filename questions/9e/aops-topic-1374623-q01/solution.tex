Combined variation means $f=\dfrac{kg}{h}$, so the quantity that stays constant is $\dfrac{fh}{g}=k$. From $f=6$, $g=24$, $h=2$:
\[
k=\dfrac{6\cdot2}{24}=\dfrac{1}{2} .
\]
Now $f=18$ and $h=-3$ give
\[
18=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2} g}{-3}=-\dfrac{g}{6},\qquad g=\boxed{-108}.
\]
Check: $\dfrac{fh}{g}=\dfrac{18\cdot(-3)}{-108}=\dfrac{1}{2}$.

The objection is wrong because ``$f$ varies inversely as $h$'' here means \emph{with $g$ held fixed}: for a fixed $g$, $fh=kg$ is constant. When $g$ changes as well, $fh=kg$ changes with it; in the two situations above $fh=12$ and $fh=-54$, and both equal $\dfrac{1}{2} g$.
