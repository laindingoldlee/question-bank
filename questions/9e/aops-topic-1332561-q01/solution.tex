\textbf{(a)} The right triangles $ACD$ and $BED$ have right angles at $C$ and $E$, legs $AC=BE$ and $DC=DE$, so they are congruent by SAS. Hence $AD=BD$. Also $DE=DC$ is given, so triangle $DEC$ is isosceles with apex $D$.

\textbf{(b)} The hypotheses hold in \emph{every} acute isosceles triangle with $AD=BD$: by symmetry in the axis of the triangle through $D$, the altitude from $B$ is the mirror image of the altitude from $A$, so $BE=AC$ and $DE=DC$. Take $AD=BD$ with $\angle ADB=50^\circ$.

usepackage("amsmath");

path ra(pair P,pair u,pair v,real s=0.2){return P+s*unit(u)--P+s*unit(u)+s*unit(v)--P+s*unit(v);}
pair D=(0,0), A=4*dir(70), B=4*dir(20);
pair Ep=dot(B,unit(A))*unit(A), C=dot(A,unit(B))*unit(B);
draw(A--B--D--cycle,linewidth(0.9)); draw(B--Ep,blue); draw(A--C,blue); draw(Ep--C,red);
draw(ra(Ep,D-Ep,B-Ep,0.2)); draw(ra(C,D-C,A-C,0.2));
label("$D$",D,SW); label("$A$",A,N); label("$B$",B,E); label("$E$",Ep,NW); label("$C$",C,SE);
draw(arc(D,0.6,20,70)); label("$50^\circ$",0.85*dir(45),fontsize(8pt));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Then triangle $DEC$ has apex angle $\angle EDC=50^\circ$ and base angles $65^\circ$, so it is not equilateral. In fact $DEC$ is equilateral exactly when $\angle ADB=60^\circ$, i.e. when $ABD$ itself is equilateral, which the hypotheses do not force. So the statement as given is false, and the correct conclusion is only that $DEC$ is isosceles. $\blacksquare$
