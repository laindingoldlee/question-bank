If $b=1$, then $a$ is prime and $c$ is the product of two different primes distinct from $a$. The only product of three distinct primes at most $30$ is $2\cdot3\cdot5$, giving three choices for $a$. Otherwise primality of $ab$ forces $a=1$ and $b$ prime; then $c$ is a different prime. The unordered distinct prime pairs with product at most $30$ are $(2,3),(2,5),(2,7),(2,11),(2,13),(3,5),(3,7)$, each with two orders. Total: $3+14=\boxed{17}$.
