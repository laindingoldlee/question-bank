Multiplying by $ab$ gives $ab(a+b)=a^2+b^2$. By symmetry assume $a\le b$.

\textbf{Case $a=1$.} The equation becomes $b(1+b)=1+b^2$, i.e. $b=1$.

\textbf{Case $a\ge2$.} Then $a(a+b)\ge 2(a+b)>2b$, so $ab(a+b)>2b^2\ge a^2+b^2$, a contradiction.

Hence $a=b=1$ and $a^2+b^2=\boxed{2}$.

For real numbers the value is not fixed. Taking $a=2$, the equation $2b(2+b)=4+b^2$ becomes $b^2+4b-4=0$, which has the root $b=2\sqrt2-2\ne0$. Then $a^2+b^2=4+(12-8\sqrt2)=16-8\sqrt2\ne2$, while $a=b=1$ gives $2$. So without integrality $a^2+b^2$ can take different values.
