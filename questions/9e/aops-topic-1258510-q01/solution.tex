First, $(x+y+z)(xy+yz+zx)-xyz=(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)$ (expand both sides), so the modulus is $-(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)$, and it suffices to show that
\[P(x,y,z)=(x+y+z)^n-x^n-y^n-z^n\]
is divisible by $(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)$ as a polynomial with integer coefficients.

Regard $P$ as a polynomial in $x$. Substituting $x=-y$ gives $z^n-(-y)^n-y^n-z^n=0$, because $n$ is odd. By the factor theorem, $x+y$ divides $P$; since $x+y$ is monic in $x$, the quotient has integer coefficients. By symmetry, $y+z$ and $z+x$ also divide $P$. These three linear factors are distinct and irreducible, so their product divides $P$ (in the unique-factorisation ring $\mathbb{Z}[x,y,z]$). Thus $P(x,y,z)=(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)Q(x,y,z)$ with $Q$ having integer coefficients, and substituting integers gives the congruence. (When the modulus is $0$ the congruence means equality, which also follows.) $\blacksquare$
