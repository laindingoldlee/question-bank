Let the numbers of $5$-, $3$-, $2$- and $1$-pound weights be $d,c,b,a$, so $5d+3c+2b+a=8$. Choose $d$ and $c$ first; if $r=8-5d-3c\ge0$ pounds remain, then $b$ can be any of $0,1,\ldots,\left\lfloor\dfrac {r}{2}\right\rfloor$ and $a=r-2b$ is determined, giving $\left\lfloor\dfrac {r}{2}\right\rfloor+1$ ways.
\begin{itemize}
\item $d=0,\ c=0$: $r=8$, $5$ ways.
\item $d=0,\ c=1$: $r=5$, $3$ ways.
\item $d=0,\ c=2$: $r=2$, $2$ ways.
\item $d=1,\ c=0$: $r=3$, $2$ ways.
\item $d=1,\ c=1$: $r=0$, $1$ way.
\end{itemize}
The total is $5+3+2+2+1=13$, answer $\boxed{\mathrm{(C)}}$.

(Algebraically, the answer is the coefficient of $x^8$ in $\dfrac{1}{(1-x)\left(1-x^2\right)\left(1-x^3\right)\left(1-x^5\right)}$; the casework above is the quickest way to extract it.)
