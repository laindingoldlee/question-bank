\textbf{No.} We show that
\[
(a+b+c)^3-a^3-b^3-c^3=3(a+b)(b+c)(c+a). \qquad (*)
\]
Regard the left side as a polynomial in $a$. Putting $a=-b$ gives $c^3+b^3-b^3-c^3=0$, so $a+b$ is a factor; by symmetry so are $b+c$ and $c+a$. The left side has degree $3$, so it equals $k(a+b)(b+c)(c+a)$ for a constant $k$. Comparing the coefficients of $a^2b$ ($3$ on the left, $k$ on the right) gives $k=3$.

So the equation holds exactly when $(a+b)(b+c)(c+a)=0$, that is, when \textbf{two of the numbers are opposites}: $a=-b$, $b=-c$ or $c=-a$. None of the numbers has to be zero; for example $(a,b,c)=(1,-1,2)$ gives $(1-1+2)^3=8=1-1+8$.
