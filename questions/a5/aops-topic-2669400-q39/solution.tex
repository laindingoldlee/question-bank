Write $n=2^a5^bm$ with $\gcd(m,10)=1$, and let $t=\varphi(m)\ge1$, so $10^t\equiv1\pmod m$ by Euler's theorem. Put
\[s=10^{a+b}\left(10^{t}+10^{2t}+\cdots+10^{nt}\right).\]
Its decimal digits are $n$ ones (in different positions) and zeros, so its digit sum is $n$. The bracket is $\equiv1+1+\cdots+1=n\equiv0\pmod m$, and $10^{a+b}$ is divisible by $2^a5^b$. Since $m$ and $2^a5^b$ are coprime, $n=2^a5^bm$ divides $s$. $\blacksquare$
