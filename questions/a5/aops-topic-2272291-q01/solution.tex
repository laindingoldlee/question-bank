\textbf{(a)} Let $p=4013=2\cdot2006+1$. By Wilson's theorem, $(p-1)!=4012!\equiv-1\pmod p$. Split the product:
\[4012!=\left(1\cdot2\cdots2006\right)\cdot\left(2007\cdot2008\cdots4012\right).\]
Each factor of the second product is $p-k$ for some $k=1,\ldots,2006$, and $p-k\equiv-k\pmod p$. So the second product is congruent to
\[(-1)(-2)\cdots(-2006)=(-1)^{2006}\cdot2006!=2006!.\]
Hence $(2006!)^2\equiv4012!\equiv-1\pmod{4013}$.

\textbf{(b)} By (a), $2\cdot(2006!)^2\equiv-2\equiv\boxed{4011}\pmod{4013}$.
