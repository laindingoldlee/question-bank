Let the diagonal vectors be $\mathbf u,\mathbf v$. The two midpoint-joining vectors are $\dfrac{\mathbf u+\mathbf v}{2}$ and $\dfrac{\mathbf u-\mathbf v}{2}$, up to signs. Equality of their lengths gives $\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v=0$. Thus the diagonals are perpendicular. Splitting the quadrilateral into the four triangles about their intersection gives area $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot12\cdot16=\boxed{96}$. This value is attained, for example, by a rhombus with the stated diagonals, and is actually fixed by the conditions.
