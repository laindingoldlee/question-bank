The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, so they meet at a point $O$ with $OA=OC=5$ and $OB=OD=4$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(7cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair O=(0,0),A=(-5,0),C=(5,0),B=-4*dir(45),D=4*dir(45);
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(1));draw(A--C,blue);draw(B--D,blue);
dot(O);
label("$A$",A,W);label("$B$",B,S);label("$C$",C,E);label("$D$",D,N);label("$O$",O,(0.3,-1.2));
label("$5$",(A+O)/2,S,blue);label("$5$",(O+C)/2,S,blue);label("$4$",(O+D)/2,NW,blue);label("$4$",(O+B)/2,NW,blue);
draw(arc(O,0.9,0,45),red);label("$45^\circ$",0.9*dir(22.5),E,red);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The diagonals cut the parallelogram into four triangles, each with two sides $5$ and $4$ and an included angle of $45^\circ$ or $135^\circ$; since $\sin135^\circ=\sin45^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$, each has area
\[
\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot5\cdot4\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}=5\sqrt2 .
\]
So the area is $4\cdot5\sqrt2=\boxed{20\sqrt2\text{ cm}^2}\approx28.3\text{ cm}^2$. (In general the area is $\dfrac{1}{2}d_1d_2\sin\theta$.)
