Induction says that if a statement is true at the starting integer and its truth at any integer $n$ implies its truth at $n+1$, then it is true at every integer from the starting point onward. A least-counterexample proof justifies the principle: a least failing value cannot be the starting one, and its predecessor would force it to hold.

For the sum formula, $n=1$ gives $1=\dfrac{1(2)}{2}$. Assuming the formula at $n$, add $n+1$ to get
\[
1+\cdots+n+(n+1)=\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}+(n+1)
=\dfrac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2}.
\]
This proves the next case and completes the induction.
