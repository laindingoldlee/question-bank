Since \(f(-1)=0\), the factor theorem says \(x+1\) divides \(f(x)\). Dividing,
\[
f(x)=x(x^2+2x+1)=x(x+1)^2 .
\]
So the real roots are \(\boxed{x=0\text{ and }x=-1}\) (the latter a double root).

In general, if \(f(r)=0\) then \(f(x)=(x-r)q(x)\) with \(q\) of lower degree (found by long or synthetic division), and the remaining roots are the roots of \(q\).
