Swapping the coordinates of every point is the reflection in the line $y=x$.

\textbf{(a), (b)} A point $(x,y)$ of the correct line satisfies $y=3x+2$, and Bob plots $(X,Y)=(y,x)$. So Bob's points satisfy $X=3Y+2$, i.e.
\[
Y=\dfrac{X-2}{3}\qquad\text{or}\qquad \boxed{y=\dfrac{x}{3}-\dfrac{2}{3}} .
\]
The correct line has slope $3$ and Bob's line has slope $\dfrac{1}{3}$. The two lines are mirror images in $y=x$ and cross on it at $(-1,-1)$; for instance, the point $(0,2)$ of the correct line is plotted as $(2,0)$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
import graph;
draw((-4.3,0)--(4.3,0),gray(0.7),Arrow(5));
draw((0,-4.3)--(0,4.3),gray(0.7),Arrow(5));
draw((-4,-4)--(4,4),gray(0.5)+dashed);
draw((-2.1,-4.3)--(0.75,4.25),blue+linewidth(0.9));
draw((-4.3,-2.1)--(4.25,0.75),red+linewidth(0.9));
pair p=(0,2),q=(2,0),r=(-1,-1);
draw(p--q,dotted);
dot(p,blue);dot(q,red);dot(r);
label("$(0,2)$",p,W,blue);label("$(2,0)$",q,S,red);label("$(-1,-1)$",r,SE);
label("$y=3x+2$",(0.75,4.25),E,blue);
label("$y=\dfrac{x}{3}-\dfrac{2}{3}$",(4.25,0.75),N,red);
label("$y=x$",(4,4),SE,gray(0.4));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\textbf{(c)} If the line is $y=mx+c$ with $m\ne0$, Bob's points satisfy $x=my+c$, i.e.\ $y=\dfrac{1}{m}x-\dfrac {c}{m}$, so his slope is $\boxed{\dfrac{1}{m}}$. If $m=0$, the line $y=c$ becomes the vertical line $x=c$, whose slope is undefined. A vertical line $x=c$ becomes the horizontal line $y=c$, of slope $0$.
