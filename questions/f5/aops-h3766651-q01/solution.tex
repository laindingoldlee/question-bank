Modulo $3$, the eighth-power condition says that the number of entries not divisible by $3$ is either zero or three. For an integer $u$ not divisible by $3$, writing $u=3v\pm1$ and expanding shows $u^9\equiv\pm1\pmod{27}$. Three such residues sum to one of $-3,-1,1,3$, never zero modulo $27$. The ninth powers of divisible entries are zero modulo $27$. Thus all four entries must be divisible by $3$.

Write the entries as $3u,3v,3w,3z$. The eighth-power condition is automatic, while the ninth-power condition becomes
\[
u^9+v^9+w^9+z^9\equiv0\pmod3
\iff u+v+w+z\equiv0\pmod3.
\]
There are $3^6=729$ full compact blocks of length $81$ below $3^{10}$. Each contains $27$ multiples of $3$, with $9$ reduced entries in each residue class modulo $3$. Choose the first three reduced entries arbitrarily and the fourth in its forced residue class: $27^3\cdot9=3^{11}$ choices per block.
The endpoint $3^{10}$ is a separate singleton block, contributing the all-equal quadruple. Hence
\[
N=3^6\cdot3^{11}+1=3^{17}+1=129140164,
\qquad\boxed{N\equiv164\pmod{1000}}.
\]
