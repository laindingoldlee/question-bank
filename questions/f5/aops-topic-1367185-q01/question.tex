A standard $3\times3\times3$ Rubik's cube has $8$ corner pieces (each with $3$ possible orientations) and $12$ edge pieces (each with $2$ possible orientations); the $6$ centre pieces are fixed relative to each other.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] In how many ways can the corner and edge pieces be placed if the cube is taken apart and reassembled arbitrarily?
\item[(b)] It is known that the positions reachable by legal turns are exactly those satisfying three conditions: the total twist of the corners is $0$ modulo $3$, the total flip of the edges is $0$ modulo $2$, and the permutation of the corners has the same parity as the permutation of the edges. How many positions are reachable?
\end{enumerate}
