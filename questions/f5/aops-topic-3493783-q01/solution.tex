Place the target square at $[0,\dfrac{21}{10}]^2$. Four axis-aligned unit squares cover $[0,2]^2$. A fifth unit square covers $[\dfrac{11}{10},\dfrac{21}{10}]^2$. Every point still uncovered belongs to one of the two rectangles $[0,\dfrac{11}{10}]\times[2,\dfrac{21}{10}]$ and $[2,\dfrac{21}{10}]\times[0,\dfrac{11}{10}]$. Each has dimensions $\dfrac{11}{10}$ by $\dfrac{1}{10}$. A unit square rotated $45^\circ$ and centered at the center of such a rectangle has equation $|x-x_0|+|y-y_0|\le\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$. Every point of the rectangle satisfies $|x-x_0|+|y-y_0|\le\dfrac{11}{20}+\dfrac{1}{20}=\dfrac{3}{5}<\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$. Thus one rotated unit square covers each rectangle, completing the covering with $4+1+2=\boxed7$ unit squares.
