Take reciprocals: $\dfrac{a+b}{ab}=\dfrac{1}{a}+\dfrac{1}{b}$, so
\[
\dfrac{1}{a}+\dfrac{1}{b}=\dfrac{1}{3},\qquad\dfrac{1}{b}+\dfrac{1}{c}=\dfrac{1}{4},\qquad\dfrac{1}{c}+\dfrac{1}{a}=\dfrac{1}{5} .
\]
Adding, $2\left(\dfrac{1}{a}+\dfrac{1}{b}+\dfrac{1}{c}\right)=\dfrac{20+15+12}{60}=\dfrac{47}{60}$, so $\dfrac{1}{a}+\dfrac{1}{b}+\dfrac{1}{c}=\dfrac{47}{120}$. Now
\[
\dfrac{ab+bc+ca}{abc}=\dfrac{1}{c}+\dfrac{1}{a}+\dfrac{1}{b}=\dfrac{47}{120},\qquad\text{so}\qquad\dfrac{abc}{ab+bc+ca}=\dfrac{120}{47}.
\]
Since $\gcd(120,47)=1$, $m+n=120+47=\boxed{167}$. (Such numbers exist: $\dfrac{1}{a}=\dfrac{47}{120}-\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{17}{120}$, $\dfrac{1}{b}=\dfrac{23}{120}$, $\dfrac{1}{c}=\dfrac{7}{120}$.)
