Let the expressions be $\dfrac{1}{ax^p}$ (Alice), $\dfrac{1}{bx^q}$ (Bob) and $\dfrac{1}{cx^r}$ (Carol). The simplest common denominator of $\dfrac{1}{ax^p}$ and $\dfrac{1}{bx^q}$ is $\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)\,x^{\max(p,q)}$. So the conditions are
\[
\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)=4,\quad \operatorname{lcm}(b,c)=12,\quad \operatorname{lcm}(a,c)=6,\qquad \max(p,q)=2,\quad \max(q,r)=3,\quad \max(p,r)=3 .
\]

\textbf{Exponents.} From $\max(p,q)=2$ we get $p\le2$ and $q\le2$, so $\max(q,r)=3$ forces $r=3$.

\textbf{Coefficients.} $a$ divides both $4$ and $6$, so $a\in\{1,2\}$; then $\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)=4$ forces $b=4$. Next $c$ divides $6$ and $\operatorname{lcm}(4,c)=12$ needs $3\mid c$, so $c\in\{3,6\}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $c=3$: $\operatorname{lcm}(a,3)=6$ needs $a=2$. Example: Alice $\dfrac{1}{2x^2}$, Bob $\dfrac{1}{4x}$, Carol $\dfrac{1}{3x^3}$.
\item $c=6$: $a=1$ or $a=2$ both work. Example: Alice $\dfrac{1}{x^2}$, Bob $\dfrac{1}{4x}$, Carol $\dfrac{1}{6x^3}$.
\end{itemize}
Each example satisfies all three conditions. Hence Carol's expression is
\[
\boxed{\dfrac{1}{3x^3}\ \text{ or }\ \dfrac{1}{6x^3}} .
\]
