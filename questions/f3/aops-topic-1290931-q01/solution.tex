The equation is $\boxed{y-y_0=m(x-x_0)}$.

A point $(x,y)\ne(x_0,y_0)$ lies on $\ell$ exactly when the slope between it and $(x_0,y_0)$ equals $m$, i.e. $\dfrac{y-y_0}{x-x_0}=m$ (for a non-vertical line, points on it have $x\ne x_0$). Multiplying by $x-x_0$ gives the equation, which $(x_0,y_0)$ also satisfies. Conversely, any $(x,y)$ satisfying it with $x\ne x_0$ makes slope $m$ with $(x_0,y_0)$ and so lies on the unique line through $(x_0,y_0)$ with slope $m$; and $x=x_0$ forces $y=y_0$.
