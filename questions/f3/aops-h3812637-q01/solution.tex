Since $f(x)=f(-a-x)$, every real root $x$ of the composition is paired with $-a-x$. Its unique root $-1$ must be fixed by this reflection, giving $a=2$. Thus $f(x)=(x+1)^2+b-1$ has range $[b-1,\infty)$.
The minimum value $f(-1)=b-1$ must be a root of $f$, giving $f(b-1)=b^2+b-1=0$. Hence $b=\dfrac{-1\pm\sqrt5}{2}$. The other root of $f$ is $-b-1$, since its two roots sum to $-2$. If $b<0$, this other root is greater than $b-1$ and belongs to the interior of the range of $f$, creating two additional real roots of the composition. Therefore $b=\dfrac{-1+\sqrt5}{2}>0$. For this value the other root is below the range, so the unique-root condition holds.
Now $f(1)=3+b$ and
\[f(f(1))=(3+b)^2+2(3+b)+b=15+9b+b^2=16+8b=\boxed{12+4\sqrt5}.\]
