\emph{Mod $9$.} We need $n^3\equiv 8\pmod 9$. Checking $n=0,1,\dots,8$ shows that $n^3\equiv 0,1,8\pmod 9$ according as $n\equiv0,1,2\pmod3$. So $n^3\equiv8\pmod9$ exactly when $n\equiv2\pmod3$, which is also the first condition.

\emph{Mod $5$.} $n^2\equiv4\pmod5$ means $n\equiv2$ or $3\pmod5$.

So $n\equiv 2\pmod 3$ and $n\equiv 2,3\pmod 5$: $n\equiv2$ or $8\pmod{15}$. The smallest positive such $n$ is $\boxed{2}$. (Check: $2\equiv-1\pmod 3$, $4\equiv-1\pmod5$, $8\equiv-1\pmod9$.)
