Take the line as the horizontal axis and denote the signed heights of the vertices by $h_1,h_2,h_3$. For an equilateral triangle of side $s$, projection onto any axis gives $s^2=\dfrac{2}{3}\big((h_1-h_2)^2+(h_2-h_3)^2+(h_3-h_1)^2\big)$. This follows directly by writing the vertex heights as $y_0,y_0+s\sin t,y_0+s\sin(t+60^\circ)$ and expanding. Since the squared heights sum to $39^2+35^2+13^2=2915$, maximising $s$ is equivalent to minimising $(h_1+h_2+h_3)^2$. The possible absolute sums, up to simultaneous sign reversal, are $87,61,17,9$, so the minimum is $9$. Thus $s^2=\dfrac{2}{3}(3\cdot2915-81)=5776$, giving $s\le76$. Equality is realised by $A=(0,39),B=(10\sqrt3,-35),C=(-32\sqrt3,-13)$: all three distances are $76$. The largest side is $\boxed{76}$.
