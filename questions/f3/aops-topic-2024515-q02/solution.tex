Writing $100$ with digits $0$ and $1$ in base $b$ means writing it as a sum of distinct powers of $b$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $b>100$: $100$ is a single digit different from $0$ and $1$. Not possible.
\item $b=100$: $100=10_{100}$. Possible.
\item $11\le b\le 99$: then $b<100<b^2$, so $100$ has two digits and $100\in\{b,\ b+1\}$, giving only $b=99$: $100=11_{99}$.
\item $2\le b\le10$: the units digit must be $0$ or $1$, so $b\mid100$ or $b\mid99$, i.e.\ $b\in\{2,3,4,5,9,10\}$. Checking: $100=1100100_2$ (works), $100=10201_3$, $100=1210_4$, $100=400_5$, $100=121_9$ (fail), and $100=100_{10}$ (works).
\end{itemize}
So the bases are $2,10,99,100$, and the answer is $\boxed{4}$.
