\textbf{The error.} The letters consist of one letter used twice and two other letters used once each. In $26\cdot25\cdot24$ the two single letters are chosen \emph{in order} (a second and a third letter). But the $12$ arrangements already place the letters in every possible order, so each plate is counted twice. For example, ``repeated $A$, then $B$, then $C$'' and ``repeated $A$, then $C$, then $B$'' both produce the arrangement $AABC$.

\textbf{Correct count.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Choose the repeated letter: $26$ ways.
\item Choose the \emph{set} of the two other letters: $\binom{25}{2}=300$ ways.
\item Arrange the four letters $X,X,Y,Z$: $\dfrac{4!}{2!}=12$ ways.
\item Choose the two different digits in order: $10\cdot9=90$ ways.
\end{itemize}
Total: $26\cdot300\cdot12\cdot90=\boxed{8\,424\,000}$, half of the student's count.
