Let the circle have centre $O$ and radius $r$, and let $\ell$ be a line at distance $d$ from $O$; let $F$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $O$ to $\ell$. For a point $X$ on $\ell$, $OX^2=d^2+FX^2$, so $X$ is on the circle exactly when $FX^2=r^2-d^2$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
draw(circle((0,0),1),blue); draw((-1.6,-0.7)--(1.6,0.9),red); pair P=intersectionpoints(circle((0,0),1),(-1.6,-0.7)--(1.6,0.9))[0], Q=intersectionpoints(circle((0,0),1),(-1.6,-0.7)--(1.6,0.9))[1]; dot(P); dot(Q); dot((0,0)); label("$O$",(0,0),S);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

If $d>r$ there is no solution; if $d=r$ only $X=F$; if $d<r$ exactly the two points with $FX=\sqrt{r^2-d^2}$ on either side of $F$. In every case a line meets the circle in at most two points, so three distinct points of a circle cannot be collinear. $\blacksquare$
