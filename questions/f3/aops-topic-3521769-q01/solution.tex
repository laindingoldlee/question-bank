For all integers $n$, $n^7-n^3=n^3(n^4-1)$ is divisible by $8$: if $n$ is even, use $n^3$; if odd, $n^4\equiv1\pmod{16}$. It is divisible by $3$ and $5$, since for each modulus either $n$ is zero or its fourth power is $1$. Hence it is always divisible by $120$. Conversely, if $q$ works for all large $n$, choose such an $n$ with $n\equiv2\pmod q$. Then $q$ divides $2^7-2^3=120$. Therefore the valid $q$ are exactly the divisors of $120=2^3\cdot3\cdot5$. Their sum is $(1+2+4+8)(1+3)(1+5)=\boxed{360}$.
