Of the $8$ equally likely outcomes, exactly $2$ ($HHH$ and $TTT$) have all three faces equal, so the probability is $\dfrac{2}{8}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{4}}$.

The error is in "the third coin". The two coins that agree are chosen after looking at the result, so "the third coin" is not a fixed coin whose toss is independent of the others. For example, in $HHT$ the agreeing pair is coins $1,2$, while in $HTH$ it is coins $1,3$; whichever coin is left over was picked precisely because it may disagree. Concretely, in all $8$ outcomes some two coins agree, and the remaining coin also agrees in only $2$ of them, not in half of them.

The argument would be correct for fixed coins: given that coins $1$ and $2$ show the same face (probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$), coin $3$ matches them with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$, giving $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{1}{4}$ again.
