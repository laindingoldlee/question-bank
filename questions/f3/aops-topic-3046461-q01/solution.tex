Put $m=\dfrac{p-1}{2}$. Reduce $2,4,\ldots,2m$ to signed residues in $\{-m,\ldots,-1,1,\ldots,m\}$. Their absolute values are exactly $1,\ldots,m$: equality up to sign would imply $2j\equiv\pm2k$, forcing $j=k$ in this range. The number of negative signed residues is $N=m-\lfloor \dfrac{p}{4}\rfloor$. Multiplying and cancelling the nonzero $m!$ modulo $p$ yields $2^m\equiv(-1)^N$. To see the residue criterion directly, the $m$ distinct nonzero squares are all roots of $X^m-1$ by Fermat's theorem; a degree-$m$ polynomial has at most $m$ roots over a field, so these are precisely its roots. Hence 2 is a square exactly when $N$ is even. Evaluating the parity for $p\equiv1,3,5,7\pmod8$ gives even,odd,odd,even, respectively. Thus $\boxed{p\equiv1\text{ or }7\pmod8}$.
