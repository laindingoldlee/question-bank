Let $q$ be the probability she does \emph{not} eat a chip in a given half-hour. Not eating during the hour means not eating in either half, so $q^2=1-0.64=0.36$ and $q=0.6$. The probability she eats a chip in the first half-hour is $1-0.6=\boxed{40\%}$.

The $32\%$ reasoning assumes the whole $64\%$ is spread evenly over time, as if she ate at most one chip at a uniformly random moment. Under the stated model she may eat chips in both halves, so the halves' probabilities $40\%$ each overlap: $P(\text{first or second})=0.4+0.4-0.16=0.64$.
