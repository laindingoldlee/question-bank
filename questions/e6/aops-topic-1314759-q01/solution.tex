Let the current ages be $T$, $J$, $K$.
\begin{itemize}
\item Tim was $J$ years old $T-J$ years ago; then Kim was $K-(T-J)$ and Jim $J-(T-J)$, so $K-T+J=2(2J-T)$, i.e. $K=3J-T$.
\item Kim was $T$ years old $K-T$ years ago; then Jim was $J-(K-T)=30$.
\item Jim will be $K$ in $K-J$ years; then Tim will be $T+K-J=88$.
\end{itemize}
Substituting $K=3J-T$: the second condition gives $2T-2J=30$, so $T-J=15$; the third gives $2J=88$, so $J=44$, $T=59$, $K=73$. Jim reaches Tim's current age in $15$ years, when the sum of the ages is $59+44+73+3\cdot15=\boxed{221}$.
