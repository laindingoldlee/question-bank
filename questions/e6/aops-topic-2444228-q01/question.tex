For $n\ge0$ let
\[a_n=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{k!\,(k+n)!}=\dfrac{1}{n!}+\dfrac{1}{1!\,(n+1)!}+\dfrac{1}{2!\,(n+2)!}+\cdots.\]
Prove that the infinite continued fraction
\[1+\dfrac{1}{2+\dfrac{1}{3+\dfrac{1}{4+\cdots}}}\]
(that is, the limit of the finite continued fractions $c_n=1+\dfrac{1}{2+\dfrac{1}{\ddots+\dfrac{1}{n}}}$) exists and equals $\dfrac{a_0}{a_1}$.
