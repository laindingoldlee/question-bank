Let $f(t)=4^{-t}$. We have $t_2=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\in[\dfrac{1}{3},\dfrac{3}{4}]$. The decreasing function $f$ maps this interval into itself: $f(\dfrac{3}{4})=\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt2}>\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $f(\dfrac{1}{3})=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]4}<\dfrac{3}{4}$. On it, $|f'(t)|\le(\ln4)4^{-\dfrac{1}{3}}<1$, since $\ln4<\dfrac{3}{2}$ and $4^{-\dfrac{1}{3}}<\dfrac{2}{3}$. The first bound follows from $e^{\dfrac{3}{2}}>1+\dfrac{3}{2}+\dfrac{(\dfrac{3}{2})^2}{2}+\dfrac{(\dfrac{3}{2})^3}{6}>4$. Let this derivative bound be $q<1$. Since $f(\dfrac{1}{2})=\dfrac{1}{2}$, the mean-value theorem gives $|t_n-\dfrac{1}{2}|\le q^{n-2}|t_2-\dfrac{1}{2}|\to0$. Thus the tower, defined by these finite truncations, converges to $\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}}$.
