Put the hill count of $AB$ equal to $x$. The equations imply $BC=10-x$, $CD=x$, $BD=22-x$, and $AD=23+x$, with $0\le x\le10$. The simple routes from $A$ to $D$ are $AD$, $ABD$, and $ABCD$, of costs $23+x$,22, and $10+x$, respectively. Since $10+x\le20$, $\boxed{A\to B\to C\to D}$ is cheapest. Repeated-vertex routes cannot improve a cost when all trail costs are nonnegative.

usepackage("amsmath");
size(7cm);
pair A=(0,0),B=(0,3),C=(4,3),D=(4,0);draw(A--B--C--D,blue+1.5);draw(A--D^^B--D);label("$A$",A,SW);label("$B$",B,NW);label("$C$",C,NE);label("$D$",D,SE);label("$x$",(0,1.5),W);label("$10-x$",(2,3),N);label("$x$",(4,1.5),E);label("$22-x$",(2,1.5),NE);label("$23+x$",(2,0),S);
\end{asy}
