A terminating base-$b$ expansion with $k$ digits after the point means $\dfrac {m}{n}=\dfrac{N}{b^k}$ for some integer $N$, i.e. $mb^k=nN$. So $n\mid mb^k$, and since $\gcd(m,n)=1$, $n\mid b^k$. Every prime factor of $n$ therefore divides $b^k$, hence divides $b$. The distinct primes dividing $n$ are distinct primes dividing $b$, so their product $\operatorname{rad}(n)$ divides $\operatorname{rad}(b)$, which divides $b$. Therefore $\operatorname{rad}(n)\le b$. $\blacksquare$
