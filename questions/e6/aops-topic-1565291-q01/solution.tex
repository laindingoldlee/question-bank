Multiply by $r^2$: $r^2y=\ln\left(\dfrac {x}{m}-sa\right)$. Apply the exponential function, the inverse of $\ln$:
\[
e^{r^2y}=\dfrac {x}{m}-sa\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\dfrac {x}{m}=e^{r^2y}+sa\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad x=m e^{r^2y}+msa .
\]
Writing $r^2$ as $rr$ and reordering the last product,
\[
\boxed{x=m\,e^{rry}+mas},
\]
which reads ``$x$ = merry + mas'': Merry Xmas!
