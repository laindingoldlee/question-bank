Write a candidate as $\dfrac {p}{q}$ in lowest terms. Dividing it by $\dfrac {a}{2}$ (with $a$ odd) gives $\dfrac{2p}{qa}$, which is an integer exactly when $a\mid p$ and $q\mid2$ (as $\gcd(p,q)=1$ and $\gcd(a,2)=1$). So $p$ must be a multiple of $1$, $3$ and $5$, i.e. of $15$, and $q\in\{1,2\}$. The smallest choice is $\boxed{\dfrac{15}{2}}$.

Check: $\dfrac{15}{2}$ divided by $\dfrac{1}{2}$, $\dfrac{3}{2}$, $\dfrac{5}{2}$ gives $15$, $5$, $3$; a smaller common multiple would be a positive multiple of $\dfrac{1}{2}$ with numerator divisible by $15$, so at least $\dfrac{15}{2}$.
