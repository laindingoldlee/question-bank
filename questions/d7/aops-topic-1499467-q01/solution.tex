\textbf{Sean's series} has first term $a=\dfrac{1}{8}$ and common ratio $r=\dfrac{1}{8}$, with $|r|<1$, so it converges to
\[
\dfrac{a}{1-r}=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{8}}{\dfrac{7}{8}}=\dfrac{1}{7} .
\]

\textbf{Tom's series} has the $201$ terms $i^k$ for $k=-100,\dots,100$. The powers of $i$ repeat with period $4$: $1,i,-1,-i$, and any four consecutive powers add up to $0$. The first $200$ terms ($k=-100$ to $99$) form $50$ blocks of four consecutive powers, so they add up to $0$. What remains is the last term, $i^{100}=\left(i^4\right)^{25}=1$, so Tom's sum is $1$.

Hence the total is
\[
\dfrac{1}{7}+1=\boxed{\dfrac{8}{7}}.
\]

\emph{Remark.} It is not true that the terms cancel in pairs $i^{-k}+i^{k}$. For example $i^{-2}+i^{2}=-2$ and $i^{-4}+i^4=2$, while for odd $k$ the pair sums to $0$. The grouping into blocks of four is what makes the sum easy.
