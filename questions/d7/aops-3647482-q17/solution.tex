Raise the first equation to power $y$ and the second to power $x$: $x^{xy}=2^{20}$ and $y^{xy}=2^4$. Since $xy>0$, both $x,y>1$. Dividing the logarithms yields $\dfrac{\log x}{\log y}=5$, so $x=y^5$. The second relation becomes $y^{y^6}=16$. For $y>1$, both $y^6$ and $\log y$ strictly increase and are positive, so $y^6\log y$ strictly increases; there is at most one solution. The value $y=\sqrt2$ works because $y^6=8$ and $(\sqrt2)^8=16$. Then $x=(\sqrt2)^5=4\sqrt2$, giving $\boxed{x+y=5\sqrt2}$.
