Since the right angle is at $B$, the hypotenuse is $AC=b$, and by the Pythagorean theorem
\[a^2+c^2=b^2 .\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real c=1.3, a=c*(2+sqrt(3));
pair B=(0,0), A=(a,0), C=(0,c);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw((0.22,0)--(0.22,0.22)--(0,0.22));
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);
label("$A$",A,SE);label("$B$",B,SW);label("$C$",C,NW);
label("$a$",(A+B)/2,S);label("$c$",(B+C)/2,W);label("$b$",(A+C)/2,NE);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

A quadratic with $a\ne0$ has exactly one solution exactly when its discriminant is zero, so
\[b^2-4ac=0,\qquad ac=\dfrac{b^2}{4}.\]
Therefore
\[(a+c)^2=a^2+c^2+2ac=b^2+\dfrac{b^2}{2}=\dfrac{3}{2}b^2 .\]
All lengths are positive, so taking square roots gives $a+c=b\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{2}}$, that is, $\dfrac{a+c}{b}=\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{2}}$. $\blacksquare$

(Such triangles exist: $a^2+c^2=4ac$ gives $\dfrac {a}{c}=2\pm\sqrt3$, a triangle with acute angles $15^\circ$ and $75^\circ$.)
