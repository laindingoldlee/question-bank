Let $c$ and $m$ be the total amounts of coffee and milk (both positive), and $p$ the number of people. Everybody drank $8$ ounces, so $c+m=8p$. Angela drank $\dfrac c6+\dfrac m4=8$ ounces. Hence
\[\dfrac c6+\dfrac m4=\dfrac{c+m}{p}.\]
Since $c,m>0$,
\[\dfrac{c+m}{6}<\dfrac c6+\dfrac m4<\dfrac{c+m}{4},\]
so $\dfrac{c+m}{6}<\dfrac{c+m}{p}<\dfrac{c+m}{4}$, i.e. $4<p<6$. Thus $p=\boxed{5}$, answer $\textbf{(C)}$.

Note that nothing is assumed about the other cups: the only facts used are that Angela's cup and every cup held $8$ ounces. ``Varied from cup to cup'' just says the cups need not have the same mix, and ``never zero'' gives $c,m>0$. The situation is possible: with $p=5$, total coffee $24$ and milk $16$, Angela drinks $4$ of each, and the other four could drink, for instance, $6$, $5$, $5$ and $4$ ounces of coffee with $2$, $3$, $3$ and $4$ ounces of milk.
