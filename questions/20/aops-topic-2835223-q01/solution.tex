$n=1$ works. Suppose $n>1$ and let $p$ be the smallest prime factor of $n$. Then $p\ne2$ (as $3^n-2^n$ is odd) and $p\ne3$ (as $3\nmid2^n$), so $p\nmid6$. From $3^n\equiv2^n\pmod p$, $t=3\cdot2^{-1}$ satisfies $t^n\equiv1$. Let $d$ be the order of $t$ modulo $p$; then $d\mid n$ and $d\mid p-1$, and $d>1$ because $t\not\equiv1$ ($3\not\equiv2$). A prime factor of $d$ divides $n$ and is less than $p$, contradicting the choice of $p$. So the only solution is $\boxed{n=1}$.
