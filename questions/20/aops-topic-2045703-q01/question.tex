Fix an integer \(n\ge2\). The \emph{\(n\)-anacci sequence} starts with \(n-1\) zeros followed by a \(1\), and every later term is the sum of the \(n\) terms immediately before it. (For \(n=2\) this is \(0,1,1,2,3,5,\dots\), the Fibonacci sequence; for \(n=3\) it is \(0,0,1,1,2,4,7,\dots\).) Prove that the \((n+2)\)-th nonzero term of the sequence equals \(2^n-1\).
