\emph{Odd primes.} If $p$ is an odd prime, then $\gcd(2,p)=1$, and Fermat's little theorem gives $2^{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p$, i.e. $p\mid2^{p-1}-1$.

\emph{Not all odd $n$.} For $n=9$: $2^8-1=255=3\cdot5\cdot17$, which is not divisible by $9$. So the statement is $\boxed{\text{false in general}}$: it holds for $n=1$ and every odd prime, but fails for $n=9$ (and for most odd composites; the exceptions, such as $341=11\cdot31$, are called base-$2$ pseudoprimes).
