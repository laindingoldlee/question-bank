Take an element $x$ of $A\cup B\cup C$ and suppose it lies in exactly $k$ of the three sets, $k\in\{1,2,3\}$. On the right it is counted $k$ times in $|A|+|B|+|C|$, subtracted once for each of the $\dbinom k2$ pairwise intersections containing it, and added $\dbinom k3$ times in the last term:
\begin{itemize}
\item $k=1$: $1-0+0=1$;
\item $k=2$: $2-1+0=1$;
\item $k=3$: $3-3+1=1$.
\end{itemize}
So every element of the union is counted exactly once, and elements outside the union are not counted at all; this proves the formula.

In particular, an element in all three sets is subtracted \emph{three} times (once for each of $A\cap B$, $B\cap C$, $C\cap A$), not twice, after being added three times; that is why $|A\cap B\cap C|$ must be added back. $\blacksquare$
