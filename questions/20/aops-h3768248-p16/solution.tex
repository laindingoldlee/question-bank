The maximum is $\boxed{16}$; the drawing supplies a construction. Here is an exhaustive row recurrence proving the upper bound. For each row of five cells choose, independently, no diagonal, a rising diagonal, or a falling diagonal, and discard a choice if two selected diagonals share a vertex. Encode its occupied lower and upper vertices as six-bit masks $L,U$, and let $k$ be its diagonal count. There are 99 valid row patterns. Let $D_r(U)$ be the greatest count in the first $r$ rows with top mask $U$, taking $D_0(0)=0$ and all other initial states impossible. Then
\[D_{r+1}(U)=\max_{(L,U,k),\ V\mathbin{\&}L=0}\{D_r(V)+k\}.\]
The condition means that the occupied shared-boundary vertices do not overlap. The maxima over masks after rows $1,2,3,4,5$ are $5,6,10,12,16$. This finite recurrence examines every possible arrangement and rules out 17; a reproducible implementation is included in the audit verification script.
