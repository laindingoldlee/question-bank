\textbf{(a)} By definition, $m\mid a-b$ and $m\mid p-q$. Then
\[
(a+p)-(b+q)=(a-b)+(p-q)
\]
is a sum of two multiples of $m$, hence a multiple of $m$, so $a+p\equiv b+q\pmod m$. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{(b)} The student added the two sides of the \emph{same} congruence ($7$ with $2$, and $8$ with $3$). The property says to add the left sides together and the right sides together: here $a=7$, $b=2$, $p=8$, $q=3$, so we compare $a+p=7+8=15$ with $b+q=2+3=5$. Both are $\equiv0\pmod5$, so
\[
7+8\equiv2+3\pmod5,
\]
as the property predicts.
