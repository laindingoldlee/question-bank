Let the legs be $a,b$ and the hypotenuse $c$, so $a^2+b^2=c^2$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
path ra(pair P,pair u,pair v,real s=0.2){return P+s*unit(u)--P+s*unit(u)+s*unit(v)--P+s*unit(v);}
pair C=(0,0),A=(0,10),B=(24,0);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9)); draw(ra(C,B-C,A-C,1.2));
label("$A$",A,N); label("$B$",B,E); label("$C$",C,SW);
label("$a$",(A+C)/2,W); label("$b$",(B+C)/2,S); label("$c$",(A+B)/2,NE);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Then $a^2+b^2+c^2=2c^2=1352$, so $c^2=676$ and $c=26$. Hence $a+b=60-26=34$, and
\[
2ab=(a+b)^2-\left(a^2+b^2\right)=1156-676=480 ,
\]
\[
(a-b)^2=\left(a^2+b^2\right)-2ab=676-480=196 ,
\]
so $|a-b|=\boxed{14}$ (the triangle is the $10$-$24$-$26$ right triangle), as stated in the post.
