There are $\binom{100}{2}=4950$ possible hidden pairs. A depth-$q$ decision tree with three possible answers per query has at most $3^q$ leaves. Since $3^7=2187<4950$, at least 8 queries are necessary. For an upper bound, repeatedly bisect a candidate set known to contain a hidden integer and query one half. Keep that half if the reported count is positive, and otherwise keep the other. Seven queries reduce 100 candidates to one hidden integer. Remove that known value, then binary-search the remaining 99 candidates for the other using at most seven further queries. Thus the optimal worst-case count lies between $\boxed8$ and $\boxed{14}$.
