The multiples of $6$ below $2018$ are $6k$ with $1\le k\le\left\lfloor\dfrac{2017}{6}\right\rfloor=336$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $4\mid6k$ exactly when $2\mid3k$, i.e. when $k$ is even. So $6k$ is not divisible by $4$ exactly when $k$ is odd.
\item $9\mid6k$ exactly when $3\mid2k$, i.e. when $3\mid k$. So $6k$ is not divisible by $9$ exactly when $3\nmid k$.
\end{itemize}
So we need the $k$ with $1\le k\le336$ that are odd and not multiples of $3$, i.e. $k\equiv1$ or $5\pmod6$. Since $336=6\cdot56$, each block of six consecutive values of $k$ contains exactly two of them, and the count is $2\cdot56=\boxed{112}$.

(Check by inclusion--exclusion: of the $336$ multiples of $6$, those divisible by $4$ are the multiples of $12$, $168$ of them; those divisible by $9$ are the multiples of $18$, $112$ of them; those divisible by both are the multiples of $36$, $56$ of them. So $336-(168+112-56)=112$.)

The contest wording, ``not divisible by at least one of the numbers $4$ or $9$'', is meant in this sense. Read instead as ``not divisible by both $4$ and $9$'' (that is, not a multiple of $36$), it would give $336-56=280$.
