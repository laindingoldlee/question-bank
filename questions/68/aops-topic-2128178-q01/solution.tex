If $23\mid n$ then $n^{68}\equiv0\not\equiv6$, so $n$ is not a multiple of $23$. Then by Fermat's little theorem $n^{22}\equiv1\pmod{23}$, and since $68=3\cdot22+2$,
\[n^{68}=\left(n^{22}\right)^3n^2\equiv n^2\pmod{23}.\]
So we need $n^2\equiv6\pmod{23}$. Since $11^2=121=5\cdot23+6$, this says $n^2\equiv11^2$, that is, $23\mid(n-11)(n+11)$, and as $23$ is prime, $n\equiv11$ or $n\equiv-11\equiv12\pmod{23}$.

The numbers $n<2020$ with $n\equiv11\pmod{23}$ are $11+23j$ for $j=0,1,\dots,87$ (the largest is $2012$), and those with $n\equiv12$ are $12+23j$ for $j=0,\dots,87$ (the largest is $2013$). Each list has $88$ terms and $\sum_{j=0}^{87}j=\dfrac{87\cdot88}{2}=3828$, so the total is
\[(88\cdot11+23\cdot3828)+(88\cdot12+23\cdot3828)=88\cdot23+2\cdot88044=2024+176088=\boxed{178112}.\]
