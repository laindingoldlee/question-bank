\textbf{(a)} Draw the chords one at a time. Before any chord there is $1$ region. Suppose the new chord crosses $k$ of the chords already drawn. Since no three chords meet at one interior point, these $k$ crossings are distinct points, and they cut the new chord into $k+1$ pieces. Each piece runs across one existing region from one point of its boundary to another, and splits that region into two; different pieces lie in different regions (consecutive pieces are separated by an earlier chord). So the new chord adds exactly $k+1$ regions.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair O=(0,0);draw(circle(O,2),linewidth(0.9));
pair a1=2*dir(200),b1=2*dir(10),a2=2*dir(100),b2=2*dir(290),a3=2*dir(130),b3=2*dir(345);
draw(a1--b1,linewidth(0.9));draw(a2--b2,linewidth(0.9));draw(a3--b3,blue+linewidth(0.9));
pair X=extension(a1,b1,a3,b3),Y=extension(a2,b2,a3,b3);dot(X,blue);dot(Y,blue);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

(In the figure, the blue chord crosses $k=2$ earlier chords and is cut into $3$ pieces, so it adds $3$ regions: $4$ regions become $7$.)

Every crossing point is counted exactly once, when the later of its two chords is drawn. Adding up,
\[
\text{regions}=1+\sum_{\text{chords}}(k+1)=1+n+I. \qquad\blacksquare
\]

\textbf{(b)} Two chords cross at most once, so $I\le\dbinom n2$ and the number of regions is at most
\[
1+n+\dbinom n2=\boxed{\dfrac{n^2+n+2}{2}}.
\]
This is attained: take $n$ diameters at different angles (any two cross at the centre) and then move them slightly, each by a different small amount, so that every two still cross inside the circle but no three pass through one point. For $n=1,2,3,4$ this gives $2,4,7,11$ regions.

(The number of regions is not determined by $n$ alone; it depends on how many crossings there are, as part (a) shows. If no three chords are concurrent but some pairs do not cross, there are fewer regions.)
