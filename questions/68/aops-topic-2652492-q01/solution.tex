Call the expression $E$. Swapping $a$ and $b$ turns $E$ into $b(a-c)^3+a(c-b)^3+c(b-a)^3=-E$, and similarly for the other swaps, so $E$ changes sign under every transposition of the variables. In particular $E=0$ when $a=b$, so $a-b$ divides $E$; likewise $b-c$ and $c-a$ divide $E$.

So $E=(a-b)(b-c)(c-a)\,L$ with $L$ of degree $4-3=1$. The product $(a-b)(b-c)(c-a)$ also changes sign under every transposition, so $L$ is symmetric and linear: $L=k(a+b+c)$. Setting $a=0,b=1,c=2$: $E=0+1\cdot2^3+2\cdot(-1)^3=6$ and $(a-b)(b-c)(c-a)(a+b+c)=(-1)(-1)(2)(3)=6$, so $k=1$. Therefore
\[\boxed{a(b-c)^3+b(c-a)^3+c(a-b)^3=(a+b+c)(a-b)(b-c)(c-a)}.\]
