Such a number has at least two digits, so it is $N=\overline{dd\cdots d}$ with $k\ge2$ digits all equal to $d\in\{1,\dots,9\}$.

\textbf{Last digit.} A square ends in $0,1,4,5,6$ or $9$, so $d\in\{1,4,5,6,9\}$.

\textbf{Modulo $4$.} A square is $\equiv0$ or $1\pmod4$ (even squares are multiples of $4$, odd squares are $\equiv1$). Since $100$ is a multiple of $4$, $N\equiv\overline{dd}\pmod4$, and
\[11\equiv3,\qquad 55\equiv3,\qquad 66\equiv2,\qquad 99\equiv3\pmod4.\]
So $d=1,5,6,9$ are impossible.

\textbf{$d=4$.} Then $N=4\cdot\overline{11\cdots1}$, and $N$ is a square only if $\overline{11\cdots1}$ ($k\ge2$ ones) is a square; but it is $\equiv11\equiv3\pmod4$, so it is not.

Hence no such square exists. $\blacksquare$
