\textbf{(b) first.} Let the sequence be $a,ar,ar^2,\ldots$ and let $B_1,B_2,B_3$ be the sums of terms $1$--$2011$, $2012$--$4022$ and $4023$--$6033$. Each term of the second block is the corresponding term of the first block multiplied by $r^{2011}$ (term $2011+k$ is $ar^{2010+k}=r^{2011}\cdot ar^{k-1}$). Hence
\[B_2=r^{2011}B_1,\qquad B_3=r^{2011}B_2.\]
So the block sums themselves form a geometric sequence with ratio $\rho=r^{2011}$. The student's ``common ratio'' must be $\rho$, not $r$; with that reading the shortcut is completely correct.

\textbf{(a)} $B_1=200$ and $B_2=380-200=180$, so $\rho=\dfrac{180}{200}=\dfrac{9}{10}$ and $B_3=\dfrac{9}{10}\cdot180=162$. The sum of the first $6033$ terms is
\[200+180+162=\boxed{542}.\]
(With the finite-sum formula: $\dfrac{a\left(1-\rho\right)}{1-r}=200$ and $\dfrac{a\left(1-\rho^2\right)}{1-r}=380$ give $1+\rho=\dfrac{380}{200}$, the same $\rho$, and then $\dfrac{a\left(1-\rho^3\right)}{1-r}=200\left(1+\rho+\rho^2\right)=542$.)
