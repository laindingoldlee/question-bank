There are $8$ people in $5$ rooms with $1$ or $2$ people each, so exactly three rooms have $2$ people and two rooms have $1$ (since $3\cdot2+2\cdot1=8$).

\begin{itemize}
\item Choose the boy's room: $5$ ways. He is alone in it, so it is one of the two single rooms.
\item The $7$ girls fill the other $4$ rooms, three with $2$ girls and one with $1$ girl. Choose which of the $4$ rooms is the other single room: $4$ ways.
\item Distribute the girls: choose the single girl and the pairs for the three double rooms in order, $\dfrac{7!}{2!\,2!\,2!\,1!}=630$ ways.
\end{itemize}
So the number of assignments is $5\cdot4\cdot630=\boxed{12600}$.

The common confusion is the factor $\dfrac{7!}{2!\,2!\,2!}$: the rooms are distinguishable, so the three pairs are \emph{not} divided by $3!$; only the order inside each pair is ignored.
