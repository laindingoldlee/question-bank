For nonzero $f,g$, the leading coefficients multiply to a nonzero leading coefficient, so $\deg(fg)=\deg f+\deg g$. If $f=0$, then $fg=0$ and we need $\deg0=\deg0+\deg g$ for every $g$. No integer $d$ satisfies $d=d+\deg g$ when $\deg g\ge1$. With the convention $-\infty+k=-\infty$, the rule holds. The convention also keeps $\deg(f+g)\le\max(\deg f,\deg g)$ valid. $\blacksquare$
