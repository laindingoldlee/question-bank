Every element of $M\cup N$ is a multiple of $6$ or of $35$, and both $6$ and $35$ divide $210$. So membership in $M\cup N$, and divisibility by $21$ (also a divisor of $210$), depend only on $k$ modulo $210$.

In each block of $210$ consecutive positive integers, by inclusion and exclusion, the number of elements of $M\cup N$ is
\[
\dfrac{210}{6}+\dfrac{210}{35}-\dfrac{210}{210}=35+6-1=40 .
\]
An element of $M\cup N$ is divisible by $21$ exactly when it is divisible by $\operatorname{lcm}(6,21)=42$ or by $\operatorname{lcm}(35,21)=105$ (and $\operatorname{lcm}(42,105)=210$). In each block there are
\[
\dfrac{210}{42}+\dfrac{210}{105}-\dfrac{210}{210}=5+2-1=6
\]
such numbers. For $X$ a multiple of $210$ the probability is exactly $\dfrac{6}{40}$, and for other $X$ the counts differ from these proportions by at most a bounded amount, so the limit is
\[
\dfrac{6}{40}=\boxed{\dfrac{3}{20}}.
\]
