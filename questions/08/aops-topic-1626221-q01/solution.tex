\textbf{(a)} By the Pythagorean theorem the hypotenuse is $\sqrt{1^2+1^2}=\sqrt2$, so the sides are in the ratio $1:1:\sqrt2$.

\textbf{(b)} By the triangle inequality, each side of a triangle is shorter than the sum of the other two. Sides $1,1,2$ would give $2=1+1$, not $2<1+1$: the two short sides would lie flat along the long one. So no triangle has sides in the ratio $1:1:2$.

The error is the assumption that sides are proportional to the opposite angles. They are not: doubling an angle does not double the opposite side. In any triangle the larger side is opposite the larger angle, but the exact relation is the law of sines, $\dfrac{a}{\sin A}=\dfrac{b}{\sin B}=\dfrac{c}{\sin C}$, so the sides are proportional to the \emph{sines} of the angles: $\sin45^\circ:\sin45^\circ:\sin90^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}:\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}:1=1:1:\sqrt2$.
