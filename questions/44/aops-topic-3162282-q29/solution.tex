Since $ m^2+n^2\ge2mn $, we have $ k>2 $, hence $ k\ge3 $. Choose a solution for a fixed $ k $ minimizing $ m+n $, and assume $ m\ge n $. If $ n\ge2 $, the quadratic $ F(t)=t^2-knt+n^2+k $ has root $ m $ and the other integral root $ m'=kn-m $. Its product of roots is positive, so $ m'>0 $. Moreover $ F(0)>0 $ while $ F(n)=(2-k)n^2+k<0 $. Thus the smaller root lies strictly between 0 and $ n $, and $ m $ is the larger root. Replacing $ m $ by $ m'$ contradicts minimality. Therefore $ n=1 $. Now $ k=\dfrac{m^2+1}{m-1}=m+1+\dfrac{2}{m-1}$, so $ m-1 $ is 1 or 2, and both yield $ k=5 $. This value occurs at $(m,n)=(2,1)$, so the requested sum is $\boxed5 $.
