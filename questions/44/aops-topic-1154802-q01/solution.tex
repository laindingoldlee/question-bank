(a) Write $z=a+bi$ with $a,b$ real. Then $z^2=a^2-b^2+2abi=i$ means $a^2-b^2=0$ and $2ab=1$. The first gives $b=\pm a$, and $2ab=1>0$ forces $b=a$ with $2a^2=1$, so $a=b=\pm\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$:
\[
\boxed{z=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}+\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}i\quad\text{or}\quad z=-\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}-\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}i}.
\]
Check: $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}(1+i)\right)^2=\dfrac{1}{2}(1+2i-1)=i$.

(b) In polar form $i=\cos90^\circ+i\sin90^\circ$. If $z=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)$, De Moivre's theorem gives $z^3=r^3(\cos3\theta+i\sin3\theta)$, so $r=1$ and $3\theta=90^\circ+360^\circ k$, i.e.\ $\theta=30^\circ,150^\circ,270^\circ$:
\[
\boxed{z=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}i,\quad z=-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}i,\quad z=-i}.
\]
Check: $(-i)^3=-i^3=i$, and $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2} i\right)^3=\cos90^\circ+i\sin90^\circ=i$.

(In the same way, the square roots in (a) are $\cos45^\circ+i\sin45^\circ$ and $\cos225^\circ+i\sin225^\circ$.)
