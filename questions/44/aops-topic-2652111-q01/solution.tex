Let $K=\left\lfloor\dfrac{b-1}{c-b}\right\rfloor\ge0$, and note that $\left\lfloor\dfrac{c-1}{c-b}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\dfrac{b-1}{c-b}+1\right\rfloor=K+1$. So we must show that the largest such $a$ is $(K+1)b-1$; this number is at least $b-1\ge0$.

\emph{Upper bound.} Negative $a$ are smaller than this, so let $a\ge0$ satisfy the condition and let $k=\left\lfloor\dfrac ab\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\dfrac ac\right\rfloor\ge0$. Then $kc\le a\le(k+1)b-1$, so $kc\le(k+1)b-1$, i.e. $k(c-b)\le b-1$, and $k\le K$. Hence $a\le(k+1)b-1\le(K+1)b-1$.

\emph{Attained.} Let $a=(K+1)b-1$. Then $Kb\le a<(K+1)b$, so $\left\lfloor\dfrac ab\right\rfloor=K$. Also $K(c-b)\le b-1$ gives $Kc\le a$, and $a<(K+1)b<(K+1)c$, so $\left\lfloor\dfrac ac\right\rfloor=K$.

Therefore the maximum is $(K+1)b-1=b\left\lfloor\dfrac{c-1}{c-b}\right\rfloor-1$. $\blacksquare$
