Call a positive integer $n$ with $k$ digits \emph{slippery} if $n=a+b$ for some positive integers $a$ and $b$ (not necessarily different) such that
\[
\dfrac{1}{a}+\dfrac{1}{b}=\dfrac{n}{10^k},
\]
that is, $\dfrac{1}{a}+\dfrac{1}{b}$ is the decimal ``$0.$'' followed by the digits of $n$. For example, $20$ is slippery, because $20=10+10$ and $\dfrac{1}{10}+\dfrac{1}{10}=0.20$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Show that a $k$-digit number $n$ is slippery exactly when $n=a+b$ for positive integers $a$, $b$ with $ab=10^k$.
\item[(b)] Find all slippery numbers less than $1000$.
\item[(c)] Are there infinitely many slippery numbers?
\end{enumerate}
