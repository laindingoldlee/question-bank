Alice, Bob, Clara, David, Eve, Fred, Greg, Harriet and Isaac (four women: Alice, Clara, Eve, Harriet; five men) are on a committee. They need to split into three subcommittees of three people each, the subcommittees not being labelled. If no subcommittee can be all male or all female, how many ways are there to do this?

A student argues: without restrictions there are $\dbinom82\dbinom52=280$ splits; $40$ of them have an all-female subcommittee, $100$ have an all-male one and $40$ have both, so by inclusion-exclusion $280-(40+100-40)=180$ splits are allowed. The answer key says $360$. Who is right?
