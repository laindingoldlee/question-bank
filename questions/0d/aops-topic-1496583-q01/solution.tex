We use $e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta$. In particular $i=e^{\dfrac{i\pi}{2}}$, and the principal logarithm of $i$ is $\log i=\dfrac{i\pi}{2}$.

\textbf{(a)} By definition $i^i=e^{i\log i}=e^{i\cdot\dfrac{i\pi}{2}}$, so
\[
i^i=\boxed{e^{-\dfrac{\pi}{2}}}\approx0.2079 ,
\]
a real number. (Other branches of the logarithm, $\log i=i\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}+2\pi k\right)$, give the values $e^{-\dfrac{\pi}{2}-2\pi k}$, which are also real.)

\textbf{(b)} The principal square root halves the argument:
\[
\sqrt i=e^{\dfrac{i\pi}{4}}=\boxed{\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}+\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\,i}.
\]

\textbf{(c)} Factor out half the angle:
\[
1+\sqrt i=1+e^{\dfrac{i\pi}{4}}=e^{\dfrac{i\pi}{8}}\left(e^{-\dfrac{i\pi}{8}}+e^{\dfrac{i\pi}{8}}\right)=2\cos\dfrac{\pi}{8}\cdot e^{\dfrac{i\pi}{8}}.
\]
Here $2\cos\dfrac{\pi}{8}=\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}>0$, so this is the polar form, with modulus $\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}$ and argument $\dfrac{\pi}{8}$. Its principal square root is
\[
\sqrt{1+\sqrt i}=\boxed{\sqrt[4]{2+\sqrt2}\left(\cos\dfrac{\pi}{16}+i\sin\dfrac{\pi}{16}\right)}\approx1.3332+0.2652\,i .
\]
