Let $a+b=x$, $b+c=y$, $c+a=z$ be the three squares. Since $a,b,c$ are distinct, $x,y,z$ are distinct, and
\[
a=\dfrac{x+z-y}{2},\quad b=\dfrac{x+y-z}{2},\quad c=\dfrac{y+z-x}{2},\qquad a+b+c=\dfrac{x+y+z}{2}.
\]
So we need three distinct squares with an even sum in which each is less than the sum of the other two (so that $a,b,c>0$). Every square is at least $4$, since $a+b\ge1+2$. Let $z$ be the largest.
\begin{itemize}
\item If $z\ge64$, then $x+y>z$ forces $x+y+z>2z\ge128$.
\item $z=49$: we need $49<x+y$ with $x,y\in\{4,9,16,25,36\}$ distinct: $16+36$ (total $101$, odd) or $25+36$ (total $110$).
\item $z=36$: from $\{4,9,16,25\}$ only $16+25>36$, total $77$, odd.
\item $z=25$: $9+16=25$ is not greater, so this fails; $z\le16$: $4+9<16$ fails.
\end{itemize}
So the least admissible even total is $110$, from $25,36,49$, and then $a+b+c=55$. Indeed $a+b=25$, $c+a=36$, $b+c=49$ give $(a,b,c)=(6,19,30)$, which are distinct positive integers. The answer is $\boxed{55}$.
