Divisibility of both powers plus one by five requires $n\equiv2\pmod4$. Divisibility of $2^n+1$ by thirteen requires $n\equiv6\pmod{12}$, which already implies the first condition. Write $n=2k$ with $k$ odd. The elementary lifting identity for odd $k$ gives $v_5(4^k+1)=v_5(9^k+1)=1+v_5(k)$, so the gcd is not divisible by 25 exactly when $5\nmid n$. The progression $6,18,\ldots,1998$ sums to $167334$; its terms divisible by five, $30,90,\ldots,1950$, sum to $32670$. The answer is $\boxed{134664}$.
