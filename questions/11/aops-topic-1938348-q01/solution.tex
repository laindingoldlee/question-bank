\textbf{The final hand is a uniformly random $3$-element subset of $\{20,21,\dots,50\}$.} Condition on the number $j$ of cards numbered at least $20$ in the original hand. By symmetry these $j$ cards form a uniformly random $j$-subset of the $31$ big cards, and the replacements are drawn uniformly from the remaining big cards, so the final hand is a uniformly random $3$-subset of the $31$ big cards. This holds for each $j$, hence overall.

The big cards have tens digits $2$ ($20$--$29$, ten cards), $3$ (ten), $4$ (ten) and $5$ (only $50$). The number of $3$-subsets with three different tens digits is $10^3$ (digits $2,3,4$) plus $\dbinom32\cdot10^2\cdot1=300$ (the card $50$ and two of the digits $2,3,4$), so $1300$ in all. Out of $\dbinom{31}3=4495$ subsets, the probability is
\[
1-\dfrac{1300}{4495}=\dfrac{3195}{4495}=\boxed{\dfrac{639}{899}} .
\]
