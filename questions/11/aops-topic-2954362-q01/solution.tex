Let $k^2-pk=x^2$ with $x\ge1$. Multiply by $4$: $(2k-p)^2-(2x)^2=p^2$, so
\[(2k-p-2x)(2k-p+2x)=p^2.\]
The two factors have the same parity, the second is larger by $4x>0$, and their product is $p^2$.

For $p=2$ the factorisations of $4$ into two same-parity factors are $2\cdot2$ and $(-2)(-2)$, both of which give $x=0$. So there are no solutions.

For odd $p$ the possibilities are $(1,p^2)$ and $(-p^2,-1)$. The first gives $2k-p=\dfrac{p^2+1}2$, i.e. $k=\left(\dfrac{p+1}2\right)^2$. The second gives $k=-\left(\dfrac{p-1}2\right)^2$. In both cases $x=\dfrac{p^2-1}4$, and $(p,p)$ would give $x=0$.

\textbf{Answer:} none if $p=2$; for odd $p$, $\boxed{k=\left(\dfrac{p+1}2\right)^2\text{ or }k=-\left(\dfrac{p-1}2\right)^2}$. For example, $p=3$ gives $k=4$ and $k=-1$, both with $\sqrt{k^2-3k}=2$.
