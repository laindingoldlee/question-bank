Assign each of $n$ people to one of $r+1$ committees, one of which is committee $A$: $(r+1)^n$ ways. Alternatively choose the $k$ people in committee $A$ ($\binom nk$) and assign each of the other $n-k$ people to one of the other $r$ committees ($r^{n-k}$). Summing over $k$ gives the left side. (Equivalently, this is the binomial theorem for $(r+1)^n$.) $\blacksquare$
