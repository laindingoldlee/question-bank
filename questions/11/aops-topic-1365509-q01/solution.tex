Suppose $105=a+(a+1)+\dots+(a+k-1)$ with $k\ge2$ terms and $a\ge1$. The sum is $\dfrac{k(2a+k-1)}{2}$, so
\[
k(2a+k-1)=210 .
\]
The two factors $k$ and $m=2a+k-1$ have opposite parity (their difference $2a-1$ is odd) and $m>k$ (since $a\ge1$). Conversely, any factorisation $210=k\cdot m$ with $2\le k<m$ and $k,m$ of opposite parity gives $a=\dfrac{m-k+1}{2}$, a positive integer.

Since $210=2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7$ has exactly one factor $2$, every factorisation $210=k\cdot m$ has one even and one odd factor. The factorisations with $k<m$ are $k=1,2,3,5,6,7,10,14$; excluding $k=1$ leaves $\boxed{7}$ ways:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
k=2: 52+53, & k=3: 34+35+36,\\
k=5: 19+\dots+23, & k=6: 15+\dots+20,\\
k=7: 12+\dots+18, & k=10: 6+\dots+15,\\
k=14: 1+\dots+14 . &
\end{array}
\]
In general, the number of ways to write $N$ as a sum of two or more consecutive positive integers is the number of odd divisors of $N$ minus $1$; here $105$ has $8$ odd divisors.
