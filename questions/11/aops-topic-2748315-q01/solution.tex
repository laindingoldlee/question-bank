Let $F$ be the point of the road directly below $P$. The angles of elevation increase as the observer approaches, so $A,B,C,F$ lie in this order and $3\theta<90^\circ$, i.e. $\theta<30^\circ$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(7cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real t=20; real h=3;
pair F=(0,0),P=(0,h),C=(-h/Tan(3t),0),B=(-h/Tan(2t),0),A=(-h/Tan(t),0);
draw((A.x-0.5,0)--(0.6,0));draw(P--F,dashed);
draw(A--P);draw(B--P);draw(C--P);
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);dot(P);
label("$A$",A,S);label("$B$",B,S);label("$C$",C,S);label("$F$",F,S);label("$P$",P,N);
draw(arc(A,1.2,0,t));label("$\theta$",A+1.5*dir(t/2),fontsize(8pt));
draw(arc(B,0.8,0,2t));label("$2\theta$",B+1.1*dir(t),fontsize(8pt));
draw(arc(C,0.5,0,3t));label("$3\theta$",C+0.8*dir(1.5t),fontsize(8pt));
\end{asy}

In triangle $ABP$, the exterior angle at $B$ is $\angle PBF=2\theta=\angle PAB+\angle APB=\theta+\angle APB$, so $\angle APB=\theta=\angle PAB$ and $AB=BP$.

In triangle $BPC$, $\angle PBC=2\theta$ and $\angle PCB=180^\circ-3\theta$, so $\angle BPC=\theta$. By the law of sines,
\[\dfrac{BC}{\sin\theta}=\dfrac{BP}{\sin(180^\circ-3\theta)}=\dfrac{BP}{\sin3\theta}.\]
Hence, using $\sin3\theta=3\sin\theta-4\sin^3\theta$,
\[\dfrac{AB}{BC}=\dfrac{BP}{BC}=\dfrac{\sin3\theta}{\sin\theta}=3-4\sin^2\theta.\]
Since $0<\theta<30^\circ$, $\sin^2\theta<\dfrac14$, so $\dfrac{AB}{BC}>3-1=2$, i.e. $AB>2BC$. $\blacksquare$
