Triangles $ABC$ and $ADC$ share $AC$, and $\angle BAC=\angle DAC$, $\angle BCA=\angle DCA$ (the bisected angles). By ASA they are congruent, so $AB=AD$ and $CB=CD$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
path ra(pair P,pair u,pair v,real s=0.2){return P+s*unit(u)--P+s*unit(u)+s*unit(v)--P+s*unit(v);}
pair A=(0,0),C=(6,0),B=(2,2),D=(2,-2);
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(0.9)); draw(A--C,blue); draw(B--D,red);
label("$A$",A,W); label("$B$",B,N); label("$C$",C,E); label("$D$",D,S); draw(ra((2,0),C-(2,0),B-(2,0),0.25));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Then $A$ and $C$ are both equidistant from $B$ and $D$, so both lie on the perpendicular bisector of $BD$; that line is $AC$. Hence $AC\perp BD$ (and $AC$ bisects $BD$): $ABCD$ is a kite. $\blacksquare$ (The hypothesis ``not a parallelogram'' is not needed; if it were a parallelogram it would be a rhombus, whose diagonals are also perpendicular.)
