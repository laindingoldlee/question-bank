Write $N=\overline{ABCD}$; then $D\cdot N=\overline{DCBA}$ is a four-digit number.

\textbf{$D\ge2$.} $D\ne0$, since the product is a four-digit number, and $D\ne1$, since $1\cdot N=\overline{DCBA}$ would force $A=D$.

\textbf{$A=1$.} Since $N\ge1000A$, we get $\overline{DCBA}=D\cdot N\ge1000AD$. But $\overline{DCBA}<1000(D+1)$, so $AD<D+1$, i.e.\ $D(A-1)<1$, which forces $A=1$.

\textbf{$D=9$.} The units digit of $D\cdot N$ is the units digit of $D\cdot D$, and it must be $A=1$. Among $D=2,\dots,9$, only $D=9$ has $D^2=81$ ending in $1$.

\textbf{$B$ and $C$.} Now $9\cdot\overline{1BC9}=\overline{9CB1}$:
\[
9(1009+100B+10C)=9001+100C+10B
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 9081+900B+90C=9001+100C+10B
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 8+89B=C .
\]
Since $C\le9$, $B=0$ and $C=8$.

So $\overline{ABCD}=1089$, and the product is
\[
1089\times9=\boxed{9801}.
\]
