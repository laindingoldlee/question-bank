Multiplying by $23!$,
\[
n=\sum_{k=1}^{23}\dfrac{23!}{k}.
\]
\textbf{Most terms vanish.} The prime $13$ divides $23!$ exactly once (as the factor $13$ itself; $26>23$). For $k\ne13$ the factor $13$ survives in $\dfrac{23!}{k}$, so these terms are divisible by $13$. Hence
\[
n\equiv\dfrac{23!}{13}=12!\cdot(14\cdot15\cdots23)\pmod{13}.
\]

\textbf{The remaining term.} By Wilson's theorem, $12!\equiv-1\pmod{13}$. Also $14,15,\dots,23\equiv1,2,\dots,10$, so $14\cdot15\cdots23\equiv10!$. From $12!=10!\cdot11\cdot12\equiv10!\cdot(-2)(-1)=2\cdot10!$ we get $2\cdot10!\equiv-1\equiv12$, so $10!\equiv6\pmod{13}$. Therefore
\[
n\equiv(-1)\cdot6=-6\equiv\boxed{7}\pmod{13}.
\]
