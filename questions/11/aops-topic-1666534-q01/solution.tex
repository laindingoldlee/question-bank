The angles of a quadrilateral add up to $360^\circ$. Write them as $x,2x,3x,4x$. Then $10x=360^\circ$, so $x=36^\circ$, and the angles are $36^\circ,72^\circ,108^\circ,144^\circ$. The smallest is $\boxed{36^\circ}$.

Such a quadrilateral exists. For example, with the angles in this order around $ABCD$, the angles at $A$ and $D$ add to $180^\circ$, and so do those at $B$ and $C$, so $AB\parallel DC$ and $ABCD$ is a trapezoid:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(5,0),D=3*dir(36);
pair C=extension(B,B+dir(108),D,D+(1,0));
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(arc(A,0.6,0,36),red);draw(arc(B,0.45,108,180),red);draw(arc(C,0.45,180,288),red);draw(arc(D,0.45,216,360),red);
label("$36^\circ$",A+0.95*dir(18),red+fontsize(9pt));label("$72^\circ$",B+0.85*dir(144),red+fontsize(9pt));
label("$108^\circ$",C+0.78*dir(234),red+fontsize(9pt));label("$144^\circ$",D+0.72*dir(295),red+fontsize(9pt));
label("$A$",A,SW);label("$B$",B,SE);label("$C$",C,NE);label("$D$",D,NW);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}
