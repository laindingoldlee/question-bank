Put $t=xy$ and $S_n=x^n+y^n$. Then $S_0=2,S_1=1$ and $S_n=S_{n-1}-tS_{n-2}$. In particular the leading terms of $S_{20}$ are $2t^{10}-100t^9$; these follow by induction, or from
\[
S_n=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor \dfrac{n}{2}\rfloor}(-1)^j\dfrac{n}{n-j}\binom{n-j}{j}t^j.
\]
The equation $S_{20}=20$ has ten roots counted with multiplicity, whose sum is $50$.

They are all distinct. At $t=\dfrac{1}{4}$, $x=y=\dfrac{1}{2}$ does not satisfy the equation. At any other repeated root, differentiating with $x+y=1$ gives $x^{19}=y^{19}$. Thus $\dfrac{x}{y}=\zeta$ is a nineteenth root of unity other than one, and $S_{20}=y^{19}(x+y)=y^{19}=20$. Hence $(1+\zeta)^{19}=\dfrac{1}{20}$, impossible: a root of unity is an algebraic integer, so the left side is an algebraic integer, whereas a rational algebraic integer must be an integer. (The latter follows by clearing a reduced denominator in a monic integer equation.)
Each root $t$ gives a pair $x,y$ as roots of $u^2-u+t$, and $x^2+y^2=1-2t$. Therefore the requested sum is $\boxed{10-2\cdot50=-90}$.
