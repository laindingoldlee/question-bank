\textbf{$29$ is impossible.} If $29=6x+7y$ with $x,y\ge0$, then $y\le4$, and $29-7y\in\{29,22,15,8,1\}$ must be a multiple of $6$; none is.

\textbf{Every number from $30$ on is possible.} The six consecutive numbers
\[
30=6\cdot5,\quad31=6\cdot4+7,\quad32=6\cdot3+7\cdot2,\quad33=6\cdot2+7\cdot3,\quad34=6+7\cdot4,\quad35=7\cdot5
\]
can be bought, and adding containers of $6$ then gives every larger number (each $n\ge36$ is $6$ more than a number $6$ smaller).

So the answer is $\boxed{29}$. This agrees with the general formula $mn-m-n=6\cdot7-6-7$ for coprime container sizes $m$ and $n$ (the Chicken McNugget theorem).
