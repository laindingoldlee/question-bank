Read the digits of a positive integer from left to right. Each step between two adjacent digits is \emph{up} (the next digit is larger), \emph{down} (the next digit is smaller) or \emph{level} (the two digits are equal). Delete the level steps and look at the remaining sequence of ups and downs. The number is called \emph{chaotic} if this sequence changes from up to down or from down to up at least twice, that is, if it consists of at least three blocks of consecutive equal steps. For example, $3725$ (up, down, up), $37725$ (up, level, down, up, which becomes up, down, up) and $2562178$ (up, up, down, down, up, up) are chaotic, while $78953$ (up, up, down, down) and $55267$ (level, down, up, up, which becomes down, up, up) are not. How many $6$-digit numbers are chaotic?
