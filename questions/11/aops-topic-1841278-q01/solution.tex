Let the integers be $n-1$, $n$, $n+1$. Their squares add up to
\[
(n-1)^2+n^2+(n+1)^2=3n^2+2 .
\]
Work modulo $9$. The square $n^2$ is $\equiv0$ or $1\pmod3$, so $3n^2\equiv0$ or $3\pmod9$, and $3n^2+2\equiv2$ or $5\pmod9$.

On the other hand, every integer is $\equiv0,\pm1,\pm2,\pm3,\pm4\pmod9$, and the cubes of these are
\[
0,\ \pm1,\ \pm8,\ \pm27,\ \pm64\equiv0,\ \pm1,\ \mp1,\ 0,\ \pm1\pmod9,
\]
so a cube is always $\equiv0$, $1$ or $8\pmod9$. Since $2$ and $5$ are not among these, $3n^2+2$ is never a cube. $\blacksquare$
