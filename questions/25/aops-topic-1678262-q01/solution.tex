The wrong statements are ``$N$ is divisible by $m$'' and ``$N$ is divisible by $m+1$'' for some $2\le m\le30$; every other number from $2$ to $31$ divides $N$.

\textbf{Both wrong numbers exceed $15$.} If $j\le15$ is one of $m,m+1$, then $2j\le31$ is on the list and is different from $m$ and $m+1$ (as $2j\ge j+2$ for $j\ge2$). So $2j$ divides $N$, hence $j$ divides $N$, a contradiction. Thus $m\ge16$.

\textbf{Both wrong numbers are prime powers.} If $j\in\{m,m+1\}$ is not a prime power, write $j=uv$ with $\gcd(u,v)=1$ and $1<u,v<j$. Then $u,v\le15$ are on the list and differ from $m,m+1$ (both of which are at least $16$), so $u\mid N$ and $v\mid N$, and since they are coprime, $j=uv\mid N$, a contradiction.

The prime powers from $16$ to $31$ are $16,17,19,23,25,27,29,31$, and the only two consecutive ones are $16$ and $17$. So $m=16$.

\textbf{This really happens.} Let $N$ be the least common multiple of all the numbers from $2$ to $31$ except $16$ and $17$, that is $N=2^3\cdot3^3\cdot5^2\cdot7\cdot11\cdot13\cdot19\cdot23\cdot29\cdot31$. Every listed number other than $16$ and $17$ divides $N$, while $16\nmid N$ and $17\nmid N$.

The numbers $16$ and $17$ were announced by the $\boxed{15\text{th and }16\text{th students}}$.
