A \emph{magic square} of order $n$ is an $n\times n$ array containing each of the numbers $1,2,\dots,n^2$ exactly once, in which every row, every column and both main diagonals have the same sum.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Show that this common sum must be $\dfrac{n\left(n^2+1\right)}{2}$.
\item[(b)] Let $n$ be odd. Number the rows and columns $0,1,\dots,n-1$, and for an integer $m$ let $r(m)$ be the remainder of $m$ on division by $n$. Prove that putting
\[A(i,j)=n\cdot r\!\left(i+j+\dfrac{n+1}{2}\right)+r\!\left(i-j+\dfrac{n-1}{2}\right)+1\]
in row $i$ and column $j$ gives a magic square.
\item[(c)] Write out the magic square that (b) gives for $n=7$.
\end{enumerate}
