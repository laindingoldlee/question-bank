By symmetry assume $k\le n\le m$. Then $\dfrac{3}{k}>\dfrac{19}{20}$, so $k\le3$, and $k\ne1$ since $\dfrac{1}{1}>1$.

\textbf{$k=3$.} Then $\dfrac{1}{n}+\dfrac{1}{m}>\dfrac{19}{20}-\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{37}{60}$ with $3\le n\le m$, so $\dfrac{2}{n}>\dfrac{37}{60}$ and $n=3$. Then $\dfrac{1}{m}$ must lie strictly between $\dfrac{37}{60}-\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{17}{60}$ and $1-\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{1}{3}$, i.e.\ $3<m<\dfrac{60}{17}<4$: impossible.

\textbf{$k=2$.} Then $\dfrac{9}{20}<\dfrac{1}{n}+\dfrac{1}{m}<\dfrac{1}{2}$ with $2\le n\le m$. Here $n\ge3$ (if $n=2$ the sum exceeds $\dfrac{1}{2}$), and $\dfrac{2}{n}>\dfrac{9}{20}$ gives $n\le4$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $n=3$: $\dfrac{7}{60}<\dfrac{1}{m}<\dfrac{1}{6}$, so $6<m<\dfrac{60}{7}$, i.e.\ $m=7$ or $m=8$. Check: $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{7}=\dfrac{41}{42}$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{8}=\dfrac{23}{24}$, both between $\dfrac{19}{20}$ and $1$.
\item $n=4$: $\dfrac{1}{5}<\dfrac{1}{m}<\dfrac{1}{4}$, i.e.\ $4<m<5$: impossible.
\end{itemize}
So the solutions are the permutations of $(2,3,7)$ and $(2,3,8)$, and the possible values of $k+n+m$ are $\boxed{12\text{ and }13}$.
