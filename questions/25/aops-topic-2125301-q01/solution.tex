Apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the vectors
\[\left(\sqrt a,\ \sqrt b,\ \sqrt c,\ \sqrt d\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt a},\ \dfrac{1}{\sqrt b},\ \dfrac{2}{\sqrt c},\ \dfrac{4}{\sqrt d}\right):\]
\[(a+b+c+d)\left(\dfrac{1}{a}+\dfrac{1}{b}+\dfrac{4}{c}+\dfrac{16}{d}\right)\ge\left(1+1+2+4\right)^2=64 .\]
Dividing by $a+b+c+d>0$ gives the inequality. $\blacksquare$

Equality in Cauchy-Schwarz holds exactly when the two vectors are proportional, that is, when $\sqrt a=\dfrac{\lambda}{\sqrt a}$, $\sqrt b=\dfrac{\lambda}{\sqrt b}$, $\sqrt c=\dfrac{2\lambda}{\sqrt c}$, $\sqrt d=\dfrac{4\lambda}{\sqrt d}$ for some $\lambda>0$; so $a=b=\lambda$, $c=2\lambda$, $d=4\lambda$. Equality holds exactly when $\boxed{a:b:c:d=1:1:2:4}$. (Check: $a=b=1$, $c=2$, $d=4$ give $1+1+2+4=8=\dfrac{64}{8}$.)

(This is the Engel form of Cauchy-Schwarz, $\sum\dfrac{u_i^2}{v_i}\ge\dfrac{\left(\sum u_i\right)^2}{\sum v_i}$, with $u=(1,1,2,4)$.)
