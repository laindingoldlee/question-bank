While food has not run out, the weekly stock changes by $+15$ with probability $\dfrac{3}{4}$ or $-35$ with probability $\dfrac{1}{4}$. Its mean increment is $\dfrac{5}{2}>0$, but positive drift alone is not the proof. Define $g(r)=\dfrac{3}{4}r^{15}+\dfrac{1}{4}r^{-35}$. Since $g(1)=1$ and $g'(1)=\dfrac{5}{2}>0$, some $0<r<1$ has $g(r)<1$. If $S_n$ is stock and $\tau$ first indexes a stock below 35, the stopped process $r^{S_{n\wedge\tau}}$ has nonincreasing expectation: before stopping its next conditional expectation is multiplied by $g(r)<1$, and afterwards it stays fixed. It starts at $r^{100}$. On $\tau\le n$, stock is at most 34, so $r^{S_\tau}\ge r^{34}$. Thus $\Pr(\tau\le n)r^{34}\le r^{100}$, giving $\Pr(\tau<\infty)\le r^{66}<1$. There is positive probability food never runs out, and therefore $\boxed{\mathbb E[T]=\infty}$.
