\textbf{(a)} Every pair of points gives one segment, so there are $\dbinom{10}{2}=\boxed{45}$ segments, whatever the arrangement.

\textbf{(b)} If no three points were collinear, each of the $45$ pairs would give a different line. A line containing $4$ of the points is the line of all $\dbinom42=6$ pairs among them, so it is counted $6$ times instead of once: it reduces the count by $5$. The two four-point lines are different lines and they share at most one point, so no pair of points is on both of them. Every other line through two of the points contains exactly two of them. Hence the number of lines is
\[
45-2\cdot5=\boxed{35}.
\]
This holds whether or not the two sets of four share a point: in either case each pair of points lies in at most one of the two sets.
