A sequence with first terms $x_1,x_2$ continues $x_1+x_2,\ x_1+2x_2,\ 2x_1+3x_2,\ 3x_1+5x_2,\ 5x_1+8x_2$, so its seventh term is $5x_1+8x_2$. It is non-decreasing exactly when $x_1\le x_2$.

Let the other sequence start $y_1,y_2$, with $y_1\ne x_1$. Then
\[
5x_1+8x_2=5y_1+8y_2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 5(x_1-y_1)=8(y_2-x_2).
\]
The right side is a multiple of $8$, so $8\mid5(x_1-y_1)$; since $\gcd(5,8)=1$, $8\mid x_1-y_1$. That is why $x_1\equiv y_1\pmod8$.

Say $x_1>y_1$; then $x_1\ge y_1+8\ge8$, and $x_2\ge x_1\ge8$, so $N=5x_1+8x_2\ge5\cdot8+8\cdot8=104$. Equality holds for $x_1=x_2=8$ and $y_1=0$, $y_2=13$: the sequences
\[
8,8,16,24,40,64,104\qquad\text{and}\qquad 0,13,13,26,39,65,104
\]
both work. So the smallest $N$ is $\boxed{104}$, answer $\textbf{(C)}$.
