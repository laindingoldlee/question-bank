A way of feeding is a list $a\ge b\ge c$ of nonnegative integers with $a+b+c=12$ (a partition of $12$ into at most $3$ parts). Count by the smallest part $c$; then $b$ runs from $c$ to $\left\lfloor\dfrac{12-c}{2}\right\rfloor$ and $a=12-b-c$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $c=0$: $b=0,1,\dots,6$: $7$ ways;
\item $c=1$: $b=1,\dots,5$: $5$ ways;
\item $c=2$: $b=2,\dots,5$: $4$ ways;
\item $c=3$: $b=3,4$: $2$ ways;
\item $c=4$: $b=4$: $1$ way (namely $4,4,4$).
\end{itemize}
($c\ge5$ is impossible, since then $a+b+c\ge15$.)

\textbf{(a)} All rows count: $7+5+4+2+1=\boxed{19}$.

\textbf{(b)} Only $c\ge1$: $5+4+2+1=\boxed{12}$.
