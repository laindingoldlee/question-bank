Measure angles in degrees clockwise from the $12$, and let $t$ be the number of hours after 6:00 pm. A correct clock has its hour hand at $180+30t$ and its minute hand at $360t$. The faulty clock starts correct (hour hand at $180$, minute hand at $0$), so its hour hand is at $180+360t$ and its minute hand at $30t$.

It shows the correct time when, modulo $360$,
\[180+360t\equiv180+30t\qquad\text{and}\qquad30t\equiv360t.\]
Both conditions say $330t\equiv0\pmod{360}$, i.e. $330t=360k$ for an integer $k$, so $t=\dfrac{12k}{11}$. The first time after $t=0$ is
\[t=\dfrac{12}{11}\text{ h}=1\text{ h }5\dfrac5{11}\text{ min},\]
so the clock is next correct at $\boxed{7{:}05\dfrac5{11}\text{ pm}}$ (about 7:05:27 pm).
