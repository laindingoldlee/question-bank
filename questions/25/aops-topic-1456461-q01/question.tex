Let $p$ be a prime and let $q=2\cdot3\cdot5\cdots p$ be the product of all primes up to $p$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Prove that the $p-1$ consecutive numbers $q+2,q+3,\dots,q+p$ are all composite.
\item[(b)] Hardy remarks after this argument: ``If Euclid's theorem is true, then $p$ can be as large as we please; and otherwise all numbers from some point on are composite.'' Explain this remark, and deduce that for every positive integer $m$ there are $m$ consecutive composite numbers, whether or not one already knows that there are infinitely many primes.
\end{enumerate}
