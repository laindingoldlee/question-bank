Group the terms in two pairs whose products are constant, and apply the AM-GM inequality $u+v\ge2\sqrt{uv}$ (for $u,v>0$) to each pair:
\[
3x^3+\dfrac{54}{x^3}\ge2\sqrt{3\cdot54}=2\sqrt{162}=18\sqrt2,\qquad 12x+\dfrac{27}{x}\ge2\sqrt{12\cdot27}=2\sqrt{324}=36 .
\]
Adding, and using $\sqrt2>1.41$,
\[
3x^3+12x+\dfrac{27}{x}+\dfrac{54}{x^3}\ge36+18\sqrt2>36+18\cdot1.41=61.38>61 .\qquad\blacksquare
\]

\textbf{Remark (the actual minimum).} The bound $36+18\sqrt2\approx61.456$ is not attained: equality in the first AM-GM needs $x^6=18$, i.e.\ $x\approx1.619$, and in the second $x=\dfrac{3}{2}$, and these differ. To find the true minimum one sets the derivative to zero:
\[
9x^2+12-\dfrac{27}{x^2}-\dfrac{162}{x^4}=0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad3u^3+4u^2-9u-54=0\quad\left(u=x^2\right).
\]
This cubic has no rational root, and its positive root $u\approx2.567$ gives $x\approx1.602$ and a minimum of about $61.546$. So for expressions like this one, AM-GM gives a quick lower bound, but the exact minimum is in general only available from calculus (and may not be a ``nice'' number).
