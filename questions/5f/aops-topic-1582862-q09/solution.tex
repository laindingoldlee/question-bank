In one year Earth travels once around its orbit:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair O=(0,0);
fill(circle(O,0.35),yellow);draw(circle(O,0.35));label("Sun",O,fontsize(7pt));
draw(circle(O,2.4),dashed);
pair e=2.4*dir(150);fill(circle(e,0.15),paleblue);draw(circle(e,0.15));label("Earth",e+0.15*dir(120),dir(120),fontsize(8pt));
draw(0.35*dir(150)--e-0.15*dir(150));label("$1.4947\times10^{11}$ m",(-0.3,1.3),fontsize(8pt));
draw(arc(O,2.75,160,185),Arrow(TeXHead));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

(Not to scale.) The radius is $1.4947\times10^{11}$ m $=1.4947\times10^8$ km, so one revolution is
\[
2\pi\cdot1.4947\times10^8\approx9.39147\times10^8\text{ km}.
\]
A year is $365.25\cdot24=8766$ hours, so the speed is
\[
\dfrac{9.39147\times10^8}{8766}\approx107{,}135\text{ km per hour}\approx\boxed{107{,}100\text{ km per hour}}.
\]
(Your own motion due to Earth's rotation, at most about $1700$ km per hour, changes the answer only slightly and is ignored.)
