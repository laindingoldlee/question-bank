A prime $p\ge5$ is not divisible by $2$ or $3$. Residues $0,2,4$ modulo $6$ are even and residue $3$ is divisible by $3$, so $p\equiv1$ or $5\pmod6$.

The conjecture fails for the consecutive primes $31$ and $37$ (there is no prime between them): $31=5\cdot6+1$ and $37=6\cdot6+1$, so both are $\equiv1\pmod6$. (The first such pair; others include $97$ and $103$.) So two consecutive primes can both be $\equiv1\pmod6$.
