\textbf{Coordinates.} Place $D=(0,0)$, $B=(-1,0)$, $C=(1,0)$ and $A=(p,q)$ with $q>0$. Let $c=AB$, $b=AC$ and $t=AE=AF$, where $0\le t<\min(b,c)$ because $E\ne B$, $F\ne C$. With $s=\dfrac {t}{c}$ and $r=\dfrac {t}{b}$ (both in $[0,1)$),
\[
E=A+s(B-A)=\big((1-s)p-s,\ (1-s)q\big),\qquad F=A+r(C-A)=\big((1-r)p+r,\ (1-r)q\big).
\]
Both points lie above $BC$.

\textbf{The angle condition.} For a point $X=(u,v)$ with $v>0$, the angle between $DX$ and the ray $DC$ (the positive $x$-axis) is determined by its cotangent $\dfrac {u}{v}$, and the angle between $DX$ and the ray $DB$ by $-\dfrac {u}{v}$. So $\angle EDB=\angle FDC$ means
\[
-\dfrac{(1-s)p-s}{(1-s)q}=\dfrac{(1-r)p+r}{(1-r)q}.
\]
Multiply by $(1-s)(1-r)q$:
\[
s(1-r)-p(1-s)(1-r)=p(1-s)(1-r)+r(1-s),
\]
which simplifies to
\[
s-r=2p\,(1-s)(1-r).\tag{$*$}
\]

\textbf{Conclusion.} Since $c^2-b^2=(p+1)^2-(p-1)^2=4p$, we have $c>b\iff p>0$. Suppose $p>0$. Then $c>b$; if $t>0$ this gives $s<r$, and if $t=0$ then $s=r=0$. Either way the left side of $(*)$ is at most $0$, while the right side is positive, a contradiction. In the same way $p<0$ (so $b>c$ and $s\ge r$) makes the left side at least $0$ and the right side negative. Hence $p=0$, that is, $A$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $BC$, and $AB=AC$. $\blacksquare$

(Conversely, in an isosceles triangle with $AE=AF$ the whole figure is symmetric about $AD$, so the two angles are equal. The figure shows this symmetric configuration.)
