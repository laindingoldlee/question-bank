By inclusion-exclusion, $D_n=n!\sum_{k=0}^n\dfrac{(-1)^k}{k!}$, and also $D_n=(n-1)(D_{n-1}+D_{n-2})$ with $D_1=0$, $D_2=1$. (The recurrence: object $1$ goes to position $j$, $n-1$ choices; either object $j$ goes to position $1$, leaving a derangement of $n-2$ objects, or it does not, which is equivalent to a derangement of $n-1$ objects.)
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D_3=2(1+0)=2$ and $D_4=3(2+1)=9$. The conjecture gives $\dfrac{2\cdot2!}{2}=2$ and $\dfrac{3\cdot3!}2=9$. They agree.
\item $D_5=4(9+2)=\boxed{44}$, but the conjecture gives $\dfrac{4\cdot4!}{2}=48$. So the formula is false.
\item $D_6=5(44+9)=\boxed{265}$ (the conjecture would give $300$).
\end{enumerate}
In general $D_n$ is the nearest integer to $\dfrac{n!}{e}$, so for the $26$ letters of the alphabet the answer is about $\dfrac{26!}{e}$, not $\dfrac{25}{2}\cdot25!$.
