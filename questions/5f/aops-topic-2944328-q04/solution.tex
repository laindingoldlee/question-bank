One circle gives $2$ regions. The $k$th circle meets each earlier circle in at most $2$ points, so in at most $2(k-1)$ points. These cut it into at most $2(k-1)$ arcs, each of which splits one region, so it adds at most $2(k-1)$ regions. Hence the number of regions is at most
\[2+\sum_{k=2}^n2(k-1)=n^2-n+2,\]

\end{asy}

and this is achieved when every two circles meet in two points and no three share a point (e.g. equal circles with nearby centres). The answer is $\boxed{n^2-n+2}$ for $n\ge1$.
