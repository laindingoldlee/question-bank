The sequence $0,1,1,\dots,1$ consists of $1996$ numbers: a $0$ followed by $1995$ ones. In one operation, two or more of the numbers (but not all of them) are chosen, and \emph{each} of the chosen numbers is replaced by the arithmetic mean of the chosen numbers. The number of chosen numbers may differ from one operation to the next.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Prove that, by repeating this operation, one can obtain a sequence in which all $1996$ numbers are equal.
\item[(b)] Show that this would be impossible if, instead, only \emph{one} of the chosen numbers were replaced by their mean in each operation.
\end{enumerate}
