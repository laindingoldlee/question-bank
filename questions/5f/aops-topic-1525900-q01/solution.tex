Rewrite the given line as $y=\dfrac{1}{5}x-3$; its slope is $\dfrac{1}{5}$. A perpendicular line has slope equal to the negative reciprocal, $-5$ (the product of the slopes is $-1$). Through $(-2,5)$:
\[
y-5=-5(x+2)\iff \boxed{y=-5x-5}\quad(\text{equivalently }5x+y=-5).
\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
import graph;
real f(real x){return x/5-3;}
real g(real x){return -5*x-5;}
draw((-7,0)--(8,0),Arrow(6));draw((0,-6)--(0,7),Arrow(6));
label("$x$",(8,0),E);label("$y$",(0,7),N);
draw(graph(f,-7,8),linewidth(0.9));
draw(graph(g,-2.4,0.2),blue+linewidth(0.9));
pair P=(-2,5),Q=(-5/13,-40/13);
dot(P);label("$(-2,5)$",P,W);
real s=0.35; pair u=unit((1,1/5)),v=unit((-1,5));
draw(Q+s*u--Q+s*u+s*v--Q+s*v);
label("$x-5y=15$",(6.5,f(6.5)-0.5),S);
label("$y=-5x-5$",(0.3,-5.6),E,blue);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Check: $-5\cdot(-2)-5=5$, and the slopes satisfy $\dfrac{1}{5}\cdot(-5)=-1$.
