Any common prime divides $a_1$, hence is $2$ or $3$. For divisibility by $2$, entries in positions $1,3$ must be even, giving $4\cdot3\cdot4!=288$ permutations. For divisibility by $3$, positions $1,2,4$ must contain $3,6,12$, giving $3!3!=36$. For both conditions, $a_1$ has $2$ choices ($6$ or $12$), positions $2,4$ take the remaining multiples of $3$ in $2$ orders, position $3$ takes either $2$ or $4$, and the last two entries have $2$ orders. The intersection has $16$ permutations. Inclusion--exclusion gives $288+36-16=\boxed{308}$.
