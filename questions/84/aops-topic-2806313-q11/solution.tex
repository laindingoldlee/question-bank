A median and an angle bisector from the same vertex cannot be perpendicular, so suppose the bisector from $A$ is perpendicular to the median $BM$, with $M$ the midpoint of $AC$. In triangle $ABM$ the bisector from $A$ is also an altitude, so the triangle is isosceles: $AB=AM=\dfrac{AC}2$. Thus one side is twice another. Consecutive integers $n,n+1,n+2$ with one double another: $(1,2,3)$ is degenerate, and $(2,3,4)$ works ($4=2\cdot2$). Conversely, for sides $AB=2$, $AC=4$, $BC=3$ the bisector from $A$ is perpendicular to the median from $B$.
\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),C=(4,0),B=(11/8,sqrt(135)/8),M=(2,0);pair u=unit(unit(B)+unit(C));pair F=extension(A,A+u,B,C);
draw(A--B--C--cycle);draw(B--M);draw(A--F,dashed);dot(M);label("$A$",A,SW);label("$B$",B,N);label("$C$",C,SE);label("$M$",M,S);label("$2$",(A+B)/2,NW);label("$3$",(B+C)/2,NE);
\end{asy}

The perimeter is $\boxed{9}$ cm.
