A triangle inscribed in a circle is right-angled exactly when one of its sides is a diameter (Thales' theorem).

If $n$ is odd, no two of the points are diametrically opposite, so there are $\boxed{0}$ right triangles.

If $n$ is even, there are $\dfrac {n}{2}$ diameters with both ends among the points, and each can be completed by any of the other $n-2$ points. Different diameters give different triangles (a right triangle has only one hypotenuse), so there are $\boxed{\dfrac {n}{2}(n-2)}$ right triangles.

usepackage("amsmath");

draw(unitcircle); pair[] P; for(int k=0;k<8;++k){P[k]=dir(45*k); dot(P[k]);}
draw(P[0]--P[4],red); draw(P[0]--P[2]--P[4],blue); draw(P[0]--P[6]--P[4],blue+dashed);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}
