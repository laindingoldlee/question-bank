We have $n\equiv-1\pmod{24}$, so $n\equiv-1\pmod 8$ and $n\equiv2\pmod3$.

\textbf{$n$ is not a square.} Squares are $\equiv0$ or $1\pmod3$, while $n\equiv2\pmod3$. So the divisors of $n$ split into pairs $\left\{d,\dfrac {n}{d}\right\}$ with $d\ne\dfrac {n}{d}$.

\textbf{Each pair sums to a multiple of $24$.} Since $n$ is odd and not divisible by $3$, every divisor $d$ of $n$ is odd and not divisible by $3$. For such $d$, $d^2\equiv1\pmod8$ (odd squares are $1\bmod8$) and $d^2\equiv1\pmod3$, so $d^2\equiv1\pmod{24}$. Let $e=\dfrac {n}{d}$. Then $de=n\equiv-1\pmod{24}$; multiplying by $d$ gives $d^2e\equiv-d$, i.e.\ $e\equiv-d\pmod{24}$. Hence $d+e\equiv0\pmod{24}$.

Adding over all the pairs, the sum of the divisors of $n$ is divisible by $24$. $\blacksquare$ (For example $n=95$: $1+5+19+95=120=24\cdot5$.)
