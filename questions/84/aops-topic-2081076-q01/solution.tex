(a) Combine the right-hand side over a common denominator:
\[
\dfrac{1}{b-a}\cdot\dfrac{(k+b)-(k+a)}{(k+a)(k+b)}=\dfrac{1}{b-a}\cdot\dfrac{b-a}{(k+a)(k+b)}=\dfrac{1}{(k+a)(k+b)}.
\]
(To find such a decomposition in the first place, write \(\dfrac{1}{(k+a)(k+b)}=\dfrac{A}{k+a}+\dfrac{B}{k+b}\), clear denominators to get \(1=A(k+b)+B(k+a)\), and substitute \(k=-a\) and \(k=-b\) to get \(A=\dfrac{1}{b-a}\), \(B=-\dfrac{1}{b-a}\).)

(b) With \(a=0\), \(b=2\): \(\dfrac{1}{k(k+2)}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{k}-\dfrac{1}{k+2}\right)\). The partial sum telescopes:
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{k(k+2)}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1+\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{n+1}-\dfrac{1}{n+2}\right),
\]
which tends to \(\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{3}{2}\) as \(n\to\infty\). The sum is \(\boxed{\dfrac{3}{4}}\).
