\textbf{Reese's.} I get one at the first try with probability $\dfrac1{11}$, or I miss, go back and get one with probability $\dfrac{10}{11}\cdot\dfrac19\cdot\dfrac15=\dfrac{2}{99}$. So
\[P(R)=\dfrac1{11}+\dfrac2{99}=\dfrac{11}{99}=\dfrac19.\]
\textbf{Gummy worms.} In the same way,
\[P(G)=\dfrac1{14}+\dfrac{13}{14}\cdot\dfrac35\cdot\dfrac1{10}=\dfrac{50}{700}+\dfrac{39}{700}=\dfrac{89}{700}.\]
\textbf{Either.} The two events cannot both happen, so their probabilities add:
\[P(R\text{ or }G)=\dfrac19+\dfrac{89}{700}=\dfrac{700+801}{6300}=\boxed{\dfrac{1501}{6300}}\approx0.238.\]
