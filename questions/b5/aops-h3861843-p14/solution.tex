Exactly two rows have a red majority: three would contain at least nine red cells, and one would contain at most $4+1+1+1=7$. Similarly exactly two columns have a red majority. Choose these rows and columns in $\binom42^2=36$ ways. Each red-majority row needs a red cell among the two blue-majority columns. Each such column allows at most one red, so these two red cells form a permutation matrix. Consequently both remaining cells of each red-majority row are red. Likewise the blue-majority-row/red-majority-column block has one red in each row and column, and the final block is all blue. The two mixed blocks each have two choices, giving $36\cdot2\cdot2=\boxed{144}$.
