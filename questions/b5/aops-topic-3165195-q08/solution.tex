The trailing-zero count is the exponent of 5 in the factorial, since factors 2 are more numerous. For $15\le k\le19$, there are exactly three multiples of 5 and none of 25, so $k!$ has exactly three trailing zeroes. Below 15 the count is smaller, while from 20 onward it is at least four. Therefore $m=15,n=19$, and $\boxed{m+n=34}$.
