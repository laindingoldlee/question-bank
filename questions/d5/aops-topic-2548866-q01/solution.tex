Using differences and sums of squares and cubes,
\[x^{12}-1=\left(x^6-1\right)\left(x^6+1\right)=\left(x^3-1\right)\left(x^3+1\right)\left(x^2+1\right)\left(x^4-x^2+1\right),\]
so
\[\boxed{x^{12}-1=(x-1)(x+1)\left(x^2+x+1\right)\left(x^2-x+1\right)\left(x^2+1\right)\left(x^4-x^2+1\right)}.\]
The quadratics have negative discriminants, so they have no real (hence no rational) roots and are irreducible. The quartic has no rational roots ($\pm1$ give $1$). If it factored as $\left(x^2+px+q\right)\left(x^2+rx+s\right)$ with integers, then $qs=1$, $r=-p$ and $q+s-p^2=-1$; with $q=s=\pm1$ this gives $p^2=3$ or $p^2=-1$, impossible. So the factorisation is complete.
