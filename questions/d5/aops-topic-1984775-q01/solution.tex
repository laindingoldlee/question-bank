Take $A=(0,0)$, $B=(5,0)$, $C=(8,4)$, $D=(8,9)$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(5,0),C=(8,4),D=(8,9);
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(1));
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);dot(D);
label("$A(0,0)$",A,SW);label("$B(5,0)$",B,SE);label("$C(8,4)$",C,E);label("$D(8,9)$",D,NE);
label("$5$",(A+B)/2,S);label("$5$",(B+C)/2,SE);label("$5$",(C+D)/2,E);label("$\sqrt{145}$",(D+A)/2,NW);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Then $AB=5$, $BC=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5$, $CD=5$ and $DA=\sqrt{8^2+9^2}=\sqrt{145}$, so three sides are congruent.

\textbf{Convex.} Going around $A\to B\to C\to D$, the cross products of consecutive side vectors are
\[
(5,0)\times(3,4)=20,\quad (3,4)\times(0,5)=15,\quad (0,5)\times(-8,-9)=40,\quad (-8,-9)\times(5,0)=45,
\]
all positive, so every turn is to the left and the quadrilateral is convex.

\textbf{No parallel sides.} $AB$ is horizontal and $CD$ is vertical, so $AB\nparallel CD$. The vectors $\overrightarrow{BC}=(3,4)$ and $\overrightarrow{AD}=(8,9)$ are not parallel since $3\cdot9-4\cdot8=-5\ne0$. So $ABCD$ has no pair of parallel sides and is not a trapezoid, let alone an isosceles one. $\blacksquare$

(The idea: an equilateral ``chain'' $A,B,C,D$ of three equal sides can be bent at $B$ and $C$ by different angles; it is an isosceles trapezoid only when the two bends are equal.)
