Since $x^2-2x-35=(x-7)(x+5)$, multiplying by it shows that the required identity is equivalent to
\[x+7=A(x+5)+B(x-7)\qquad\text{for all }x\ne7,-5 .\]
Both sides are polynomials of degree at most $1$, and they agree at infinitely many values of $x$. Their difference is a polynomial of degree at most $1$ with infinitely many roots, so it is the zero polynomial, and the two sides agree for \emph{every} $x$, including $x=7$ and $x=-5$. So the student's substitution is valid.
\begin{itemize}
\item $x=7$: $14=12A$, so $A=\dfrac{7}{6}$.
\item $x=-5$: $2=-12B$, so $B=-\dfrac{1}{6}$.
\end{itemize}
(Comparing coefficients gives the same: $A+B=1$ and $5A-7B=7$.) Check:
\[\dfrac{7}{6(x-7)}-\dfrac{1}{6(x+5)}=\dfrac{7(x+5)-(x-7)}{6(x-7)(x+5)}=\dfrac{6x+42}{6(x-7)(x+5)}=\dfrac{x+7}{x^2-2x-35}.\]
So
\[\boxed{A=\dfrac{7}{6},\qquad B=-\dfrac{1}{6}}.\]
