For the absolute product to be prime $ p $, the absolute values must be $ 1,1,p $. A positive product has either no negatives or two. All-positive values $ 1,1,p $ cannot be a nonconstant arithmetic progression, and a constant progression has cube product, not prime. With two negatives, the possibilities are $-1,-1,p $ or $-p,-1,1 $. The first has unequal successive gaps; the second is an arithmetic progression exactly when $ p-1=2 $, so $ p=3 $. The only increasing triple is $\boxed{(-3,-1,1)}$, with product 3.
