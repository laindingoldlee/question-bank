Put $y=x^2$: $y^2-5y+10=0$ has discriminant $25-40=-15<0$, so $y=\dfrac{5\pm i\sqrt{15}}{2}$ are two distinct non-real numbers. Each has two distinct square roots, which are negatives of each other, so the equation has four distinct solutions, $\pm\sqrt{y_1}$ and $\pm\sqrt{y_2}$, none of them real (the square of a real number is real). The solutions come in pairs $x,-x$, so their sum is $\boxed{0}$, and there are $\boxed{0}$ real solutions.

(By Vieta's formulas, the sum of the four roots of $x^4+0x^3-5x^2+0x+10$ is the negative of the $x^3$ coefficient, which is again $0$.)
