Set $ g(x)=f(x)-x^2 $. Then $ g(a+b)=g(a)+g(b)$, and $ g(3)=-9 $. Additivity gives $ g(1)=-3 $ and $ g(\dfrac{1}{2})=-\dfrac{3}{2}$. Therefore $ f(\dfrac{1}{2})=\dfrac{1}{4}-\dfrac{3}{2}=\boxed{-\dfrac{5}{4}}$. No continuity assumption is needed for this rational argument.
