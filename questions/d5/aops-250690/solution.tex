The conditions say $n=p^2$ and $n+48=q^2$ for primes $p<q$. Thus $(q-p)(q+p)=48$. If one prime were $2$, the other square would be $52$, impossible. Both are odd, so the two factors are positive even integers. The factor pairs $(2,24),(4,12),(6,8)$ give $(p,q)=(11,13),(4,8),(1,7)$. Only the first pair consists of primes. Hence there is exactly $\boxed1$ solution, $n=121$.
