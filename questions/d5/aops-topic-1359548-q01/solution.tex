Since $15=3\cdot5$ with $\gcd(3,5)=1$, it is enough to show that $3$ and $5$ both divide $10^n+5$.
\begin{itemize}
\item For $n\ge1$, $10^n$ is a multiple of $5$, so $10^n+5$ is too.
\item $10\equiv1\pmod3$, so $10^n+5\equiv1+5=6\equiv0\pmod3$. (Equivalently, the digits of $10^n+5$ are a $1$, then zeros, then a $5$, with digit sum $6$.)
\end{itemize}
Hence $15\mid10^n+5$ for all $n\ge1$. $\blacksquare$

For $n=0$ the claim fails: $10^0+5=6$ is not a multiple of $15$ (it is divisible by $3$ but not by $5$). So the statement needs $n\ge1$: $\boxed{\text{no}}$ for $n=0$.
