(a) The equation \(3a+bc-a^2=2\) contains \(+bc\), but the substitution was made into \(3a-bc-a^2=2\): the sign of \(bc\) was flipped. Done correctly, \(bc=(3-a-c)(3-a-b)=a^2+(ab+bc+ca)-6a-3(b+c)+9=a^2-3a+2\) (using \(b+c=3-a\)). Substituting into \(3a+bc-a^2=2\) gives the identity \(2=2\), so no restriction on \(a\) follows. (Indeed (b) gives \(abc=-\dfrac{2}{3}\), so no ordering of \(1,0,2\) could ever have worked.)

(b) Let \(p_k=a^k+b^k+c^k\) and \(e_1=a+b+c=3\), \(e_2=ab+bc+ca=\dfrac{p_1^2-p_2}{2}=2\), \(e_3=abc\). Newton's identities give
\[
p_3=e_1p_2-e_2p_1+3e_3\implies 7=15-6+3e_3\implies e_3=-\dfrac{2}{3},
\]
and
\[
p_4=e_1p_3-e_2p_2+e_3p_1=21-10-2=\boxed{9}.
\]
