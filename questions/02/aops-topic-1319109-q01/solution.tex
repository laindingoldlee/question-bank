By Bouton's theorem, a position is a loss for the player to move exactly when the nim-sum (binary XOR) of the pile sizes is $0$. Here $1\oplus2\oplus6=001\oplus010\oplus110=101_2=5\ne0$, so Malik can win by moving to a position with nim-sum $0$.

Taking from pile $1$ would need it to become $2\oplus6=4>1$; from pile $2$, it would need to become $1\oplus6=7>2$. From the pile of $6$, it must become $1\oplus2=3$. So the unique winning move is to \textbf{take $3$ chips from the pile of $6$}, leaving $1,2,3$. (From $1,2,3$ every move leaves a position Pattie's opponent can answer to restore nim-sum $0$, and eventually Malik takes the last chip.)
