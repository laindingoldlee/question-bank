By partial fractions,
\[\dfrac{1}{k^2-1}=\dfrac{1}{(k-1)(k+1)}=\dfrac12\left(\dfrac1{k-1}-\dfrac1{k+1}\right).\]
Summing from $k=2$ to $n$, each term $\dfrac1j$ with $3\le j\le n-1$ appears once with $+$ and once with $-$, and the sum telescopes:
\[S_n=\dfrac12\left(1+\dfrac12-\dfrac1n-\dfrac1{n+1}\right)=\boxed{\dfrac34-\dfrac{2n+1}{2n(n+1)}}.\]
(Check: $S_2=\dfrac34-\dfrac5{12}=\dfrac13$ and $S_3=\dfrac34-\dfrac7{24}=\dfrac{11}{24}=\dfrac13+\dfrac18$.)

As $n\to\infty$, $\dfrac{2n+1}{2n(n+1)}\to0$, so the infinite sum is $\boxed{\dfrac34}$.
