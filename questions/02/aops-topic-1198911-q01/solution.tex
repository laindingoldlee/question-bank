Measure wear as a fraction of a tire's life: a mile on a front wheel uses $\dfrac{1}{1000}$ of a tire and a mile on a rear wheel uses $\dfrac{1}{2000}$. Each mile the car uses $2\cdot\dfrac{1}{1000}+2\cdot\dfrac{1}{2000}=\dfrac{3}{1000}$ in total, and the four tires have total life $4$. So the distance is at most $\dfrac{4000}{3}$ miles.

This is achieved by swapping front and rear tires after $\dfrac{2000}{3}$ miles: each tire then spends $\dfrac{2000}{3}$ miles in front and $\dfrac{2000}{3}$ in the rear, using $\dfrac{2}{3}+\dfrac{1}{3}=1$ life. So the answer is $\boxed{\dfrac{4000}{3}\approx1333}$ miles. (Without swapping, the front tires would indeed wear out after $1000$ miles; swapping shares the heavy front wear among all four tires.)
