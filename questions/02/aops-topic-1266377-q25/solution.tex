\emph{At least $8$.} Any $2\times2\times2$ block of unit cubes consists of $8$ cubes, every two of which share a vertex, so they need $8$ different colours.

\emph{$8$ suffice.} Give the cube in position $(x,y,z)$, with $x,y,z\in\{1,2,3\}$, the colour $(x\bmod2,\ y\bmod2,\ z\bmod2)$, one of $8$ colours. Two cubes sharing a vertex differ by at most $1$ in each coordinate and are different cubes, so they differ by exactly $1$ in at least one coordinate, and then that coordinate has different parity. So adjacent cubes always get different colours.

The answer is $\boxed{8}$.
