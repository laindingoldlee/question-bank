The bag weights are $30,60,60,90$ grams. Label the bags $A,B,C,D$ and first weigh $A+B$ against $C+D$.
If unequal, the heavier pair must contain the $90$-gram bag and one $60$-gram bag, while the lighter pair contains the $30$-gram bag and the other $60$-gram bag. Comparing the two bags within each pair identifies the $30$- and $90$-gram bags in two further operations. Inspect one coin from either $60$-gram bag: silver identifies the all-silver bag; bronze or gold identifies the mixture. This is four operations.
If the first weighing balances, one pair consists of the $30$- and $90$-gram bags and the other of the two $60$-gram bags. Compare $A$ with $B$. If unequal, their lighter/heavier identities are known and $C,D$ both weigh $60$. If equal, $A,B$ both weigh $60$ and one additional comparison separates $C,D$. Inspecting one $60$-gram bag as above finishes in at most four operations.
