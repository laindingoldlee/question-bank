The number $3$ may not stand next to $2$ or $4$, so its neighbours can only be $1$ and $5$. Also $1,2$ and $4,5$ may not be neighbours.

\textbf{Case 1: $3$ is not at an end.} Then its two neighbours are $1$ and $5$, forming a block $135$ or $531$, and $2$ and $4$ fill the two remaining places. Since $2$ may not touch $1$ and $4$ may not touch $5$:
\begin{itemize}
\item block in places $1$--$3$: $13524$, $53142$;
\item block in places $3$--$5$: $24135$, $42531$;
\item block in places $2$--$4$: $41352$, $25314$.
\end{itemize}
That gives $6$ arrangements (in each case the placement of $2$ and $4$ is forced, and it works).

\textbf{Case 2: $3$ is at an end.} Suppose $3$ is first; its neighbour is $1$ or $5$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $31\_\,\_\,\_$: the next number is not $2$, so it is $4$ or $5$, giving $31425$ and $31524$.
\item $35\_\,\_\,\_$: the next number is not $4$, so it is $1$ or $2$, giving $35142$ and $35241$.
\end{itemize}
(In each case the last two places are forced.) That gives $4$ arrangements with $3$ first, and by reversing them $4$ with $3$ last.

In total there are $6+4+4=\boxed{14}$ arrangements.
