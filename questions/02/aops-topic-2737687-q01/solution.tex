\textbf{(a)} A square of side $1$ and a rhombus of side $1$ with a $60^\circ$ angle have all four sides equal to $1$ but are not congruent (their angles differ).

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(1,0),C=(1,1),D=(0,1);
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(0.8));
pair P=(1.6,0),Q=(2.6,0),R=Q+dir(60),S=P+dir(60);
draw(P--Q--R--S--cycle,linewidth(0.8));
label("$1$",(A+B)/2,S,fontsize(8pt));label("$1$",(B+C)/2,E,fontsize(8pt));label("$1$",(P+Q)/2,S,fontsize(8pt));label("$1$",(Q+R)/2,E,fontsize(8pt));
\end{asy}

\textbf{(b)} Triangles $ABC$ and $A'B'C'$ are congruent by SAS ($AB=A'B'$, $\angle B=\angle B'$, $BC=B'C'$), so $AC=A'C'$ and $\angle ACB=\angle A'C'B'$. Since the quadrilaterals are convex, $\angle ACD=\angle C-\angle ACB=\angle C'-\angle A'C'B'=\angle A'C'D'$. Then triangles $ACD$ and $A'C'D'$ are congruent by SAS ($AC=A'C'$, $\angle ACD=\angle A'C'D'$, $CD=C'D'$). Placing the two pairs of congruent triangles together shows the quadrilaterals are congruent. $\blacksquare$
