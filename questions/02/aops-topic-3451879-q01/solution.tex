Continuity implies $2=x^2$, so $x=\sqrt2$. For this value $t_1<2$. The map $f(t)=2^{\dfrac{t}{2}}$ is increasing, $f(2)=2$, and $t_2>t_1$. Induction shows $t_n$ increases and stays below $2$, so it converges to $L\in[\sqrt2,2]$. On this interval $g(t)=\log t-\dfrac{t}{2}\log2$ has derivative $g^{\prime}(t)=\dfrac{1}{t}-\dfrac{\log2}{2}>0$, since $\log2<1$. Thus its sole zero in the interval is $2$, and the fixed-point equation forces $L=2$. Hence $\boxed{x=\sqrt2}$.
