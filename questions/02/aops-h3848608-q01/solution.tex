Write $n=\prod p^{e_p}$ in its unique prime factorization. Being a square means every exponent $e_p$ is divisible by $2$; being a cube means every exponent is divisible by $3$. Therefore each is divisible by $\operatorname{lcm}(2,3)=6$. Write $e_p=6f_p$ and put $c=\prod p^{f_p}$. Then $\boxed{n=c^6}$. The case $n=1$ gives $c=1$.
