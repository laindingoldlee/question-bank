Each horizontal row is initially a route from fire to trees, and the five rows are disjoint. Thus at least one firebreak is needed per row; with exactly five, there is one in each. Number the middle columns $1,\ldots,4$, and write $c_r$ for the firebreak column in row $r$.

The condition is exactly $|c_{r+1}-c_r|\le1$. If a gap is at least $2$, fire can move along the row whose break is farther right, cross vertically at a column strictly between the two breaks, and continue right in the other row. Conversely, when all gaps are at most $1$, squares strictly left of their row's break have no side-adjacent route to squares strictly right of the break; the breaks form a barrier.

Let $v_r(j)$ count valid first $r$ rows ending in column $j$. Then $v_1=(1,1,1,1)$ and $v_{r+1}(j)=v_r(j-1)+v_r(j)+v_r(j+1)$, omitting out-of-range terms. The next vectors are
\[
(2,3,3,2),\ (5,8,8,5),\ (13,21,21,13),\ (34,55,55,34).
\]
Hence the answer is $\boxed{178}$.
