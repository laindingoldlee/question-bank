$A$ and $C$ are opposite vertices, so the diagonals meet at the midpoint $M=(3,2)$ of $AC$. The other diagonal $BD$ has the same length, is perpendicular to $AC$, and is bisected by $M$. Since $\overrightarrow{MC}=(2,-1)$, rotating it by $90^\circ$ gives $(1,2)$, so $B$ and $D$ are $M\pm(1,2)$:
\[
\boxed{(4,4)\text{ and }(2,0)}.
\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(1,3),C=(5,1),B=(4,4),D=(2,0),M=(3,2);
draw((-0.5,0)--(6,0),gray,Arrow(5));draw((0,-0.5)--(0,5),gray,Arrow(5));
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(0.9));draw(A--C,dashed);draw(B--D,dashed);
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);dot(D);dot(M);
label("$A(1,3)$",A,dir(95));label("$B(4,4)$",B,N);label("$C(5,1)$",C,E);label("$D(2,0)$",D,dir(-60));label("$M$",M,dir(15));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Going around $A,B,C,D$ in order, either $B=(4,4)$, $D=(2,0)$ or the reverse. (Check: all four sides have length $\sqrt{10}$ and $\overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{BC}=(3,1)\cdot(1,-3)=0$.)
