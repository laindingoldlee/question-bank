$f'(x)=\dfrac{x-2x\ln x}{x^4}=\dfrac{1-2\ln x}{x^3}$, which is positive for $x<\sqrt e$ and negative for $x>\sqrt e$. So $f$ is strictly increasing on $(0,\sqrt e\,]$ and strictly decreasing on $[\sqrt e,\infty)$; on either interval it is injective, so $f(a)=f(b)$ forces $a=b$.

In general it fails: $f(1)=0$, $f(\sqrt e)=\dfrac1{2e}>0$ and $f(x)\to0^+$ as $x\to\infty$, so every value $c\in\left(0,\dfrac1{2e}\right)$ is taken once in $(1,\sqrt e)$ and once in $(\sqrt e,\infty)$. For example $f(1.2)=f(b)$ for some $b\approx2.900$. $\blacksquare$
