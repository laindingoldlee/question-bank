Let the bisector of angle $C$ meet $AB$ at $D'$. By the angle bisector theorem, $\dfrac{BD'}{AD'}=\dfrac{BC}{AC}=\dfrac{BD}{AD}$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(6,0),C=(1.5,3.5);
real t=abs(C-A)/(abs(C-A)+abs(C-B));pair D=A+t*(B-A);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.8));draw(C--D);
dot(D);
label("$A$",A,SW);label("$B$",B,SE);label("$C$",C,N);label("$D$",D,S);
\end{asy}

On segment $AB$ the ratio $\dfrac{BX}{AX}=\dfrac{AB-AX}{AX}=\dfrac{AB}{AX}-1$ is strictly decreasing in $AX$, so exactly one point $X$ of the segment has a given ratio. Hence $D'=D$, and $CD$ is the bisector of angle $C$. $\blacksquare$
