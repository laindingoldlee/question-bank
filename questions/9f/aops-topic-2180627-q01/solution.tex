Use the box three times, with the two people's padlocks overlapping in time:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[1.] Jan puts the ring in the box, locks it with one of \emph{his} padlocks and mails it to Maria. It is locked, so it arrives safely; Maria cannot open it yet.
\item[2.] Maria adds one of \emph{her} padlocks and mails the box back. It now has two padlocks and arrives safely.
\item[3.] Jan removes his own padlock with his key and mails the box to Maria again. It is still locked by Maria's padlock, so it arrives safely.
\item[4.] Maria opens her padlock with her key and takes the ring.
\end{enumerate}
At every stage the box travels locked, and no key is ever mailed, so the ring reaches Maria safely. (Each person only ever removes his or her own padlock, so the order ``lock, lock, unlock, unlock'' is what makes it work; this is the idea behind some ``three-pass'' methods of secret communication.)
