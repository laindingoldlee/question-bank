If $n=\prod p_i^{e_i}$, then $n^3$ has $\prod(3e_i+1)$ divisors. The choice $n=60=2^2\cdot3\cdot5$ gives $7\cdot4\cdot4=112$. For $n<60$, four distinct primes are impossible. Three distinct primes either have all exponents 1, giving exactly 64 divisors, or require at least $2^2\cdot3\cdot5=60$. With two primes, assign the larger exponent $e$ to 2 and the smaller $f$ to 3 to minimize $n$. If $f=1$, the divisor condition requires $e\ge6$, giving $n\ge192$; if $f=2$, it requires $e\ge3$, giving $n\ge72$; if $f\ge3$, then $n\ge216$. With one prime, $3e+1>64$ requires $e\ge22$. Hence no smaller $n$ works and $\boxed{60}$ is minimal.
