\textbf{(a)} Let $u=\log_a x$, so $a^u=x$. Take $\log_b$ of both sides: $\log_b(a^u)=\log_b x$, that is $u\log_b a=\log_b x$. Since $a\ne1$, $\log_b a\ne0$, and dividing gives
\[
\log_a x=u=\dfrac{\log_b x}{\log_b a}.
\]
Nothing in this argument needs $b=10$: any positive base $b\ne1$ works.

\textbf{(b)} With $a=4$ and $b=7$:
\[
\log_4 x=\dfrac{\log_7 x}{\log_7 4}.
\]
Hence
\[
\log_4 7\cdot\log_7 16=\dfrac{\log_7 7}{\log_7 4}\cdot\log_7 16=\dfrac{\log_7 16}{\log_7 4}=\dfrac{2\log_7 4}{\log_7 4}=\boxed{2}.
\]
