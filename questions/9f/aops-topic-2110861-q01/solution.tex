Let $S$ students take Spanish, so $2S$ take French, and let $n$ take neither, so $3n$ take both. By inclusion-exclusion,
\[2S+S-3n+n=100,\qquad\text{so}\qquad 3S=100+2n .\]
Now $3n$ is even exactly when $n$ is even, and $0\le n\le9$. Also $100+2n$ must be divisible by $3$; since $100\equiv1\pmod3$, we need $2n\equiv2$, that is, $n\equiv1\pmod3$, so $n\in\{1,4,7\}$. The only even value is $n=4$, which gives
\[S=\dfrac{108}{3}=\boxed{36}.\]
Check: $72$ take French, $36$ Spanish, $12$ both and $4$ neither; $72+36-12+4=100$, and $12=3\cdot4$ is even.

The other values all break a condition: $S=30$ would need $2n=-10$; $S=34$ gives $n=1$, so $3$ students take both, which is odd; $S=38$ gives $n=7$, so $21$ take both, also odd.
