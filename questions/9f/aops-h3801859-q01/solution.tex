Modulo three, the equation forces $3\mid b$. Write $b=3B$ and divide by three to get $3a^3+90B^3+2c^3=0$, which forces $3\mid c$. Writing $c=3C$ and dividing again yields $a^3+30B^3+18C^3=0$, so $3\mid a$ as well. Since the original equation is homogeneous, dividing all three coordinates by three gives another integer solution. A nonzero solution could therefore be divided by arbitrarily high powers of three, impossible. Only $(0,0,0)$ remains, so the number is $\boxed1$.
