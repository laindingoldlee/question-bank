\textbf{(a)} Take $c=b$ in the first identity and use $b\diamondsuit b=1$:
\[a\diamondsuit1=(a\diamondsuit b)\cdot b\qquad\text{for all nonzero }a,b.\]
With $b=a$ this gives $a\diamondsuit1=(a\diamondsuit a)\cdot a=a$. Substituting back, $(a\diamondsuit b)\cdot b=a$, so $a\diamondsuit b=\dfrac ab$. Conversely, division does satisfy both identities: $\dfrac{a}{\dfrac bc}=\dfrac{ac}{b}=\dfrac ab\cdot c$ and $\dfrac aa=1$. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{(b)} By the first identity, $2016\diamondsuit(6\diamondsuit x)=(2016\diamondsuit6)\cdot x=336x$. So $336x=100$ and $x=\boxed{\dfrac{25}{84}}$.
