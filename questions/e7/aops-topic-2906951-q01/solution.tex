With diagonals meeting at $P$, $[APB]\cdot[CPD]=[BPC]\cdot[DPA]$, since each side equals $\dfrac14PA\cdot PB\cdot PC\cdot PD\sin^2\angle APB$.
\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(4,0),C=(3.2,2.8),D=(0.6,2.2);pair P=extension(A,C,B,D);draw(A--B--C--D--cycle);draw(A--C);draw(B--D);dot(P);
label("$A$",A,SW);label("$B$",B,SE);label("$C$",C,NE);label("$D$",D,NW);label("$P$",P,S);
\end{asy}

So the unknown $t$ times the area opposite it equals the product of the other two. If $t$ is opposite $1$: $t=6$; opposite $2$: $t=\dfrac32$; opposite $3$: $t=\dfrac23$. The sum is $\dfrac{49}6$, so $p+q=\boxed{55}$.
