The last two digits of a number $N$ are determined by $N\bmod100$, and $N\equiv11\pmod{100}$ would imply $N\equiv11\equiv3\pmod4$ (since $4\mid100$). But $13\equiv1\pmod4$, so
\[13^b\equiv1^b=1\pmod4\]
for every $b\ge0$. Hence $13^b\not\equiv11\pmod{100}$: the last two digits of $13^b$ are never $11$. $\blacksquare$
