Try small integer divisors of $21$. At $x=1$: $1+1+2+17-21=0$, so $x-1$ is a factor and synthetic division gives
\[x^4+x^3+2x^2+17x-21=(x-1)(x^3+2x^2+4x+21).\]
At $x=-3$ the cubic is $-27+18-12+21=0$, so $x+3$ is a factor:
\[x^3+2x^2+4x+21=(x+3)(x^2-x+7).\]
The quadratic $x^2-x+7$ has discriminant $1-28=-27<0$, so its roots are complex:
\[x=\dfrac{1\pm\sqrt{-27}}{2}=\dfrac{1\pm3i\sqrt3}{2}.\]
The roots are $\boxed{1,\ -3,\ \dfrac{1+3i\sqrt3}{2},\ \dfrac{1-3i\sqrt3}{2}}$. (The poster's factorization $(x+3)(x-1)(x^2-x+7)$ is correct; a non-real pair of roots is nothing unusual.)
