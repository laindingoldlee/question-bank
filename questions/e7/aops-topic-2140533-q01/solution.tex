Subtracting $x^3+y^2$ and collecting like terms, the identity says
\[(4-b)\,x^2y+(a+7)\,xy^2+(3+d)\,xy=0\qquad\text{for all real }x,y .\]
A polynomial in $x$ and $y$ that vanishes for all real $x,y$ has all coefficients equal to $0$ (fix $y$: a polynomial in $x$ with infinitely many roots is the zero polynomial, so each coefficient, a polynomial in $y$, vanishes for every $y$). Hence $4-b=0$, $a+7=0$ and $3+d=0$:
\[\boxed{a=-7,\quad b=4,\quad d=-3}.\]
