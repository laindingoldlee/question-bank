Write $n=d_k10^k+\cdots+d_110+d_0$ with digits $d_i$. Since $10\equiv1\pmod9$, every power $10^i\equiv1\pmod9$, so
\[n\equiv d_k+\cdots+d_1+d_0\pmod9,\]
i.e. $n$ is congruent to its digit sum modulo $9$.

The digits of $R(n)$ are the digits of $n$ in reverse order, except that zeros at the end of $n$ become leading zeros and disappear (as in $R(450)=54$). Dropping zeros does not change the digit sum, so $n$ and $R(n)$ have the same digit sum $S$. Hence $n\equiv S\equiv R(n)\pmod9$, i.e. $9\mid n-R(n)$, and so $9$ divides $|n-R(n)|$. $\blacksquare$
