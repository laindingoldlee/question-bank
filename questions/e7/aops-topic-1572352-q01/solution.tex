Let the triangle be $ABC$ with base $BC=15$, and let $K$ be one third of its area.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real w=15; pair B=(0,0),C=(w,0),A=(5,11);
pair L1=A+sqrt(1/3)*(B-A),R1=A+sqrt(1/3)*(C-A),L2=A+sqrt(2/3)*(B-A),R2=A+sqrt(2/3)*(C-A);
fill(A--L1--R1--cycle,paleyellow);fill(L1--L2--R2--R1--cycle,palegreen);fill(L2--B--C--R2--cycle,paleblue);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));draw(L1--R1,linewidth(0.8));draw(L2--R2,red+linewidth(0.9));
label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,SW);label("$C$",C,SE);
label("$15$",(B+C)/2,S);label("$x$",L2,W,red);
label("$K$",(A+L1+R1)/3+(0,-0.6));label("$K$",(L1+L2+R1+R2)/4);label("$K$",(L2+B+C+R2)/4);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The parallel closer to the base cuts off, at the top, a triangle similar to $ABC$ (corresponding angles are equal because the lines are parallel). That top triangle consists of the two upper regions, so its area is $2K$, which is $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of the area of $ABC$.

Areas of similar figures are in the ratio of the squares of corresponding lengths. So if this parallel has length $x$,
\[
\left(\dfrac{x}{15}\right)^2=\dfrac{2}{3}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad x=15\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{3}}=15\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt6}{3}=\boxed{5\sqrt6\text{ inches}}\approx12.25\text{ inches}.
\]
(The other parallel has length $15\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{3}}=5\sqrt3$ inches.)
