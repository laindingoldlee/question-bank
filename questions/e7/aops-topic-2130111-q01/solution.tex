\textbf{No.} Take $x^2-3x+2=0$, with roots $1,2$, and $x^2-7x+12=0$, with roots $3,4$. The sum is
\[2x^2-10x+14=0,\quad\text{that is,}\quad x^2-5x+7=0,\]
whose discriminant $25-28=-3$ is negative, so it has no real roots at all, while the proposed roots are real: $\dfrac{1+3}{2}=2$ and $\dfrac{2+4}{2}=3$ (or, pairing the roots the other way, $\dfrac{5}{2}$ and $\dfrac{5}{2}$). Indeed $2^2-5\cdot2+7=1\ne0$ and $\left(\dfrac{5}{2}\right)^2-5\cdot\dfrac{5}{2}+7=\dfrac{3}{4}\ne0$.

\textbf{Why it cannot work in general.} Multiplying the second equation by any nonzero constant $\lambda$ does not change its roots $r_2,s_2$, but it changes the sum equation to $(a_1+\lambda a_2)x^2+(b_1+\lambda b_2)x+(c_1+\lambda c_2)=0$, whose roots depend on $\lambda$. So the roots of the sum equation are not determined by $r_1,s_1,r_2,s_2$ alone, and no formula in the four roots, such as the averages, can always give them.
