\textbf{(a)} A palindrome $h{:}mm$ with one-digit hour $h$ has the form $h\,a\,h$, so the minutes are $10a+h$ with $0\le a\le5$. The number $\overline{hah}$ is divisible by $4$ exactly when its last two digits $\overline{ah}$ are, which needs $h$ even: $h\in\{2,4,6,8\}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $h=2,6$: $\overline{a2}$ or $\overline{a6}$ divisible by $4$ needs $a$ odd: $a=1,3,5$, giving $3$ each.
\item $h=4,8$: $\overline{a4}$ or $\overline{a8}$ divisible by $4$ needs $a$ even: $a=0,2,4$, giving $3$ each.
\end{itemize}
That is $12$ times. Two-digit hours $10,11,12$ give the palindromes $10{:}01$, $11{:}11$, $12{:}21$, which are all odd. So the answer is $\boxed{12}$.

\textbf{(b)} Hours $1$ to $9$ give the same $12$ times. Hour $0$ gives the palindromes $0{:}a0$, i.e. the numbers $0,10,20,30,40,50$; the multiples of $4$ are $0,20,40$, giving $3$ more. Two-digit hours $10$ to $23$ give palindromes $\overline{h_1h_2h_2h_1}$, which end in $h_1\in\{1,2\}$; the number must be even, so $h_1=2$: the hours $20,21,22,23$ give $20{:}02$, $21{:}12$, $22{:}22$, $23{:}32$, of which $2112$ and $2332$ are divisible by $4$. The total is $12+3+2=\boxed{17}$.
