\emph{A value that works.} Since
\[
(2^{40}+2^{19})^2=2^{80}+2\cdot2^{40}\cdot2^{19}+2^{38}=2^{80}+2^{60}+2^{38},
\]
\(n=38\) works.

\emph{Nothing smaller works.} Suppose \(n<60\) and \(2^{60}+2^{80}+2^n\) is a square. Write it as \(2^n M\) with
\[
M=1+2^{a}+2^{a+20},\qquad a=60-n\ge1,
\]
which is odd. Then \(n\) must be even and \(M\) must be an odd square, say \(M=x^2\) with \(x\) odd, \(x>1\). Then
\[
(x-1)(x+1)=2^a(1+2^{20}).
\]
Both factors are even, so the left side is divisible by \(8\) and \(a\ge3\). Put \(x-1=2u\), \(x+1=2(u+1)\); then
\[
u(u+1)=2^{a-2}\,(2^{20}+1).
\]
Of the consecutive integers \(u,u+1\), exactly one is odd, and since they are coprime, the factor \(2^{a-2}\) lies entirely in the even one. Thus the two numbers are \(2^{a-2}d\) and \(e\) with \(de=2^{20}+1=17\cdot61681\) (both prime), so \((d,e)\) is one of
\[
(1,1048577),\ (17,61681),\ (61681,17),\ (1048577,1).
\]
They differ by \(1\):
\begin{itemize}
\item \(d=1\): \(2^{a-2}=1048577\pm1\); only \(1048576=2^{20}\) is a power of \(2\), giving \(a=22\), \(n=38\).
\item \(d=17\): \(17\cdot2^{a-2}=61681\pm1\); neither \(61680\) nor \(61682\) is divisible by \(17\).
\item \(d=61681\) or \(d=1048577\): then \(2^{a-2}d\ge d>e+1\), impossible.
\end{itemize}
So among \(n<60\) the only solution is \(n=38\). Hence the least \(n\) is \(\boxed{38}\). (Other solutions, such as \(n=71\) and \(n=98\), are larger.)
