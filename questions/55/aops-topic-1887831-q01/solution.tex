Let the squares have side $1$; the cross has area $5$, so the square to be formed has side $\sqrt5$. Put the central square at $[0,1]\times[0,1]$ with centre $O=\left(\dfrac{1}{2},\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$.

\textbf{The cuts.} Let $P=\left(0,\dfrac{3}{2}\right)$ and $P'=\left(1,-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$ be the midpoints of the left side of the top arm and of the right side of the bottom arm, and $Q=\left(-\dfrac{1}{2},0\right)$, $Q'=\left(\dfrac{3}{2},1\right)$ the midpoints of the bottom side of the left arm and of the top side of the right arm. Cut along the segments $PP'$ and $QQ'$. Both have midpoint $O$, and $\overrightarrow{PP'}=(1,-2)$, $\overrightarrow{QQ'}=(2,1)$ are perpendicular vectors of length $\sqrt5$. So the cuts cross at right angles at $O$ and divide the cross into four pieces $1,2,3,4$. Each piece has a right angle at $O$ between two straight edges of length $\dfrac{\sqrt5}{2}$, and the quarter turn about $O$, which maps the cross to itself, maps the pieces onto each other, so they are congruent.

\begin{asy}

path p1=O--Qp--(1,1)--(1,2)--(0,2)--P--cycle;
path p2=O--Pp--(1,0)--(2,0)--(2,1)--Qp--cycle;
path p3=O--Q--(0,0)--(0,-1)--(1,-1)--Pp--cycle;

\textbf{The square.} Keep piece $1$ and translate pieces $2$, $3$, $4$ by the vectors $(-1,2)$, $(1,3)$, $(2,1)$. The right-angle corners then land at
\[
O,\quad O+(2,1),\quad O+(1,3),\quad O+(-1,2),
\]
which are the vertices of a square of side $\sqrt5$ (the sides are the perpendicular vectors $(2,1)$ and $(-1,2)$), and each side of this square is made of two half-cuts of length $\dfrac{\sqrt5}{2}$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");

path p1=O--Qp--(1,1)--(1,2)--(0,2)--P--cycle;
path p2=O--Pp--(1,0)--(2,0)--(2,1)--Qp--cycle;

\textbf{Why the pieces fit exactly.} Copies of the cross translated by all vectors $m(2,1)+n(-1,2)$ ($m,n$ integers) tile the plane. The lines through the centres of all these crosses in the directions $(2,1)$ and $(1,-2)$ form a grid of squares of side $\sqrt5$ whose vertices are exactly the centres. Each cross meets only the two grid lines through its own centre: every point of the cross is at distance at most $\dfrac{3.5}{\sqrt5}<\sqrt5$ from each of these two lines, so it cannot reach the parallel grid lines, which are $\sqrt5$ away. So these two lines are our two cuts, and each piece lies inside one grid square, with its right-angle corner at a corner of that square. Hence each grid square consists of exactly one piece from each of the four crosses centred at its corners, and these are translates of pieces $1,2,3,4$. Since the crosses cover the plane without overlap, the four pieces fill the grid square exactly: total area $4\cdot\dfrac{5}{4}=5=\left(\sqrt5\right)^2$.

So the two cuts $PP'$ and $QQ'$ through the midpoints of the four arm edges shown above solve the problem.
