From $x^3=y^2$ with $y>0$ we get $y=x^{\dfrac32}$. Substituting into the first equation,
\[x^{x^{\dfrac32}}=\left(x^{\dfrac32}\right)^x=x^{\dfrac{3x}{2}}.\]
If $x=1$, then $y=1$, and $(1,1)$ is a solution. If $x\ne1$, two powers of the same positive base $x\ne1$ are equal only when the exponents are equal, so
\[x^{\dfrac32}=\dfrac{3x}{2},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad\sqrt x=\dfrac32,\qquad x=\dfrac94,\qquad y=\left(\dfrac32\right)^3=\dfrac{27}{8}.\]
Check: $x^y=\left(\dfrac32\right)^{\dfrac{27}{4}}$ and $y^x=\left(\dfrac32\right)^{3\cdot\dfrac94}=\left(\dfrac32\right)^{\dfrac{27}{4}}$.

The solutions are $\boxed{(x,y)=(1,1)\text{ and }\left(\dfrac94,\dfrac{27}{8}\right)}$.
