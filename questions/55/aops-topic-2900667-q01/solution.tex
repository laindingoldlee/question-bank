Expansion gives $x^3+3x=u^3+v^3+3(uv+1)(u+v)=u^3+v^3$. We need $u^3+v^3=2$ and $u^3v^3=-1$, so $u^3,v^3$ are roots of $t^2-2t-1=0$, namely $1\pm\sqrt2$. Taking real cube roots gives
\[\boxed{x=\sqrt[3]{1+\sqrt2}+\sqrt[3]{1-\sqrt2}}.\]
Their product is indeed the real cube root of $-1$, namely $-1$, verifying the substitution. Finally $x^3+3x-2$ is strictly increasing because its derivative is $3x^2+3>0$, so this real root is unique.
