Let $P$ be the payment and $C$ the change. Removing any payment coin must leave less than $P-C$, so $C$ is smaller than every payment coin. Because two change coins total at least $2$, every payment coin must therefore be $5$ or $10$. If a $5$ is used, change can only be $(1,1),(1,2),(2,2)$, totalling $2,3,4$. Payments $15,20,25$ give prices $13,12,11$; $18,17,16$; and $23,22,21$. If all payment coins are $10$, then change may additionally be $(1,5)$ or $(2,5)$, with totals $6,7$; the valid totals are $2,3,4,6,7$. Payment $30$ yields $28,27,26,24,23$. Taking the union gives $\boxed{11,12,13,16,17,18,21,22,23,24,26,27,28}$.
