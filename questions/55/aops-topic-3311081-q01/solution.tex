Colour edges red for knowing, blue otherwise. We prove for $m\ge n\ge1$ that $2m+n-1$ vertices force a red matching of size $m$ or a blue matching of size $n$. Induct on $n$. When $n=1$, a blue edge works, or all $2m$ vertices form a red clique giving $m$ red pairs.
For $n>1$, a monochromatic complete graph works. Otherwise there is a triangle containing both colours: if no vertex had incident edges of different colours, connectivity would force all edges to have one colour. Remove such a triangle. The remaining $2(m-1)+(n-1)-1$ vertices contain a red $(m-1)$-matching or blue $(n-1)$-matching by induction. Add an edge of that colour from the removed triangle. Thus $55$ vertices suffice.
For a counterexample on $54$ vertices, make a $47$-vertex red clique and colour all other edges blue. Red matchings have at most $23$ edges. Every blue edge touches one of the remaining $7$ vertices, so blue matchings have at most $7$ edges. The minimum is $\boxed{55}$.
