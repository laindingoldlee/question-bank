\textbf{Principle.} Let $P(n)$ be a statement about positive integers $n$. If $P(1)$ is true, and for every $k\ge1$ the truth of $P(k)$ implies the truth of $P(k+1)$, then $P(n)$ is true for every positive integer $n$.

\textbf{Proof.} Let $P(n)$ be the statement $6\mid7^n-1$.
\begin{itemize}
\item Base case: $7^1-1=6$ is divisible by $6$.
\item Inductive step: suppose $6\mid7^k-1$ for some $k\ge1$, say $7^k-1=6m$. Then
\[
7^{k+1}-1=7\cdot7^k-1=7(7^k-1)+6=7\cdot6m+6=6(7m+1),
\]
which is divisible by $6$.
\end{itemize}
By induction, $\boxed{6\mid7^n-1}$ for all positive integers $n$.
