Follow one element $x$. Choosing a subset uniformly at random means each available element is included independently with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$. So
\[
P(x\in A)=\dfrac{1}{2},\qquad P(x\in C)=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{1}{4},\qquad P(x\in B)=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{1}{4},\qquad P(x\in D)=\dfrac{1}{4}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{1}{8} .
\]
Hence $P(x\in E)=\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{1}{8}=\dfrac{3}{8}$, and by linearity of expectation
\[
E\bigl[|E|\bigr]=\dfrac{3}{8}\cdot2016=\boxed{756}.
\]
Solution 1 is correct. Solution 2 lists the five states correctly but wrongly assumes they are equally likely. Their probabilities are
\[
\text{only in }S:\ \dfrac{1}{4},\quad A\setminus C:\ \dfrac{1}{4},\quad B\setminus D:\ \dfrac{1}{8},\quad C:\ \dfrac{1}{4},\quad D:\ \dfrac{1}{8},
\]
which add to $1$; the two states in $E$ have total probability $\dfrac{3}{8}$, not $\dfrac{2}{5}$.
