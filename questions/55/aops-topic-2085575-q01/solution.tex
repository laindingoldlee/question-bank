Let \(S_0=0\) and let \(S_i\) be the number of games played on days \(1,\dots,i\). Since she plays at least one game every day,
\[
0=S_0<S_1<\cdots<S_{30}\le 45.
\]
Consider the \(62\) numbers
\[
S_0,S_1,\dots,S_{30},\qquad S_0+14,S_1+14,\dots,S_{30}+14 .
\]
All of them lie in \(\{0,1,\dots,59\}\), a set of \(60\) integers. The \(S_i\) are distinct, and so are the \(S_i+14\). By the pigeonhole principle two of the \(62\) numbers are equal, and they must be one from each list: \(S_j=S_i+14\) for some \(i,j\). Then \(j>i\), and on days \(i+1,\dots,j\) she plays \(S_j-S_i=14\) games. \(\blacksquare\)
