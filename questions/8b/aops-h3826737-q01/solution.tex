Dividing by $1+k$ and completing the square gives
\[
x^2+\left(y-\dfrac{2k}{1+k}\right)^2
=\dfrac{2(k^2+1)}{(k+1)^2}>0.
\]
Thus each is a genuine circle. The two expressions multiplied by $1,k$ vanish simultaneously when $y=1$ and $x^2=1$, so the fixed points are $\boxed{(-1,1),(1,1)}$.

Substitute $y=x^2$ into the family equation:
\[
(1+k)x^4+(1-3k)x^2+2k-2
=(x^2-1)((1+k)x^2+2-2k).
\]
Tangency occurs exactly when this intersection polynomial has a repeated real root: its derivative is the derivative of the circle equation along the smooth parabola, so a zero derivative means the two tangent directions agree.

At $x=\pm1$, a repeated root requires $k=3$. Any other repeated root must be a repeated root of the second factor, which requires $x=0,k=1$. Both work. Therefore the circles are
\[
\boxed{x^2+(y-1)^2=1}
\]
(tangent at the origin) and
\[
\boxed{x^2+\left(y-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)^2=\dfrac{5}{4}}
\]
(tangent at both fixed points).

\end{asy}
