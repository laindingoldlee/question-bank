The reflection $(x,y)\mapsto(16-x,y)$ sends the triangle $T$ with vertices $(6,5)$, $(8,-3)$, $(9,1)$ to the triangle $T'$ with vertices $(10,5)$, $(8,-3)$, $(7,1)$.

\textbf{Area of each triangle.} By the shoelace formula,
\[
[T]=\dfrac{1}{2}\left|6(-3-1)+8(1-5)+9(5+3)\right|=\dfrac{1}{2}\left|-24-32+72\right|=8,
\]
and $[T']=8$ as well.

\textbf{The kite is the overlap.} The side of $T$ through $(6,5)$ and $(8,-3)$ passes through $(7,1)$, and the side of $T'$ through $(10,5)$ and $(8,-3)$ passes through $(9,1)$. The top side of $T$ (through $(6,5)$ and $(9,1)$) and the top side of $T'$ (through $(10,5)$ and $(7,1)$) meet on the mirror line at $\left(8,\dfrac{7}{3}\right)$, since the first has equation $y=5-\dfrac{4}{3}(x-6)$, which gives $y=\dfrac{7}{3}$ at $x=8$. So the region common to both triangles is the kite with vertices $(8,-3)$, $(9,1)$, $\left(8,\dfrac{7}{3}\right)$, $(7,1)$, shaded in the figure. Its diagonals have lengths $9-7=2$ and $\dfrac{7}{3}+3=\dfrac{16}{3}$, so its area is $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot2\cdot\dfrac{16}{3}=\dfrac{16}{3}$.

\textbf{The union.} The student's kite is the \emph{intersection} $T\cap T'$, not the union. The union consists of both triangles, with the kite counted once:
\[
[T\cup T']=[T]+[T']-[T\cap T']=8+8-\dfrac{16}{3}=\boxed{\textbf{(E) }\dfrac{32}{3}}.
\]
