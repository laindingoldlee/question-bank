(a) Put $u=x^{2k}$ and $v=y^{2k}$, so $x^{2n}+y^{2n}=u^m+v^m$. For odd $m$,
\[
u^m+v^m=(u+v)\left(u^{m-1}-u^{m-2}v+u^{m-3}v^2-\cdots-uv^{m-2}+v^{m-1}\right),
\]
which can be checked by multiplying out (the terms telescope; the last sign is $+$ because $m-1$ is even). Hence
\[
x^{2n}+y^{2n}=\left(x^{2k}+y^{2k}\right)\left(x^{2k(m-1)}-x^{2k(m-2)}y^{2k}+\cdots+y^{2k(m-1)}\right).
\]
(b) For $x^6+y^6$ take $n=3$, $k=1$, $m=3$; for $x^{12}+y^{12}$ take $n=6$, $k=2$, $m=3$:
\[
x^6+y^6=\left(x^2+y^2\right)\left(x^4-x^2y^2+y^4\right),\qquad x^{12}+y^{12}=\left(x^4+y^4\right)\left(x^8-x^4y^4+y^8\right).
\]
(c) Complete the square:
\[
x^4+y^4=\left(x^2+y^2\right)^2-2x^2y^2=\left(x^2+\sqrt2\,xy+y^2\right)\left(x^2-\sqrt2\,xy+y^2\right).
\]
So whether $x^{2n}+y^{2n}$ factors over the integers depends on $n$: it does whenever $n$ has an odd divisor greater than $1$. (When $n$ is a power of $2$, such as $x^2+y^2$ or $x^4+y^4$, there is no factorisation with integer coefficients, although $x^4+y^4$ factors over the reals as in (c).)
