The identity
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{5}\left\lfloor x+\dfrac{j}{6}\right\rfloor=\lfloor6x\rfloor
\]
follows by writing $x=q+r$ with integer $q$ and $0\le r<1$: exactly $\lfloor6r\rfloor$ of the six fractional terms reach the next integer. Hence $A(x)$ takes every nonnegative integer value.

Define $F(k)=k+\lfloor\sqrt k\rfloor+\lceil\sqrt[3]k\rceil$. This is strictly increasing for nonnegative integer $k$, and $F(0)=0$. Since
\[
F(1969)=1969+44+13=2026,\qquad F(1970)=2027,
\]
there are exactly $1969$ positive attained values at most $2026$. The missing count is $\boxed{2026-1969=57}$.
