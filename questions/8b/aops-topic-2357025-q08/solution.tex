Each copy of ``We need zero'' has $10$ letters, one of which is n and one of which is o.

\textbf{Old cake.} $100$ letters, of which $10$ are n and $10$ are o. There are $\dbinom{100}{2}=4950$ pairs, and $10\cdot10=100$ of them consist of one o and one n, so the probability is $\dfrac{100}{4950}=\dfrac{2}{99}$.

\textbf{New cake.} $20$ letters, $10$ n and $10$ o. There are $\dbinom{20}{2}=190$ pairs, $100$ of them with one of each, so the probability is $\dfrac{100}{190}=\dfrac{10}{19}$.

The difference is
\[\dfrac{10}{19}-\dfrac{2}{99}=\dfrac{990-38}{1881}=\boxed{\dfrac{952}{1881}}.\]
