A four-digit number cannot begin with $0$ (then it would be a three-digit number such as $0204=204$), but a $0$ anywhere else is an ordinary digit: $2040$ and $4200$ are genuine four-digit numbers.

So the first digit is $2$ or $4$. The other three places hold the remaining three digits, which are two $0$'s and one non-zero digit; such an arrangement is fixed by choosing which of the $3$ places gets the non-zero digit. That gives $2\cdot3=\boxed{6}$ numbers:
\[
2004,\ 2040,\ 2400,\ 4002,\ 4020,\ 4200 .
\]
(The count ``$2$ choices for the first non-zero digit and $3$ places for the second'' in the post is the same computation: the zeros fill whatever places are left.)
