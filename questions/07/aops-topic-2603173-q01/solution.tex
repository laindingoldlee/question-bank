\textbf{(a)} $f(g(x))=\ln\left(e^x\right)=x$; since $e^x>0$ for every real $x$, the domain is all real numbers. Next, $f(h(x))=\ln\left(x^2\right)=2\ln|x|$, defined when $x^2>0$, i.e. for all $x\ne0$. (Writing $2\ln x$ would wrongly drop the negative values of $x$.)

\textbf{(b)} $f(x)=\ln x$ needs $x>0$, and then $g(f(x))=e^{\ln x}=x$ and $h(g(f(x)))=x^2$. So $x^2=100$ with $x>0$, giving $\boxed{x=10}$.
