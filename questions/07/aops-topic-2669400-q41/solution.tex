Let $p=p_n$. Every prime less than $p$ divides $n$, so $n$ is at least the product of all primes less than $p$.

If $p\ge5$, Bertrand's postulate with $x=\dfrac{p-1}{2}$ and $x=\dfrac{p-1}{4}$ gives primes $q_1\in\left(\dfrac{p-1}2,\,p-1\right]$ and $q_2\in\left(\dfrac{p-1}4,\,\dfrac{p-1}2\right]$, which are distinct and less than $p$. Hence $n\ge q_1q_2>\dfrac{(p-1)^2}{8}$ and
\[\dfrac{p_n}{n}<\dfrac{8p}{(p-1)^2}\qquad(p=p_n\ge5).\]
Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose $M\ge5$ such that $\dfrac{8p}{(p-1)^2}<\varepsilon$ for all $p>M$. For $n>\dfrac{M}{\varepsilon}$: if $p_n\le M$ then $\dfrac{p_n}{n}\le\dfrac Mn<\varepsilon$, and if $p_n>M$ then $\dfrac{p_n}{n}<\dfrac{8p_n}{(p_n-1)^2}<\varepsilon$. So $\dfrac{p_n}{n}\to0$. $\blacksquare$
