Number the positions $0$ (fallen), $1,\dots,1000$ (rungs) and $1001$ (escaped). The walk moves up with probability $p=\dfrac{2}{3}$ and down with $q=\dfrac{1}{3}$. Let $P_k$ be the probability of escaping from position $k$, so $P_0=0$, $P_{1001}=1$ and $P_k=\dfrac{2}{3}P_{k+1}+\dfrac{1}{3}P_{k-1}$. Then $P_{k+1}-P_k=\dfrac{1}{2}(P_k-P_{k-1})$, so the differences form a geometric sequence: $P_k=P_1\left(1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{2^{k-1}}\right)=2P_1\left(1-2^{-k}\right)$. From $P_{1001}=1$, $P_1=\dfrac{1}{2\left(1-2^{-1001}\right)}$. Hence
\[
P_1=\boxed{\dfrac{2^{1000}}{2^{1001}-1}},
\]
just over $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
