Put $x=a$, $y=3b$, $z=-4c$. Then
\[
x+y+z=a+3b-4c=-3,\qquad xy+yz+zx=3ab-12bc-4ac=-6,\qquad xyz=-12abc=8 .
\]
By Vieta's formulas, $x$, $y$, $z$ are the roots of
\[
t^3+3t^2-6t-8=(t-2)(t+1)(t+4)=0,
\]
so $\{x,y,z\}=\{2,-1,-4\}$ in some order. Then $a=x$, $b=\dfrac{y}{3}$, $c=-\dfrac{z}{4}$. Checking the six orders against $a<b<c$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $a=2$: then $b\in\left\{-\dfrac{1}{3},-\dfrac{4}{3}\right\}$, both less than $a$. No.
\item $a=-4$: $(b,c)=\left(-\dfrac{1}{3},-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$ or $\left(\dfrac{2}{3},\dfrac{1}{4}\right)$; in both $b>c$. No.
\item $a=-1$: $(b,c)=\left(-\dfrac{4}{3},-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$ has $b<a$; $(b,c)=\left(\dfrac{2}{3},1\right)$ works.
\end{itemize}
So $(a,b,c)=\left(-1,\dfrac{2}{3},1\right)$ (check: $-1+2-4=-3$, $-4+8+2=6$, $3\cdot(-1)\cdot\dfrac{2}{3}\cdot1=-2$), and
\[
a+b+c=\boxed{\dfrac{2}{3}} .
\]
