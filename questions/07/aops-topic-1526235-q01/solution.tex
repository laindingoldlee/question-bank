Let $P$ be the point of segment $AB$ with $\angle PCB=60^\circ$ (it exists because $\angle ACB=90^\circ>60^\circ$). We show $P=M$; then $CP$ is the segment $CM$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real s=3;
pair C=(0,0),B=(0,s),A=(s*sqrt(3),0),M=(A+B)/2;
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(1));
draw(C--M,blue+linewidth(0.9));
real r=0.2; draw((r,0)--(r,r)--(0,r));
draw(arc(A,0.9,150,180),red);label("$30^\circ$",A+1.25*dir(165),red);
draw(arc(B,0.6,-90,-30),red);label("$60^\circ$",B+0.95*dir(-60),red);
draw(arc(C,0.75,30,90),blue);label("$60^\circ$",C+1.1*dir(60),blue);
label("$A$",A,SE);label("$B$",B,N);label("$C$",C,SW);label("$M$",M,NE);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\textbf{Triangle $PCB$.} Its angles at $C$ and $B$ are both $60^\circ$, so its third angle is $60^\circ$ and it is equilateral: $PB=PC=BC$.

\textbf{Triangle $APC$.} Its angle at $C$ is $\angle ACB-\angle PCB=90^\circ-60^\circ=30^\circ$, equal to its angle at $A$. So it is isosceles with $PA=PC$.

Hence $PA=PC=PB$, so $P$ is the midpoint $M$ of $AB$, and
\[
BC=PB=\dfrac{1}{2}AB .
\]
(Also $CM=\dfrac{1}{2}AB$.) Finally, by the Pythagorean theorem,
\[
AC^2=AB^2-BC^2=AB^2-\dfrac{1}{4}AB^2=\dfrac{3}{4}AB^2,\qquad AC=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}AB .
\]
So the sides are in the ratio $BC:AC:AB=1:\sqrt3:2$. $\blacksquare$
