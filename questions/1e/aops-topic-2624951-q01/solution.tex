Write $(1+r)^k=(1+r)(1+r)\cdots(1+r)$ with $k$ factors. Expanding the product means choosing either $1$ or $r$ from each factor and multiplying the choices. Choosing $r$ from exactly $n$ of the factors gives $r^n$, and this can be done in $\dbinom kn$ ways. Collecting terms,
\[(1+r)^k=\sum_{n=0}^{k}\dbinom kn r^n,\]
for every real $r$ (this is the binomial theorem with $x=1$, $y=r$). $\blacksquare$

\emph{Counting interpretation} (for a positive integer $r$): $(r+1)^k$ counts the strings of length $k$ over an alphabet of $r+1$ symbols, one of which is a blank. Classifying the strings by the number $n$ of non-blank positions gives $\dbinom kn$ choices of positions and $r^n$ fillings, i.e. the left side.
