\textbf{(a)} Expanding, $(px+q)(rx+s)=prx^2+(ps+qr)x+qs$, so $a=pr$, $b=ps+qr$ and $c=qs$. Put $m=ps$ and $n=qr$. Then $m+n=b$ and $mn=pqrs=(pr)(qs)=ac$.

Conversely, writing $ax^2+bx+c=prx^2+psx+qrx+qs$ and grouping the first two and the last two terms gives $px(rx+s)+q(rx+s)=(px+q)(rx+s)$. So to factor, look for two integers with product $ac$ and sum $b$, split the middle term, and factor by grouping.

\textbf{(b)} Here $ac=6\cdot(-10)=-60$ and $b=11$. The pair $15$ and $-4$ has product $-60$ and sum $11$. Then
\[
6x^2+11x-10=6x^2+15x-4x-10=3x(2x+5)-2(2x+5)=\boxed{(3x-2)(2x+5)} .
\]
Check: $(3x-2)(2x+5)=6x^2+15x-4x-10=6x^2+11x-10$.
