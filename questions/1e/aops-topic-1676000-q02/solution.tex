Build up the factorial: $10!=3\,628\,800$, so
\[
11!=39\,916\,800,\qquad 12!=479\,001\,600,\qquad 13!=6\,227\,020\,800 .
\]
The digit sum is $6+2+2+7+0+2+0+8+0+0=\boxed{27}$.

Check: $9\mid13!$, so the digit sum must be a multiple of $9$; and $11\mid13!$, and indeed the alternating sum of the digits from the right, $0-0+8-0+2-0+7-2+2-6=11$, is a multiple of $11$. There is no general formula for digit sums of factorials, so some multiplication is unavoidable; the divisibility rules for $9$ and $11$ are the quick checks.
