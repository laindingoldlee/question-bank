\textbf{The centroid.} $P$ is the intersection of two medians, so it is the centroid of the triangle, which divides each median in the ratio $2:1$ from the vertex. (In the language of mass points: put mass $1$ at each of $A$, $B$, $C$. Then $D$, the midpoint of $BC$, carries mass $2$, and the balance point of $A$ and $D$ divides $AD$ with $AP:PD=2:1$; likewise $E$ carries mass $2$ and $CP:PE=2:1$. The mass at $B$ is \emph{not} split between $BA$ and $BC$: a mass-point system puts one mass at each point, and each cevian is balanced using the full masses at its two ends. Splitting a mass is only needed when a line through the triangle does not pass through a vertex, and no such line is used here.) Hence
\[
AP=2PD=4,\qquad CP=2PE=3,\qquad AD=6,\qquad CE=4.5 .
\]

\textbf{A right angle.} In triangle $PED$ the sides are $1.5$, $2$, $2.5$, and $1.5^2+2^2=6.25=2.5^2$, so by the converse of the Pythagorean theorem $\angle DPE=90^\circ$. Thus the diagonals $AD$ and $CE$ of the quadrilateral $AEDC$ are perpendicular:

\textbf{The area.} A quadrilateral whose diagonals are perpendicular has area half the product of the diagonals (it splits along $AD$ into triangles $AED$ and $ACD$ with heights $PE$ and $PC$ on the common base $AD$). So
\[
[AEDC]=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot AD\cdot CE=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot6\cdot4.5=\boxed{\textbf{(B)}\ 13.5}.
\]
