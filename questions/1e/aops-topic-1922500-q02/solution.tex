\textbf{Bag A.} There are $\dbinom52=10$ pairs of balls, of which $\dbinom32+\dbinom22=3+1=4$ have the same colour: probability $\dfrac{2}{5}$.

\textbf{Bag B.} There are $\dbinom92=36$ pairs, of which $\dbinom62+\dbinom32=15+3=18$ have the same colour: probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

Hence
\[
P(\text{same colour})=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{2}{5}+\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{1}{5}+\dfrac{1}{4}=\boxed{\dfrac{9}{20}} .
\]
The student's fractions $\dfrac{2}{5},\dfrac{3}{5}$ and $\dfrac{1}{3},\dfrac{2}{3}$ are the probabilities that a \emph{single} ball from bag A or bag B is red or white. Adding them over both colours just adds up the probabilities of all outcomes of one draw, which is $1$; the second ball was ignored.
