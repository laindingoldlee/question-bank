Let $p_1,\dots,p_7\ge1$ be the numbers of pills on the seven days, with $p_1+\cdots+p_7=20$. Line up the $20$ pills; there are $19$ gaps between neighbouring pills, and choosing $6$ of these gaps for dividers splits the pills into $7$ nonempty groups, in order. So the number of ways is
\[
\binom{19}{6}=\boxed{27132}.
\]
(Equivalently, give each day one pill first and distribute the remaining $13$ freely: $\binom{13+6}{6}=\binom{19}{6}$.)

The student's count $\binom{26}{6}$ arranges $20$ pills and $6$ dividers freely, which allows dividers to be next to each other or at the ends, that is, days with \emph{no} pills. It counts the solutions with $p_i\ge0$, not $p_i\ge1$.
