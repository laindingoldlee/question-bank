List the elements as $a_1,a_2,\dots,a_9$ and form the partial sums
\[
s_1=a_1,\quad s_2=a_1+a_2,\quad\dots,\quad s_9=a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_9 .
\]
If some $s_k$ is divisible by $9$, take $B=\{a_1,\dots,a_k\}$.

Otherwise the $9$ numbers $s_1,\dots,s_9$ have remainders modulo $9$ in the $8$-element set $\{1,2,\dots,8\}$. By the pigeonhole principle two of them, say $s_i$ and $s_j$ with $i<j$, have the same remainder. Then
\[
s_j-s_i=a_{i+1}+a_{i+2}+\cdots+a_j
\]
is divisible by $9$, so $B=\{a_{i+1},\dots,a_j\}$ (which is nonempty) works. $\blacksquare$
