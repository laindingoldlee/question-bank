For each situation, decide whether the order of the selected objects matters (a permutation-type count) or not (a combination-type count), and find the number of possibilities. (The numbering follows the original list.)
\begin{enumerate}
\item[1.] Determining the top $5$ winners (first to fifth place) in a math olympiad with $n\ge5$ contestants. (Give the answer in terms of $n$.)
\item[3.] Choosing $2$ colours from the $7$ colours of the rainbow.
\item[4.] Choosing $4$ questions to answer out of $5$ questions on a test.
\item[6.] The number of signals that can be sent using at most four clicks, each a dot or a dash, in Morse code.
\item[7.] The number of ways a coach can choose $2$ players from $6$ volleyball players.
\item[9.] Choosing $2$ of your $4$ friends to bring along on a Ferris wheel ride.
\item[10.] The number of $5$-member teams that can be formed from a class of $40$ students.
\end{enumerate}
