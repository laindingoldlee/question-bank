\textbf{$P$ on $AC$.} By the angle bisector theorem $\dfrac{AP}{PC}=\dfrac{AB}{BC}=\dfrac{13}{14}$, so
\[
AP=\dfrac{13}{27}\cdot15=\dfrac{65}{9},\qquad PC=\dfrac{14}{27}\cdot15=\dfrac{70}{9}.
\]

\textbf{$Q$ on $BC$.} The point $C$ lies outside the circle, and the lines $CPA$ and $CQB$ are secants, so by the power of the point $C$,
\[
CQ\cdot CB=CP\cdot CA\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad CQ=\dfrac{\dfrac{70}{9}\cdot15}{14}=\dfrac{25}{3},\qquad BQ=14-\dfrac{25}{3}=\dfrac{17}{3}.
\]
Since $CQ<CB$, $Q$ lies on segment $BC$.

\textbf{$PQ=AP$.} In the circle, the chords $PA$ and $PQ$ subtend the inscribed angles $\angle PBA$ and $\angle PBQ$ at $B$, and these are equal because $BP$ bisects $\angle ABC$. Equal inscribed angles cut off equal chords, so $PQ=PA=\dfrac{65}{9}$.

\textbf{Perimeter.}
\[
AB+BQ+QP+PA=13+\dfrac{17}{3}+\dfrac{65}{9}+\dfrac{65}{9}=\dfrac{117+51+130}{9}=\dfrac{298}{9}=\boxed{33\dfrac{1}{9}}.
\]
