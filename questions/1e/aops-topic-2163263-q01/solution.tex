Factor the denominator as a difference of squares:
\[x^4+x^2+1=\left(x^2+1\right)^2-x^2=\left(x^2-x+1\right)\left(x^2+x+1\right).\]
Since $\left(x^2+x+1\right)-\left(x^2-x+1\right)=2x$,
\[\dfrac{x}{x^4+x^2+1}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{x^2-x+1}-\dfrac{1}{x^2+x+1}\right).\]
Also $(x+1)^2-(x+1)+1=x^2+x+1$, so the sum telescopes:
\[\sum_{x=1}^{n}\dfrac{x}{x^4+x^2+1}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{1}-\dfrac{1}{n^2+n+1}\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{2\left(n^2+n+1\right)}.\]
Every partial sum is less than $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so $t=\dfrac{1}{2}$ works. If $t<\dfrac{1}{2}$, then for $n$ large enough $\dfrac{1}{2(n^2+n+1)}<\dfrac{1}{2}-t$, and the partial sum exceeds $t$. So the smallest such $t$ is $\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}}$.
