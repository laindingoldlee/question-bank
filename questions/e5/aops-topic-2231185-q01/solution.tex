By Fermat's little theorem, $29^p\equiv29\pmod p$ for every prime $p$ (this holds also when $p\mid29$). Hence
\[29^p+1\equiv30\pmod p,\]
so $p\mid29^p+1$ if and only if $p\mid30$, i.e. $p\in\{2,3,5\}$. (Check: $29^2+1=842$ is even, $29^3+1=24\,390$ is divisible by $3$, and $29^5+1=20\,511\,150$ is divisible by $5$.) There are $\boxed{3}$ such primes.
