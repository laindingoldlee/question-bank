If there are $n$ students, standing in equal rows of $d$ students is possible exactly when $d$ is a divisor of $n$. On June $1$ to $12$ twelve different divisors are used, and on June $13$ no unused divisor is left, so $n$ has exactly $12$ positive divisors. Also $15\mid n$ and $6\mid n$, so $30\mid n$.

The multiples of $30$ are $30,60,\dots$, and $30=2\cdot3\cdot5$ has only $2\cdot2\cdot2=8$ divisors, while $60=2^2\cdot3\cdot5$ has $3\cdot2\cdot2=12$ divisors. So the smallest possible number is $\boxed{60}$. (Its divisors $1,2,3,4,5,6,10,12,15,20,30,60$ give the twelve arrangements.)
