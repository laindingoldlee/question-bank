\textbf{(a)} Measure angles clockwise from the $12$. The minute hand turns $360^\circ$ in $60$ minutes, so it is at $6m$ degrees. The hour hand turns $30^\circ$ per hour, i.e. $\dfrac{30}{60}=\dfrac12$ degree per minute, so (writing $h=0$ for $12$ o'clock) it is at $30h+\dfrac m2$ degrees. Both positions lie between $0$ and $360$, so the two angles between the hands are
\[\theta=\left|6m-30h-\dfrac m2\right|=\left|\dfrac{11m}{2}-30h\right|\quad\text{and}\quad360-\theta.\]
The student's formula has $\dfrac m{12}$ instead of $\dfrac m2$, and it lacks the absolute value (it is negative, for example, at $9{:}00$).

At $3{:}30$ the minute hand is at $180^\circ$ and the hour hand halfway between $3$ and $4$, at $105^\circ$; the angles are $75^\circ$ and $285^\circ$, as the correct formula gives: $\left|165-90\right|=75$. The student's formula gives $180-90-2.5=87.5$, which is wrong.

\textbf{(b)} At $3{:}40$: $\theta=\left|\dfrac{11\cdot40}{2}-90\right|=|220-90|=130$. The angles are $\boxed{130^\circ\text{ and }230^\circ}$ (minute hand at $240^\circ$, hour hand at $110^\circ$).
