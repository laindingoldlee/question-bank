Let $c_n$ be the number of such permutations of length $n$. Every permutation has a unique smallest initial segment whose entries are $\{1,\ldots,k\}$; that segment has $c_k$ orders and the remainder has $(n-k)!$ orders. Hence $n!=\sum_{k=1}^n c_k(n-k)!$, so $c_n=n!-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}c_k(n-k)!$. Starting with $c_1=1$ gives $c_2=1,c_3=3,c_4=13,c_5=71$, then $c_6=720-(120+24+18+26+71)=\boxed{461}$.
