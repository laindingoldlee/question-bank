The statement is false. Use Heron's formula in the form $16K^2=p(p-2a)(p-2b)(p-2c)$, where $p$ is the perimeter.

For the triangles with sides $4,11,11$ and $7,7,12$ (both of perimeter $26$):
\[
26\cdot18\cdot4\cdot4=7488\qquad\text{and}\qquad26\cdot12\cdot12\cdot2=7488 .
\]
So both have area $\sqrt{468}=6\sqrt{13}$, but they are not congruent.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair P=(0,0),Q=(4,0); pair R=(2,sqrt(121-4)); draw(P--Q--R--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
label("$4$",(P+Q)/2,S); label("$11$",(Q+R)/2,E); label("$11$",(P+R)/2,W);
pair U=(9,0),V=(21,0),X=(15,sqrt(49-36)); draw(U--V--X--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
label("$12$",(U+V)/2,S); label("$7$",(V+X)/2,NE); label("$7$",(U+X)/2,NW);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

A search over all integer triangles of perimeter less than $26$ (for each perimeter, list the triangles and compare the values of $p(p-2a)(p-2b)(p-2c)$) finds no other coincidence, so the smallest counterexample is the pair $\boxed{(4,11,11)\text{ and }(7,7,12)}$, with perimeter $26$ and area $6\sqrt{13}$.
