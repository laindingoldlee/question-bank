Since $5$ divides $abc$ and $5$ is prime, one of $a,b,c$ equals $5$. The equation is symmetric, so first find the unordered solutions with $a=5$:
\[
5bc=5(5+b+c)\iff bc-b-c=5\iff (b-1)(c-1)=6 .
\]
With $b\le c$, the factorisations $6=1\cdot6=2\cdot3$ give $(b,c)=(2,7)$ or $(3,4)$. The second fails because $4$ is not prime, so $\{b,c\}=\{2,7\}$. (If two of the numbers were $5$, then $25c=5(10+c)$ gives $c=\dfrac{5}{2}$, which is impossible; this case is already covered above anyway, since $(b-1)(c-1)=6$ has no solution with $b=5$ or $c=5$.)

Check: $2\cdot5\cdot7=70=5(2+5+7)$. So the primes are $2,5,7$ in some order, and the ordered solutions are the $\boxed{6}$ permutations of $(2,5,7)$:
\[
(2,5,7),\ (2,7,5),\ (5,2,7),\ (5,7,2),\ (7,2,5),\ (7,5,2).
\]
