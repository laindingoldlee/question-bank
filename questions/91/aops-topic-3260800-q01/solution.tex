(a) Each position has four suit possibilities, so there are $\boxed{16}$ outcomes.
(b) They are not equally likely. For a specified repeated suit, the probability is $\dfrac{13}{52}\dfrac{12}{51}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{17}}$. For a specified ordered pair of distinct suits it is $\dfrac{13}{52}\dfrac{13}{51}=\boxed{\dfrac{13}{204}}$. The four repeated and twelve distinct outcomes sum to one.
(c) Add the four repeated-suit probabilities: $\boxed{\dfrac{4}{17}}$. Equivalently, after the first card, $12$ of the remaining $51$ cards have its suit.
