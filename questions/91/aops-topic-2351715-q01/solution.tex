Let the progressions have first terms $a,a'$ and common differences $d,d'$, with sums $S_n=\dfrac n2\bigl(2a+(n-1)d\bigr)$ and $S'_n=\dfrac n2\bigl(2a'+(n-1)d'\bigr)$. Their $11$th terms are $a+10d$ and $a'+10d'$.

\textbf{Solution 1.} For $n=21$,
\[\dfrac{S_{21}}{S'_{21}}=\dfrac{\dfrac{21}{2}(2a+20d)}{\dfrac{21}{2}(2a'+20d')}=\dfrac{a+10d}{a'+10d'},\]
so the ratio of the $11$th terms is $\dfrac{7\cdot21+1}{4\cdot21+27}=\dfrac{148}{111}=\dfrac43$.

\textbf{Solution 2.} The correct formula is $t_n=S_n-S_{n-1}$ (the first $n$ terms minus the first $n-1$ terms); $S_n-S_{n+1}$ would be $-t_{n+1}$. To use it we need the sums themselves, not only their ratio. Write $L(n)=2a+(n-1)d$ and $M(n)=2a'+(n-1)d'$, linear functions of $n$. The condition says $L(n)(4n+27)=M(n)(7n+1)$ for all positive integers $n$; both sides are polynomials of degree at most $2$, so this is an identity. Putting $n=-\dfrac17$ gives $L\left(-\dfrac17\right)=0$, so $L(n)=\mu(7n+1)$ for a constant $\mu\ne0$, and then $M(n)=\mu(4n+27)$. Hence $S_n=\dfrac\mu2n(7n+1)$ and $S'_n=\dfrac\mu2n(4n+27)$, and
\[\dfrac{t_{11}}{t'_{11}}=\dfrac{11\cdot78-10\cdot71}{11\cdot71-10\cdot67}=\dfrac{858-710}{781-670}=\dfrac{148}{111}=\dfrac43.\]

The ratio of the $11$th terms is $\boxed{4:3}$.
