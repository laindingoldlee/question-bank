Let $\angle B=\beta$ and $\angle C=\gamma$, so $\beta+\gamma=70^\circ$. Since $AB=BD$, triangle $ABD$ is isosceles and $\angle BAD=90^\circ-\dfrac{\beta}{2}$. Similarly $AC=CE$ gives $\angle CAE=90^\circ-\dfrac{\gamma}{2}$. Because $BD+CE=AB+AC>BC$, the order on $BC$ is $B,E,D,C$. Thus the two angles at $A$ overlap in $\angle DAE$, and\[\angle DAE=\angle BAD+\angle CAE-\angle BAC=180^\circ-\dfrac{70^\circ}{2}-110^\circ=\boxed{35^\circ}.\]

import graph;import geometry;usepackage("amsmath");size(7cm);
pair B=(-1,0),C=(1,0),A=(0,tan(35*pi/180));real l=abs(A-B);pair D=B+(l,0),E=C-(l,0);draw(A--B--C--cycle);draw(A--D^^A--E,dashed);label("$A$",A,(0,1));label("$B$",B,(-1,-1));label("$C$",C,(1,-1));label("$D$",D,(0,-1));label("$E$",E,(0,-1));
\end{asy}
