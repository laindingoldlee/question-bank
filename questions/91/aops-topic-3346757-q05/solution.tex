An attempted task produces $2,1,0$ replacement tasks with probabilities $x,(1-x)x,(1-x)^2$. Their mean is $\mu=2x+x(1-x)=3x-x^2$. Starting from three tasks, the expected population of generation $j$ is $3\mu^j$, so expected total attempts are $3\sum_{j\ge0}\mu^j$. This is finite exactly when $\mu<1$. Since $3x-x^2$ increases on $[0,1]$, the threshold solves $x^2-3x+1=0$, giving $\boxed{x=\dfrac{3-\sqrt5}{2}}$. Thus $|2^3-2\cdot5|=\boxed2$.
