Fix a person $v$. Let $A$ be the $k$ neighbours and $B$ the remaining $n-k-1$ people. Each vertex in $A$ has $k$ neighbours in total: one is $v$, exactly $\ell$ are in $A$, and therefore $k-1-\ell$ are in $B$. The number of edges from $A$ to $B$ is consequently $k(k-1-\ell)$. Each vertex in $B$ has exactly $m$ neighbours in $A$, because these are its common acquaintances with $v$. Counting the same edges gives $m(n-k-1)$. Equating and rearranging yields the stated identity.
