Let $a,b,c$ be positive integers with $a^2-b^2=c^2$. A student claims that there are no nonzero integers $p,q$ with $(a+p)^2-(b+q)^2=c^2$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Show that the claim is false for every such triple $(a,b,c)$.
\item[(b)] Show that for $(a,b,c)=(5,4,3)$ there are no \emph{positive} integers $p,q$ with $(a+p)^2-(b+q)^2=c^2$, and find all positive $p,q$ that work for $(a,b,c)=(10,6,8)$.
\end{enumerate}
