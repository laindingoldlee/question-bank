(a) By Brahmagupta's formula with $s=\dfrac{5+14+10+11}{2}=20$,
\[
[ABCD]=\sqrt{(20-5)(20-14)(20-10)(20-11)}=\sqrt{15\cdot6\cdot10\cdot9}=\sqrt{8100}=\boxed{90}.
\]
(b) Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary, so $\cos D=-\cos B$ and $\cos C=-\cos A$. The law of cosines for $AC$ in triangles $ABC$ and $ADC$:
\[
AC^2=25+196-140\cos B=100+121+220\cos B\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \cos B=0,\ AC^2=221 .
\]
So $\angle B=90^\circ$ (and $AC$ is a diameter). For $BD$ in triangles $ABD$ and $CBD$:
\[
BD^2=25+121-110\cos A=196+100+280\cos A\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \cos A=-\dfrac{150}{390}=-\dfrac{5}{13},
\]
\[
BD^2=146+110\cdot\dfrac{5}{13}=\dfrac{2448}{13}.
\]

Hence $AC^2+BD^2=221+\dfrac{2448}{13}=\boxed{\dfrac{5321}{13}}$.

Checks: Ptolemy gives $AC\cdot BD=5\cdot10+14\cdot11=204$, and indeed $221\cdot\dfrac{2448}{13}=17\cdot2448=41616=204^2$. The area is also $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot5\cdot14+\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot10\cdot11=35+55=90$, since $\angle B=\angle D=90^\circ$.
