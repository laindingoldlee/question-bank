Let $k$ be the number of chickens counted twice. Then $c+g+k=24$ with $c,g,k>0$ and $k<c$. There are $\binom{23}{2}=253$ positive triples before imposing $k<c$. Equality $k=c$ occurs for $c=1,\ldots,11$, giving $11$ triples. Swapping $c,k$ pairs all remaining triples into the cases $k<c$ and $k>c$. Thus the answer is $\boxed{\dfrac{253-11}{2}=121}$. Each admissible $(c,g)$ determines its unique $k=24-c-g$, so counting triples does not overcount pairs.
