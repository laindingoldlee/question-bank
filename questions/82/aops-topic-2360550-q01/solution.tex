The prism has $10$ vertices, so $\dbinom{10}{2}=45$ segments join two vertices. Of these, $15$ are edges ($5+5$ around the pentagons and $5$ vertical).

\textbf{Face diagonals.} Each pentagon has $5$ diagonals, and each rectangle has $2$: in total $2\cdot5+5\cdot2=20$.

\textbf{Space diagonals.} The remaining segments: $45-15-20=10$. (Directly: each top vertex is joined by a space diagonal to the $2$ bottom vertices that are not below it or below one of its neighbours, giving $5\cdot2=10$.)

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real h=2.2; real yk=0.38;
pair q(int i){return (cos(2*pi*i/5-pi/2), yk*sin(2*pi*i/5-pi/2));}
pair[] Bt, Tp;
for(int i=0;i<5;++i){ Bt.push(q(i)); Tp.push(q(i)+(0,h)); }
for(int i=0;i<5;++i){
  bool hid=(Bt[i].y+Bt[(i+1)%5].y>0);
  draw(Bt[i]--Bt[(i+1)%5], hid ? dashed+gray : black);
  draw(Tp[i]--Tp[(i+1)%5]);
  draw(Bt[i]--Tp[i], (Bt[i].y>0) ? dashed+gray : black);
}
draw(Bt[1]--Tp[3],red+linewidth(1.1));
draw(Tp[0]--Tp[2],blue+linewidth(1.1));
dot(Bt[1],red); dot(Tp[3],red);
\end{asy}

So there are $\boxed{20}$ face diagonals (blue: one of them) and $\boxed{10}$ space diagonals (red: one of them), $30$ diagonals in all.
