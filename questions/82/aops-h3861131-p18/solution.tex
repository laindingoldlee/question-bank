Take $B$ at central angle zero and $A$ at $180^\circ$. Then $C$ is at $135^\circ$ and $D$ at $67.5^\circ$. Triangles $OAD$ and $OCD$ have equal area because their included central angles $112.5^\circ,67.5^\circ$ have equal sines, and all their radii are six. In the desired curvilinear triangle those equal contributions cancel, leaving the sector $AOC$, whose angle is $45^\circ$. Its area is $\dfrac{45}{360}\pi6^2=\boxed{\dfrac{9\pi}{2}}$. 
\begin{asy}
texpreamble("\usepackage{amsmath,amssymb}");
size(220);
defaultpen(fontsize(9pt));
pair O=(0,0),A=(-6,0),B=(6,0),C=6*dir(135),D=6*dir(67.5);draw(arc(O,6,0,180));draw(A--B);draw(A--D--C);draw(O--D,dashed);draw(O--C,dashed);label("$O$",O,(0,-1));label("$A$",A,(-1,0));label("$B$",B,(1,0));label("$C$",C,(-1,1));label("$D$",D,(0,1));
\end{asy}
