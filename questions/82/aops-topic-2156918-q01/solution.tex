Each game has two players, so the number of games is $\dfrac{8+12+14}{2}=17$.

The player who sits out a game always plays the next game, so nobody sits out two games in a row.

\textbf{Kenny.} Kenny sat out $17-8=9$ of the $17$ games, no two of them consecutive. The only way to choose $9$ pairwise non-consecutive numbers from $1,2,\dots,17$ is $1,3,5,\dots,17$ (any gap of two or more would leave too little room). So Kenny sat out exactly the odd-numbered games and played exactly the even-numbered games. In particular, he played games $2,4,\dots,16$ and sat out the game after each of them, so he lost every game he played.

\textbf{Abby.} Abby sat out $17-12=5$ games. In the odd-numbered games Kenny sits out, so Abby plays; hence her $5$ games off are even-numbered. She played the last seven games, $11$ to $17$, so she did not sit out games $12$, $14$ or $16$. So her five games off are exactly games $2,4,6,8,10$.

\textbf{Game 4.} Abby sat out game $4$, so it was Kenny against Ned, and Kenny lost it. Therefore
\[\boxed{\text{Ned beat Kenny in the fourth game.}}\]

(Such an afternoon exists: Ned wins games $1$ to $10$, alternately beating Abby in the odd games and Kenny in the even games; Abby then wins games $11$ to $17$. This gives Kenny $8$, Abby $12$ and Ned $14$ games.)
