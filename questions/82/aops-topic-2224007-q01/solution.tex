To find the base-$8$ digits after the point, multiply the fractional part by $8$ repeatedly; the integer parts are the digits.
\[\begin{array}{rcl}
\dfrac{1}{10}\cdot8=\dfrac{4}{5}&\Rightarrow&\text{digit }0,\ \text{remainder }\dfrac{4}{5}\\[2mm]
\dfrac{4}{5}\cdot8=6+\dfrac{2}{5}&\Rightarrow&\text{digit }6,\ \text{remainder }\dfrac{2}{5}\\[2mm]
\dfrac{2}{5}\cdot8=3+\dfrac{1}{5}&\Rightarrow&\text{digit }3,\ \text{remainder }\dfrac{1}{5}\\[2mm]
\dfrac{1}{5}\cdot8=1+\dfrac{3}{5}&\Rightarrow&\text{digit }1,\ \text{remainder }\dfrac{3}{5}\\[2mm]
\dfrac{3}{5}\cdot8=4+\dfrac{4}{5}&\Rightarrow&\text{digit }4,\ \text{remainder }\dfrac{4}{5}
\end{array}\]
The remainder $\dfrac{4}{5}$ has appeared before, so the digits $6,3,1,4$ repeat forever:
\[\dfrac{1}{10}=\boxed{0.0\overline{6314}_8}=0.063146314\ldots_8.\]
\textbf{Check.} $6314_8=6\cdot512+3\cdot64+1\cdot8+4=3276$, and $0.0\overline{6314}_8=\dfrac{1}{8}\cdot\dfrac{3276}{8^4-1}=\dfrac{3276}{8\cdot4095}=\dfrac{3276}{32760}=\dfrac{1}{10}$.
