The number of adjacent swaps needed equals the number of inversions (pairs of toys in the wrong order). Let $c(i)$ be the correct place of the toy in place $i$, so $|c(i)-i|\le1$. If toys in places $i<j$ are inverted, then $c(i)>c(j)$ with $c(i)\le i+1$ and $c(j)\ge j-1$, which forces $j=i+1$, $c(i)=i+1$ and $c(i+1)=i$. So the inversions are swapped neighbouring pairs, and no toy is in two of them. There are at most $50$ such pairs, so at most $\boxed{50}$ swaps are needed, attained by the order $2,1,4,3,\dots,100,99$.
