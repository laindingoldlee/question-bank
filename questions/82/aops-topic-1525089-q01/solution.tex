\textbf{(a)} For $m\ge1$, $2^m$ is even, while every number congruent to $1$ modulo $24$ is odd. (The method above works for $63$ because $\gcd(2,63)=1$; here $\gcd(2,24)=2$.)

\textbf{(b)} Split $24=8\cdot3$ with $\gcd(8,3)=1$.
\begin{itemize}
\item Modulo $8$: $2^{100}\equiv0$, since $2^3\mid2^{100}$.
\item Modulo $3$: $2\equiv-1$, so $2^{100}\equiv(-1)^{100}=1$.
\end{itemize}
The residue modulo $24$ that is $0$ mod $8$ and $1$ mod $3$ is $16$. (Indeed $2^4=16$, and from $2^3$ on the powers of $2$ modulo $24$ alternate $8,16,8,16,\dots$; even exponents give $16$.) So $2^{100}\equiv16\pmod{24}$, and the smallest positive $k$ with $2^{100}+k\equiv0$ is
\[
k=24-16=\boxed{8}.
\]
