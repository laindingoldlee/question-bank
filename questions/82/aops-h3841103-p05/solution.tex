Liang should deliberately miss while all three remain. Jiang then aims at Shang: missing deliberately guarantees Jiang\textquotesingle s elimination, while hitting offers a chance to survive. Shang, if reached, targets the stronger Jiang. If Jiang hits Shang (probability $\dfrac{2}{3}$), Liang shoots first in a duel with Jiang and wins with probability $\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{3}}{1-(\dfrac{2}{3})(\dfrac{1}{3})}=\dfrac{3}{7}$. If Jiang misses, Shang eliminates him and Liang gets one shot at Shang, winning with probability $\dfrac{1}{3}$. Thus deliberate missing gives $\dfrac{2}{3}\dfrac{3}{7}+\dfrac{1}{3}\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{25}{63}$. Shooting Shang instead gives $\dfrac{1}{3}\dfrac{1}{7}+\dfrac{2}{3}\dfrac{25}{63}<\dfrac{25}{63}$; shooting Jiang gives only $\dfrac{2}{3}\dfrac{25}{63}$. Hence the optimum is $\boxed{\dfrac{25}{63}}$.
