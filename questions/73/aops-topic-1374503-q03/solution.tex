Here casework by colour patterns becomes very long, and the efficient tool is Burnside's lemma: the number of classes equals the average, over the $48$ symmetries of the cube (rotations and reflections), of the number of colourings that the symmetry leaves unchanged. A symmetry whose permutation of the $8$ vertices has $c$ cycles fixes $5^c$ colourings (each cycle must be one colour).

\[
\begin{array}{lcc}
\text{symmetry}&\text{number}&\text{cycles on vertices}\\\hline
\text{identity}&1&8\\
\text{rotation }\pm90^\circ\text{ about a face axis}&6&2\\
\text{rotation }180^\circ\text{ about a face axis}&3&4\\
\text{rotation }\pm120^\circ\text{ about a vertex diagonal}&8&4\\
\text{rotation }180^\circ\text{ about an edge axis}&6&4\\
\text{central inversion}&1&4\\
\text{reflection in a plane parallel to faces}&3&4\\
\text{reflection in a plane through two opposite edges}&6&6\\
\text{rotary reflection by }\pm90^\circ&6&2\\
\text{rotary reflection by }\pm60^\circ&8&2
\end{array}
\]
(For instance, a reflection in a plane through two opposite edges fixes the $4$ vertices on those edges and swaps the other $4$ in two pairs: $6$ cycles. A rotary reflection about a body diagonal swaps the two end vertices of the diagonal and moves the other six around one $6$-cycle: $2$ cycles.) So
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{sum}&=5^8+6\cdot5^2+3\cdot5^4+8\cdot5^4+6\cdot5^4+5^4+3\cdot5^4+6\cdot5^6+6\cdot5^2+8\cdot5^2\\
&=390625+150+1875+5000+3750+625+1875+93750+150+200=498000,
\end{aligned}
\]
and the number of colourings is $\dfrac{498000}{48}=\boxed{10375}$.
