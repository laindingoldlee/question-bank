Take $f(x)=\dfrac{1}{x}$ (for $x\ne0$), a quotient of polynomials. If it agreed with a polynomial $p(x)$ for all $x\ne0$, then $xp(x)=1$ for all $x\ne0$, so the polynomial $xp(x)-1$ would have infinitely many roots and be identically $0$; but its constant term is $-1$. Hence $\dfrac{1}{x}$ is not a polynomial. (Alternatively, a polynomial is bounded near $0$, while $\dfrac{1}{x}$ is not.)
