\textbf{(a)} Consider the gap triangle at $A$. Let $E$ be the vertex of square I next to $A$ and $D$ the vertex of square III next to $A$, so $AE=AB$, $AE\perp AB$, $AD=AC$ and $AD\perp AC$.

Label the triangle so that $A,B,C$ go around counterclockwise. Since the squares lie outside the triangle, the vector $\overrightarrow{AE}$ is $\overrightarrow{AB}$ turned clockwise through $90^\circ$, and $\overrightarrow{AD}$ is $\overrightarrow{CA}$ turned clockwise through $90^\circ$. So the counterclockwise quarter turn about $A$ takes $E$ to $B$ and takes $D$ to the point $C'$ with $\overrightarrow{AC'}=\overrightarrow{CA}$, the reflection of $C$ in $A$. Hence triangle $ADE$ is congruent to triangle $AC'B$.

In triangle $BCC'$, the point $A$ is the midpoint of $CC'$, so the median $BA$ splits it into two triangles of equal area (equal bases $AC=AC'$ and the same height from $B$). Hence
\[[ADE]=[AC'B]=[ABC].\]
(Equivalently, $\angle DAE=360^\circ-90^\circ-90^\circ-\angle BAC=180^\circ-\angle BAC$, so $[ADE]=\dfrac{1}{2}\,AD\cdot AE\sin\left(180^\circ-A\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}\,AB\cdot AC\sin A=[ABC]$.)

The same argument at $B$ (with sides $BA$ and $BC$) and at $C$ (with sides $CA$ and $CB$) shows that every shaded triangle has area $[ABC]$.

\textbf{(b)} The total shaded area is $3[ABC]$. Taking $AB$ as the base, the height from $C$ is at most $AC=3$, with equality exactly when $\angle BAC=90^\circ$. So $[ABC]\le\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot2\cdot3=3$, and the greatest total shaded area is
\[3\cdot3=\boxed{9},\]
attained when the angle at $A$ is a right angle.
