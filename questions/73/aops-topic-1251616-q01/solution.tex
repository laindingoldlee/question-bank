Scale areas so that each of the five triangles has area $1$; at the end multiply by $2$.

Triangles $ABC$ and $BCD$ have the same base $BC$ and equal areas, so $A$ and $D$ are equally far from line $BC$, and (by convexity) on the same side: $AD\parallel BC$. In the same way every diagonal is parallel to the opposite side; in particular $CE\parallel AB$ and $BD\parallel AE$.

Let $P$ be the intersection of $BD$ and $CE$. Then $ABPE$ is a parallelogram, so $[BPE]=[ABE]=1$. Let $x=[CPD]$. Then $[BCP]=[BCD]-x=1-x$ and $[DPE]=[CDE]-x=1-x$. Triangles with a common vertex and bases on the same line have areas in the ratio of the bases:
\[\dfrac{[BCP]}{[CPD]}=\dfrac{BP}{PD}=\dfrac{[BPE]}{[DPE]}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\dfrac{1-x}{x}=\dfrac{1}{1-x}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^2-3x+1=0,\]
so $x=\dfrac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ (as $x<1$). The pentagon is the parallelogram $ABPE$ (area $2$) plus triangles $BCD$ (area $1$) and $PDE$ (area $1-x$):
\[2+1+1-x=\dfrac{5+\sqrt5}{2}.\]
With triangles of area $2$, the pentagon has area $\boxed{5+\sqrt5}\text{ cm}^2$.
