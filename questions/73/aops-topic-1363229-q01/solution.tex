A diagonal joins two vertices that are not adjacent. From each of the $n$ vertices there are $n-3$ diagonals (to every vertex except itself and its two neighbours). This counts every diagonal twice, once from each end, so an $n$-gon has
\[
\boxed{\dfrac{n(n-3)}{2}}
\]
diagonals. Equivalently, of the $\dbinom n2$ segments joining two vertices, $n$ are sides, leaving $\dbinom n2-n=\dfrac{n(n-3)}{2}$.

For the hexagon, $n=6$ gives $\dfrac{6\cdot3}{2}=\boxed{9}$ diagonals (dashed in the sketch: $6$ short ones and $3$ long ones through the centre).

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair[] V; for(int k=0;k<6;++k) V.push(dir(60*k));
for(int i=0;i<6;++i) for(int j=i+2;j<6;++j) if(!(i==0 && j==5)) draw(V[i]--V[j],red+dashed);
draw(V[0]--V[1]--V[2]--V[3]--V[4]--V[5]--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
for(int k=0;k<6;++k) dot(V[k]);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}
