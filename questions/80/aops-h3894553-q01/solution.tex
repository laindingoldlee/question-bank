Let the total be $S$. Every five-term sum exceeds $2$, so is odd. Thus the six terms have the same parity, and they must all be odd; consequently $S$ is even and $S\ge1+3+5+7+9+11=36$. The following finite search is an exact way to finish. For each even $S$ from $36$ to $74$, form $L_S=\{a:1\le a<S,\ a\text{ odd},\ S-a\text{ prime}\}$. Test the sums of all six-element subsets of $L_S$. There are no subsets summing to $S$ for $S=36,38,\ldots,72$; at $S=74$, the subset $\{1,3,7,15,21,27\}$ works. The six complementary sums are $73,71,67,59,53,47$, all prime. Thus the minimum is $\boxed{74}$. For a reproducible finite check, generate candidates by trial division up to their square roots, and recursively choose six candidates in increasing order, discarding a branch as soon as its partial sum exceeds $S$. This enumerates every possible smaller set exactly once.
