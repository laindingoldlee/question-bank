The symbol $\infty$ is not a real number. This problem shows why expressions such as $\infty-\infty$ and $0\cdot\infty$ cannot be given a value.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Suppose there were a number $w$ that obeys the usual rules of arithmetic (in particular, $w-w=0$, and addition is associative) and satisfies $w+1=w$. Show that this leads to $1=0$.
\item[(b)] For each $c$ in $\{0,5\}$, give sequences $a_n$ and $b_n$ that both grow without bound such that $a_n-b_n=c$ for all $n$. Give also an example where $a_n-b_n$ grows without bound.
\item[(c)] Give sequences $x_n\to0$ and $y_n$ growing without bound such that $x_ny_n=3$ for all $n$, and another pair with $x_ny_n\to0$.
\item[(d)] Show that $\dfrac{1}{n}\to0$ as $n$ grows, and explain in what sense ``$\dfrac{1}{\infty}=0$''.
\end{enumerate}
