\textbf{Where can $3$ go?} Its forbidden neighbours are $2$ and $4$; its allowed neighbours are $1$ and $5$.

\textbf{Case 1: $3$ at an end.} By the reversal symmetry, count permutations starting with $3$ and double. The next entry is $1$ or $5$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $3,1,\ldots$: the rest is $2,4,5$ arranged after $1$, with $1$ not next to $2$ and $4$ not next to $5$. The entry after $1$ is $4$ or $5$: $3,1,4,2,5$ works; $3,1,5,2,4$ works; $3,1,4,5,\dots$ and $3,1,5,4,\dots$ fail. So $2$ permutations.
\item $3,5,\ldots$: by the symmetry $x\mapsto6-x$ (which preserves the differences), also $2$: $3,5,2,4,1$ and $3,5,1,4,2$.
\end{itemize}
So $4$ start with $3$, and $8$ have $3$ at an end.

\textbf{Case 2: $3$ in the interior.} Then its two neighbours are $1$ and $5$, so the permutation contains the block $1,3,5$ or $5,3,1$, and $2$ and $4$ fill the other two places ($2$ and $4$ may be adjacent, as they differ by $2$). For the block $1,3,5$, the place next to $1$ cannot hold $2$ and the place next to $5$ cannot hold $4$:
\begin{itemize}
\item block in places $1$--$3$: $1,3,5,x,y$ needs $x\ne4$, giving $1,3,5,2,4$;
\item block in places $2$--$4$: $x,1,3,5,y$ needs $x\ne2$, giving $4,1,3,5,2$;
\item block in places $3$--$5$: $x,y,1,3,5$ needs $y\ne2$, giving $2,4,1,3,5$.
\end{itemize}
All three are distant. The block $5,3,1$ gives their reverses, $3$ more.
So $6$ have $3$ in the interior.

Total: $8+6=\boxed{14}$.
