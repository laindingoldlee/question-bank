At an intersection point $x^2+3=\dfrac{x}{2}-3$, that is, $2x^2-x+12=0$. Its discriminant is $(-1)^2-4\cdot2\cdot12=1-96=-95<0$, so there is no real solution. (Equivalently, $x^2-\dfrac{x}{2}+6=\left(x-\dfrac{1}{4}\right)^2+\dfrac{95}{16}>0$ for every $x$, so the parabola always lies above the line.)

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
import graph;
real f(real x){return x^2+3;} real g(real x){return x/2-3;}
draw((-4,0)--(4.4,0),gray,Arrow(4)); draw((0,-5.5)--(0,13),gray,Arrow(4));
label("$x$",(4.4,0),E,gray); label("$y$",(0,13),N,gray);
draw(graph(f,-3.15,3.15),blue+linewidth(0.9)); draw(graph(g,-4,4.2),red+linewidth(0.9));
label("$y=x^2+3$",(2.2,f(2.2)),E,blue); label("$y=\dfrac{x}{2}-3$",(4.2,g(4.2)),SE,red);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The graphs have $\boxed{\text{no point of intersection}}$. (Over the complex numbers the equation has the roots $x=\dfrac{1\pm i\sqrt{95}}{4}$, but these do not give points of the real plane.)
