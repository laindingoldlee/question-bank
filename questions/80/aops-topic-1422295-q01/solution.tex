For $x\ne2$, multiply by $x-2$:
\[
(x+2)(x-2)+(m-1)=x-2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^2-x+(m-3)=0 .
\]
By Vieta's formulas the product of its roots is $m-3$, so $m-3=2$ and $m=\boxed{5}$.

Then the equation is $x^2-x+2=0$; $x=2$ is not a root ($4-2+2\ne0$), so both roots are roots of the original equation. Its discriminant is $1-8=-7<0$, so the two roots $\dfrac{1\pm i\sqrt7}{2}$ are non-real: there is no value of $m$ for which the equation has two \emph{real} roots with product $2$.
