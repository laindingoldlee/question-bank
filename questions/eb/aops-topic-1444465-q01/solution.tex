The digit $0$ cannot occur (no number is divisible by $0$).

\emph{If $5$ is a digit,} the number is divisible by $5$, so its last digit is $5$ (not $0$), and the number is odd; then no even digit can occur. The digits are among $1,3,5,7,9$: at most $5$ digits.

\emph{If $5$ is not a digit,} the digits are among $1,2,3,4,6,7,8,9$. All eight would give digit sum $40$; but $9$ would be a digit, so the number would be a multiple of $9$, while its digit sum $40$ is not. So at most $7$ digits are possible. With $7$ of these digits including $9$, the digit sum $40-d$ must be a multiple of $9$, which forces the missing digit to be $d=4$: the digits are $1,2,3,6,7,8,9$ (sum $36$). Such a number exists, for example
\[
9867312=8\cdot1233414=7\cdot1409616=9\cdot1096368,
\]
which is even and a multiple of $3$, $6$, $7$, $8$ and $9$, hence of every one of its digits.

So the largest possible number of digits is $\boxed{7}$.
