Alex runs at $\dfrac{900}{100}=9$ metres per second and Bob at $\dfrac{900}{150}=6$ metres per second. Running in opposite directions, they meet each time their combined distance increases by one lap, $900$ m, which takes
\[
\dfrac{900}{9+6}=60\text{ seconds}.
\]
So the ninth meeting is at time $9\cdot60=540$ seconds. By then Bob has run $6\cdot540=3240$ m $=3$ laps $+\,540$ m. He is $540$ m along the track from the start (in his direction), so he still has
\[
900-540=\boxed{360}\text{ m}
\]
to run to reach the starting point.
