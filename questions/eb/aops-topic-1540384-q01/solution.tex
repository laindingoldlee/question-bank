\textbf{A linear factor.} At $x=-1$ the polynomial is $3+8+13-24=0$, so $x+1$ is a factor (factor theorem). Synthetic division of $3,-8,0,-13,-24$ by $x+1$ gives
\[
3x^4-8x^3-13x-24=(x+1)\left(3x^3-11x^2+11x-24\right).
\]
(Note the $0x^2$ term of the quartic; the quotient is $3x^3-11x^2+11x-24$.)

\textbf{The cubic does not factor further.} If the cubic $g(x)=3x^3-11x^2+11x-24$ factored over the integers, one factor would be linear, so $g$ would have a rational root $\dfrac {p}{q}$ with $p\mid24$ and $q\mid3$ (rational root theorem). For $x\le0$ every term of $g$ is $\le0$ and $-24<0$, so $g(x)<0$; so a rational root would be positive. Checking the positive candidates:
\[
g(1)=-21,\ g(2)=-22,\ g(3)=-9,\ g(4)=36,
\]
and $g$ increases for $x\ge4$ (each of $3x^3-11x^2=x^2(3x-11)$ and $11x-24$ increases there), so no candidate $6,8,12,24$ works. The remaining candidates are $\dfrac{1}{3},\dfrac{2}{3},\dfrac{4}{3},\dfrac{8}{3}$; for these
\[
27g\!\left(\dfrac {p}{3}\right)=3p^3-33p^2+99p-648,
\]
which is $-579,-558,-588,-432$ for $p=1,2,4,8$, never $0$. So $g$ has no rational root and is irreducible over the integers.

Hence the complete factorisation is
\[
\boxed{(x+1)\left(3x^3-11x^2+11x-24\right)}.
\]
