\textbf{All combinations.} With $n$ nickels, $d$ dimes and $q$ quarters, $5n+10d+25q=105$. For each $q$ the remaining amount $105-25q$ is made of dimes and nickels, with $d$ from $0$ up to $\left\lfloor\dfrac{105-25q}{10}\right\rfloor$:
\[
q=0:\ 11,\quad q=1:\ 9,\quad q=2:\ 6,\quad q=3:\ 4,\quad q=4:\ 1
\]
choices, so there are $31$ combinations and $31$ students.

\textbf{When change is impossible.} Jack can hand over exactly $100$ cents if and only if the coins he keeps are worth exactly $5$ cents, that is, a single nickel. So he can give change exactly when he has at least one nickel. Without nickels, $10d+25q=105$ forces $q$ odd: $(q,d)=(1,8)$ or $(3,3)$. These $2$ students cannot give change.

The probability is $\boxed{\dfrac{2}{31}}$.
