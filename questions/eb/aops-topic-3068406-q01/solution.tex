(a) No: $f(x)=e^x$ is everywhere positive and is not a polynomial. If a nonconstant polynomial is positive for every real input, its leading degree must be even and its leading coefficient positive. An odd degree would give opposite signs for sufficiently large positive and negative inputs; a negative leading coefficient of even degree would give negative values at both ends. These conditions are necessary, not sufficient. (b) The function $g(z)=|z|^2+1=z\overline z+1$ is always positive and nonconstant. It is not a polynomial in $z$ alone.
