A solution has $x>0$. Put $u=\sqrt x>0$. Then $\ln b=\dfrac{2\ln u}{u}$. The function on the right has derivative $\dfrac{2(1-\ln u)}{u^2}$, so it increases up to $u=e$ and then decreases, with maximum $\dfrac{2}{e}$. Its limit at $0$ is $-\infty$, and its limit at infinity is $0$. Its range is therefore $(-\infty,\dfrac{2}{e}]$. Excluding $b=1$, the required bases are $\boxed{(0,1)\cup(1,e^{\dfrac{2}{e}}]}$.
