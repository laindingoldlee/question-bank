Normalize the cake mass to 1. Cut a linear cake at the internal multiples of $\dfrac{1}{11}$ and $\dfrac{1}{12}$. None coincide, so there are $10+11+1=22$ pieces, and either equal division can be assembled. For a lower bound, make a bipartite graph with 11 vertices representing one allocation and 12 representing the other. Each piece is an edge between its two recipient groups. A connected component with $a$ first-allocation groups and $b$ second-allocation groups has equal mass $\dfrac{a}{11}=\dfrac{b}{12}$. Coprimality forces $a=11,b=12$, so the graph is connected. A connected graph on 23 vertices has at least 22 edges. Thus $\boxed{22}$ pieces are necessary and sufficient.
