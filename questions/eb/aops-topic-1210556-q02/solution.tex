Let $t=b^2+b$. Then $t^2=b^4+2b^3+b^2$, so the left side is $t^2+2b^2+2b=t^2+2t$. Multiply $a^2+a=t^2+2t$ by $4$ and add $1$:
\[(2a+1)^2=4t^2+8t+1=(2t+2)^2-3,\qquad\text{so}\qquad (2t+2)^2-(2a+1)^2=3.\]
Two squares of non-negative integers differing by $3$ must be $4$ and $1$. Then $2t+2=2$, i.e. $t=0$, and $b^2+b=0$ forces $b=0$ (or $b=-1$), not a positive integer. Hence there are no solutions in positive integers. (The only solution in non-negative integers is $a=b=0$.)
