Two rounds suffice: query $(1,100,0,0)$ and $(0,0,1,100)$. The first answer is $a+100b$, whose last two decimal digits recover $a$ and remaining digits recover $b$; similarly recover $c,d$. One round cannot suffice: there are $90^4=65,610,000$ possible quadruples, but for fixed allowed coefficients the integer answer lies between $0$ and $99(4\cdot 999)=395604$, giving at most $395605$ answers. Pigeonhole forces two distinct quadruples to share an answer. Thus the minimum is $\boxed 2$.
