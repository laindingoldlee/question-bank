Twenty people, including $A$ and $B$, are split at random into $10$ pairs, all pairings being equally likely.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Find the probability that $A$ and $B$ are paired with each other.
\item[(b)] A student argues: "There are $20\cdot19$ ordered pairs of people, and $2$ of them, $(A,B)$ and $(B,A)$, are what we want, so the probability is $\dfrac{2}{20\cdot19}=\dfrac{1}{190}$." Explain what is wrong with this argument.
\end{enumerate}
