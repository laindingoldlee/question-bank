Let $S=x^2+y^2+z^2>0$ and $t=xyz$. Multiplying the three equalities by $x,y,z$ gives $Sx=t+8$, $Sy=2t-2$, $Sz=3t+18$. Summing the squared expressions gives $S^3=14t^2+116t+392$, while multiplying them gives $tS^3=6(t+8)(t-1)(t+6)$. Eliminating $S^3$ yields $2(t+2)(4t^2+11t+72)=0$. The quadratic has discriminant $121-1152<0$, so $t=-2$. Then $S^3=216$, hence $S=6$ and $(x,y,z)=(1,-1,2)$. Substitution gives common value $6$, so the unique solution is $\boxed{(1,-1,2)}$.
