Four guesses suffice: the all-1 word, the all-2 word, and their versions with only the first digit flipped. An unbalanced word agrees with one constant word in at least eleven places. A balanced word agrees with one of the flipped words in eleven places. For the lower bound, among any three guesses, two have the same parity of the number of 1s, so they differ in an even number of positions. Construct a word $w$ agreeing with each of these two guesses in exactly ten positions: split both their agreeing positions and their differing positions equally. Its digitwise complement also agrees with each in ten. A third guess cannot agree with both $w$ and its complement in eleven positions. Thus at least one is missed, proving $\boxed{N=4}$, choice B.
