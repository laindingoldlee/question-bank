From the condition $a\ne0$ and $a^2+1=3a$, so $a+\dfrac{1}{a}=3$. Then
\[
a^2+\dfrac{1}{a^2}=\left(a+\dfrac{1}{a}\right)^2-2=7,\qquad a^3+\dfrac{1}{a^3}=\left(a+\dfrac{1}{a}\right)^3-3\left(a+\dfrac{1}{a}\right)=27-9=18 .
\]
Dividing numerator and denominator by $a^3$,
\[
\dfrac{a^3}{a^6+\cdots+1}=\dfrac{1}{\left(a^3+\dfrac{1}{a^3}\right)+\left(a^2+\dfrac{1}{a^2}\right)+\left(a+\dfrac{1}{a}\right)+1}=\dfrac{1}{18+7+3+1}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{29}}.
\]
