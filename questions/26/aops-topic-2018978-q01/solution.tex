Since $\dfrac{1}{n(n+1)}=\dfrac{1}{n}-\dfrac{1}{n+1}$,
\[
\dfrac{1}{n^2(n+1)^2}=\left(\dfrac{1}{n}-\dfrac{1}{n+1}\right)^2=\dfrac{1}{n^2}+\dfrac{1}{(n+1)^2}-\dfrac{2}{n(n+1)} .
\]
Summing over $n\ge1$ (all three series converge):
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\dfrac{1}{n^2}=\dfrac{\pi^2}{6},\qquad\sum_{n\ge1}\dfrac{1}{(n+1)^2}=\dfrac{\pi^2}{6}-1,\qquad\sum_{n\ge1}\dfrac{2}{n(n+1)}=2\sum_{n\ge1}\left(\dfrac{1}{n}-\dfrac{1}{n+1}\right)=2 .
\]
So the sum is $\dfrac{\pi^2}{3}-3=\dfrac{\pi^2-9}{3}$, giving $(a,b,c,d)=(1,0,-9,3)$. This representation is the only one with $d>0$ and $\gcd(a,b,c,d)=1$: two different representations would give a nontrivial relation $A\pi^2+B\pi+C=0$ with integer coefficients, impossible because $\pi$ is transcendental.

Hence $a+b+c+d=1+0-9+3=\boxed{-5}$.
