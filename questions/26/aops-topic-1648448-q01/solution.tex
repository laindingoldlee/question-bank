\textbf{No.} Suppose such a polynomial $p$ exists. Then $x+5=(x-1)p(x)$ for every $x\ne1$. Both sides are polynomials, and they agree at infinitely many points, so their difference, a polynomial with infinitely many roots, is the zero polynomial. Hence $x+5=(x-1)p(x)$ holds for every $x$, including $x=1$, where it says $6=0$. This contradiction shows that no such $p$ exists.

(Another view: $\dfrac{x+5}{x-1}=1+\dfrac{6}{x-1}$, which is unbounded near $x=1$, while a polynomial is bounded on every bounded interval.)
