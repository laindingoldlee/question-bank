Classify the vertices by their distance (number of edges) from the start: distance $0$ (the start), $1$ (three vertices), $2$ (three vertices), $3$ (the opposite vertex). Each move changes the distance by $1$, so after an even number of moves the bug is at distance $0$ or $2$.

Look at two moves at a time.
\begin{itemize}
\item From distance $0$: the bug goes to distance $1$, then back to $0$ with probability $\dfrac{1}{3}$ (or to $2$ with probability $\dfrac{2}{3}$).
\item From distance $2$: it goes to distance $1$ with probability $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and then to $0$ with probability $\dfrac{1}{3}$; or to distance $3$ and then back to $2$. So it reaches $0$ with probability $\dfrac{2}{3}\cdot\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{2}{9}$.
\end{itemize}
Let $p_k$ be the probability of being at the start after $2k$ moves. Then
\[p_{k+1}=\dfrac{1}{3}p_k+\dfrac{2}{9}(1-p_k)=\dfrac{2}{9}+\dfrac{1}{9}p_k,\qquad p_0=1.\]
The fixed point is $p=\dfrac{1}{4}$, and $p_{k+1}-\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{1}{9}\left(p_k-\dfrac{1}{4}\right)$, so $p_k=\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{3}{4}\cdot\dfrac{1}{9^k}$. After $10$ moves ($k=5$):
\[p_5=\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{3}{4\cdot59049}=\dfrac{59049+3}{4\cdot59049}=\boxed{\dfrac{4921}{19683}}.\]
