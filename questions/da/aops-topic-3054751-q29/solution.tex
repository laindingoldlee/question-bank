At each endpoint, the probability of an attacked sheep is $(\dfrac{7}{10})(\dfrac{3}{9})=\dfrac{7}{30}$. At an interior position, conditional on being a sheep, the chance neither neighbour is a wolf is $(\dfrac{6}{9})(\dfrac{5}{8})=\dfrac{5}{12}$. Thus its chance of being attacked is $(\dfrac{7}{10})(\dfrac{7}{12})=\dfrac{49}{120}$. Linearity over two endpoints and eight interior positions gives $2(\dfrac{7}{30})+8(\dfrac{49}{120})=\boxed{\dfrac{56}{15}}$. A sheep between two wolves is counted only once.
