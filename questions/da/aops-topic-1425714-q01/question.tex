\emph{Newton's little formula} says that if a sequence $a_1,a_2,a_3,\dots$ has constant $k$th differences, then
\[
a_n=a_1\dbinom{n-1}{0}+\Delta_1\dbinom{n-1}{1}+\Delta_2\dbinom{n-1}{2}+\dots+\Delta_k\dbinom{n-1}{k},
\]
where $\Delta_j$ is the first entry of the row of $j$th differences.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Explain why the formula holds.
\item[(b)] The sequence $1, 5, 14, 30, 55, 91,\dots$ has constant third differences. Use the formula to find $a_n$ as a polynomial in $n$.
\end{enumerate}
