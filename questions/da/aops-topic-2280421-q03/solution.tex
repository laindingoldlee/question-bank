Let $x=1+a$, $y=1+b$, $z=1+c$, all positive. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
\[(x+y+z)\left(\dfrac{1}{x}+\dfrac{1}{y}+\dfrac{1}{z}\right)\ge\left(\sqrt x\cdot\dfrac{1}{\sqrt x}+\sqrt y\cdot\dfrac{1}{\sqrt y}+\sqrt z\cdot\dfrac{1}{\sqrt z}\right)^2=9 .\]
Since $x+y+z=3+a+b+c$, dividing gives the claim. Equality holds when $x=y=z$, i.e. $a=b=c$.
