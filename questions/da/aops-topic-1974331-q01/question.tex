A student argues as follows. ``Let $N$ be the product of the first $n$ primes. $N$ has $2^n$ positive divisors. We can also count the divisors by casework on how many of the $n$ primes divide them: a divisor uses $1$, $2$, \dots, or $n$ of the primes, giving $\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom nk$ divisors. Therefore $2^n=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom nk$.'' Find the error, and state and prove the correct identity.
