For $ q=0,1,2 $ quarters, select the number $ d $ of dimes and then $ n $ of nickels; pennies fill the remaining value uniquely. The counts for each $ q $ are respectively $\sum_{d=0}^{6}(\lfloor\dfrac{67-10d}{5}\rfloor+1)=56 $, $\sum_{d=0}^{4}(\lfloor\dfrac{42-10d}{5}\rfloor+1)=25 $, and $\sum_{d=0}^{1}(\lfloor\dfrac{17-10d}{5}\rfloor+1)=6 $. Thus the total is $\boxed{87}$.
