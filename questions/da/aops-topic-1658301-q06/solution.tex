There are two equilateral triangles on $BC$, one on each side of line $BC$. Then $BP=BA=10$ and $\angle ABP=\angle ABC\pm60^\circ$. Since $AP^2=200-200\cos\angle ABP$ increases with the angle, $AP$ is largest when $P$ is on the side of $BC$ away from $A$, where $\angle ABP=\angle ABC+60^\circ$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(-6,0),C=(6,0),B=(0,8);
pair P=B+rotate(60)*(C-B);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));draw(B--P--C,linewidth(0.9));
draw(A--P,red+dashed);
label("$A$",A,W);label("$B$",B,N);label("$C$",C,S);label("$P$",P,E);
label("$10$",(A+B)/2,NW);label("$10$",B+0.75*(C-B),W);label("$12$",(A+C)/2,S);label("$10$",(B+P)/2,NE);label("$10$",(C+P)/2,SE);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

In triangle $ABC$, $\cos B=\dfrac{10^2+10^2-12^2}{2\cdot10\cdot10}=\dfrac{7}{25}$ and $\sin B=\dfrac{24}{25}$. So
\[
\cos(B+60^\circ)=\dfrac{7}{25}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{24}{25}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}=\dfrac{7-24\sqrt3}{50},
\]
and by the law of cosines in triangle $ABP$,
\[
AP^2=10^2+10^2-2\cdot10\cdot10\cdot\dfrac{7-24\sqrt3}{50}=200-28+96\sqrt3=172+96\sqrt3 .
\]
Since $(8+6\sqrt3)^2=64+108+96\sqrt3=172+96\sqrt3$, the maximum is
\[
AP=\boxed{8+6\sqrt3}\approx18.4 .
\]
(For $P$ on the same side as $A$, $AP^2=172-96\sqrt3$, giving $AP=6\sqrt3-8\approx2.4$.)
