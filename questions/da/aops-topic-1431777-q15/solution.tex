Let the fraction be $\dfrac{n}{d}$ with $nd=60$. The second condition says
\[
\dfrac{n+1}{\dfrac{d}{2}}=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d=2(n+1).
\]
Substituting, $n\cdot2(n+1)=60$, so $n^2+n-30=0$, that is $(n+6)(n-5)=0$. As $n>0$, $n=5$ and $d=12$. The fraction is $\boxed{\dfrac{5}{12}}$ (check: $5\cdot12=60$ and $\dfrac{6}{6}=1$).
