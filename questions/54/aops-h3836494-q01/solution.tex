Write the progression as $a,a+d,a+2d,\ldots$, with integer $d>0$. If it contains a square $m^2$, take $m\ge 0$. For every positive integer $t$, $(m+td)^2-m^2=d(2mt+t^2d)$ is a nonnegative multiple of $d$. Thus $(m+td)^2$ is another term of the progression, no earlier than $m^2$. These squares are distinct as $t$ increases. Hence one square implies infinitely many; otherwise there are none.
