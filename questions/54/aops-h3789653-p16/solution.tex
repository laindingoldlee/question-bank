Write a pair as $(n,n+1)$ with $1001\le n\le1999$. If $n$ does not end in nine, its last digit must be $0,1,2,3,4$, and its tens and hundreds digits each must be at most four. This gives $5^3-1=124$ choices, excluding 1000. If it ends in exactly one nine, the tens and hundreds digits each have five choices, giving 25. Exactly two final nines allow five hundreds digits. Exactly three final nines gives 1999 alone. Thus there are $124+25+5+1=\boxed{155}$ good pairs.
