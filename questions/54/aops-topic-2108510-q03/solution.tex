\textbf{(a)} Let the bisector of angle $A$ meet $BC$ at $D$. By the angle bisector theorem, $BD:DC=c:b$, so $D=\dfrac{bB+cC}{b+c}$ and $BD=\dfrac{ac}{b+c}$. The incentre $I$ lies on $AD$, and $BI$ bisects angle $B$ of triangle $ABD$, so again by the angle bisector theorem
\[\dfrac{AI}{ID}=\dfrac{AB}{BD}=\dfrac{c}{\dfrac{ac}{b+c}}=\dfrac{b+c}{a}.\]
Hence $I=\dfrac{aA+(b+c)D}{a+b+c}=\dfrac{aA+bB+cC}{a+b+c}$.

\textbf{(b)} Take $I$ as the origin, so by (a) $a\vec A+b\vec B+c\vec C=\vec0$. Then
\[\sum a\left|\vec A-\vec X\right|^2=\sum a\left|\vec A\right|^2-2\left(a\vec A+b\vec B+c\vec C\right)\cdot\vec X+(a+b+c)\left|\vec X\right|^2=\sum a\,IA^2+(a+b+c)\,XI^2,\]
where the sums run over the three vertices with their opposite sides.

\textbf{(c)} By (b), the expression is smallest exactly when $XI=0$, that is, at $\boxed{X=I}$, the incentre, and the minimum is $M=a\cdot IA^2+b\cdot IB^2+c\cdot IC^2$.

To evaluate $M$, put $X=A$, $X=B$ and $X=C$ into (b), writing $p=a+b+c$:
\[bc^2+cb^2=M+p\,IA^2,\qquad ac^2+ca^2=M+p\,IB^2,\qquad ab^2+ba^2=M+p\,IC^2 .\]
Multiply these by $a$, $b$ and $c$ respectively and add. The left side becomes $abc(b+c)+abc(c+a)+abc(a+b)=2p\cdot abc$, and the right side becomes $(a+b+c)M+p\,M=2pM$. Hence $M=\boxed{abc}$.
