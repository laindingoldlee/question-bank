Let the master be $M$ and the apprentice $A$ years old now, and $d=M-A$, the constant age difference.

When the master was $A$ (that is, $d$ years ago) the apprentice was $A-d$, so $A=3(A-d)$, which gives $2A=3d$.

When the apprentice is $M$ (in $d$ years) the master will be $M+d$, so $M+(M+d)=108$, that is $2A+3d=108$, using $M=A+d$.

From $2A=3d$ we get $6d=108$, so $d=18$, $A=27$ and $M=45$. The master is $\boxed{45}$ and the apprentice is $\boxed{27}$ years old.

(Check: $18$ years ago they were $27$ and $9$; in $18$ years they will be $63$ and $45$, with sum $108$.)

A bar-model view for younger students: the age difference $d$ is constant. Then the apprentice's age $d$ years ago is $A-d$, the master's is $A$, and $A=3(A-d)$ means the apprentice then was one unit $u$ and the gap is $2u$; so now the apprentice is $3u$ and the master $5u$, and in the future they will be $5u$ and $7u$, total $12u=108$, $u=9$.
