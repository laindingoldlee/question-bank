Let $S_n=a+ar+\cdots+ar^{n-1}$ be the sum of the first $n$ terms. The sum of an infinite series is defined as the limit of $S_n$ as $n\to\infty$, if that limit exists.

For $r\ne1$, multiply by $r$ and subtract:
\[
S_n-rS_n=\left(a+ar+\cdots+ar^{n-1}\right)-\left(ar+\cdots+ar^{n-1}+ar^n\right)=a-ar^n,
\]
so
\[
S_n=\dfrac{a\left(1-r^n\right)}{1-r}=\dfrac{a}{1-r}-\dfrac{a}{1-r}\,r^n .
\]

\textbf{(a)} If $|r|<1$, then $|r^n|=|r|^n\to0$ as $n\to\infty$, so $S_n\to\dfrac{a}{1-r}$. Hence the series converges and
\[
a+ar+ar^2+\cdots=\dfrac{a}{1-r}. \qquad\blacksquare
\]

\textbf{(b)} If $|r|\ge1$, the terms satisfy $|ar^n|=|a||r|^n\ge|a|>0$, so they do not tend to $0$. But if $S_n$ had a limit $S$, then the terms $ar^n=S_{n+1}-S_n$ would tend to $S-S=0$. So the series diverges. $\blacksquare$

The formula $\dfrac{a}{1-r}$ is therefore the sum of a \emph{convergent} geometric series; for $|r|\ge1$ (for example $r=2$, where $\dfrac{a}{1-r}=-a$) it is not the sum of anything.
