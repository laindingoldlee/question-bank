A $3$-digit palindrome has the form $\overline{aba}$ with $a\in\{1,\dots,9\}$ and $b\in\{0,\dots,9\}$, so there are $90$ of them. By the divisibility rule for $11$, $\overline{aba}$ is divisible by $11$ exactly when the alternating sum $a-b+a=2a-b$ is. Since $-9\le2a-b\le18$, this means $2a-b=0$ or $2a-b=11$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $b=2a$: $a=1,2,3,4$, giving $121,242,363,484$;
\item $b=2a-11$: $a=6,7,8,9$, giving $616,737,858,979$.
\end{itemize}
So $8$ of the $90$ palindromes work, and the probability is $\dfrac{8}{90}=\boxed{\dfrac{4}{45}}$.
