Dividing a number by $-1$ gives its opposite, so
\[\dfrac{1-\sqrt2}{-1}=-\left(1-\sqrt2\right)=-1+\sqrt2=\boxed{\sqrt2-1}.\]
(This step appears, for example, when rationalising a denominator: $\dfrac{1}{1+\sqrt2}=\dfrac{1-\sqrt2}{\left(1+\sqrt2\right)\left(1-\sqrt2\right)}=\dfrac{1-\sqrt2}{1-2}=\dfrac{1-\sqrt2}{-1}$.)
