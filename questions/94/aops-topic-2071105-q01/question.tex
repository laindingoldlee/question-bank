Let $N$ be the greatest positive integer that can be written as a Roman numeral using each of the seven symbols $I,V,X,L,C,D,M$ exactly once, and let $n$ be the least such positive integer. (The arrangement $CM$ is never used in a numeral that also contains $D$; the usual subtractive pairs are $IV,IX,XL,XC,CD,CM$.) Find $N-n$.

A student argues that the value cannot depend on the arrangement, because ``addition is commutative''. Explain why this is wrong.
