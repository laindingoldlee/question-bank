Strictly increasing decimal digits cannot include $0$. Since a multiple of $56$ is even, its last digit is at most $8$, so there are at most eight digits. The only eight-digit possibility, $12345678$, is not divisible by $8$.

For a seven-digit possibility, the last three digits must be an increasing triple divisible by $8$. Among the seven-digit subsets of $1,\ldots,8$, only omitting $7$ gives such a suffix, yielding $1234568$; this is $6$ modulo $7$ and fails.

For six digits, the first of the last three digits is at least $4$. The only increasing triples from $4,\ldots,8$ divisible by $8$ are $456$ and $568$. The possible six-digit numbers are consequently $123456,123568,124568,134568,234568$. The largest, $234568$, is $5$ modulo $7$; the next is
\[
134568=56\cdot2403.
\]
Thus the maximum ascending multiple is $\boxed{134568}$.
