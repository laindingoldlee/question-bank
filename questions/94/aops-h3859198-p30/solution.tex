If $p=2$, parity rules out every $q$. If $p=3$, positivity of $82-q^2$ restricts $q$ to $2,3,5,7$, and only $q=3$ makes both expressions prime. Otherwise $q=3$ makes the first expression divisible by three and greater than three, so $q\ne3$. Then the second expression is divisible by three and must equal three: $q^2=21p+16$. For odd $q$, the factors $q-4,q+4$ are coprime odd integers with product $21p$, so one is a divisor of 21. Testing $q-4\in\{1,3,7,21\}$ or $q+4\in\{1,3,7,21\}$ gives prime solutions $(p,q)=(5,11),(13,17)$. Their first expressions are 41 and 191, both prime. Including $(3,3)$ gives $\boxed{3}$ pairs, choice C.
