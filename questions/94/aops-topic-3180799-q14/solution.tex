The prime-factorization forms are $p^5$ or $p^2q$, with distinct primes $p,q$. The fifth-power form gives 32 only. For the second form, $p$ can be 2, 3, 5, 7. Respectively, the possible $q$ lists are $(3,5,7,11,13,17,19,23)$, $(2,5,7,11)$, $(2,3)$, and $(2)$, giving 8, 4, 2, 1 numbers. Thus the total is $1+8+4+2+1=\boxed{16}$.
