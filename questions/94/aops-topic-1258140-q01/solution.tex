Let $p_n=\dfrac{6-|n-7|}{36}$ be the probability that one roll of two dice has sum $n$. The chance of winning with choice $n$ is $W_n=1-(1-p_n)^n$. For $n\le7$ both $p_n$ and $n$ increase with $n$, so $W_n$ increases: $W_7=1-\left(\dfrac{5}{6}\right)^7\approx0.721$. For $n>7$ the per-roll chance drops. Comparing,
\[W_8=1-\left(\dfrac{31}{36}\right)^8\approx0.698,\quad W_9=1-\left(\dfrac{8}{9}\right)^9\approx0.654,\quad W_{10}\approx0.581,\quad W_{11}\approx0.467,\quad W_{12}\approx0.287,\]
all smaller than $W_7$. So the best choice is $\boxed{n=7}$, with winning probability $1-\left(\dfrac{5}{6}\right)^7=\dfrac{201811}{279936}\approx0.721$.
