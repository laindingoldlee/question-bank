(a) Pick one person $P$. Of the other $5$ people, by the pigeonhole principle at least $3$ are friends of $P$ or at least $3$ are strangers to $P$. Suppose $A$, $B$, $C$ are friends of $P$ (the other case is the same with the words swapped). If any two of $A,B,C$ are friends, those two together with $P$ are three mutual friends. Otherwise $A$, $B$, $C$ are three mutual strangers. Either way the claim holds.

(b) Seat $5$ people around a round table and let two people be friends exactly when they sit next to each other. Any $3$ of the $5$ people include two who are not neighbours (the friendships form a $5$-cycle, which contains no triangle), so there are no three mutual friends. Strangers are pairs at distance two around the table; these also form a $5$-cycle (person $1$ with $3$, $3$ with $5$, $5$ with $2$, $2$ with $4$, $4$ with $1$), so there are no three mutual strangers either. Hence $5$ people are not enough, and $R(3,3)=\boxed{6}$.
