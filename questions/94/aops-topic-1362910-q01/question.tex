The simplest case of Ramsey's theorem says that $R(3,3)=6$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Prove that among any $6$ people there are always $3$ who are mutual friends or $3$ who are mutual strangers (friendship is mutual, and any two people are either friends or strangers).
\item[(b)] Show that this can fail for $5$ people.
\end{enumerate}
