Two distinct lines meet in at most one point, and every intersection point lies on at least one pair of the lines. So $n$ lines have at most $\dbinom n2$ intersection points.

This bound is reached when no two lines are parallel and no three pass through one point: then every pair meets, and different pairs meet at different points. Such lines exist: for example the lines $y=kx+k^2$ for $k=1,2,\ldots,n$. Two of them, with slopes $j\ne k$, meet where $jx+j^2=kx+k^2$, that is at $\bigl(-(j+k),\,-jk\bigr)$; this point determines $j+k$ and $jk$, hence the pair $\{j,k\}$, so different pairs give different points.

Hence the maxima are
\[\binom42=\boxed6,\qquad\binom52=\boxed{10},\qquad\binom{20}{2}=\boxed{190},\qquad\binom n2=\boxed{\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}}.\]
