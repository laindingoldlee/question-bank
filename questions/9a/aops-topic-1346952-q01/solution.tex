Suppose, for contradiction, that $a$, $b$, $c$ are all odd, and let $x=\dfrac {p}{q}$ be a rational root in lowest terms ($q>0$), so $p$ and $q$ are not both even. Multiplying by $q^2$:
\[
ap^2+bpq+cq^2=0 .
\]
\begin{itemize}
\item If $p$ and $q$ are both odd, all three terms are odd, and the sum of three odd numbers is odd, not $0$.
\item If exactly one of $p$, $q$ is even, then exactly two of the terms are even (those containing the even number) and the remaining term ($ap^2$ or $cq^2$) is odd, so the sum is odd, not $0$.
\end{itemize}
Either way we have a contradiction. Hence at least one of $a$, $b$, $c$ is even. $\blacksquare$
