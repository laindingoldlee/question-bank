In an effective first proposal, every losing crew member can give up only one coin, while each winner needs at least one. Since winners outnumber losers, the captain cannot gain and must spend the initial coin. Consequently the first proposal must have $d$ losers, $d+1$ winners, each loser ending at zero and each winner at two, with all others unchanged. The captain then has zero and no crew member has more than two coins. In the second proposal, if there are $u$ losers and $v>u$ winners, then $u+v\le100$ implies $u\le49$. At most $2u$ coins are collected and at least $u+1$ paid out, so the captain gets at most $u-1\le48$. To attain $48$, first make $49$ crew members give up their coins and give those coins plus the captain's coin to $50$ other crew members. Then take both coins from $49$ of those enriched members and give one coin to $50$ of the remaining $51$ crew. The votes are $50$ to $49$ each time, and the second proposal leaves $98-50=48$ coins with the captain. Thus the maximum is $\boxed{48}$.
