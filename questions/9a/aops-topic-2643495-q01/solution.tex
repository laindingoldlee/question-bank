Suppose $a,a+1,\dots,a+n-1$ (with $a\ge1$) add up to $2014$. Then
\[\dfrac{n(2a+n-1)}{2}=2014,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad n(2a+n-1)=4028=2^2\cdot19\cdot53.\]
The two factors $n$ and $2a+n-1$ have opposite parity (their difference $2a-1$ is odd), and $2a+n-1>n$. Since $4028$ has odd part $1007$, one factor must be odd and the other must contain the whole factor $4$. The factorisations into an odd and a multiple of $4$ are $1\cdot4028$, $4\cdot1007$, $19\cdot212$ and $53\cdot76$, so $n\in\{1,4,19,53\}$. The largest is $n=53$, with $2a+52=76$, $a=12$:
\[12+13+\cdots+64=\dfrac{53\cdot76}{2}=2014.\]
The answer is $\boxed{53}$.

(If negative integers were allowed, the $4028$ integers $-2013,-2012,\dots,2014$ would also work.)
