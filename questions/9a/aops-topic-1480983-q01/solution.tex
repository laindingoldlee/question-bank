\textbf{(a)} Let the sides be $a,b,c$ and the semiperimeter $s=\dfrac{a+b+c}{2}$. The triangle inequality says $a<b+c$. Adding $a$ to both sides,
\[
2a<a+b+c=2s,\qquad\text{so } a<s .
\]
The same argument works for $b$ and $c$. Conversely, if three positive lengths are each less than $s$, then for example $a<s$ gives $a<b+c$, so they form a triangle. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{(b)} Here $s=\dfrac{7}{2}$, so every side is at most $3$. Let $a\le b\le c\le3$ with $a+b+c=7$. Then $c\ge\dfrac{7}{3}$, so $c=3$ and $a+b=4$ with $a\le b\le3$: $(a,b)=(1,3)$ or $(2,2)$. Both satisfy the condition of (a), so there are $\boxed{2}$ triangles: $1,3,3$ and $2,2,3$.
