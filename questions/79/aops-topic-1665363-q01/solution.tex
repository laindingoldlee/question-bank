Solve (b) first.

\textbf{(b) Packs of $4$, $6$, $9$.} \emph{Even amounts.} Every even $n\ge4$ can be bought from $4$s and $6$s: if $4\mid n$, use $\dfrac {n}{4}$ fours; otherwise $n\ge6$ and $n-6$ is a multiple of $4$. The even amount $2$ cannot be bought.

\emph{Odd amounts.} An odd amount needs an odd number of $9$s, so at least one. Hence an odd $n$ can be bought exactly when $n-9$ is $0$ or an even amount that can be bought, that is $n-9\in\{0,4,6,8,10,\dots\}$, so $n=9$ or $n\ge13$.

So the amounts that cannot be bought are $1,2,3,5,7,11$, and the greatest is $\boxed{11}$.

\textbf{(a) With the pack of $20$ as well.} Every amount above $11$ could already be bought. And $11$ still cannot: a pack of $20$ is too big to use in an amount of $11$. So the answer is again $\boxed{11}$.

(The formula $ab-a-b$ of the Chicken McNugget theorem is for two coprime pack sizes, so it does not apply directly to four sizes.)
