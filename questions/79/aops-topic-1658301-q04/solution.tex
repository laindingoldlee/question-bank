The first person flicks the switch with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$, independently of everyone else. Fix what persons $2$ to $5$ do; their flicks determine a state $s$ (on or off) that the light would end in if person $1$ did nothing. Person $1$'s flick (probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$) reverses the final state, and no flick (probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$) keeps it. So whatever the others do, the light ends off with conditional probability exactly $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

Hence the probability is $\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}}$.

(Alternatively, the probability of an even number of flicks is $\dfrac{1+\prod_{n}(1-2p_n)}{2}$, and the factor $1-2p_1=0$.)
