Occupied chairs form runs of length at least 2. If there are $k$ runs containing $m$ dolls, their lengths can be chosen in $\binom{m-k-1}{k-1}$ ways. The $10-m$ empty chairs must include at least one between successive runs; distributing the rest among the $k+1$ gaps gives $\binom{11-m}{k}$ choices. One has $1\le k\le3$ and $2k\le m\le11-k$. Summing gives 45 arrangements for $k=1$,126 for $k=2$, and 28 for $k=3$ (the latter contributions are 10, 12, 6 for $m=6,7,8$). Including the empty arrangement gives $\boxed{1+45+126+28=200}$.
