On each original die, $4$ of the $6$ faces ($1,3,5,7$) are odd, so a die shows an odd number with probability $\dfrac23$ and an even number with probability $\dfrac13$, independently.

Rolling the new die picks one of the $6$ pairs of original dice uniformly at random, and the face is even exactly when the two dice of that pair have the same parity. For any fixed pair,
\[P(\text{same parity})=\left(\dfrac23\right)^2+\left(\dfrac13\right)^2=\dfrac59.\]
Averaging over the $6$ equally likely pairs (each contributes $\dfrac16\cdot\dfrac59$), the probability of an even number is $\boxed{\dfrac59}$.
