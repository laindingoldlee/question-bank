Write $Q-P=(k,mk)$, where the finite slope $m$ and nonzero horizontal difference $k$ are integers. If the length is the integer $L$, then
\[
\sqrt{1+m^2}=\dfrac{L}{|k|}
\]
is rational. A rational square root of an integer is an integer: writing it in lowest terms, its denominator must divide its numerator squared and hence equal $1$. Thus $1+m^2=r^2$ for some positive integer $r$. Now
\[
(r-|m|)(r+|m|)=1.
\]
Both factors are positive integers, so both equal $1$ and $m=0$. Therefore the hypotenuse is horizontal. In fact the argument only needs the two endpoints, not the third vertex.
\begin{asy}
texpreamble("\usepackage{amsmath,amssymb}");
size(240);
defaultpen(fontsize(9pt));
pair P=(0,0),Q=(6,0),R=(3,3);draw(P--Q--R--cycle);draw((-1,0)--(7,0),gray);label("$P$",P,SW);label("$Q$",Q,SE);label("$R$",R,N);label("Horizontal hypotenuse",(3,-.5));
\end{asy}
