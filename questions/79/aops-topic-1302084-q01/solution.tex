Call the original face $O$, the opposite face $P$, and each of the four faces adjacent to $O$ an $A$-face. From $O$ the ant always moves to an $A$-face, and from $P$ also. From an $A$-face, of its four neighbours one is $O$, one is $P$, and two are $A$-faces.

Let $(o_t,a_t,p_t)$ be the probabilities of being on $O$, on some $A$-face, and on $P$ after $t$ minutes. Then $o_{t+1}=\dfrac14a_t$, $p_{t+1}=\dfrac14a_t$ and $a_{t+1}=o_t+\dfrac12a_t+p_t$:
\[\begin{array}{c|ccc}
t&o_t&a_t&p_t\\\hline
0&1&0&0\\
1&0&1&0\\[6pt]
2&\dfrac14&\dfrac12&\dfrac14\\[6pt]
3&\dfrac18&\dfrac34&\dfrac18\\[6pt]
4&\dfrac{3}{16}&\dfrac58&\dfrac{3}{16}
\end{array}\]
The probability is $o_4=\boxed{\dfrac{3}{16}}$.
