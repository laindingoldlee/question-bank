\textbf{The factors are coprime.} Any common divisor of $2m+3$ and $3m+4$ divides $3(2m+3)-2(3m+4)=1$, so $\gcd(2m+3,3m+4)=1$.

\textbf{Splitting the primes.} Both factors are at least $3$, so $n\ge1$. Since the product is $5^n7^n$ and the factors are coprime, all the factors $5$ go into one of them and all the factors $7$ into one of them; as neither factor is $1$, one factor is $5^n$ and the other is $7^n$. Since $3m+4>2m+3$,
\[2m+3=5^n,\qquad3m+4=7^n.\]
Eliminating $m$: $m=\dfrac{5^n-3}{2}$, and $3\cdot\dfrac{5^n-3}{2}+4=7^n$, i.e.
\[3\cdot5^n-1=2\cdot7^n.\]
\textbf{Only $n=1$ works.} For $n=1$: $15-1=14=2\cdot7$. For $n\ge2$,
\[2\cdot7^n=2\left(\dfrac75\right)^n5^n\ge2\cdot\dfrac{49}{25}\cdot5^n>3\cdot5^n>3\cdot5^n-1,\]
so there is no solution. With $n=1$ we get $m=\dfrac{5-3}{2}=1$.

The only solution is $\boxed{(m,n)=(1,1)}$: indeed $5\cdot7=35$.
