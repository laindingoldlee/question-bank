Measure weights as percentages of the total. Let the lightest two weigh $a_1\le a_2$, the heaviest three $h_1\le h_2\le h_3$, and suppose there are $k$ other kangaroos, each weighing between $a_2$ and $h_1$. They weigh $100-25-60=15$ in total.
\begin{itemize}
\item Since $a_1+a_2=25$, we get $a_2\ge12.5$; so each of the $k$ middle kangaroos weighs at least $12.5$, and $k\cdot12.5\le15$ gives $k\le1$.
\item If $k=0$, the total would be $25+60=85\ne100$. So $k=1$.
\end{itemize}
Hence there are $2+1+3=\boxed{6}$ kangaroos. Such a group exists, e.g.\ weights $12,13,15,20,20,20$: the two lightest give $25$ and the three heaviest $60$.
