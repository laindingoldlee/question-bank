Since $1+x-2x^2=-(2x+1)(x-1)$, the numbers $a\ne b$ are the two roots $1$ and $-\dfrac12$. So the base of the triangle is the segment from $\left(-\dfrac12,0\right)$ to $(1,0)$ on the $x$-axis, of length $\dfrac32$.

The third vertex lies on the parabola $y=1+x-2x^2$ above the $x$-axis, and the height of the triangle is $d$. Completing the square,
\[1+c-2c^2=\dfrac98-2\left(c-\dfrac14\right)^2\le\dfrac98,\]
with equality at $c=\dfrac14$. So the maximum area is
\[\dfrac12\cdot\dfrac32\cdot\dfrac98=\boxed{\dfrac{27}{32}}.\]

pair V=(0.25,1.125);
fill((-0.5,0)--(1,0)--V--cycle,rgb(1,0.85,0.85)); draw((-0.5,0)--(1,0)--V--cycle,red);
draw(graph(f,-0.62,1.12),blue);
dot((-0.5,0)); dot((1,0)); dot(V);
label("$\left(-\dfrac12,0\right)$",(-0.5,0),SW,fontsize(7pt)); label("$(1,0)$",(1,0),SE,fontsize(7pt)); label("$\left(\dfrac14,\dfrac98\right)$",V,NE,fontsize(7pt));
label("$y=1+x-2x^2$",(0.62,1.0),E,blue+fontsize(7pt));
