Use $B=(0,0)$, $C=(14,0)$, $A=(5,12)$. At an interior minimizer, the weighted unit vectors toward $A,B,C$ sum to zero. Since the weights toward $B,C$ are equal, $XA$ is opposite their internal angle bisector and $\cos\angle BXC=\dfrac{7^2-5^2-5^2}{2\cdot5\cdot5}=-\dfrac{1}{50}$. Put $s=\sqrt{51}$. The circle through $B,C$ on whose upper arc this angle occurs has centre $O=(7,-\dfrac{1}{s})$ and radius $\dfrac{50}{s}$. Its lower arc midpoint is $Y=(7,-s)$. The internal bisector from $X$ passes through $Y$, so $A,X,Y$ are collinear. Write $X=Y+t(A-Y)$. Intersecting this line with the circle, excluding $Y$, gives\[t=\dfrac{100(12+s)}{s(199+24s)},\qquad X=(7-2t,-s+t(12+s))\approx(5.552712,6.710152).\]This point is inside $ABC$ and its unit vectors satisfy the stated equilibrium. The weighted sum of distances is a convex function, so equilibrium certifies a global minimum; strict convexity for three noncollinear fixed vertices gives uniqueness. Let $b=|X-C|$ and $c=|X-B|$. The incenter formula for $XBC$ gives\[I=\dfrac{14X+bB+cC}{14+b+c}\approx(5.960803,2.804428).\]Thus $AI\approx9.245630$. For a check sufficient for rounding, substituting $7.1414284285<s<7.1414284286$ in the exact formulas gives $9.2455<AI<9.2458$, entirely between $8.5$ and $9.5$. Therefore the requested integer is $\boxed9$.
