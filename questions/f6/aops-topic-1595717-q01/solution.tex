In the first layout $14\mid N$ and in the second $12\mid N$, so $N$ is a common multiple of $14=2\cdot7$ and $12=2^2\cdot3$, that is, a multiple of
\[
\operatorname{lcm}(12,14)=2^2\cdot3\cdot7=84 .
\]
The least positive choice is $N=84$. With $14$ suites per story it has $\dfrac{84}{14}=\boxed{6}$ stories (and with $12$ per story, $7$ stories).

The answer is not $0$: although $0$ is a multiple of both $12$ and $14$, a building with $0$ stories has no suites, and the problem is about an actual building, so $N$ must be positive. (Mathematically, ``least common multiple'' always means the least \emph{positive} common multiple for this reason.)
