There are $5\cdot4\cdot3=60$ equally likely numbers.

A multiple of $15$ is a multiple of $5$ and of $3$. It must end in $5$, so it has the form $\overline{ab5}$ with $a\ne b$ from $\{1,2,3,4\}$, and its digit sum $a+b+5$ must be a multiple of $3$, i.e.\ $a+b\equiv1\pmod3$. The pairs from $\{1,2,3,4\}$ have sums $3,4,5,5,6,7$; the sums $\equiv1\pmod 3$ are $4=1+3$ and $7=3+4$. Each pair gives two orders, so the numbers are $135$, $315$, $345$, $435$, and the probability is
\[
\dfrac{4}{60}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{15}}.
\]
