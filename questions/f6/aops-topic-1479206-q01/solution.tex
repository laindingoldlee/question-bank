Take a list with the largest product (there are finitely many lists) and improve it step by step if possible.
\begin{itemize}
\item A $1$ can be added to another number $k$: $k+1>1\cdot k$. So there is no $1$ (the list has more than one entry since $20$ alone is not best).
\item A number $k\ge5$ can be split into $2$ and $k-2$, and $2(k-2)=2k-4>k$. So all entries are at most $4$.
\item A $4$ can be replaced by $2+2$ with the same product, so we may assume there are only $2$s and $3$s.
\item Three $2$s can be replaced by two $3$s: $3\cdot3=9>8=2\cdot2\cdot2$. So there are at most two $2$s.
\end{itemize}
So the list consists of $a$ threes and $b\le2$ twos with $3a+2b=20$. For $b=0$ or $b=2$, $3a=20$ or $3a=16$ is impossible, so $b=1$ and $a=6$. The best list is six $3$s and one $2$, and the maximum product is
\[
3^6\cdot2=\boxed{1458}.
\]
