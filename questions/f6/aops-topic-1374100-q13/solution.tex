Each family has exactly one girl. The number of boys in a family is $k$ with probability $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{k+1}$ (the first $k$ children are boys and the next is a girl), so the expected number of boys per family is
\[
\sum_{k\ge0}k\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{k+1}=\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{2}{8}+\dfrac{3}{16}+\cdots=1 .
\]
So over many families there are, on average, $1$ boy and $1$ girl per family, and by the law of large numbers the fraction of boys tends to
\[
\dfrac{1}{1+1}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}} .
\]
(Every birth is a fair coin flip, whatever the stopping rule, so the stopping rule cannot change the proportion.)
