\textbf{The triangle.} Integer right triangles with perimeter $40$: the multiples of $3$-$4$-$5$ and $5$-$12$-$13$ have perimeters $12k$ and $30k$, which never equal $40$, and the only primitive triple with perimeter dividing $40$ is $8$-$15$-$17$ (perimeter exactly $40$). So the legs are $8$ and $15$ and the hypotenuse is $17$.

\textbf{The crescents.} The two semicircles on the legs together with the triangle cover the semicircle on $DF$ and the two crescents, each exactly once:
\[
\text{crescents}=\left(\text{semicircle on }DE\right)+\left(\text{semicircle on }EF\right)+[DEF]-\left(\text{semicircle on }DF\right).
\]
The semicircle on a segment of length $s$ has area $\dfrac{\pi s^2}{8}$, and $DE^2+EF^2=DF^2$, so the three semicircle terms cancel:
\[
\text{crescents}=\dfrac{\pi}{8}\left(DE^2+EF^2-DF^2\right)+[DEF]=[DEF]=\dfrac{8\cdot15}{2}=\boxed{60}\ \text{m}^2 .
\]
