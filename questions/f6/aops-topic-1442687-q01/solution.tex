\textbf{(a)} A product is $0$ exactly when a factor is $0$, so $(x+r)(x+s)=0$ when $x=-r$ or $x=-s$. The roots are $\boxed{-r\text{ and }-s}$ (not $r$ and $s$).

\textbf{(b)} Expanding, $(x+r)(x+s)=x^2+(r+s)x+rs$, so $p=r+s$ and $q=rs$. The sum of the roots is $(-r)+(-s)=-(r+s)=\boxed{-p}$ and their product is $(-r)(-s)=rs=\boxed{q}$.

\textbf{(c)} $x^2+5x+6=(x+2)(x+3)$, so the roots are $-2$ and $-3$, with sum $-5=-p$ and product $6=q$.

So ``roots'' and ``solutions'' mean the same thing here; the numbers $r$ and $s$ in the factored form are the \emph{negatives} of the roots.
