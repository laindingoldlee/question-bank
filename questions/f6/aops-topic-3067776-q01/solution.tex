The finite geometric identity gives $\dfrac{1}{1+x^2}=\sum_{k=0}^{N}(-1)^kx^{2k}+\dfrac{(-1)^{N+1}x^{2N+2}}{1+x^2}$. Integrating from 0 to 1, the left side is $\arctan1=\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, while the sum integrates to $\sum_{k=0}^{N}\dfrac{(-1)^k}{2k+1}$. The absolute value of the integrated remainder is at most $\int_0^1x^{2N+2}\,dx=\dfrac{1}{2N+3}$, which tends to zero. This proves the identity.
