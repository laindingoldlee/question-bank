A and the dog occupy position four. The cat cannot be in position three (next to the dog) or position one (it needs a left neighbour), so it occupies position two and the cup is in position one. B has the hamster and must be in position one or three.
If B is first, the remaining C,D occupy positions two and three. For C second and D third, the colours are forced to green,yellow,blue,red. For D second and C third, they are red,blue,yellow,green.
If B is third, putting C first would force green second, contradicting D's blue house. Thus D is first and C second, with colours blue,yellow,green,red.
The complete possibilities are:
\[\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{residents left to right}&\text{pets left to right}&\text{colours left to right}&\text{holder}\\
B,C,D,A&\text{hamster,cat,bird,dog}&\text{green,yellow,blue,red}&B\\
B,D,C,A&\text{hamster,cat,bird,dog}&\text{red,blue,yellow,green}&B\\
D,C,B,A&\text{bird,cat,hamster,dog}&\text{blue,yellow,green,red}&D
\end{array}\]
Thus (a) the holder is $\boxed{B\text{ or }D}$, not uniquely determined. (b) Yes: the three possible colour orders are distinct and identify the corresponding row and holder.
