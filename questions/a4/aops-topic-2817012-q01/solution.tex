A $10$-digit number with exactly $z$ zeros has $10-z\ge2$ nonzero digits, the leading digit being nonzero. Choose which of the last $9$ positions hold the zeros: $\binom9z$ ways. The nonzero digits are a composition of $10$ into $10-z$ positive parts; since there are at least $2$ parts, each is at most $9$, so all $\binom9{9-z}=\binom9z$ compositions are valid. Hence $A(z)=\binom9z^2$, and $B(z)=A(9-z)=\binom9{9-z}^2=\binom9z^2=A(z)$. $\blacksquare$
