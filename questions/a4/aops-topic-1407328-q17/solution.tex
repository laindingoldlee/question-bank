We find $2^{2011}\bmod 1000$ using $1000=8\cdot125$. Clearly $2^{2011}\equiv0\pmod 8$. Since $\gcd(2,125)=1$ and $\varphi(125)=100$, Euler's theorem gives $2^{100}\equiv1\pmod{125}$, so
\[
2^{2011}=\left(2^{100}\right)^{20}\cdot2^{11}\equiv2^{11}=2048\equiv48\pmod{125}.
\]
The number $48$ is also divisible by $8$, so $2^{2011}\equiv48\pmod{1000}$: the last three digits are $0,4,8$. Their product is $0\cdot4\cdot8=\boxed{0}$.
