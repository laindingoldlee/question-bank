A point $(a,b)$ lies on $y=f(x)$ exactly when $\left(\dfrac {a}{2},b\right)$ lies on $y=f(2x)$, since $f\left(2\cdot\dfrac {a}{2}\right)=f(a)=b$. So the graph of $y=f(2x)$ is the graph of $y=f(x)$ \emph{compressed horizontally by a factor of $2$} towards the $y$-axis (heights unchanged). By contrast $y=2f(x)$ stretches the graph vertically by $2$, which is a common source of confusion.
