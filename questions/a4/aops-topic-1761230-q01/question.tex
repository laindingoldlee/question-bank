Four fair coins are flipped. A student argues: ``For at least $3$ heads we need three heads and do not care about the last flip, so the probability is $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^3=\dfrac{1}{8}$.'' Find the correct probability that at least $3$ of the flips are heads, and explain the error in the student's argument.
