If $7$ divides one of $a$, $b$, $c$, it divides the product. Otherwise, look at cubes modulo $7$: for $t\equiv1,2,3,4,5,6\pmod7$,
\[
t^3\equiv1,\ 8\equiv1,\ 27\equiv6,\ 64\equiv1,\ 125\equiv6,\ 216\equiv6\pmod 7 .
\]
So each of $a^3,b^3,c^3$ is congruent to $1$ or $-1$ modulo $7$. Three numbers take only two possible residues, so by the pigeonhole principle two of them are congruent, say $a^3\equiv b^3$ (the other cases are the same). Then $7\mid a^3-b^3$, which is a factor of the product. In every case $7$ divides the product. $\blacksquare$
