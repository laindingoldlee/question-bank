Since $AB+BC=2+3=5=AC$, the triangle inequality holds with equality, which happens only when $B$ lies on segment $AC$. (So $A,B,C$ do not form a genuine triangle; that is allowed here, since they are just three points.)

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
draw((0,0)--(5,0)); dot((0,0));dot((2,0));dot((5,0));dot((2.5,0),red);
label("$A$",(0,0),S); label("$B$",(2,0),S); label("$C$",(5,0),S); label("$M$",(2.5,0),N,red);
label("$2$",(1,0),N); label("$3$",(3.5,0),N);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

On the line, $AB=2$ and $AM=\dfrac{5}{2}$, so $MB=\dfrac{5}{2}-2=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}}$.
