Expanding $(1-t^2)^n$ and integrating term by term on $[0,1]$ gives the sum $I_n=\int_0^1(1-t^2)^n\,dt$. Integration by parts yields $I_n=\dfrac{2n}{2n+1}I_{n-1}$, with $I_0=1$. Set $J_n=\sqrt{n+1}I_n$. Then $\left(\dfrac{J_n}{J_{n-1}}\right)^2=\dfrac{4n(n+1)}{(2n+1)^2}<1$. Thus $J_n<J_0=1$ for every positive $n$, proving the strict inequality.
