There are $\dbinom{25}{3}=2300$ triangles with vertices in $V$. Since $25$ is odd, no two vertices are diametrically opposite, so $C$ never lies on a side of such a triangle: it is either inside or outside. The triangle does \emph{not} contain $C$ exactly when its three vertices lie within an arc of less than a semicircle, that is, when going clockwise from one of its vertices, the other two are among the next $12$ vertices.

\begin{asy}

pair[] V={(0.0000,1.0000),(0.2487,0.9686),(0.4818,0.8763),(0.6845,0.7290),(0.8443,0.5358),(0.9511,0.3090),(0.9980,0.0628),(0.9823,-0.1874),(0.9048,-0.4258),(0.7705,-0.6374),(0.5878,-0.8090),(0.3681,-0.9298),(0.1253,-0.9921),(-0.1253,-0.9921),(-0.3681,-0.9298),(-0.5878,-0.8090),(-0.7705,-0.6374),(-0.9048,-0.4258),(-0.9823,-0.1874),(-0.9980,0.0628),(-0.9511,0.3090),(-0.8443,0.5358),(-0.6845,0.7290),(-0.4818,0.8763),(-0.2487,0.9686)};
draw(circle(O,1),gray(0.7)+dashed);
for(int k=0;k<25;++k){draw(V[k]--V[(k+1)%25]); dot(V[k],linewidth(2.5));}
draw((0,1)--(0,-0.99),gray+dotted);
filldraw(V[0]--V[10]--V[17]--cycle,lightgreen+opacity(0.5),heavygreen);
filldraw(V[2]--V[5]--V[8]--cycle,lightred+opacity(0.5),red);
dot(O); label("$C$",O,W);
label("$V_0$",V[0],N); label("$V_{12}$",V[12],S); label("$V_{13}$",V[13],SW);

\end{asy}

(The green triangle contains $C$; the red one does not.) Count the triangles not containing $C$ by their first vertex in clockwise order: choose that vertex ($25$ ways) and the other two among the next $12$ vertices ($\dbinom{12}{2}=66$ ways). Each such triangle is counted exactly once, because the first vertex is the only vertex from which the other two lie within the next $12$. So $25\cdot66=1650$ triangles do not contain $C$, and
\[
2300-1650=\boxed{650}
\]
triangles contain it.
