Put the four positive sides as a,b,c,d, so ac=10 and bd=$\dfrac{13}{2}$. For fixed sides, cyclic placement maximises area. Brahmagupta's formula rearranges to $16K^2=[2(ac+bd)]^2-(a^2+c^2-b^2-d^2)^2$. Hence $K\le\dfrac{(ac+bd)}{2}=\dfrac{33}{4}$. Equality is feasible: take $a=c=\sqrt{10}$ and $b=\dfrac{(\sqrt{33}+\sqrt7)}{2}$, $d=\dfrac{(\sqrt{33}-\sqrt7)}{2}$. These satisfy the products, equal squared-side sums and the strict polygon inequality, so admit a cyclic quadrilateral. Thus the maximum is $\dfrac{33}{4}$ and $m+n=\boxed{37}$.
\begin{asy}
import graph;import geometry;usepackage("amsmath");size(7cm);
real b=(sqrt(33)+sqrt(7))/2,d=(sqrt(33)-sqrt(7))/2,h=sqrt(33)/2;pair A=((b-d)/2,h),B=(0,0),C=(b,0),D=((b+d)/2,h);draw(A--B--C--D--cycle);draw((b/2,0)--(b/2,h),dashed);label("$a=\sqrt{10}$",(A+B)/2,(-1,0));label("$b$",(B+C)/2,(0,-1));label("$c=\sqrt{10}$",(C+D)/2,(1,0));label("$d$",(D+A)/2,(0,1));label("$\dfrac{\sqrt{33}}{2}$",(b/2,h/2),(1,0));
\end{asy}
