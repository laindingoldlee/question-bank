The side of the triangle is $4$, so $PS=2$. In triangle $PTS$ we have $PT=1$, $PS=2$ and $\angle P=60^\circ$, so by the law of cosines $TS^2=1+4-2=3$. Since $PT^2+TS^2=1+3=4=PS^2$, the angle at $T$ is a right angle: $ST\perp PQ$.

Extend $TS$ to meet line $QR$ at $X$. Triangle $QTX$ has a right angle at $T$ and $\angle Q=60^\circ$, so it is a $30^\circ$-$60^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangle with $QT=3$, $QX=6$, $TX=3\sqrt3$.

The quadrilateral $QRST$ lies inside triangle $QTX$, so every circle inside $QRST$ lies inside triangle $QTX$ and has radius at most the inradius of $QTX$:
\[
r=\dfrac{QT+TX-QX}{2}=\dfrac{3+3\sqrt3-6}{2}=\dfrac{3\sqrt3-3}{2}.
\]
This incircle actually fits inside $QRST$. Put $Q=(-2,0)$, $R=(2,0)$, $P=(0,2\sqrt3)$. The incircle touches $QX$ at distance $\dfrac{QT+QX-TX}{2}=\dfrac{9-3\sqrt3}{2}\approx1.90<4=QR$ from $Q$, so its centre is $I=\left(\dfrac{5-3\sqrt3}{2},\,r\right)\approx(-0.10,\,1.10)$. The distance from $I$ to line $RS$ (that is, $\sqrt3x+y=2\sqrt3$) is
\[
\dfrac{2\sqrt3-\sqrt3\cdot\dfrac{5-3\sqrt3}{2}-r}{2}\approx1.27>r ,
\]
so the circle does not cross side $RS$. It lies inside $QRST$, touching $QR$, $ST$ and $TQ$. The largest radius is
\[
\boxed{\dfrac{3\sqrt3-3}{2}\approx1.10}.
\]
