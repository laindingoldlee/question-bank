For square-free $n$, multiplication by $n$ gives $\prod_{p\mid n}(p+1)=2\prod_{p\mid n}p$. If $n$ is odd and has $r$ prime factors, the left side has at least $r$ factors of $2$ while the right side has exactly one. Thus $r\le1$, but $1 + \dfrac{1}{p} \ne 2$ for an odd prime and $n=1$ also fails. If $n=2m$ with $r$ distinct odd prime factors, the right side has exactly two factors of $2$, hence $r\le2$. For $r=0$ the sum is $\dfrac{3}{2}$, not $2$. For $r=1$, $3(p+1)=4p$, so $p=3$ and $n=6$. For $r=2$, $3(p+1)(q+1)=4pq$ implies $(p-3)(q-3)=12$. A prime cannot be $3$, and the only positive even factor pair $2,6$ yields $p,q=5,9$, not two primes. Thus $\boxed{n=6}$.
