No. If there were exactly two distinct intersection points, choose three lines that already produce both points. These three must consist of two parallel lines and a transversal; three nonparallel lines would be concurrent or form three intersection points. A fourth line parallel to the first two creates a third point on the transversal. A fourth line not parallel to them meets both; to avoid new points, it would have to pass through both existing points, making it the same line as the transversal. That is forbidden. Thus four distinct lines cannot have exactly $\boxed2$ distinct intersection points.
