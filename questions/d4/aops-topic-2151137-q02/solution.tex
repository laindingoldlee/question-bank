By symmetry (swap heads and tails), more heads and more tails are equally likely. The probability of equally many, $3$ heads and $3$ tails, is $\dfrac{1}{64}\dbinom63=\dfrac{20}{64}$. So
\[P(\text{more heads})=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1-\dfrac{20}{64}\right)=\dfrac{22}{64}=\boxed{\dfrac{11}{32}}.\]
(Directly: $\dfrac{\binom64+\binom65+\binom66}{64}=\dfrac{15+6+1}{64}$.)
