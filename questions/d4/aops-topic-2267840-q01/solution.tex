Consider $p(x)=x\,q(x)-1$, a polynomial of degree $99$. For $k=1,2,\ldots,99$ we have $p(k)=k\cdot\dfrac{1}{k}-1=0$, so $p$ has the $99$ roots $1,\ldots,99$ and
\[p(x)=c(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-99)\]
for some constant $c$. Putting $x=0$: $p(0)=-1$, while the right side is $c\cdot(-1)^{99}\cdot99!=-c\cdot99!$. So $c=\dfrac{1}{99!}$.

Then $p(100)=\dfrac{99\cdot98\cdots1}{99!}=1$, so $100\,q(100)-1=1$ and
\[q(100)=\dfrac{2}{100}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{50}}.\]
