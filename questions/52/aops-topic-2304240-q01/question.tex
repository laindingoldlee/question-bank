A trick for the $19$ times table: to find $19\cdot k$ for $k=1,2,\dots,9$, write the odd number $2k-1$ followed by the digit $10-k$. For example, $19\cdot3$ gives $5$ followed by $7$, that is, $57$, and $19\cdot7$ gives $13$ followed by $3$, that is, $133$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Prove that the trick always gives the right answer for $k=1,\dots,9$.
\item[(b)] Show how the same identity still gives $19\cdot12$, even though $10-k$ is no longer a digit.
\end{enumerate}
