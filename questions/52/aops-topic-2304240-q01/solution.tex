\textbf{(a)} Writing $2k-1$ followed by the single digit $10-k$ produces the number
\[10(2k-1)+(10-k)=20k-10+10-k=19k.\]
For $k=1,\dots,9$, the number $10-k$ is a digit from $9$ down to $1$, so the written number really is $10(2k-1)+(10-k)$, and the trick gives $19k$. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{(b)} The identity $19k=10(2k-1)+(10-k)$ holds for every $k$. For $k=12$ it gives $10\cdot23+(-2)=230-2=228$, and indeed $19\cdot12=\boxed{228}$; the ``digit'' $-2$ must be handled by borrowing.
