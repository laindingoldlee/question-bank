Among $1,2,\dots,m$ there are $\lfloor\sqrt m\rfloor$ perfect squares, so the number of non-squares up to $m$ is
\[
N(m)=m-\lfloor\sqrt m\rfloor .
\]
$N$ increases by $1$ at each non-square and stays the same at each square. The $1000$th non-square is the smallest $m$ with $N(m)=1000$.

Try $m$ near $1000+31$: for $1024=32^2\le m<33^2=1089$ we have $N(m)=m-32$, so $N(m)=1000$ gives $m=1032$. Since $1032$ is not a square and $N(1031)=999$, the $1000$th non-square is $\boxed{1032}$.

(Check: the squares up to $1032$ are $1^2,\dots,32^2$, so $1032-32=1000$ non-squares.)
