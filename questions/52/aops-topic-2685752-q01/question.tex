Let $a,b,c$ be real numbers with $a\ne0$. The roots of $ax^2+bx+c=0$ are $x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If $b^2-4ac<0$, show that the two roots are complex conjugates of each other.
\item[(b)] If $a,b,c$ are rational and $d=b^2-4ac$ is positive but not the square of a rational number, show that the roots have the form $p+q\sqrt d$ and $p-q\sqrt d$ with $p,q$ rational and $q\ne0$.
\item[(c)] Explain why the denominator $2a$ does not affect these conclusions.
\end{enumerate}
