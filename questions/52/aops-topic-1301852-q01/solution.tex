Since $f$ has at most one real root, its discriminant is $b^2-4ac\le0$, and $f(x)=a\left(x+\dfrac{b}{2a}\right)^2+\dfrac{4ac-b^2}{4a}$ never changes sign: it has the sign of $a$ wherever it is nonzero. But $f(-1)=a-b+c>0$, so that sign is positive, i.e. $a>0$, and then $f(x)\ge\dfrac{4ac-b^2}{4a}\ge0$ for every $x$. $\blacksquare$

(The condition $a\ne0$ is needed: $f(x)=x+2$ has no two distinct roots and $a+c=2>b=1$, but $f(-3)<0$.)
