The mixed number $a\dfrac {b}{c}$ equals $a+\dfrac {b}{c}$, so the mean is the mean of the whole parts plus the mean of the fractional parts.

\textbf{Whole parts.} Choosing $a$ first ($4$ ways) and then the pair $b<c$ from the other three numbers ($3$ ways) gives the $12$ mixed numbers, and each of $1,2,3,5$ is the whole part exactly $3$ times. So the mean whole part is $\dfrac{1+2+3+5}{4}=\dfrac{11}{4}$.

\textbf{Fractional parts.} Each of the $6$ pairs $b<c$ occurs exactly twice (with either of the two remaining numbers as $a$). So the mean fractional part is the mean of the six fractions
\[
\dfrac{1}{2},\ \dfrac{1}{3},\ \dfrac{1}{5},\ \dfrac{2}{3},\ \dfrac{2}{5},\ \dfrac{3}{5},
\]
whose sum is $\dfrac{1}{2}+\left(\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{2}{3}\right)+\left(\dfrac{1}{5}+\dfrac{2}{5}+\dfrac{3}{5}\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}+1+\dfrac{6}{5}=\dfrac{27}{10}$; the mean is $\dfrac{27}{60}=\dfrac{9}{20}$.

\textbf{Mean.}
\[
\dfrac{11}{4}+\dfrac{9}{20}=\dfrac{55+9}{20}=\dfrac{64}{20}=\dfrac{16}{5}=\boxed{3\dfrac{1}{5}}.
\]
The symmetry argument replaces adding all twelve mixed numbers by hand.
