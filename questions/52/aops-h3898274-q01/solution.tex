Subtracting pairs of equations gives $(x-z)(x+z-y-1)=0$, $(y-x)(x+y-z-1)=0$, and $(z-y)(y+z-x-1)=0$. If all three variables were distinct, the three linear factors would vanish and force $x=y=z=1$, a contradiction. If all are equal to $t$, the equations give $t^2+t=0$, so $t=0,-1$, producing magnitudes $0,8$. Otherwise exactly two are equal, say $x=y=t\ne z$. The first factored equation forces $z=1$, and substitution gives $t^2=-1$, so $t=\pm i$. These and their permutations all satisfy the system and give magnitude $4$. The distinct values are $0,4,8$, whose sum is $\boxed{12}$.
