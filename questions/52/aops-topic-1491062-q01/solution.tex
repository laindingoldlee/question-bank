\textbf{(a)}
\begin{itemize}
\item $2^x$ is defined for all real $x$ and is always positive. Every $y>0$ is attained, at $x=\log_2y$. Range: $(0,\infty)$.
\item $\log x$ is defined for $x>0$, and every real $y$ is attained, at $x=10^y$ (or $e^y$ for the natural logarithm). Range: all real numbers.
\item $x^2+1\ge1$, with equality at $x=0$, and every $y\ge1$ is attained, at $x=\sqrt{y-1}$. Range: $[1,\infty)$.
\end{itemize}
A good general method: to find the range, solve $y=f(x)$ for $x$ and ask for which $y$ a solution in the domain exists.

\textbf{(b)} Each application of $f$ adds $2$: $f(f(x))=x+4$, $f(f(f(x)))=x+6$, and so on. After six applications,
\[
f^6(x)=\boxed{x+12}.
\]
