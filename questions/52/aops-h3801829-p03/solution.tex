Axis-aligned squares contribute $3^2+2^2+1^2=14$. For a tilted square, take a lower side vector $(a,b)$ with positive integers $a,b$; its bounding box has side $a+b\le3$. Vector $(1,1)$ gives four placements, and $(1,2),(2,1)$ give one each. Thus there are six tilted squares, for a total of $\boxed{20}$.

texpreamble("\usepackage{amsmath,amssymb}");

label("Examples of two tilted lattice squares",(1.5,-.5));
\end{asy}
