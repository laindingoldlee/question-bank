\textbf{(a)} The ratio says $\dfrac{L_1+0.05}{L_2+0.05}=15$, so
\[L_1+0.05=15(L_2+0.05)=15L_2+0.75\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\boxed{L_1=15L_2+0.7}.\]

\textbf{(b)} No. There is one equation and two unknowns: every value of $L_2$ (with $L_2\ne-0.05$) gives a matching $L_1$. A second, independent piece of information is needed to pin both down.

\textbf{(c)} $L_1=15\cdot0.2+0.7=\boxed{3.7}$. (Check: $\dfrac{3.75}{0.25}=15$.)
