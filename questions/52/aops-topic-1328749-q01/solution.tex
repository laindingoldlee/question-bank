We show the answer is $\boxed{59}=7\cdot11-7-11$.

\textbf{$59$ is impossible.} If $7a+11b=59$ with $a,b\ge0$, then $11b\equiv59\pmod7$, i.e. $4b\equiv3\pmod7$, so $b\equiv6\pmod7$; but $b=6$ already gives $66>59$.

\textbf{Every score from $60$ on is possible.} The seven consecutive scores
\[
60=7\cdot7+11,\ 61=7\cdot4+11\cdot3,\ 62=7\cdot1+11\cdot5,\ 63=7\cdot9,\ 64=7\cdot6+11\cdot2,\ 65=7\cdot3+11\cdot4,\ 66=11\cdot6
\]
are all obtainable, and adding groups of $7$ to them reaches every larger score.
