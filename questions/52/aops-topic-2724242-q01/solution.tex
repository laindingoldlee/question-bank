In ASA the given side lies \emph{between} the two given angles; in AAS it is opposite one of them. Suppose triangles $ABC$ and $A'B'C'$ have $\angle A=\angle A'$, $\angle B=\angle B'$ and $BC=B'C'$ (AAS). Since the angles of a triangle add up to $180^\circ$, also $\angle C=180^\circ-\angle A-\angle B=\angle C'$. Now $BC$ is the side between the angles at $B$ and $C$, so the triangles are congruent by ASA. $\blacksquare$

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(3,0),C=(1,1.8);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.8));
draw(arc(A,0.4,0,degrees(C-A)));draw(arc(B,0.4,degrees(C-B),180));draw(arc(B,0.5,degrees(C-B),180));
label("$A$",A,SW);label("$B$",B,SE);label("$C$",C,N);
\end{asy}
