Both fractions are positive integers, and their squared difference is $\left(\dfrac{m^2+1}{2}\right)^2-\left(\dfrac{m^2-1}{2}\right)^2=m^2$. Thus the Pythagorean equation holds. The two fractional entries differ by $1$, so their gcd is $1$; any common divisor of all three entries must therefore be $1$. More strongly, any common divisor of $m$ and either other entry divides both $m^2$ and $m^2\pm1$, so is also $1$. Hence the triple is primitive.
