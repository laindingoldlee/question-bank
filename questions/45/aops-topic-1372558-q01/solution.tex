By definition, $a\equiv b\pmod m$ means that $m$ divides $a-b$. Here
\[
9-(-1)=10=5\cdot2,
\]
so $5$ divides $9-(-1)$ and $-1\equiv9\pmod5$. Equivalently, $-1$ and $9$ leave the same remainder on division by $5$.

Division with remainder writes $-1=5q+r$ with an integer $q$ and $0\le r<5$. Taking $q=-1$ gives $-1=5\cdot(-1)+4$, so the remainder is $\boxed{4}$. And $9=5\cdot1+4$ also has remainder $4$, which is another way to see the congruence. (The examples $8\equiv2$ and $14\equiv0$ work the same way: $8-2=6$ and $14-0=14$ are multiples of $3$ and $7$.)
