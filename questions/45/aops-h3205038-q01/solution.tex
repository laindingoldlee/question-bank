Take $A$ as the origin and unit vectors $u,v$ along $AB,AC$. Then $B=4u,C=6v$, and the cosine rule gives $u\cdot v=\dfrac{9}{16}$. Write $D=du,E=ev$, using signed distances. The condition is $|d-4|=|e-6|>0$, so $d-4=\pm(e-6)$.

A circle through the origin has equation $|X|^2=w\cdot X$. For the first circle, $w\cdot u=4$, $w\cdot v=6$; for the second, $w'\cdot u=d$, $w'\cdot v=e$. Their radical axis $AP$ is perpendicular to $w'-w$. It is consequently parallel to $u-v$ when $d-4=e-6$, and to $u+v$ when $d-4=-(e-6)$.

For a unit direction $n$ along this axis, substitution $X=tn$ into the first circle gives the nonzero intersection parameter $t=w\cdot n$. Hence the two squared lengths are
\[
\dfrac{(4-6)^2}{|u-v|^2}
=\dfrac{4}{2-\dfrac{9}{8}}=\dfrac{32}{7},
\quad
\dfrac{(4+6)^2}{|u+v|^2}
=\dfrac{100}{2+\dfrac{9}{8}}=32.
\]
Both occur, for example with $(d,e)=(5,7)$ and $(5,5)$, respectively; their triangles are nondegenerate. The sum is $\dfrac{256}{7}$, so $\boxed{p+q=263}$.
