The middle digit of $x$ must be $4$, so $x=\overline{a4c}=100a+40+c$ with digits $a\ge1$ and $c$, and cancelling the $4$s gives $\dfrac{22}{10a+c}$. The values must be equal:
\[
\dfrac{242}{100a+40+c}=\dfrac{22}{10a+c}\iff 11(10a+c)=100a+40+c\iff a+c=4 .
\]
So $x\in\{143,242,341,440\}$, giving
\[
\dfrac{242}{143}=\dfrac{22}{13},\qquad \dfrac{242}{242}=\dfrac{22}{22},\qquad \dfrac{242}{341}=\dfrac{22}{31},\qquad \dfrac{242}{440}=\dfrac{22}{40}.
\]
The values are equal in every case, but the problem asks for the cancelling to produce the \emph{common fraction}, that is, the fraction in lowest terms, as it did for Monica ($\dfrac{14}{23}$ is in lowest terms). For $x=440$ cancelling gives $\dfrac{22}{40}$, which is not in lowest terms ($\dfrac{242}{440}=\dfrac{11}{20}$), and for $x=242$ it gives $\dfrac{22}{22}$, not $1$. The fractions $\dfrac{22}{13}$ and $\dfrac{22}{31}$ are in lowest terms, so the greatest possible value is $x=\boxed{341}$.
