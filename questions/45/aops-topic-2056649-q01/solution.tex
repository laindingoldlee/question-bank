For $\dfrac{(x-h)^2}{a^2}-\dfrac{(y-k)^2}{b^2}=1$, the asymptotes are obtained by replacing $1$ with $0$: far from the center the constant $1$ is negligible compared with the two squared terms. Here
\[\dfrac{(x-3)^2}{16}=\dfrac{(y+2)^2}{4}\iff \dfrac{x-3}{4}=\pm\dfrac{y+2}{2}\iff y+2=\pm\dfrac{1}{2}(x-3).\]
So the asymptotes are the two lines through the center $(3,-2)$ with slopes $\pm\dfrac{1}{2}$:
\[\boxed{y+2=\dfrac{1}{2}(x-3)\quad\text{and}\quad y+2=-\dfrac{1}{2}(x-3)}.\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(8cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
import graph;
real a=4, bb=2; pair c0=(3,-2);
pair hp(real t){return c0+(a*cosh(t),bb*sinh(t));}
pair hm(real t){return c0+(-a*cosh(t),bb*sinh(t));}
draw(graph(hp,-1.25,1.25),blue+linewidth(0.9));
draw(graph(hm,-1.25,1.25),blue+linewidth(0.9));
draw((-5.5,-2+(-5.5-3)/2)--(11.5,-2+(11.5-3)/2),red+dashed);
draw((-5.5,-2-(-5.5-3)/2)--(11.5,-2-(11.5-3)/2),red+dashed);
draw((-6,0)--(12,0),gray,Arrow);draw((0,-7)--(0,3.5),gray,Arrow);
label("$x$",(12,0),E);label("$y$",(0,3.5),N);
dot(c0);label("$(3,-2)$",c0,S);
label("$y+2=\dfrac{1}{2}(x-3)$",(11.5,-2+(11.5-3)/2),E,red);
label("$y+2=-\dfrac{1}{2}(x-3)$",(11.5,-2-(11.5-3)/2),E,red);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The two forms agree. Multiplying $y+2=\pm\dfrac{1}{2}(x-3)$ by $2$ gives $2(y+2)=\pm(x-3)$, that is, $x-3=\pm2(y+2)$ (the sign $\pm$ absorbs the sign change). The book's $\pm\dfrac{2}{4}$ is just $\pm\dfrac{1}{2}$ unsimplified. So the student's equations and the book's equations describe the same pair of lines; only the form differs (the book's is solved for $y$, which shows the slopes directly).
