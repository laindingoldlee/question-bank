Subtracting gives $(x-y)(x+y+1)=0$. On $x=y$, solutions satisfy $x^2-x-C=0$. On $x+y=-1$, they satisfy $x^2+x+1-C=0$. For $C>\dfrac{3}{4}$, both branches have two distinct real solutions, all four distinct. At $C=\dfrac{3}{4}$, the second branch has the repeated solution $(-\dfrac{1}{2},-\dfrac{1}{2})$, already on the first branch; the other is $(\dfrac{3}{2},\dfrac{3}{2})$. Thus exactly two distinct solutions remain and all algebraic solutions are real. No larger $C$ works, so the maximum is $\boxed{\dfrac{3}{4}}$.
