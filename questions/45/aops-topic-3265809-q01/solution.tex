F's maximum is at most $19$, so M's maximum is at most $17$. Thus M's sum is at most $85$ and F's sum is below $85$. A sum of five odd primes is odd; an even sum requires a score of $2$, in which case the sum is at most $2+4(19)=78$. Consequently F's sum is at most $83$.
The lists $(7,19,19,19,19)$ and $(13,13,19,19,19)$ both sum to $83$ and work with M's list $(17,17,17,17,17)$. Hence the maximum mean is $\dfrac{83}{5}$.
For completeness, at this maximum M must have all five scores $17$: changing even one to a smaller prime reduces its sum to at most $81$. F's unique mode must therefore be $19$. If four scores are $19$, the remaining one is $7$. If three are $19$, the other two sum to $26$ and, excluding another $19$, must both be $13$. If exactly two are $19$, the other three primes below $19$ sum to $45$ only as $11,17,17$, which creates a mode tie. One occurrence of $19$ cannot be a unique mode. Therefore F's possible smallest scores are $\boxed{7\text{ and }13}$.
