(a) The differences are $3,4,5,\dots$: these are triangular numbers starting from the second, $f(n)=\dfrac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2}$.

(b) Squares: $f(n)=n^2$.

(c) One less than the squares $4,9,16,\dots$: $f(n)=(n+1)^2-1=n(n+2)$.

(d) Subtract $2$: $2,4,8,16,\dots$, powers of $2$. So $f(n)=2^n+2$.

(e) Products of consecutive integers: $1\cdot2,2\cdot3,\dots$, so $f(n)=n(n+1)$.

$\boxed{\text{(a) }\dfrac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2},\ \text{(b) }n^2,\ \text{(c) }n(n+2),\ \text{(d) }2^n+2,\ \text{(e) }n(n+1)}$. Each formula reproduces all seven given terms (a finite list never determines a formula uniquely, but these are the natural ones).
