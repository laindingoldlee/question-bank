\emph{Newton's little formula} says: if a sequence $f(1),f(2),f(3),\dots$ has first term $f(1)$, first difference $\Delta_1=f(2)-f(1)$, second difference $\Delta_2$, third difference $\Delta_3$, \dots\ (each taken at the start of the difference table), and some row of differences is constant, then
\[
f(n)=f(1)+\dbinom{n-1}{1}\Delta_1+\dbinom{n-1}{2}\Delta_2+\dbinom{n-1}{3}\Delta_3+\cdots .
\]
The sequence $1,5,14,30,\dots$ has constant third differences. Use the formula to find its $10$th term, and verify the formula for this sequence for $n=5$.
