Let the integers be $n$, $n+2$, $n+4$ (consecutive even or consecutive odd integers differ by $2$). The condition is
\[
4n=2(n+4)+6\iff 4n=2n+14\iff n=7 .
\]
\textbf{(a)} The only solution is $n=7$, which is odd. So no three consecutive \emph{even} integers satisfy the condition.

\textbf{(b)} With $n=7$ the integers are $\boxed{7,\ 9,\ 11}$, which are consecutive odd integers. Check: $4\cdot7=28$ and $2\cdot11+6=28$.
