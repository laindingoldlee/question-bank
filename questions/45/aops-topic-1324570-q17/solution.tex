Put a single green ball in one box and the other $9$ balls ($4$ green, $5$ red) in the other. Then
\[
P(\text{win})=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot1+\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{4}{9}=\dfrac{13}{18}.
\]
This is the best possible. One box can give probability at most $1$, and if box $1$ has $g$ green and $r$ red balls, box $2$ has $5-g$ green and $5-r$ red. Making box $1$ all green ($r=0$) gives $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{5-g}{10-g}$, which is largest for $g=1$; and any distribution with red balls in both boxes is beaten by moving the red balls of the box with the higher green fraction into the other box. (A check over all $35$ distributions confirms the maximum.) The answer is $\boxed{\dfrac{13}{18}}$.
