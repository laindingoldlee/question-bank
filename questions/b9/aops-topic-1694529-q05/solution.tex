Let the digits be $a\ge1$, $b$, $c$ with $a+b+c=12$ and all digits at most $9$. Put $a'=a-1$: then $a'+b+c=11$ with $a'\le8$, $b,c\le9$. Without upper bounds there are $\dbinom{13}{2}=78$ solutions in non-negative integers. Subtract those that break a bound: $a'\ge9$ leaves $a''+b+c=2$, $\dbinom42=6$ solutions; $b\ge10$ leaves a sum of $1$, $\dbinom32=3$ solutions; the same for $c\ge10$: $3$. No two bounds can fail together. So there are $78-6-3-3=\boxed{66}$ pretty numbers.
