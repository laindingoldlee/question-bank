For the first square, similar triangles give side length $s_1=\dfrac{ab}{a+b}$. For the second, the altitude to the hypotenuse is $h=\dfrac{ab}{c}$. At distance $s_2$ from that hypotenuse, the triangle's parallel cross-section has length $c(1-\dfrac{s_2}{h})$. This equals the square side, so
\[s_2=\dfrac{ch}{c+h}=\dfrac{abc}{c^2+ab}.\]
Both are positive, and $s_1>s_2$ is equivalent to $c^2+ab>c(a+b)$. Since $c^2=a^2+b^2$,
\[c^2+ab-c(a+b)=\dfrac{(a+b-c)^2}{2}>0,\]
where strictness follows from the triangle inequality. Thus the square at the right-angle vertex is always strictly larger; the two squares are never congruent in a nondegenerate triangle.
