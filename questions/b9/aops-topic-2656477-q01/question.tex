Let $n\ge1$ be an integer. Prove that for any $n+1$ points $(x_0,y_0),(x_1,y_1),\dots,(x_n,y_n)$ with distinct $x_i$, there is exactly one polynomial $p$ of degree at most $n$ such that $p(x_i)=y_i$ for every $i$. Show also that $n$ points are not enough to determine a polynomial of degree $n$.
