Kyle has three identical chocolate bars, each a rectangular block of length $6$ and height (thickness) $1$; its width is not given. He gives one bar to each of his friends Meit and RJ and keeps one himself. Meit makes two straight vertical cuts from one corner of the top face of his bar that trisect the right angle at that corner, and one of the cuts runs along a diagonal of the top face (so the bar breaks into $3$ pieces). Meit gives the smallest piece to Kyle, keeps the biggest piece and gives the remaining piece to RJ. Kyle and RJ still have their own full bars. Let $K$, $R$ and $M$ be the total volumes of chocolate that Kyle, RJ and Meit now have. Find $K\cdot(R-M)$, assuming that the length $6$ is the longer side of the top face.
