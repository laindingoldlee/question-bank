Six people randomly sit down at a table with six chairs, one chair assigned to each person, all $6!$ seatings being equally likely.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] What is the probability that exactly three of them sit in their assigned seats? Express your answer as a common fraction.
\item[(b)] A student argues: ``The probability that persons $1,2,3$ are correct and $4,5,6$ are wrong is $\dfrac{1}{6}\cdot\dfrac{1}{5}\cdot\dfrac{1}{4}\cdot\dfrac{2}{3}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}$; multiply by $\dbinom63$ to get $\dfrac{1}{18}$. But if person $4$ sits in person $5$'s seat (probability $\dfrac{1}{3}$), then person $5$ is automatically wrong and person $6$ must be right, so this case, of probability $\dbinom63\cdot\dfrac{1}{6}\cdot\dfrac{1}{5}\cdot\dfrac{1}{4}\cdot\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{1}{18}$, must be subtracted, giving $0$.'' Explain what is wrong.
\end{enumerate}
