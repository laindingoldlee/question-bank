Suppose such an $f$ exists. Put $x=2$ and then $x=\dfrac{1}{2}$:
\[
f(2)+f\!\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)=2,\qquad f\!\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)+f(2)=\dfrac{1}{2} .
\]
The two left-hand sides are the same number, so $2=\dfrac{1}{2}$, a contradiction. Hence no such function exists.

In general, replacing $x$ by $\dfrac{1}{x}$ leaves the left-hand side unchanged, so the right-hand side would have to satisfy $x=\dfrac{1}{x}$ for all $x\ne0$, which holds only for $x=\pm1$.
