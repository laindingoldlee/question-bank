The angle-bisector theorem in the two triangles gives $\dfrac{BP}{PD}=\dfrac{AB}{AD}=\dfrac{BC}{CD}=\dfrac{7}{9}$, so $AD=\dfrac{990}{7}$. Thus $BD<110+\dfrac{990}{7}=\dfrac{1760}{7}$, and the largest candidate is $251$. It is attainable: construct triangles with common base $BD=251$ and side pairs $(110,\dfrac{990}{7})$ and $(140,180)$ on opposite sides. Each satisfies the strict triangle inequalities. Their perpendicular projections onto $BD$ are internal, so their union is convex. The two angle bisectors meet the same point dividing $BD$ in the ratio $7:9$. Hence $\boxed{251}$.

real L=251,xa=(110^2+L^2-(990.0/7)^2)/(2L),xc=(140^2+L^2-180^2)/(2L);pair B=(0,0),D=(L,0),A=(xa,sqrt(110^2-xa^2)),C=(xc,-sqrt(140^2-xc^2)),P=(7L/16,0);draw(A--B--C--D--cycle);draw(B--D,dashed);draw(A--P--C);label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,W);label("$C$",C,S);label("$D$",D,E);label("$P$",P,NE);
\end{asy}
