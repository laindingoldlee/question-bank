\emph{Yes.} For every positive integer $k$, take $b=5k$ and $c=6k^2$:
\[x^2+5kx+6k^2=(x+2k)(x+3k),\qquad x^2+5kx-6k^2=(x+6k)(x-k).\]
Both have integer roots, and different $k$ give different quadratics, so there are $\boxed{\text{infinitely many}}$.

(How to find it: integer roots require $b^2-4c=m^2$ and $b^2+4c=n^2$, so $m^2+n^2=2b^2$. The smallest instance $1^2+7^2=2\cdot5^2$ gives $b=5$, $c=\dfrac{49-1}{8}=6$, and scaling by $k$ preserves everything. Without the condition $c\ne0$, the quadratics $x^2+bx$ would already give a trivial infinite family.)
