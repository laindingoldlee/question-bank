Every $k(j)\ge1$. Only perfect powers can contribute more than $1$. Up to $X$, there are at most $\sum_{r=2}^{\lfloor\log_2X\rfloor} \lfloor X^{\dfrac{1}{r}} \rfloor \le \sqrt{X} \log_2 X$ perfect powers, counting repetitions if necessary. Also $k(j)\le\log_2X$. Hence the excess above the baseline of $N$ is at most $\sqrt{N+1}(\log_2(N+1))^2$. Dividing by $N$, this tends to zero. The squeeze theorem gives the limit $\boxed1$.
