\textbf{(a)} Positive lengths $x,y,z$ are the sides of a (nondegenerate) triangle exactly when each is less than the sum of the other two:
\[
z<x+y,\qquad x<y+z,\qquad y<x+z .
\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0), B=(5,0), C=(1.6,2.4);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
label("$x$",(A+B)/2,S); label("$y$",(B+C)/2,NE); label("$z$",(A+C)/2,NW);
dot(A); dot(B); dot(C);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The last two inequalities say $z>x-y$ and $z>y-x$, that is $z>|x-y|$. So the possible third sides are exactly
\[
|x-y|<z<x+y .
\]
Every such $z$ works, since then all three triangle inequalities hold.

\textbf{(b)} The integers strictly between $|x-y|$ and $x+y$ are $|x-y|+1,\dots,x+y-1$, which number
\[
(x+y-1)-|x-y|=\boxed{2\min(x,y)-1}.
\]
For example, with sides $3$ and $7$ the third side can be $5,6,7,8,9$: five values, and $2\cdot3-1=5$.
