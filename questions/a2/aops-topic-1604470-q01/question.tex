Let $ABC$ be a triangle with side lengths $a=BC$, $b=CA$, $c=AB$, area $K$ and semiperimeter $s=\dfrac{a+b+c}{2}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Prove that the bisectors of the exterior angles of the triangle at $B$ and at $C$ and the bisector of the interior angle at $A$ pass through one point $I_A$, and that some circle centred at $I_A$ is tangent to line $BC$ and to the extensions of sides $AB$ and $AC$ beyond $B$ and $C$. (This circle is the \emph{excircle} of the triangle opposite $A$.)
\item[(b)] Prove that the radius of this circle is $r_A=\dfrac{K}{s-a}$.
\end{enumerate}
