\textbf{(a)} The exterior angles at $B$ and $C$ (on the side of line $BC$ away from $A$) are $180^\circ-B$ and $180^\circ-C$. Their bisectors make angles $90^\circ-\dfrac {B}{2}$ and $90^\circ-\dfrac {C}{2}$ with segment $BC$, on the same side of it, and these add up to $180^\circ-\dfrac{B+C}{2}<180^\circ$. So the two bisectors meet at a point $I_A$ on the opposite side of line $BC$ from $A$.

A point lies on the bisector of an angle exactly when it is equidistant from the two sides of the angle (inside the angle). So $I_A$, being on the exterior bisector at $B$, is equidistant from lines $AB$ and $BC$; being on the exterior bisector at $C$, it is equidistant from lines $BC$ and $CA$. Let $r_A$ be this common distance. Then $I_A$ is at distance $r_A$ from lines $AB$ and $AC$, and it lies inside angle $BAC$ (it is beyond $BC$, between rays $AB$ and $AC$), so it lies on the bisector of the interior angle at $A$. This proves the concurrency.

The circle with centre $I_A$ and radius $r_A$ is at distance $r_A$ from each of the three lines, so it is tangent to all three: it touches line $BC$ at the foot $X$ of the perpendicular from $I_A$, and lines $AC$, $AB$ at the feet $Y$, $Z$. Since $I_A$ is in the exterior angle at $B$, the foot $Z$ on line $AB$ lies beyond $B$, and similarly $Y$ lies beyond $C$:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(1.3,3.2),B=(0,0),C=(4,0);
real a=abs(B-C),bb=abs(C-A),c=abs(A-B);
pair J=(-a*A+bb*B+c*C)/(-a+bb+c);
real s=(a+bb+c)/2; real K=abs(cross(B-A,C-A))/2; real ra=K/(s-a);
pair TA=B+((s-c)/a)*(C-B);
pair TC=A+s*unit(B-A), TB=A+s*unit(C-A);
draw(circle(J,ra),gray);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(B--TC,linewidth(0.9));draw(C--TB,linewidth(0.9));
draw(A--J,blue+dashed);
draw(B--J,red+dashed);draw(C--J,red+dashed);
draw(J--TA,deepgreen);draw(J--TB,deepgreen);draw(J--TC,deepgreen);
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);dot(J);dot(TA,deepgreen);dot(TB,deepgreen);dot(TC,deepgreen);
label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,W);label("$C$",C,NE);label("$I_A$",J,S);
label("$X$",TA,N,deepgreen);label("$Y$",TB,E,deepgreen);label("$Z$",TC,W,deepgreen);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\textbf{(b)} The quadrilateral $ABI_AC$ is the union of triangles $ABI_A$ and $ACI_A$, and also the union of triangles $ABC$ and $BI_AC$. The three triangles $ABI_A$, $ACI_A$, $BCI_A$ have heights $r_A$ onto the lines $AB$, $AC$, $BC$ (the radii $I_AZ$, $I_AY$, $I_AX$). Hence
\[
K+\dfrac{1}{2}a\,r_A=\dfrac{1}{2}c\,r_A+\dfrac{1}{2}b\,r_A,\qquad\text{so}\qquad K=\dfrac{r_A(b+c-a)}{2}=r_A(s-a),
\]
and $r_A=\dfrac{K}{s-a}$. $\blacksquare$

(For comparison, the incircle has radius $r=\dfrac {K}{s}$; there are three excircles, one opposite each vertex.)
