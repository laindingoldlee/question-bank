Let $\gamma$ be the angle opposite the longest side $c$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(9,0),C=(94/18,sqrt(49-(94/18)^2));
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(arc(C,0.9,degrees(A-C),degrees(B-C)),red);
label("$\gamma$",C+1.3*unit(unit(A-C)+unit(B-C)),red);
label("$A$",A,SW); label("$B$",B,SE); label("$C$",C,N);
label("$c$",(A+B)/2,S); label("$b$",(A+C)/2,NW); label("$a$",(B+C)/2,NE);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The largest angle of a triangle is opposite the longest side, so the triangle is acute, right or obtuse according as $\gamma$ is less than, equal to or greater than $90^\circ$. By the law of cosines,
\[c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos\gamma,\qquad\text{so}\qquad a^2+b^2-c^2=2ab\cos\gamma .\]
Since $2ab>0$, the sign of $a^2+b^2-c^2$ is the sign of $\cos\gamma$, which is positive for $\gamma<90^\circ$, zero for $\gamma=90^\circ$ and negative for $\gamma>90^\circ$. This proves the three statements.

(a) $6^2+7^2=85>81=9^2$: \textbf{acute}.

(b) $5^2+12^2=169=13^2$: \textbf{right}.

(c) $4^2+5^2=41<49=7^2$: \textbf{obtuse}.

(Each set satisfies the triangle inequality, so the triangles exist.)
