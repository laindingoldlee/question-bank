The chain rule gives
\[
\boxed{\dfrac{d}{dy}(1+2\ln y)^{-2}=-\dfrac{4}{y(1+2\ln y)^3}}.
\]
The constant positive solution is $\boxed{y=e^{-\dfrac{1}{2}}}$. Away from it, put $v=(1+2\ln y)^{-2}$. Then $v'=-\dfrac{4}{3}\sin2x$, so
\[
v=C+\dfrac{2}{3}\cos2x.
\]
Consequently the nonconstant solutions are
\[
\boxed{y=\exp\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\pm\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{C+\dfrac{2}{3}\cos2x}}\right)}
\]
on any connected interval in $(-\dfrac{\pi}{2},\dfrac{\pi}{2})$ where the radicand is positive, with a fixed choice of sign. The differential equation is smooth for $y>0$, so a nonconstant solution cannot cross the constant solution.

The initial condition has $1+2\ln y=1>0$, selecting the plus sign, and $1=C+\dfrac{2}{3}\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, so $C=\dfrac{2}{3}$. Since $\cos x>0$ on the specified interval,
\[
1+2\ln y=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\sec x,\qquad
\boxed{y=e^{\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}\sec x-\dfrac{1}{2}},\quad A=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}}.
\]
