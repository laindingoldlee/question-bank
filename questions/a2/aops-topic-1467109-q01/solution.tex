A word is an arrangement of a multiset of letters with $s,i,y\in\{0,1\}$ copies of $S,I,Y$ and $\ell\in\{0,1,2\}$ copies of $L$, not all zero. (Any such word has at most $5$ letters automatically.) Let $j=s+i+y$ be the number of letters chosen from $S,I,Y$; there are $\dbinom3j$ ways to choose them. A multiset with $j$ of these letters and $\ell$ copies of $L$ has $\dfrac{(j+\ell)!}{\ell!}$ arrangements.
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 & \ell=0 & \ell=1 & \ell=2\\ \hline
j=0 & - & 1 & 1\\
j=1\ (3\text{ ways}) & 1 & 2 & 3\\
j=2\ (3\text{ ways}) & 2 & 6 & 12\\
j=3\ (1\text{ way}) & 6 & 24 & 60
\end{array}
\]
The total is
\[
(1+1)+3(1+2+3)+3(2+6+12)+(6+24+60)=2+18+60+90=\boxed{170}.
\]
