Let $x=a-b>0$ and $y=b-c>0$, so $a-c=x+y$ and $c-a=-(x+y)$. The condition becomes
\[
xy(x+y)=1 .
\]
By AM-GM, $xy\le\left(\dfrac{x+y}{2}\right)^2$, so
\[
1=xy(x+y)\le\dfrac{(x+y)^3}{4},\qquad\text{that is}\qquad (a-c)^3=(x+y)^3\ge4 .
\]
Hence $a-c\ge\sqrt[3]{4}$. Since $1.5^3=3.375<4$, we have $\sqrt[3]{4}>1.5$, so $a-c>1.5$. $\blacksquare$

The bound $\sqrt[3]{4}\approx1.587$ is the best possible: equality holds when $x=y=\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{4}}{2}$, since then $xy(x+y)=\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{16}}{4}\cdot\sqrt[3]{4}=\dfrac{4}{4}=1$.
