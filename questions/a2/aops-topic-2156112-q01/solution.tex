\textbf{The shortcut.} Given that the first three balls are not red, the $42$ remaining balls are the $20$ red ones and $22$ of the $25$ non-red ones. So the fourth ball is non-red with probability $\dfrac{22}{42}$. If it is non-red, then the four balls drawn are four non-red balls, and by symmetry every ordering of every set of four non-red balls is equally likely; in particular the fourth of them is equally likely to be any of the $25$ non-red balls, so it is green with probability $\dfrac{15}{25}$. Hence
\[P(\text{4th green}\mid\text{first three not red})=\dfrac{22}{42}\cdot\dfrac{15}{25}=\dfrac{11}{21}\cdot\dfrac{3}{5}=\boxed{\dfrac{11}{35}}.\]

\textbf{Check by cases.} Given that the first three balls are not red, let $G$ be the number of green balls among them. They are a random $3$-element subset of the $25$ non-red balls, so $E[G]=3\cdot\dfrac{15}{25}=\dfrac{9}{5}$. Given $G$, the fourth ball is green with probability $\dfrac{15-G}{42}$. Averaging over $G$,
\[P=\dfrac{15-E[G]}{42}=\dfrac{15-\dfrac{9}{5}}{42}=\dfrac{66}{210}=\dfrac{11}{35}.\]
