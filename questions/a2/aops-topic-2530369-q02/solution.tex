We need hundreds $>$ units $>$ tens, so the three digits are distinct. Conversely, any $3$ distinct digits from $0$ to $9$ can be placed in exactly one such way: largest in the hundreds place (it is not $0$), middle in the units place and smallest in the tens place. So there are $\dbinom{10}{3}=120$ such numbers out of $900$, and the probability is
\[\dfrac{120}{900}=\boxed{\dfrac{2}{15}}.\]
