Some students argue about the ``interior angle sum'' of a circle. One says $-180^\circ$, because a circle has one side and $180^\circ(1-2)=-180^\circ$; another says $360^\circ$; another says it is infinite.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The formula $180^\circ(n-2)$ for the interior angle sum of a convex $n$-gon is proved by cutting the polygon into triangles by the diagonals from one vertex. For which $n$ does this proof apply, and why is the computation $180^\circ(1-2)$ meaningless for a circle?
\item[(b)] For a regular $n$-gon, find the interior angle sum $S_n$, each interior angle $a_n$, and the sum $E_n$ of the exterior angles. What happens to each as $n$ grows?
\item[(c)] Explain why no number can sensibly be called the interior angle sum of a circle.
\end{enumerate}
