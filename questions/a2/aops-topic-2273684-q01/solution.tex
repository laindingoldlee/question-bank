Complete the square:
\[x^2+x+1=\left(x+\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2+\dfrac{3}{4}.\]
A square of a real number is at least $0$, so $x^2+x+1\ge\dfrac{3}{4}>0$ for every real $x$: the expression is never $0$, and the graph lies entirely above the $x$-axis. Equality $x^2+x+1=\dfrac{3}{4}$ holds only at $x=-\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the lowest point of the graph is $\boxed{\left(-\dfrac{1}{2},\dfrac{3}{4}\right)}$.

Equivalently, the discriminant is $1^2-4\cdot1\cdot1=-3<0$. The solutions are the complex numbers
\[x=\dfrac{-1\pm\sqrt{-3}}{2}=\boxed{\dfrac{-1\pm i\sqrt3}{2}}.\]
