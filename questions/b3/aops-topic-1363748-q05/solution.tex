Since $59049=3^{10}$ is coprime to $10$, the decimal of $\dfrac{1}{3^{10}}$ is purely periodic and the length of its repeating block is the least $k\ge1$ with $3^{10}\mid10^k-1$ (the multiplicative order of $10$ modulo $3^{10}$).

\textbf{Claim:} $v_3\left(10^k-1\right)=2+v_3(k)$, where $v_3(m)$ is the exponent of $3$ in $m$. Write $10=1+9$.
\begin{itemize}
\item If $3\nmid k$: $\dfrac{10^k-1}{9}=1+10+\cdots+10^{k-1}\equiv k\not\equiv0\pmod3$, so $v_3=2$.
\item For $k=3m$: $10^{3m}-1=\left(10^m-1\right)\left(10^{2m}+10^m+1\right)$. With $x=10^m\equiv1\pmod9$, write $x=1+9t$; then $x^2+x+1=3+27t+81t^2$, which is divisible by $3$ but not by $9$. So multiplying $k$ by $3$ raises $v_3$ by exactly $1$.
\end{itemize}
So $3^{10}\mid10^k-1$ exactly when $2+v_3(k)\ge10$, i.e. $3^8\mid k$. The repeating block has $3^8=\boxed{6561}$ digits.
