The set $\{1,3,7,9\}$ works: its four triple sums are $11,13,17,19$. For an upper bound, suppose there are at least five selected numbers. Reduce any five modulo three. If one residue class contains at least three numbers, their sum is a multiple of three. Otherwise each class has at most two numbers, so all three classes occur; choosing one from each again gives a multiple of three. A sum of three distinct positive integers is at least $6$, so such a multiple is not prime. This contradiction proves the maximum is $\boxed4$.
