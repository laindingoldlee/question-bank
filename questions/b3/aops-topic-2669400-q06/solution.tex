A point $P=(a,b)$ is on the graph of $f$ $\iff$ $f(a)=b$ $\iff$ $f^{-1}(b)=a$ $\iff$ $P'=(b,a)$ is on the graph of $f^{-1}$.

It remains to show that the reflection in $y=x$ maps $(a,b)$ to $(b,a)$. If $a=b$ the point is on the line. Otherwise the midpoint of $(a,b)$ and $(b,a)$ is $\left(\dfrac{a+b}2,\dfrac{a+b}2\right)$, on the line $y=x$, and the segment joining them has direction $(b-a,a-b)$, perpendicular to the direction $(1,1)$ of the line. So $(b,a)$ is the mirror image of $(a,b)$, and the two graphs are reflections of each other. $\blacksquare$

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
import graph;real f(real t){return 2^t;}real g(real t){return log(t)/log(2);}
draw(graph(f,-2.5,1.6),linewidth(0.8));draw(graph(g,0.18,3.03),linewidth(0.8)+gray);draw((-2.5,-2.5)--(3.2,3.2),dashed);
pair P=(1,2),Q=(2,1);draw(P--Q,dotted);dot(P);dot(Q);label("$P$",P,NW,fontsize(8pt));label("$P'$",Q,SE,fontsize(8pt));
label("$y=f(x)$",(-2.4,f(-2.4)),N,fontsize(7pt));label("$y=f^{-1}(x)$",(3.03,g(3.03)),E,fontsize(7pt));label("$y=x$",(3.2,3.2),N,fontsize(7pt));
draw((-2.7,0)--(3.3,0),gray,Arrow);draw((0,-2.7)--(0,3.3),gray,Arrow);
\end{asy}
