The two geometric sums are $\dfrac{a}{1-r}$ and $\dfrac{a}{1+r}$. Their product is $\dfrac{a^2}{1-r^2}$, exactly the series on the right. Alternatively, in the product the coefficient of $a^2r^n$ is $1-1+\cdots+(-1)^n$, which is 1 for even $n$ and 0 for odd $n$. This follows first for finite polynomial truncations for each fixed coefficient, and absolute convergence permits the corresponding infinite Cauchy product.
