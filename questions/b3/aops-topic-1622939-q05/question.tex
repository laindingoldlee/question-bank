A number from $1,2,3,\dots,19$ is said to be a \emph{follower} of a second number from $1,2,\dots,19$ if either the second number is $10$ to $18$ more than the first, or the first number is $1$ to $9$ more than the second. Thus $6$ is a follower of $16,17,18,19,1,2,3,4$ and $5$. In how many ways can we choose three numbers $x,y,z$ from $1,2,\dots,19$, in this order, such that $x$ is a follower of $y$, $y$ is a follower of $z$, and $x$ is also a follower of $z$?
