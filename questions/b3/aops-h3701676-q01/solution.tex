Use complex coordinates with the circumcircle of $ABC$ the unit circle and $O=0$. Write $a,b,c,p,q$ for the point coordinates and $k$ for the center of the circle through $0,A,P,Q$. Its equation is
\[
z\bar z=\bar k z+k\bar z.
\]
At $z=a$, this gives $a\bar k+\dfrac{k}{a}=1$. The line $AB$ has equation $z+ab\bar z=a+b$. Substituting this into the circle equation produces a quadratic in $z$ with roots $a,p$ and product $k(a+b)$. Hence $p=\dfrac{k(a+b)}{a}$; similarly $q=\dfrac{k(a+c)}{a}$.

For a triangle with vertices $u,v,w$ and circumcenter $k$, its orthocenter is $u+v+w-2k$. Indeed, the vector from this point to $u$ is $(v-k)+(w-k)$, which is perpendicular to $v-w$ because $|v-k|=|w-k|$. Therefore the orthocenter here has coordinate
\[
h=p+q-2k=\dfrac{k(b+c)}{a}.
\]
Using $|a|=|b|=|c|=1$,
\[
h+bc\bar h=(b+c)(\dfrac{k}{a}+a\bar k)=b+c.
\]
This is precisely the equation of line $BC$, proving the result.
