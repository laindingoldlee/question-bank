Use finite truncations with final denominator one. Their continuants satisfy $p_j=p_{j-1}+j(j-1)p_{j-2}$ and the same recurrence for $q_j$, with $p_0=q_0=1,p_1=2,q_1=1$. Induction gives the even convergents $\dfrac{((2m)!!)^2}{(2m-1)!!(2m+1)!!}$ and odd convergents $\dfrac{(2m)!!(2m-2)!!}{((2m-1)!!)^2}$. Wallis\textquotesingle s integral argument places $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ between these: apply integration by parts to $I_k = \int_0^{\dfrac{\pi}{2}} \sin^k t \, d t$, obtaining $I_k=\dfrac{k-1}{k}I_{k-2}$, and use $I_{2m+1}<I_{2m}<I_{2m-1}$. The ratio of the displayed upper and lower convergents is $\dfrac{2m+1}{2m}\to1$, so both converge to $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. Hence the continued fraction converges to $\boxed{\dfrac{\pi}{2}}$.
