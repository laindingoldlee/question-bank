Extend $AO$ beyond $O$ until it meets side $BC$ at $P$; since $O$ is inside the triangle, $P$ lies on side $BC$, strictly between $B$ and $C$, and $O$ lies between $A$ and $P$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(2.2,3.6),C=(6,0),O=(2.6,1.1);
pair P=extension(A,O,B,C);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(A--O--C,blue+linewidth(0.8));draw(O--P,dashed);
dot(O);dot(P);
label("$A$",A,SW);label("$B$",B,N);label("$C$",C,SE);label("$O$",O,S);label("$P$",P,NE);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

By the triangle inequality in triangle $OPC$, $OC<OP+PC$. Hence
\[
AO+OC<AO+OP+PC=AP+PC .
\]
By the triangle inequality in triangle $ABP$, $AP<AB+BP$, so
\[
AP+PC<AB+BP+PC=AB+BC .
\]
Combining, $AO+OC<AB+BC$. $\blacksquare$
