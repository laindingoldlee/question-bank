A sphere touching both the wall and the floor has its centre at the same distance $r$ (its radius) from both. Look at the cross-section by the vertical plane through the centre of the ball, perpendicular to the wall; the gap is narrowest there.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real R=3;real r=(3-2*sqrt(2))*R;
pair Ob=(R,R),Os=(r,r);
draw((0,0)--(0,6.6),linewidth(1.2));draw((0,0)--(7,0),linewidth(1.2));
draw(circle(Ob,R),linewidth(0.9));draw(circle(Os,r),blue+linewidth(0.9));
draw((0,0)--Ob,dashed);
draw(Ob--Ob+R*dir(0));label("$R$",Ob+(R/2,0),N);
dot(Ob);dot(Os,blue);
draw(Os--(0,r),blue);
label("$r$",(Os.x/2,Os.y),N,blue+fontsize(8pt));
label("wall",(0,6.2),W);label("floor",(6.5,0),S);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

In this plane the wall and the floor are perpendicular lines, the ball is a circle of radius $R$ with centre $(R,R)$, and the small sphere is a circle of radius $r$ with centre $(r,r)$. Both centres lie on the diagonal through the corner. The small sphere passes the ball just touching it when the distance between the centres is $R+r$:
\[
\sqrt2\,(R-r)=R+r\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r=\dfrac{\sqrt2-1}{\sqrt2+1}\,R=\left(\sqrt2-1\right)^2R=\boxed{\left(3-2\sqrt2\right)R}\approx0.17R .
\]
In any other position along the wall the centres are farther apart (the distance gains a component along the wall), so a sphere of this radius fits everywhere, and a larger one cannot pass this narrowest section.
