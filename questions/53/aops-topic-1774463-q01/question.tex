The factorial satisfies $0!=1$ and $n!=n\cdot(n-1)!$ for every positive integer $n$. Show that there is no way to define $(-1)!,(-2)!,\dots$ as numbers so that the rule $n!=n\cdot(n-1)!$ also holds for $n=0,-1,-2,\dots$.
