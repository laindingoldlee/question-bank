The identity $\gcd(u,v)=\sum_{d\mid u,\ d\mid v}\phi(d)$ implies that the sum is $\sum_{d=1}^9\phi(d)\binom{r_d}{2}$, where $r_d$ counts multiples of $d$ in the interval. No larger $d$ divides a difference from $1$ to $9$. For $d=1,\ldots,9$, the possible counts are respectively $10,5,(3\text{ or }4),(2\text{ or }3),2,(1\text{ or }2),(1\text{ or }2),(1\text{ or }2),(1\text{ or }2)$. Their minimum and maximum contributions sum to $67$ and $95$. The interval $1,\ldots,10$ attains all the lower counts, and $63,\ldots,72$ attains all upper counts. Thus the greatest difference is $\boxed{95-67=28}$.
