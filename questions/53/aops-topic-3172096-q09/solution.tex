The exponent patterns for divisor count $9$ are $8$ or $(2,2)$. The first type is $p^8$, and only $2^8=256$ lies below $2015$. The second type is $p^2q^2$ for distinct primes $p<q$, with $pq<\sqrt{2015}<45$. For $p=2$, the possible $q$ are $3,5,7,11,13,17,19$; for $p=3$, they are $5,7,11,13$; for $p=5$, only $7$ works. Larger smaller primes give product at least $7\cdot11>44$. Thus the count is $1+7+4+1=\boxed{13}$.
