Add the two upper bounds to get $3x+2y\le14$. Equality holds at their intersection $x=2,y=4$, which is feasible, so the maximum is $\boxed{14}$.
On any segment, a linear function at an interior point is the corresponding weighted average of its endpoint values. Likewise, every point of a convex polygon is a convex combination of its vertices, so some vertex attains at least its value. This proves that a vertex maximizer exists. It need not be the only maximizer: for example, maximizing $x+y$ here gives value $6$ along the entire feasible edge from $(0,6)$ to $(2,4)$.
