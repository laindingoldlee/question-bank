Let $\gamma$ be the angle between the sides $a$ and $b$ (opposite $c$). By the law of cosines, $a^2+b^2-c^2=2ab\cos\gamma$, so the condition becomes
\[
4a^2b^2\cos^2\gamma=4a^2b^2-64\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad 4a^2b^2\left(1-\cos^2\gamma\right)=64\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad a^2b^2\sin^2\gamma=16 .
\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair C=(0,0),A=(5,0),B=(1.6,2.6);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
label("$A$",A,SE);label("$B$",B,N);label("$C$",C,SW);
label("$b$",(A+C)/2,S);label("$a$",(B+C)/2,NW);label("$c$",(A+B)/2,NE);
draw(arc(C,0.6,0,degrees(B)),red);label("$\gamma$",C+0.85*dir(degrees(B)/2),red);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Since $\sin\gamma>0$, $ab\sin\gamma=4$, and the area is
\[
\dfrac{1}{2}ab\sin\gamma=\boxed{2}.
\]
(Equivalently, $16K^2=4a^2b^2-\left(a^2+b^2-c^2\right)^2$ is Heron's formula in another form.)
