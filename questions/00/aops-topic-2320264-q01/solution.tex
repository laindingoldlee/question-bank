A number $y$ is one of $q-1,r-1,s-1$ exactly when $y+1$ is a root of $x^3-3x^2+1$. So the required polynomial is obtained by substituting $x=y+1$:
\[(y+1)^3-3(y+1)^2+1=(y^3+3y^2+3y+1)-(3y^2+6y+3)+1=y^3-3y-1.\]
This is monic of degree $3$, and its roots are $q-1$, $r-1$, $s-1$ (with multiplicity). The answer is
\[\boxed{x^3-3x-1}.\]
(Check with Vieta's formulas: $(q-1)+(r-1)+(s-1)=3-3=0$, which matches the missing $x^2$ term.)
