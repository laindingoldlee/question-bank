An index $0$ or $1$ contributes $2$, index $2$ contributes $4$, and an index at least three contributes a multiple of eight that is $1\pmod7$.
If all indices are at least three, the sum is $3\pmod7$, not a cube. If exactly two are at least three, the sum is $2$ or $4\pmod8$, also impossible. If exactly one is at least three, the other contributions give residues $4,6,0\pmod8$; only the last survives, requiring both small indices to equal two. But then the sum is $4+4+1=2\pmod7$, again impossible.
Thus all indices lie in $\{0,1,2\}$. The sum is one of $6,8,10,12$, and only $8$ is a cube. It requires two contributions of $2$ and one of $4$. The unordered triples are $(0,0,2),(0,1,2),(1,1,2)$, giving $\boxed3$.
