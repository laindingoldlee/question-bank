The $10$ seats form $5$ opposite pairs, and the condition says that each opposite pair holds one boy and one girl.

Count arrangements in fixed (labelled) seats first. For each of the $5$ opposite pairs, choose which of its two seats gets the boy: $2^5$ ways. Seat the boys in the $5$ boy seats: $5!$ ways. Seat the girls in the $5$ girl seats: $5!$ ways. That is $2^5\cdot5!\cdot5!=32\cdot120\cdot120=460800$ seatings. All $10$ people are different, so each arrangement up to rotation corresponds to exactly $10$ seatings, and the answer is
\[
\dfrac{460800}{10}=\boxed{46080}.
\]
The student's method has the right idea, and the seat count $\dfrac{10\cdot8\cdot6\cdot4\cdot2}{10}=384$ is correct. The error is the first step: matching the $5$ boys with the $5$ girls (each boy with a different girl) can be done in $5!=120$ ways, not $5\cdot5=25$ ways ($25$ is the number of ways to choose just one boy-girl pair). With $120$ the method gives $120\cdot384=46080$, so $9600$ is wrong.
