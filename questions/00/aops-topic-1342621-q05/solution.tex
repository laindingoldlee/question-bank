Drop the perpendicular $CH$ to $AB$. By symmetry the overhang is $HB=\dfrac{AB-CD}{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}$, so $CH=\sqrt{1-\dfrac{1}{4}}=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ and $AH=2-\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{3}{2}$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0), B=(2,0), C=(1.5,sqrt(3)/2), D=(0.5,sqrt(3)/2);
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(A--C,red+dashed);
pair H=(1.5,0); draw(C--H,gray+dashed);
label("$A$",A,SW); label("$B$",B,SE); label("$C$",C,NE); label("$D$",D,NW);
label("$2$",(1,0),S); label("$1$",(B+C)/2,E); label("$1$",(C+D)/2,N); label("$1$",(A+D)/2,W);
label("$\sqrt{3}$",A+0.42*(C-A),NW,red); label("$H$",H,S,gray);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Then
\[
AC=\sqrt{AH^2+CH^2}=\sqrt{\dfrac{9}{4}+\dfrac{3}{4}}=\boxed{\sqrt3}.
\]
(The trapezoid is half of a regular hexagon of side $1$, and $AC$ is a short diagonal of that hexagon.)
