Each move decreases total health by $3$, so $3\mid\dfrac{N(N+1)}{2}$. Thus $N\equiv0$ or $2\pmod3$ is necessary. The values $N=2,3$ are impossible: two positions allow no move, and three unequal health values cannot all vanish under moves affecting all three equally.

We construct every remaining candidate. Let $c_i$ count moves on positions $i,i+1,i+2$, with counts outside $1,\ldots,N-2$ zero. The health required at position $j$ is $c_j+c_{j-1}+c_{j-2}$. The following four base constructions list a permutation and its move counts:
\[
\begin{array}{c|l|l}
N&\text{health ordering}&(c_1,\ldots,c_{N-2})\\ \hline
5&(1,2,5,4,3)&(1,1,3)\\
6&(1,2,3,6,5,4)&(1,1,1,4)\\
8&(1,2,4,5,7,8,6,3)&(1,1,2,2,3,3)\\
9&(1,2,3,5,6,8,9,7,4)&(1,1,1,3,2,3,4)
\end{array}
\]
These counts can be performed in any order: whenever a planned move remains, each of its positions has at least that one remaining unit of health, so none is a gap.

To extend a construction from $N$ to $N+6$, append the block
\[
N+1,N+2,N+3,N+6,N+5,N+4.
\]
Its internal move counts are $(N+1,1,1,N+4)$, which produce exactly these health values. Treat the two blocks independently. The four bases therefore cover all residues $0,2,3,5$ modulo $6$ from $5$ onward.

There are $1290$ multiples of $3$ and $1291$ integers congruent to $2$ modulo $3$ in the interval. Excluding $2,3$ leaves $\boxed{1290+1291-2=2579}$.
