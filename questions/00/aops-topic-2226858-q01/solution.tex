\textbf{Alternation.} Two numbers of the same parity have an even sum, which is at least $1+3=4$, so it is not prime. Hence neighbours have opposite parity, and since there are $10$ odd and $10$ even numbers, the row alternates between odd and even numbers.

\textbf{A construction.} One arrangement is
\[1,2,3,4,7,6,5,8,9,10,13,16,15,14,17,12,19,18,11,20,\]
with neighbouring sums
\[3,5,7,11,13,11,13,17,19,23,29,31,29,31,29,31,37,29,31,\]
all prime. So such an arrangement exists, for example the one shown.
