There are $\dbinom{52}{3}=22100$ equally likely sets of three cards.

\textbf{Structure of a favourable set.} Exactly two cards share a rank, and exactly two share a suit. Two cards of the same rank have different suits, so the same-suit pair is a different pair; two different pairs among three cards share exactly one card, call it $P$. So the set is $\{P,Q,R\}$ where $Q$ has the rank of $P$ (and another suit) and $R$ has the suit of $P$ (and another rank). Conversely every such set is favourable: $Q$ and $R$ differ in rank (the rank of $Q$ is that of $P$, which is not that of $R$) and in suit, so there is exactly one rank pair and one suit pair.

\textbf{Counting.} $P$ can be any of $52$ cards, $Q$ any of the other $3$ cards of its rank, and $R$ any of the other $12$ cards of its suit. Each favourable set arises once, since $P$ is determined as the card in both pairs. So there are $52\cdot3\cdot12=1872$ favourable sets, and the probability is
\[\dfrac{1872}{22100}=\boxed{\dfrac{36}{425}}.\]
