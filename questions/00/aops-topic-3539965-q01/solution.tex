The final three digits must have sum divisible by 3. The residues of 1 through 6 form two copies of each residue 0,1,2. A three-digit subset can therefore qualify only by selecting one of each residue, giving $2^3=8$ subsets. The first three remaining digits also contain one of each residue. For a given remaining set with e even digits, its first three positions have $2 e$ orders ending in an even digit; its final block has $3!=6$ orders. Across the 8 subsets, each of the three even digits occurs in the remaining set 4 times, so $\sum e=12$. Total arrangements are $2\cdot 6\cdot 12=\boxed{144}$.
