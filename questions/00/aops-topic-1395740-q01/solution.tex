If the integers are $m,m+1,\dots,m+n-1$ with $m\ge1$, their sum is
\[
nm+\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}=10 .
\]
Since $m\ge1$, the sum is at least $1+2+\cdots+n=\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}$, so $\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}\le10$ and $n\le4$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $n=1$: $m=10$ works ($10$ itself).
\item $n=2$: $2m+1=10$ has no integer solution.
\item $n=3$: $3m+3=10$ has no integer solution.
\item $n=4$: $4m+6=10$ gives $m=1$: $1+2+3+4=10$.
\end{itemize}
So $n\in\{1,4\}$, and there are $\boxed{2}$ possible values. (If a single integer is not counted as a sum of consecutive integers, only $n=4$ remains.)
