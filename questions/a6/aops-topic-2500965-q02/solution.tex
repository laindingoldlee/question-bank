Let $u=\dfrac{x-3}{x-5}$ (so $x\ne3,5$ and $u\ne0$). The equation is $u+\dfrac1u=-2$, i.e. $u^2+2u+1=0$, so $u=-1$. Then $x-3=-(x-5)$, so $2x=8$ and $x=4$. Check: $\dfrac{1}{-1}+\dfrac{-1}{1}=-2$. Hence $x+1=\boxed{5}$.
