To construct the perpendicular bisector of a segment $AB$ of length $2a$, one draws arcs of the same radius $r$ centred at $A$ and at $B$ and joins their two intersection points.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Prove that the two circles of radius $r$ about $A$ and $B$ meet in two points exactly when $r>a$, and that the line through these two points is the perpendicular bisector of $AB$.
\item[(b)] What happens if $r=a$ (half the segment), and why does the construction then fail?
\end{enumerate}
