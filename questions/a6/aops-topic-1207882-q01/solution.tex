Let $g=\gcd(d,m)$ and write $d=gd'$, $m=gm'$, where $\gcd(d',m')=1$. Since $m\mid a-b=d\left(\dfrac {a}{d}-\dfrac {b}{d}\right)$, we have
\[gm'\mid gd'\left(\dfrac {a}{d}-\dfrac {b}{d}\right),\qquad\text{so}\qquad m'\mid d'\left(\dfrac {a}{d}-\dfrac {b}{d}\right).\]
As $\gcd(m',d')=1$, it follows that $m'\mid\dfrac {a}{d}-\dfrac {b}{d}$, which is the claim because $m'=\dfrac {m}{g}$.
