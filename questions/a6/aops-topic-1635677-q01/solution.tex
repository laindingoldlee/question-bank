Kendra takes $\dfrac{200-K}{4}$ steps and Mason takes $\dfrac{200-M}{6}$ steps; these are whole numbers, so $K\equiv200\equiv0\pmod 4$ and $M\equiv200\equiv2\pmod 6$. The condition is
\[
\dfrac{200-K}{4}=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{200-M}{6}\iff3(200-K)=200-M\iff M=3K-400 .
\]
Then $K-M=400-2K$, which is largest when $K$ is smallest. We need $M=3K-400\ge1$, so $K\ge\dfrac{401}{3}$, i.e.\ $K\ge134$, and $K$ must be a multiple of $4$: the smallest choice is $K=136$, giving $M=8$. This works: $8\equiv2\pmod6$, Kendra takes $\dfrac{64}{4}=16$ steps and Mason takes $\dfrac{192}{6}=32$ steps. (Whenever $K\equiv0\pmod4$, $M=3K-400\equiv2\pmod 6$ automatically holds, since $3K\equiv0\pmod{12}$.)

The greatest value is $K-M=136-8=\boxed{128}$.
