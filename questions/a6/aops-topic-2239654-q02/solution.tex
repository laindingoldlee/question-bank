\textbf{(a)} From $3a+3b=123$ we get $a+b=41$. Using $a^3+b^3=(a+b)^3-3ab(a+b)$,
\[82=41^3-3ab\cdot41\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2=41^2-3ab\quad\Longrightarrow\quad ab=\dfrac{41^2-2}{3}=\dfrac{1679}{3}.\]
(We divided by $41$, using $82=2\cdot41$.) Hence
\[\dfrac{1}{a}+\dfrac{1}{b}=\dfrac{a+b}{ab}=\dfrac{41\cdot3}{1679}=\boxed{\dfrac{123}{1679}}.\]
(Note $1679=23\cdot73$, so the fraction is in lowest terms.)

\textbf{(b)} $a$ and $b$ are the roots of $t^2-41t+\dfrac{1679}{3}=0$, whose discriminant is
\[41^2-4\cdot\dfrac{1679}{3}=\dfrac{5043-6716}{3}=-\dfrac{1673}{3}<0 .\]
So $a$ and $b$ are complex conjugates, not real numbers. (For real $a,b$ with $a+b=41$ we would have $a^3+b^3\ge\dfrac{41^3}{4}$, far more than $82$.)
