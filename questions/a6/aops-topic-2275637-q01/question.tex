A student tries to write
\[\dfrac{1}{mn(m+n+1)}=\dfrac{A}{mn}+\dfrac{B}{m+n+1}\]
with constants $A$ and $B$. Putting $m=n=0$ in $A(m+n+1)+Bmn=1$ gives $A=1$, then $m=n=1$ gives $B=-2$, but $m=0$, $n=1$ gives $A=\dfrac{1}{2}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Show that no constants $A$ and $B$ make the equation an identity, and explain what went wrong.
\item[(b)] Regard $m$ as a fixed positive number and decompose the expression into partial fractions in the variable $n$: find $C$ and $D$, depending on $m$ only, such that
\[\dfrac{1}{mn(m+n+1)}=\dfrac{C}{n}+\dfrac{D}{m+n+1}.\]
\item[(c)] For a fixed positive integer $m$, evaluate $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{mn(m+n+1)}$ in terms of $H_{m+1}=1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{m+1}$.
\end{enumerate}
