All six vertices lie on the circumcircle of the hexagon. By Thales' theorem, a triangle inscribed in a circle has a right angle exactly when one of its sides is a diameter. In the hexagon $ABCDEF$ the diameters joining two vertices are the three long diagonals $AD$, $BE$, $CF$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");

defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair[] V; for(int k=0;k<6;++k) V[k]=dir(60*k);
draw(V[0]--V[1]--V[2]--V[3]--V[4]--V[5]--cycle,linewidth(0.9));

for(int k=0;k<6;++k){dot(V[k]);label(nm[k],V[k],dir(60*k));}

For each long diagonal, any of the other $4$ vertices completes a right triangle (for example $ABD$ has its right angle at $B$), and no triangle contains two long diagonals as sides, since they meet only at the centre. So there are $3\cdot4=12$ right triangles among the $\dbinom63=20$ triangles, and the probability is
\[
\dfrac{12}{20}=\boxed{\dfrac{3}{5}}.
\]
