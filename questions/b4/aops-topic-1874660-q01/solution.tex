If a square has side $s$, the segment joining the midpoints of two adjacent sides is the hypotenuse of a right isosceles triangle with legs $\dfrac {s}{2}$, so it has length $\dfrac{s}{\sqrt2}$. Hence each new square has half the area of the previous one.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair[] P={(0,0),(8,0),(8,8),(0,8)};
pen[] pc={black+linewidth(1),blue+linewidth(0.9),red+linewidth(0.8),heavygreen+linewidth(0.8)};
for(int k=0;k<4;++k){
 draw(P[0]--P[1]--P[2]--P[3]--cycle,pc[k]);
 pair[] Q={(P[0]+P[1])/2,(P[1]+P[2])/2,(P[2]+P[3])/2,(P[3]+P[0])/2};
 P=Q;}
label("$A$",(0,0),SW);label("$B$",(8,0),SE);label("$C$",(8,8),NE);label("$D$",(0,8),NW);
label("$A_1$",(4,0),S,blue);label("$B_1$",(8,4),E,blue);label("$C_1$",(4,8),N,blue);label("$D_1$",(0,4),W,blue);
label("$64$",(1.33,1.33));label("$32$",(6.67,4),blue);label("$16$",(2.67,2.67),red);label("$8$",(4,4),heavygreen);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

(The labels $64,32,16,8$ in the figure sit in the regions just inside each square's boundary and give that square's area.) The areas are $64$, $32$, $16$ and $8$, with sum
\[
64+32+16+8=\boxed{120}.
\]
