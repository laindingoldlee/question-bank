We need $A+B=C$ and $A-B=D$, all four distinct digits.
\begin{itemize}
\item $B\ne0$, since otherwise $C=A$. Then $D=A-B\ge0$ forces $A>B\ge1$.
\item $C=A+B\le9$.
\item $C$ differs from $A$, $B$ and $D$ automatically (it exceeds each of them), and $D=A-B$ differs from $A$ (as $B\ne0$). The only remaining condition is $D\ne B$, i.e.\ $A\ne2B$.
\end{itemize}
Count the pairs $A>B\ge1$ with $A+B\le9$: for $B=1,2,3,4$ there are $7,5,3,1$ choices of $A$, a total of $16$. Remove the three pairs with $A=2B$, namely $(2,1)$, $(4,2)$, $(6,3)$. That leaves $\boxed{13}$ assignments.
