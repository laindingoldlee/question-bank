Let $x=\dfrac{a}{b}$, $y=\dfrac{b}{c}$, $z=\dfrac{c}{a}$; these are nonzero rational numbers with $xyz=1$. Then
\[
xy=\dfrac{a}{c},\qquad yz=\dfrac{b}{a},\qquad zx=\dfrac{c}{b},
\]
so the two given integers are $p=x+y+z$ and $q=xy+yz+zx$. By Vieta's formulas $x$, $y$, $z$ are the roots of
\[
t^3-pt^2+qt-1=0,
\]
a monic polynomial with integer coefficients.

Each root is rational. If a rational number $\dfrac{u}{v}$ in lowest terms ($v>0$) is a root, then multiplying by $v^3$ gives $u^3-pu^2v+quv^2-v^3=0$. So $v\mid u^3$, which with $\gcd(u,v)=1$ forces $v=1$; and $u\mid v^3=1$, so $u=\pm1$. Hence each of $x$, $y$, $z$ is $1$ or $-1$.

In particular $\left|\dfrac{a}{b}\right|=1$ and $\left|\dfrac{b}{c}\right|=1$, so $|a|=|b|=|c|$. $\blacksquare$
