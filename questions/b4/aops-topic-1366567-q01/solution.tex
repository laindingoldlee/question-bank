Since $D$ lies on $AB$, $\angle CBD=\angle CBA=\angle B$. As $AB>AC$ we have $\angle C>\angle B$, so the ray $CD$ with $\angle ACD=\angle B<\angle ACB$ meets side $AB$ at an interior point $D$.

\textbf{$AC$ is tangent to the circle $BCD$.} The angle between the chord $CD$ and the line $CA$ equals $\angle ACD=\angle CBD$, which is the inscribed angle on chord $CD$ from the point $B$ of the circle on the other side of $CD$ from $A$. By the converse of the tangent--chord angle theorem, line $AC$ is tangent at $C$ to the circle through $B$, $C$, $D$. (Equivalently: triangles $ACD$ and $ABC$ share the angle at $A$ and have $\angle ACD=\angle ABC$, so they are similar and $AC^2=AD\cdot AB$, the power of $A$.)

\textbf{Two right angles.} A radius to a point of tangency is perpendicular to the tangent, so $SC\perp AC$ and $\angle ACS=90^\circ$. Also $E$ is the midpoint of the chord $BD$ of this circle, so $SE\perp BD$, that is, $\angle AES=90^\circ$ (here $E\ne S$ because $\angle BCD=\angle C-\angle B<90^\circ$, so $BD$ is not a diameter).

Hence $E$ and $C$ both see the segment $AS$ at a right angle, so both lie on the circle with diameter $AS$ (Thales). Therefore $A$, $E$, $S$, $C$ are concyclic. $\blacksquare$
