By Bayes' theorem,
\[
P(\text{virus}\mid +)=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{150}\cdot0.96}{\dfrac{1}{150}\cdot0.96+\dfrac{149}{150}\cdot0.04}=\dfrac{0.96}{0.96+5.96}=\dfrac{96}{692}=\boxed{\dfrac{24}{173}}\approx13.9\%.
\]
Think of $15000$ people: $100$ have the virus and $96$ of them test positive; of the $14900$ healthy people, $4\%$, that is $596$, also test positive. Only $96$ of the $692$ positives have the virus. The brother's $\dfrac{24}{25}=96\%$ is the probability of a positive test \emph{given} the virus, not the other way round.
