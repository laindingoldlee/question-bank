\textbf{Bev is taller.} Let $X$ be the person standing in Ana's row and Bev's column. (If Ana and Bev share a row, $X$ is Bev; if they share a column, $X$ is Ana.)
\begin{itemize}
\item Ana is the shortest person in her row and $X$ is in that row, so Ana is not taller than $X$ (Ana $=X$ or Ana is shorter than $X$).
\item Bev is the tallest person in her column and $X$ is in that column, so $X$ is not taller than Bev.
\end{itemize}
Hence Ana is at most as tall as Bev, and since they are different people with different heights, $\boxed{\text{Bev is taller than Ana}}$. $\blacksquare$

(This single argument covers the poster's three cases at once. Note that the choice of Ana and Bev as ``tallest of the row-minima'' and ``shortest of the column-maxima'' is not even needed: every row minimum is shorter than or equal to every column maximum.)
