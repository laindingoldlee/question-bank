Because the points are in the order $A,B,C,D$, the segments $AC$ and $BD$ together cover $AD$, and they overlap exactly in $BC$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(8cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real u=0.2;
pair A=(0,0),B=(12*u,0),C=(18*u,0),D=(47*u,0);
draw(A--D,linewidth(0.9));
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);dot(D);
label("$A$",A,S);label("$B$",B,S);label("$C$",C,S);label("$D$",D,S);
real h1=1.1,h2=2.0,h3=-1.3;
draw((0,h1)--(18*u,h1),blue,Bars);label("$AC=18$",(5*u,h1),N,blue);
draw((12*u,h2)--(47*u,h2),red,Bars);label("$BD=35$",(29.5*u,h2),N,red);
draw((0,h3)--(47*u,h3),Bars);label("$AD=47$",(23.5*u,h3),S);
draw((12*u,0)--(12*u,h2),dashed+gray);draw((18*u,0)--(18*u,h1),dashed+gray);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

In lengths: $AC=AB+BC$, $BD=BC+CD$ and $AD=AB+BC+CD$, so
\[
AC+BD=(AB+BC+CD)+BC=AD+BC .
\]
Therefore $BC=AC+BD-AD=18+35-47=\boxed{6}$. (Then $AB=12$ and $CD=29$.)
