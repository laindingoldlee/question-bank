Let $k=\dfrac {a}{b}=\dfrac {c}{d}$. Then $a=kb$ and $c=kd$, so $a+c=k(b+d)$. Since $b+d\ne0$,
\[
\dfrac{a+c}{b+d}=\dfrac{k(b+d)}{b+d}=k=\dfrac {a}{b}=\dfrac {c}{d} .
\]
The condition $b+d\ne0$ is needed: for example $\dfrac{1}{1}=\dfrac{-1}{-1}$, but $\dfrac{1+(-1)}{1+(-1)}$ is undefined. $\blacksquare$
