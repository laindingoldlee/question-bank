Let the lines $AB$, $CD$, $EF$ form triangle $XYZ$ with
\[
X=EF\cap AB,\qquad Y=AB\cap CD,\qquad Z=CD\cap EF .
\]
So $A$, $B$, $P$ lie on line $XY$; $C$, $D$, $R$ lie on line $YZ$; and $E$, $F$, $Q$ lie on line $ZX$:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");

defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));

pair P=extension(A,B,D,E),Q=extension(B,C,E,F),R=extension(C,D,F,A);
pair X=extension(E,F,A,B),Y=extension(A,B,C,D),Z=extension(C,D,E,F);
draw(unitcircle,gray(0.5));
draw(A--B--C--D--E--F--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(X--Y--Z--cycle,blue+dashed);
draw(D--P,dotted);draw(A--R,dotted);
draw(Q-0.15*(R-Q)--R+0.04*(R-Q),red+linewidth(0.9));

Use directed segments along each line. Menelaus' theorem for triangle $XYZ$ says that points $U$, $V$, $W$ on the lines $XY$, $YZ$, $ZX$ are collinear if and only if $\dfrac{XU}{UY}\cdot\dfrac{YV}{VZ}\cdot\dfrac{ZW}{WX}=-1$.

Apply it to the three lines $DE$, $BC$, $FA$, each of which meets all three sides:
\begin{align*}
\text{line }PDE:&\quad \dfrac{XP}{PY}\cdot\dfrac{YD}{DZ}\cdot\dfrac{ZE}{EX}=-1,\\
\text{line }BCQ:&\quad \dfrac{XB}{BY}\cdot\dfrac{YC}{CZ}\cdot\dfrac{ZQ}{QX}=-1,\\
\text{line }ARF:&\quad \dfrac{XA}{AY}\cdot\dfrac{YR}{RZ}\cdot\dfrac{ZF}{FX}=-1.
\end{align*}
Multiply the three equations and regroup the nine factors:
\[
\left(\dfrac{XP}{PY}\cdot\dfrac{YR}{RZ}\cdot\dfrac{ZQ}{QX}\right)\cdot\dfrac{XA\cdot XB}{EX\cdot FX}\cdot\dfrac{YC\cdot YD}{AY\cdot BY}\cdot\dfrac{ZE\cdot ZF}{CZ\cdot DZ}=-1 .
\]
By the power of a point (with directed lengths), for the point $X$ and the secants $XAB$ and $XEF$ we have $XA\cdot XB=XE\cdot XF=EX\cdot FX$; for $Y$, $YC\cdot YD=YA\cdot YB=AY\cdot BY$; for $Z$, $ZE\cdot ZF=ZC\cdot ZD=CZ\cdot DZ$. So the last three fractions all equal $1$, and
\[
\dfrac{XP}{PY}\cdot\dfrac{YR}{RZ}\cdot\dfrac{ZQ}{QX}=-1 .
\]
Since $P$, $R$, $Q$ lie on the lines $XY$, $YZ$, $ZX$, the converse of Menelaus' theorem shows that $P$, $Q$, $R$ are collinear (the red line in the figure). $\blacksquare$
