Harry places checkers numbered $1,2,\dots,2012^2$ on a $2012\times2012$ board, one per square. Then Professor Snape picks a row and a column. Can Harry arrange the checkers so that, whatever Snape picks, among the checkers in that row and column there are always three, $a$, $b$, $c$, with $ab=c$?
