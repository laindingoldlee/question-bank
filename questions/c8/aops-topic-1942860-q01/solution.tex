An expression is evaluated by evaluating its parts; ``undefined'' is not a number, so there is no arithmetic of the form ``undefined $-$ undefined''. Since $\dfrac{1}{0^2}$ is not defined, $f(0)$ is not defined.

Could we still choose a natural value, as with a removable gap? For $x\ne0$,
\[
f(x)=\dfrac{x-1}{x^3}.
\]
As $x\to0$ the numerator tends to $-1$ while the denominator tends to $0$. For small $x>0$ the denominator is a tiny positive number, so $f(x)$ is a huge negative number (for example $f(0.1)=-900$); for small $x<0$ the denominator is a tiny negative number, so $f(x)$ is huge and positive ($f(-0.1)=1100$). So $|f(x)|$ grows without bound and $f(x)$ approaches no finite value from either side. Hence $f(0)$ is \boxed{\text{undefined}}, and no value (in particular not $0$) can be assigned to make $f$ continuous at $0$.
