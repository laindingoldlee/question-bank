A student says that at $x=0$ both $\dfrac{1}{x^2}$ and $\dfrac{1}{x^3}$ are undefined, so ``undefined minus undefined'' might be $0$, and asks what value
\[
f(x)=\dfrac{1}{x^2}-\dfrac{1}{x^3}
\]
has at $x=0$. Show that no value can sensibly be assigned: $f(0)$ is undefined, and $f(x)$ does not approach any finite number as $x$ approaches $0$.
