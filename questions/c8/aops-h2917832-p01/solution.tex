If $n\equiv1\pmod3$, then $1+2n$ is a multiple of $3$; for it to equal $3$ would require $n=1$, not prime. If $n\equiv2\pmod3$, then $1+4n$ is divisible by $3$ and greater than $3$, impossible. Hence $3\mid n$, and primality forces $n=3$. This gives $3,7,13$, all prime. There is $\boxed1$ such integer.
