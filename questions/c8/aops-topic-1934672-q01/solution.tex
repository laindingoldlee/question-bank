\textbf{Powers of $2$ and $5$.} The exponent of $5$ in $135!$ is $\left\lfloor\dfrac{135}{5}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\dfrac{135}{25}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\dfrac{135}{125}\right\rfloor=27+5+1=33$, and the exponent of $2$ is $67+33+16+8+4+2+1=131$. So $135!$ ends in exactly $33$ zeros, and the last nonzero digit of $135!$ is the units digit of $M=\dfrac{135!}{10^{33}}$. Since $M$ still contains the factor $2^{131-33}=2^{98}$, $M$ is even, so it is enough to find $M$ modulo $5$.

\textbf{Removing the factors of $5$.} Let $F(n)=\dfrac{n!}{5^{e}}$, where $5^e$ is the highest power of $5$ dividing $n!$, and write $n=5q+r$ with $q=\left\lfloor\dfrac {n}{5}\right\rfloor$ and $0\le r\le4$. The multiples of $5$ up to $n$ are $5\cdot1,5\cdot2,\dots,5q$, with product $5^q\,q!$. Hence $F(n)=P(n)\,F(q)$, where $P(n)$ is the product of the numbers up to $n$ that are not divisible by $5$. Each complete block $5j+1,\dots,5j+4$ contributes $1\cdot2\cdot3\cdot4=24\equiv-1\pmod5$, and the incomplete last block contributes $r!$, so
\[
F(n)\equiv(-1)^{q}\,r!\;F(q)\pmod5 .
\]
Now $F(1)=1$, then $F(5)\equiv(-1)^1\cdot0!\cdot F(1)=-1$, $F(27)\equiv(-1)^5\cdot2!\cdot F(5)=2$, and
\[
F(135)\equiv(-1)^{27}\cdot0!\cdot F(27)=-2\equiv3\pmod5 .
\]

\textbf{Dividing by $2^{33}$.} We have $F(135)=2^{33}M$. Since $2^4\equiv1\pmod5$, $2^{33}\equiv2$, so $2M\equiv3\pmod5$, and multiplying by $3$ (the inverse of $2$) gives $M\equiv9\equiv4\pmod5$.

So $M$ is even and $M\equiv4\pmod5$, hence $M\equiv4\pmod{10}$. The last nonzero digit of $135!$ is $\boxed{4}$.
