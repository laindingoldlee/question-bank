Completing fourth powers reduces the equation to $(x-4)^4+(y-7)^4=(z-6)^4$. We first prove that $a^4+b^4=c^2$ has no positive integer solution. Choose a solution with least $c$ and divide out any common factor. Both $a,b$ cannot be odd, since fourth powers are $1$ modulo $16$ and $2$ is not a square modulo $16$. Relabel so $b$ is even. The primitive Pythagorean parametrization gives $a^2=m^2-n^2$, $b^2=2mn$, $c=m^2+n^2$, with coprime $m>n$ of opposite parity. Here $m$ must be odd, since otherwise $m^2-n^2$ would be $3$ modulo $4$ rather than a square. Thus so coprimality and $2mn$ being a square force $m=u^2$, $n=2v^2$. Now $a^2+n^2=u^4$ is another primitive Pythagorean triple, hence $a=r^2-s^2$, $n=2rs$, $u^2=r^2+s^2$. Since $rs=v^2$ and $r,s$ are coprime, write $r=h^2,s=k^2$. This yields $h^4+k^4=u^2$, a new positive solution with $u<c$, contradiction. Applying this result to the completed equation shows $x-4=0$ or $y-7=0$. Thus all solutions are $\boxed{(x,y,z)=(4,7+t,6+\varepsilon t)\text{ or }(4+t,7,6+\varepsilon t)}$, where $t\in\mathbb Z$ and $\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}$. Direct substitution proves sufficiency.
