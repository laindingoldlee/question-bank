\textbf{(a)} Let a hole have sides $a\le b\le c$ (in inches) with $a+b+c=16$. The triangle inequality $a+b>c$ gives $16-c>c$, so $c\le7$; and $3c\ge a+b+c=16$ gives $c\ge6$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $c=7$: $a+b=9$ with $a\le b\le7$: $(2,7,7)$, $(3,6,7)$, $(4,5,7)$.
\item $c=6$: $a+b=10$ with $a\le b\le6$: $(4,6,6)$, $(5,5,6)$.
\end{itemize}
So there are exactly $5$ non-congruent triangles with integer sides and perimeter $16$, and since no two holes are congruent, there are at most $5$ holes. Six holes are impossible.

\textbf{(b)} With $5$ holes, the holes are exactly these five triangles. By Heron's formula with semiperimeter $8$, their areas are
\[\sqrt{8\cdot6\cdot1\cdot1}=4\sqrt3,\quad \sqrt{8\cdot5\cdot2\cdot1}=4\sqrt5,\quad \sqrt{8\cdot4\cdot3\cdot1}=4\sqrt6,\quad \sqrt{8\cdot4\cdot2\cdot2}=8\sqrt2,\quad \sqrt{8\cdot3\cdot3\cdot2}=12,\]
in the order listed above:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(8cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
void tri(pair o, real a, real bb, real c, string s){
  real x=(bb^2-a^2+c^2)/(2c); real y=sqrt(bb^2-x^2);
  pair P=o, Q=o+(c,0), R=o+(x,y);
  fill(P--Q--R--cycle,paleblue); draw(P--Q--R--cycle,linewidth(0.8));
  label("$"+(string)c+"$",(P+Q)/2,S,fontsize(8pt));
  label("$"+(string)bb+"$",(P+R)/2,W,fontsize(8pt));
  label("$"+(string)a+"$",(Q+R)/2,E,fontsize(8pt));
  label(s,o+(c/2,-1.6),fontsize(8pt));
}
tri((0,0),7,2,7,"$4\sqrt3$"); tri((9.5,0),6,3,7,"$4\sqrt5$"); tri((19,0),5,4,7,"$4\sqrt6$");
tri((4,-8.5),6,4,6,"$8\sqrt2$"); tri((14,-8.5),5,5,6,"$12$");
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

In inches, the cloak has bases $24$ and $60$ and height $72$, so its area is $\dfrac{24+60}{2}\cdot72=3024$ square inches. The remaining area is
\[3024-\left(12+8\sqrt2+4\sqrt3+4\sqrt5+4\sqrt6\right)=\boxed{3012-8\sqrt2-4\sqrt3-4\sqrt5-4\sqrt6}\approx2975.0\text{ square inches}.\]
(The holes are tiny compared with the cloak, so five non-overlapping holes certainly fit.)
