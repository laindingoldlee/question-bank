Let $c$, $w$ and $k$ be the numbers of correct, wrong and blank answers, so $c+w+k=10$ and $5c-2w+k=28$. Subtracting the first equation from the second gives
\[4c-3w=18,\qquad\text{so}\qquad c=\dfrac{18+3w}{4}.\]
For $c$ to be an integer, $3w\equiv-18\equiv2\pmod4$, that is, $w\equiv2\pmod4$, so $w=2$, $6$ or $10$. Then $w=2$ gives $c=6$, $k=2$; $w=6$ gives $c=9$ and $c+w>10$; $w=10$ gives $c=12$. So the only possibility is $(c,w,k)=(6,2,2)$.

\textbf{(a)} There is exactly $\boxed{1}$ combination: $6$ correct, $2$ wrong and $2$ blank.

\textbf{(b)} Choose which $6$ problems are correct and which $2$ of the remaining $4$ are wrong:
\[\binom{10}{6}\binom42=\dfrac{10!}{6!\,2!\,2!}=210\cdot6=\boxed{1260}.\]
