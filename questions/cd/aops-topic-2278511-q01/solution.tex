Let there be $k$ bags.

\textbf{Orange candies.} Each bag has the same number of them, so $k$ divides $100$.

\textbf{Chewy candies.} The $k$ bags have $k$ different numbers of chewy candies, which are nonnegative integers, so their total is at least $0+1+\cdots+(k-1)=\dfrac{k(k-1)}{2}$. Hence $\dfrac{k(k-1)}{2}\le80$, which gives $k\le13$ (since $13\cdot12=156\le160$ but $14\cdot13=182>160$).

The divisors of $100$ that are at most $13$ are $1,2,4,5,10$, so $k\le10$. Ten bags work: put $10$ orange candies in each bag and $0,1,2,\ldots,8$ and $44$ chewy candies (total $36+44=80$). The answer is $\boxed{10}$.

(If every bag must contain at least one chewy candy, the bound becomes $\dfrac{k(k+1)}{2}\le80$, i.e. $k\le12$, and the answer is still $10$, e.g. with $1,2,\ldots,9$ and $35$ chewy candies.)
