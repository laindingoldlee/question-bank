Let $y=x^2-3x$. Then the right side is $-3\left(x^2-3x\right)-2=-3y-2$, and the equation becomes
\[
(y+2)^2=-3y-2\iff y^2+7y+6=0\iff(y+1)(y+6)=0 .
\]
\begin{itemize}
\item $y=-1$: $x^2-3x+1=0$, so $x=\dfrac{3\pm\sqrt{9-4}}{2}=\dfrac{3\pm\sqrt5}{2}$.
\item $y=-6$: $x^2-3x+6=0$ has discriminant $9-24<0$, so no real solutions.
\end{itemize}
The real solutions are $\boxed{x=\dfrac{3+\sqrt5}{2}\text{ and }x=\dfrac{3-\sqrt5}{2}}$.

(Note the quadratic formula: $ax^2+bx+c=0$ gives $x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}$. Here $b=-3$, so $-b=3$, and the whole numerator is divided by $2$; writing the roots as $-1.5\pm\sqrt5$ makes two mistakes.)
