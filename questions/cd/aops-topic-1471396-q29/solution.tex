Let $P$ and $Q$ be pieces with $P=Q+5$, and let $R$ be the third piece. The ``twice'' relation joins two of the three pieces.

\textbf{It joins $P$ and $Q$.} Then $P=2Q$ (since $Q<P$), so $Q=5$, $P=10$, $R=21$: longest $21$.

\textbf{It joins $R$ with $P$ or $Q$.} Using $P+Q+R=36$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $R=2P$: $4P-5=36$, pieces $10.25,\ 5.25,\ 20.5$; longest $20.5$.
\item $P=2R$: $\dfrac{5}{2}P-5=36$, pieces $16.4,\ 11.4,\ 8.2$; longest $16.4$.
\item $R=2Q$: $4Q+5=36$, pieces $12.75,\ 7.75,\ 15.5$; longest $15.5$.
\item $Q=2R$: $\dfrac{5}{2}Q+5=36$, pieces $17.4,\ 12.4,\ 6.2$; longest $17.4$.
\end{itemize}
These five sets of pieces are all different and all lengths are positive, so the answer is
\[
21+20.5+16.4+15.5+17.4=\boxed{90.8}.
\]

\textbf{The student's errors.} Cases (1), (2), (3) and (5) are correct (they are $R=2Q$, $Q=2R$, $R=2P$ and $P=2R$). Case (4) is wrong: $c=2a$ and $b=2a-5$ give $5a-5=36$, $a=8.2$, so the pieces are $8.2,\ 11.4,\ 16.4$, the same rope as case (5), not $20.5$. So case (4) counts case (5) a second time, with a wrong value. And the case in which the doubled piece and the longer-by-$5$ piece are the same pair ($a$, $a+5=2a$: pieces $5,10,21$) is missing. Replacing the $20.5$ of case (4) by $21$ gives $90.8$.
