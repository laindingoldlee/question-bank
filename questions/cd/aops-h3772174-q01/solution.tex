Let $u,v$ be the two similarity ratios produced by one altitude cut. Every descendant has scale $u^iv^j$ relative to the original, for nonnegative integers $i,j$. Label it by its ancestry counts $(i,j)$; cutting replaces $(i,j)$ by $(i+1,j)$ and $(i,j+1)$. Give a triangle of type $(i,j)$ the artificial weight $2^{-i-j}$. Total weight remains $4$, since a cut replaces a weight by two halves. If no two current triangles were congruent, each type could occur at most once. But a finite subset of the nonnegative integer pairs has total weight strictly less than $\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-i-j}=4$, a contradiction. Equal types always give congruent triangles; possible additional coincidences only strengthen the argument.
