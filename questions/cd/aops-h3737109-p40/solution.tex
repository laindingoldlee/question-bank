Since $AB^2+BC^2=AC^2$ and $AD^2+CD^2=AC^2$, both $B,D$ are right angles. Take $A=(1,0),B=(0,0),C=(0,7),D=(4,4)$ and let $M=(\dfrac{1}{2},\dfrac{7}{2})$ be the midpoint of $AC$. For interior $P=(x,y)$, its distances to $AB$ and $BC$ are $y,x$, so the ratio condition gives $y=7x$: thus $P$ lies on $BM$. Equal distances to $DA,DC$ put $P$ on the internal angle bisector of $D$. Because $DA=DC$, that bisector is $DM$. The two lines intersect at $M$, so $P=M$ and its distance to $AB$ is $\boxed{\dfrac{7}{2}}$.

texpreamble("\usepackage{amsmath,amssymb}");

pair A=(1,0),B=(0,0),C=(0,7),D=(4,4),M=(.5,3.5);draw(A--B--C--D--cycle);draw(A--C,dashed);draw(B--M--D,blue);draw(M--(.5,0),red+dashed);label("A",A,SE);label("B",B,SW);label("C",C,N);label("D",D,E);label("P=M",M,NE);label("1",(A+B)/2,S);label("7",(B+C)/2,W);
\end{asy}
