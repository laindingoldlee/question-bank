Two people $X$ and $Y$ can shake hands only if neither is taller than the other, that is, only if they have the same height; and since everyone shakes as many hands as possible, every pair of equally tall people shakes exactly once. So if the people split into height classes of sizes $n_1,n_2,\dots$ with $n_1+n_2+\dots=10$, the number of handshakes is
\[\binom{n_1}{2}+\binom{n_2}{2}+\cdots,\]
where the relevant values are $\binom22=1,\binom32=3,\binom42=6,\binom52=10,\binom62=15,\binom72=21$.

\textbf{Totals $1$ to $18$ occur.} Listing only the classes of size at least $2$ (everyone else has a unique height):
\[
\begin{array}{c|c||c|c||c|c}
1 & 2 & 7 & 4,2 & 13 & 5,3\\
2 & 2,2 & 8 & 4,2,2 & 14 & 5,3,2\\
3 & 3 & 9 & 4,3 & 15 & 6\\
4 & 3,2 & 10 & 5 & 16 & 6,2\\
5 & 3,2,2 & 11 & 5,2 & 17 & 6,2,2\\
6 & 4 & 12 & 4,4 & 18 & 6,3
\end{array}
\]
Each uses at most $10$ people.

\textbf{The total $19$ is impossible.} Let the largest class have size $k$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $k\ge7$: that class alone gives at least $21>19$.
\item $k=6$: the other $4$ people must contribute $4$, but $4$ people contribute one of $0,1,2,3,6$.
\item $k=5$: the other $5$ people (in classes of size at most $5$) must contribute $9$, but $5$ people contribute one of $0,1,2,3,4,6,10$.
\item $k=4$: the other $6$ people in classes of size at most $4$ contribute at most $6+1=7$, so the total is at most $13$.
\item $k\le3$: the total is at most $3+3+3=9$.
\end{itemize}
Hence the answer is
\[\boxed{19}.\]
