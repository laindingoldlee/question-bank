A student thinks that $1,1,1$ is a Pythagorean triple, and is puzzled that after converting inches to centimetres ($1$ inch $=2.54$ cm) the triangle no longer seems to fit the Pythagorean theorem.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Show that no right triangle has all three sides equal.
\item[(b)] A right triangle has both legs equal to $1$ inch. Find its hypotenuse in inches and in centimetres, and check that the side lengths in centimetres satisfy the Pythagorean theorem.
\item[(c)] Prove that if positive numbers $a,b,c$ satisfy $a^2+b^2=c^2$, then so do $ka,kb,kc$ for every $k>0$. Explain why this settles the student's worry.
\end{enumerate}
