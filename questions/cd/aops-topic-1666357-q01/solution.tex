Each interior angle of a regular decagon is $\dfrac{(10-2)\cdot180^\circ}{10}=144^\circ$.

Triangle $ABC$ is isosceles with $AB=BC$ and apex angle $\angle ABC=144^\circ$, so $\angle BCA=\dfrac{180^\circ-144^\circ}{2}=18^\circ$. Likewise triangle $BCD$ is isosceles with $BC=CD$, so $\angle CBD=18^\circ$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair[] V;string[] L={"A","B","C","D","E","F","G","H","I","J"};
for(int k=0;k<10;++k) V.push(dir(-126+36*k));
draw(V[0]--V[1]--V[2]--V[3]--V[4]--V[5]--V[6]--V[7]--V[8]--V[9]--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
pair X=extension(V[0],V[2],V[1],V[3]);
draw(V[0]--V[2],blue);draw(V[1]--V[3],blue);
for(int k=0;k<10;++k) label("$"+L[k]+"$",V[k],V[k]);
dot(X);label("$X$",X,dir(115));
draw(arc(X,0.2,degrees(V[1]-X),degrees(V[0]-X)),red);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

In triangle $XBC$ the angles at $B$ and $C$ are $\angle XBC=\angle CBD=18^\circ$ and $\angle XCB=\angle ACB=18^\circ$, so $\angle BXC=180^\circ-36^\circ=144^\circ$. The angle $AXB$ is its supplement, since $A$, $X$, $C$ are collinear:
\[
\angle AXB=180^\circ-144^\circ=\boxed{36^\circ}.
\]
