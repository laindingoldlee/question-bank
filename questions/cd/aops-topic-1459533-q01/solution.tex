\textbf{(a)} Take $x=1$, $y=0$: the inequality would say $1\le0$. (Any $x,y$ with $xy<0$, such as $x=1$, $y=-1$, also fail, since then the right side is negative and the left side is positive.)

\textbf{(b)} Divide by $y^2>0$: the inequality becomes $t^2-3t+1\le0$. The roots of $t^2-3t+1$ are $\dfrac{3\pm\sqrt5}{2}$, so the inequality holds exactly when
\[
\boxed{\dfrac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\le\dfrac {x}{y}\le\dfrac{3+\sqrt5}{2}}\qquad(\approx0.382\le t\le2.618).
\]
For example $x=y$ works ($2\le3$), but $x=3$, $y=1$ does not ($10>9$). So the inequality can only be used in a problem whose conditions force $x$ and $y$ to be close in this sense.
