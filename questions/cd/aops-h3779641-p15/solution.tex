Choose the X cells and then the O cells in $\binom93\binom62=1260$ ways. Os cannot win with only two marks. A winning X configuration consists of one of eight lines, with the two Os chosen among the other six cells, giving $8\binom62=120$ bad boards. Three Xs cannot complete two different lines, so none is counted twice. Thus $1260-120=\boxed{1140}$ boards remain.
