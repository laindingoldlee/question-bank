\textbf{(a)} For $b=4$: $\dbinom22+\dbinom32=1+3=4$, but $\dbinom42=6$.

\textbf{(b)} Count the $3$-element subsets of $\{1,2,\dots,b\}$ in two ways. There are $\dbinom b3$ of them. On the other hand, sort them by their largest element $m+1$, where $2\le m\le b-1$: the other two elements are chosen from $\{1,\dots,m\}$, in $\dbinom m2$ ways. Hence
\[
\sum_{m=2}^{b-1}\dbinom m2=\dbinom b3 .
\]
$\blacksquare$ This is the \emph{hockey stick identity}: in Pascal's triangle the entries $\dbinom22,\dbinom32,\dots,\dbinom{b-1}{2}$ run down a diagonal (the handle), and their sum $\dbinom b3$ sits just below and to the side of the last one (the blade). In general $\sum_{m=k}^{n}\dbinom mk=\dbinom{n+1}{k+1}$, by the same argument.
