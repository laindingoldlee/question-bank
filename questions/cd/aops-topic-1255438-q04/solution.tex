The two diagonals and the two midlines cut the square into $8$ small congruent right isosceles triangles meeting at the centre.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");

draw((0,0)--(2,2)); draw((2,0)--(0,2)); draw((1,0)--(1,2)); draw((0,1)--(2,1));
fill((0,0)--(1,0)--(1,1)--cycle,lightblue); fill((0,2)--(2,2)--(1,1)--cycle,palegreen);
draw((0,0)--(2,0)--(2,2)--(0,2)--cycle,linewidth(1));
draw((0,0)--(2,2)); draw((2,0)--(0,2)); draw((1,0)--(1,2)); draw((0,1)--(2,1));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Count by size.
\begin{itemize}
\item Small triangles (one piece, e.g. shaded blue): $8$.
\item Triangles made of two adjacent small pieces, with a side of the square as base and the centre as apex (e.g. shaded green): $4$, one on each side.
\item Half-squares cut off by a diagonal: $4$, two for each diagonal.
\end{itemize}
No other three lines of the figure bound a triangle (for instance, two small pieces meeting along a half-diagonal form a small square, not a triangle). In total $8+4+4=\boxed{16}$ triangles.
