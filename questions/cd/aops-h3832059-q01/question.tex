An incidence structure consists of points and designated subsets called lines. Consider the axiom: any two distinct points lie on exactly one line.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Give a finite structure satisfying this axiom in which every point lies on one common line.
\item Give another satisfying it in which three points are not all on any one line.
\item Explain what this shows about whether the first axiom implies the existence of three noncollinear points.
\end{enumerate}
