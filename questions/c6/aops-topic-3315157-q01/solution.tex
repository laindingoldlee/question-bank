Divisibility by 9 forces the digit sum to be 27 or 36, since six distinct digits sum between 21 and 39. Let $U,V$ be the sums of the three odd and three even positions. Divisibility by 11 requires $U-V$ to be a multiple of 11. If $U+V=27$, the groups must sum 8 and 19. The sum 8 triples are 125 and 134. Disjoint sum 19 triples are 379,469,478 for 125, and 289,568 for 134: five unordered group pairs. If $U+V=36$, both groups sum 18. The three disjoint unordered pairs are 378 with 459,279 with 468, and 189 with 567. Thus there are eight unordered pairs of digit groups. Each may be assigned to odd and even positions in two ways, and each group ordered in $3!$ ways. The total is $8\cdot2\cdot(3!)^2=\boxed{576}$.
