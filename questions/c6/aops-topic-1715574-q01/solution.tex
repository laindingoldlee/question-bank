If $N=p_1^{e_1}\cdots p_r^{e_r}$, it has $(e_1+1)\cdots(e_r+1)$ divisors. Since $14=14\cdot1=7\cdot2$, either $N=p^{13}$ or $N=p^6q$ with distinct primes $p,q$. As $12=2^2\cdot3\mid N$, $N$ has the two prime factors $2$ and $3$ with $2^2\mid N$, so $N=p^6q$ with $\{p,q\}=\{2,3\}$ and the exponent of $2$ at least $2$: $p=2$, $q=3$. Hence $N=2^6\cdot3=\boxed{192}$. Check: $(6+1)(1+1)=14$ and $192=12\cdot16$.
