An equilateral triangle with side $s$ has area $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}s^2$. From $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}s^2=\sqrt3$ we get $s^2=4$, so $s=2$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair O=(0,0);
pair[] V={(2.0000,0.0000),(1.0000,1.7321),(-1.0000,1.7321),(-2.0000,0.0000),(-1.0000,-1.7321),(1.0000,-1.7321)};
draw(circle(O,2),blue);
for(int k=0;k<6;++k){draw(V[k]--V[(k+1)%6],linewidth(0.9)); draw(O--V[k],gray);}
fill(O--V[0]--V[1]--cycle,palegreen); draw(O--V[0]--V[1]--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
dot(O); label("$O$",O,W);
label("$2$",(V[0]+V[1])/2,NE); label("$R=2$",(O+V[3])/2+(0,0.12),N,blue);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Each triangle has a vertex at the centre $O$ and its other two vertices at vertices of the hexagon, so every vertex of the hexagon is at distance $s=2$ from $O$. Thus the circumcircle has radius $2$ and area $\pi\cdot2^2=\boxed{4\pi}$.
