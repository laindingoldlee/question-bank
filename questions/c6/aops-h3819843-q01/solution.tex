Place the vertices at $\pm se_1,\pm se_2,\pm se_3$, where $s=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$ and the $e_i$ are orthonormal. Let the cylinder's axis have unit direction $u=(u_1,u_2,u_3)$.

The height must be at least $2s\max_i|u_i|$. The projections of each opposite vertex pair on the plane perpendicular to the axis are separated by $2s\sqrt{1-u_i^2}$; any containing circular cross-section therefore has radius at least half this distance, even if its center is offset. Consequently
\[
h\ge2s\max|u_i|,\qquad r^2\ge s^2(1-\min u_i^2).
\]
Since $\sum u_i^2=1$, we have $\max|u_i|\ge\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$ and $\min u_i^2\le\dfrac{1}{3}$. Thus
\[
V=\pi r^2h\ge2\pi s^3\cdot\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\cdot\dfrac{2}{3}
=\dfrac{\pi\sqrt6}{9}.
\]
Equality occurs for an axis through the origin in direction $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(1,1,1)$, perpendicular to opposite faces. Then $r=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$ and $h=\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{3}}$, and the resulting cylinder contains all vertices and hence their convex hull. The minimum is $\boxed{\dfrac{\pi\sqrt6}{9}}$.
