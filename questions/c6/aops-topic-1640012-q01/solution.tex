\textbf{Claim:} after $k$ rounds the remaining numbers are exactly the multiples of $2^k$ up to $4000$, in increasing order.

This is true for $k=0$. If after $k$ rounds the row is $2^k,2\cdot2^k,3\cdot2^k,\dots$, then the numbers in the odd places are the odd multiples of $2^k$, and crossing them out leaves the even multiples, $2^{k+1},2\cdot2^{k+1},\dots$, the multiples of $2^{k+1}$. This proves the claim by induction.

After $10$ rounds the multiples of $1024$ remain: $1024,2048,3072$. The eleventh round crosses out the first and third, leaving $\boxed{2048}$, the largest power of $2$ not exceeding $4000$.
