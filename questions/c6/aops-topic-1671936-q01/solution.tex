\textbf{Similar triangles.} Since $DE\parallel BC$, the corresponding angles are equal: $\angle ADE=\angle ABC$ and $\angle AED=\angle ACB$. So triangles $ADE$ and $ABC$ are similar (AA), with ratio $\dfrac{AD}{AB}=\dfrac{1}{2}$. Hence $\dfrac{AE}{AC}=\dfrac{1}{2}$. As $E$ lies on segment $AC$ (the parallel to $BC$ through the point $D$ of side $AB$ enters the triangle and leaves it through side $AC$), $E$ is the midpoint of $AC$. Moreover $DE=\dfrac{1}{2}BC$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(1.3,3.4),B=(0,0),C=(5,0);
pair Dd=(A+B)/2,Ep=(A+C)/2;
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));draw(Dd--Ep,linewidth(0.9));
draw(Dd--C,blue+dashed);
draw((A+Dd)/2+0.12*dir(-30)--(A+Dd)/2-0.12*dir(-30));draw((B+Dd)/2+0.12*dir(-30)--(B+Dd)/2-0.12*dir(-30));
pair m1=(Dd+Ep)/2,m2=(B+C)/2;
draw(m1+(-0.12,0.1)--m1--m1+(-0.12,-0.1));draw(m2+(-0.12,0.1)--m2--m2+(-0.12,-0.1));
label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,SW);label("$C$",C,SE);label("$D$",Dd,W);label("$E$",Ep,NE);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\textbf{Without similarity (areas).} Triangles $DEB$ and $DEC$ have the same base $DE$ and the same height (the distance between the parallel lines $DE$ and $BC$), so $[DEB]=[DEC]$. Hence
\[
[ABE]=[ADE]+[DEB]=[ADE]+[DEC]=[ADC]=\dfrac{1}{2}[ABC],
\]
the last because $CD$ is a median. As triangles $ABE$ and $ABC$ have the same height from $B$, this gives $AE=\dfrac{1}{2}AC$, and $E$ is the midpoint of $AC$. $\blacksquare$

This is the converse of the midpoint theorem; in general, a line parallel to one side divides the other two sides in the same ratio.
