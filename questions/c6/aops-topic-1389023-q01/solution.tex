\textbf{Yes.} A necessary condition is fine: digit sums are congruent to the number modulo $9$, squares are $\equiv0,1,4,7\pmod9$, and $2017\equiv1\pmod9$.

For $n\ge2$ let $a_n=\underbrace{33\ldots3}_{n-1}4=\dfrac{10^n+2}{3}$. Then
\[
a_n^2=\dfrac{10^{2n}+4\cdot10^n+4}{9}=\dfrac{10^{2n}-1}{9}+4\cdot\dfrac{10^n-1}{9}+1 .
\]
Here $\dfrac{10^{2n}-1}{9}$ is the number made of $2n$ ones and $4\cdot\dfrac{10^n-1}{9}$ is $n$ fours, so their sum is $n$ ones followed by $n$ fives, and adding $1$ gives
\[
a_n^2=\underbrace{11\ldots1}_{n}\underbrace{55\ldots5}_{n-1}6 \qquad(\text{e.g. }34^2=1156,\ 334^2=111556).
\]
Its digit sum is $n+5(n-1)+6=6n+1$. Taking $n=336$ gives digit sum $2017$:
\[
\boxed{\left(\underbrace{33\ldots3}_{335}4\right)^2}\ \text{has digit sum }2017 .
\]
