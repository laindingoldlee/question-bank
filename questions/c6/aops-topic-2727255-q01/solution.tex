Consider the $n$ sums $S_k=a_1+\cdots+a_k$ for $k=1,\dots,n$. If some $S_k\equiv0\pmod n$, take $a_1,\dots,a_k$. Otherwise the $n$ sums take at most $n-1$ nonzero residues mod $n$, so by the pigeonhole principle two are congruent, say $S_i\equiv S_j$ with $i<j$. Then
\[a_{i+1}+a_{i+2}+\cdots+a_j=S_j-S_i\equiv0\pmod n.\ \blacksquare\]
(In fact the chosen numbers can always be taken consecutive.)
