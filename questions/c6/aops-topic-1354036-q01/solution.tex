Factor the denominator as a difference of squares:
\[
n^4+n^2+1=(n^2+1)^2-n^2=(n^2-n+1)(n^2+n+1).
\]
The two factors differ by $2n$, so
\[
\dfrac{n}{n^4+n^2+1}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{n^2-n+1}-\dfrac{1}{n^2+n+1}\right).
\]
Since $(n+1)^2-(n+1)+1=n^2+n+1$, the sum telescopes. The $n=0$ term is $0$, and for $N\ge1$
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{N}\dfrac{n}{n^4+n^2+1}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{N^2+N+1}\right)\to\dfrac{1}{2} .
\]
The sum is $\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}}$.
