Both sides must be positive, so $x>0$. Take logarithms (any fixed base, the same one as in the exponents):
\[
\log3\,(\log3+\log x)=\log5\,(\log5+\log x)
\ \Longrightarrow\
\log x\,(\log3-\log5)=(\log5)^2-(\log3)^2=(\log5-\log3)(\log5+\log3).
\]
Since $\log3\ne\log5$, we may divide by $\log3-\log5$:
\[
\log x=-(\log5+\log3)=-\log15,\qquad x=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{15}}.
\]
Check: $3x=\dfrac{1}{5}$ and $5x=\dfrac{1}{3}$, and $\left(\dfrac{1}{5}\right)^{\log3}=5^{-\log3}=10^{-\log5\log3}=3^{-\log5}=\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^{\log5}$ (with $\log$ to base $10$).
