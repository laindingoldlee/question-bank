Since $6^2+8^2=10^2$, triangle $ABC$ has a right angle at $B$, and its area is $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot6\cdot8=24$. Triangle $ABD$ is isosceles with base $AB=6$; its height to $AB$ is $\sqrt{5^2-3^2}=4$, so its area is $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot6\cdot4=12$.

Put $B=(0,0)$, $A=(0,6)$, $C=(8,0)$. Then $D=(\pm4,3)$. The point $D'=(4,3)$ on the same side of $AB$ as $C$ is the midpoint of $AC$ (the circumcentre of the right triangle), so $A$, $D'$, $C$ would be collinear and $AD'BC$ would not be a genuine quadrilateral. Hence $D=(-4,3)$, on the other side of $AB$, and $ADBC$ is the union of the two triangles, which meet along $AB$:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair B=(0,0),A=(0,6),C=(8,0),D=(-4,3),Dp=(4,3);
fill(A--D--B--C--cycle,rgb(0.9,0.94,1));
draw(A--D--B--C--cycle,linewidth(1));
draw(A--B,dashed);
draw((0.45,0)--(0.45,0.45)--(0,0.45));
draw(Dp,linewidth(4)+gray);
label("$D'$",Dp,NE,gray);
label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,S);label("$C$",C,SE);label("$D$",D,W);
label("$6$",(0,3),E);label("$8$",(4,0),S);label("$10$",(6.36,1.98));
label("$5$",(A+D)/2,NW);label("$5$",(D+B)/2,SW);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\[
[ADBC]=[ABC]+[ABD]=24+12=\boxed{36}.
\]
