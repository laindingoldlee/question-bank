\textbf{Which integers are differences of two squares?} Squares are $\equiv0$ or $1\pmod4$, so a difference of two squares is $\equiv0,1$ or $3\pmod4$, never $\equiv2$. Conversely, an odd $N$ equals $\left(\dfrac{N+1}2\right)^2-\left(\dfrac{N-1}2\right)^2$, and $N=4k$ equals $(k+1)^2-(k-1)^2$. So $N$ is a difference of two squares exactly when $N\not\equiv2\pmod4$.

\textbf{The products.} The first $25$ primes are the primes below $100$: $2$ and $24$ odd primes. A product of two odd primes is odd, so it works. A product $2p$ with $p$ odd is $\equiv2\pmod4$, so it does not. There are $\dbinom{25}{2}=300$ pairs, of which $24$ contain the prime $2$. So
\[\dfrac mn=\dfrac{300-24}{300}=\dfrac{276}{300}=\dfrac{23}{25},\qquad mn=23\cdot25=\boxed{575}.\]
