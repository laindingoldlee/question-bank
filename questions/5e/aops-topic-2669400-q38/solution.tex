Rearranging the equation in two ways:
\[(x-y)(2x+2y+1)=2x^2-2y^2+x-y=y^2,\qquad(x-y)(3x+3y+1)=3x^2-3y^2+x-y=x^2.\]
Since $y^2>0$ and $2x+2y+1>0$, we have $x-y>0$.

Let $d=\gcd(x-y,\,2x+2y+1)$. Then $d^2$ divides the product $y^2$, so $d\mid y$; then $d\mid(x-y)+y=x$, and so $d\mid(2x+2y+1)-2x-2y=1$. Thus $x-y$ and $2x+2y+1$ are coprime positive integers whose product is a square, so each is a perfect square (every prime appears to an even power in the product and divides only one factor).

Write $x-y=s^2$. Then $3x+3y+1=\dfrac{x^2}{s^2}=\left(\dfrac xs\right)^2$ is an integer that is the square of a rational number, hence the square of an integer. $\blacksquare$ (Example: $x=22$, $y=18$ give $4$, $81$ and $121$.)
