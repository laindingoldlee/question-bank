Subtract the third equation from the second. Since $b>c$, division by $b-c$ gives $b+c-a=-(b+c)$, hence $a=2(b+c)$. Substitute this into the first equation. Its left product is $2(b+c)^2+bc$, so the equation simplifies to $(b+c)^2+3bc+7=0$. Every term on the left is positive, contradiction. Thus $\boxed{\text{no such positive ordered triple exists}}$.
