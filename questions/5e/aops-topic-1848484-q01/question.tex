The usual proof that $\sqrt2$ is irrational uses parity: from $b^2=2a^2$ one shows that $b$, and then $a$, must be even. Give two other proofs that $\sqrt2$ is irrational:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] using prime factorisation;
\item[(b)] by descent: assume $\sqrt2=\dfrac {p}{q}$ with positive integers $p,q$ and $q$ as small as possible, and show that $\sqrt2=\dfrac{2q-p}{p-q}$ leads to a contradiction.
\end{enumerate}
