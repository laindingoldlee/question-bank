Let $S_k$ denote the sum. For every $k\ge5$, its units digit is $3$: the sum through $4!$ is $33$ and each later factorial is divisible by $10$. Thus $S_k$ is not a square.

For $k=5,6,7$,
\[
S_5=153=3^2\cdot17,\quad
S_6=873=3^2\cdot97,\quad
S_7=5913=3^4\cdot73.
\]
For $k\ge8$, $S_8=46233\equiv9\pmod{27}$ and every later factorial is divisible by $27$. Hence the exact exponent of $3$ in $S_k$ is $2$ for $k\ge8$.

If $S_k=u^v$, then $v$ divides the exact exponent of $3$. That exponent is $2$ except at $k=7$, where it is $4$. Every possible $v>1$ is therefore even, making $S_k$ a square, already ruled out by its last digit. This proves the claim.
