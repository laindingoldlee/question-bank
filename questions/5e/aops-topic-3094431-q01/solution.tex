For each integer $b\ge2$, put $M=b^2+k+1$ and $a=1-(b+1)M<-1$. The polynomial $X^2+M(b+1)X+(b^2+Mb+k)$ has root $-1$, since $M=b^2+k+1$; its other root is $a$. Consequently $a^2+b^2+k=-M(ab+a+b)$. The divisor is nonzero because $a(b+1)+b<0$. Distinct choices of $b$ give infinitely many valid pairs.
