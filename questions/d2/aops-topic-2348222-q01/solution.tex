\textbf{(a)} $\dfrac{254.5}{251}\approx1.01394$, a grade of $101.39\%$, and $\dfrac{274.5}{271}\approx1.01292$, a grade of $101.29\%$. So the grade has indeed gone down.

\textbf{(b)} Since $t>0$ and $t+10>0$, the inequality $\dfrac{p+10}{t+10}<\dfrac pt$ is equivalent (multiply both sides by $t(t+10)$) to
\[t(p+10)<p(t+10)\iff10t<10p\iff t<p,\]
which is true. So a $10$ out of $10$ lowers any grade above $100\%$.

Another way to see it: $\dfrac pt=1+\dfrac{p-t}{t}$. A perfect assignment adds $10$ to both $p$ and $t$, so the extra $p-t=3.5$ points stay the same while the total $t$ grows, and the excess $\dfrac{3.5}{t}$ over $100\%$ shrinks. A grade above $100\%$ is pulled towards $\dfrac{10}{10}=100\%$ by every assignment scored exactly $100\%$ (just as a grade below $100\%$ is pulled up by it).
