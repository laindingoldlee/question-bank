For an upper bound, take the interval $[0,1]$ and cut at every $\dfrac{k}{11}$ and $\dfrac{j}{12}$ with $1\le k\le10$, $1\le j\le11$. These 21 interior cuts are distinct, yielding 22 pieces that can be grouped into either eleven elevenths or twelve twelfths. For the lower bound, form a bipartite graph with the 11 shares in one division and the 12 shares in the other; each cake piece is an edge between the shares containing it. A connected component containing $a$ first-division shares and $b$ second-division shares has equal total mass $\dfrac{a}{11}=\dfrac{b}{12}$. Coprimality forces $a=11,b=12$ for a nonempty component. Thus the graph is connected on 23 vertices and has at least 22 edges. Therefore the minimum is $\boxed{22}$.
