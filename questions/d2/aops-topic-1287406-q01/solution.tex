\textbf{Modulo $2$.} If exactly one of $a,b$ is odd, then exactly two of $a^2,ab,b^2$ are even and $M$ is odd; if both are odd, all three terms are odd and $M$ is odd. Since $M$ is even, $a$ and $b$ are both even, and $4\mid M$.

\textbf{Modulo $5$.} Multiply by $4$: $4M=(2a+b)^2+3b^2\equiv0\pmod5$. If $5\nmid b$, then $\left((2a+b)b^{-1}\right)^2\equiv-3\equiv2\pmod5$, but the squares modulo $5$ are $0,1,4$. So $5\mid b$, then $5\mid(2a+b)^2$ gives $5\mid2a$, and $5\mid a$.

So $a$ and $b$ are both divisible by $10$, and each of $a^2,ab,b^2$ is divisible by $100$. Hence $100\mid M$. $\blacksquare$ (In the post's language, the smallest $c\ge0$ with $100\mid M-c$ is $c=0$.)
