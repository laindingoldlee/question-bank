If the blivet passes $n$ tests, then by Bayes' theorem
\[P(\text{good}\mid n\text{ passes})=\dfrac{0.8\cdot1}{0.8\cdot1+0.2\cdot0.9^n}.\]
We need this to be at least $0.95$:
\[0.8\ge0.95\left(0.8+0.2\cdot0.9^n\right)\iff 0.04\ge0.19\cdot0.9^n\iff 0.9^n\le\dfrac{4}{19}\approx0.2105 .\]
Now $0.9^{5}=0.59049$, so $0.9^{10}\approx0.3487$, $0.9^{14}\approx0.2288$ and $0.9^{15}\approx0.2059$. The powers decrease, so the least $n$ is $\boxed{15}$.

(After $14$ passes the probability of a good blivet is about $0.9459$; after $15$ it is about $0.9510$.)
