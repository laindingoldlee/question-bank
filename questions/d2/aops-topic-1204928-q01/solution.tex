Let $N=\overline{abc}$ with digit sum $S\le27<81$. The condition says $N=81q+S$, i.e. $81\mid N-S=99a+9b$, i.e. $9\mid11a+b$, i.e. $2a+b\equiv0\pmod9$.

For $a=1,\dots,8$, $-2a\not\equiv0\pmod9$, so exactly one digit $b$ works; for $a=9$ both $b=0$ and $b=9$ work. In every case $c$ is free ($10$ choices). The count is $(8\cdot1+2)\cdot10=\boxed{100}$.
