A student divides $P=x^2-y^2+x^2=2x^2-y^2$ by $D=(x+y)(x-y)=x^2-y^2$ and finds two different ``remainders'':
\[
P=2(x^2-y^2)+y^2\qquad\text{and}\qquad P=1\cdot(x^2-y^2)+x^2 .
\]
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Find the quotient and remainder when $P$ is divided by $D$ as polynomials in $x$ (treating $y$ as a constant), and when $P$ is divided by $D$ as polynomials in $y$ (treating $x$ as a constant).
\item[(b)] Explain why both of the student's computations are correct, and why the question ``what is the remainder'' has no single answer for polynomials in two variables unless one says which variable the division is with respect to.
\end{enumerate}
