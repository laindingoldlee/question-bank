$2001=3\cdot23\cdot29$, so each side is $3$, $23$ or $29$ (sides may repeat). A multiset of three sides forms a triangle exactly when the two shorter sides add up to more than the longest.
\begin{itemize}
\item Equilateral: $(3,3,3)$, $(23,23,23)$, $(29,29,29)$, all valid: $3$ triangles.
\item Two equal sides $(p,p,q)$, $p\ne q$: valid when $2p>q$. This holds for $(23,23,3)$, $(23,23,29)$, $(29,29,3)$, $(29,29,23)$, but fails for $(3,3,23)$ and $(3,3,29)$: $4$ triangles.
\item All different: $(3,23,29)$ fails, since $3+23=26<29$.
\end{itemize}
The total is $3+4=\boxed{7}$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");

real s=23,t=29;pair A=(0,0),B=(t,0),C=(t/2,sqrt(s^2-(t/2)^2));
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.8));
label("$29$",(A+B)/2,S);label("$23$",(A+C)/2,NW);label("$23$",(B+C)/2,NE);
\end{asy}
