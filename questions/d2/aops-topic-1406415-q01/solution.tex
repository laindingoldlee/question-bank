Let $h$ be the number of heads, so there are $10-h$ tails. Every outcome falls into exactly one of three cases:
\begin{itemize}
\item $h\ge6$: at least $6$ heads;
\item $h\le4$: then $10-h\ge6$, at least $6$ tails;
\item $h=5$: exactly $5$ heads and $5$ tails.
\end{itemize}
These cover every value $h=0,1,\dots,10$, so there are no other possibilities. Exchanging heads and tails shows the first two cases have the same probability $p$. The third has probability $\dbinom{10}{5}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2^{10}}=\dfrac{252}{1024}=\dfrac{63}{256}$. Hence $2p+\dfrac{63}{256}=1$, and
\[
p=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1-\dfrac{63}{256}\right)=\dfrac{193}{512}.
\]
The probability is $\boxed{\dfrac{193}{512}}$.
