Complete the square in $x$ and then in $y$:
\[x^2+2x(y-3)+3y^2-2y=(x+y-3)^2-(y-3)^2+3y^2-2y=(x+y-3)^2+2y^2+4y-9=(x+y-3)^2+2(y+1)^2-11.\]
This is at least $-11$, with equality at $y=-1$, $x=4$. So the minimum is $\boxed{-11}$.

It does not factor: if it equalled $(a_1x+b_1y+c_1)(a_2x+b_2y+c_2)$, its degree-two part $x^2+2xy+3y^2$ would equal $(a_1x+b_1y)(a_2x+b_2y)$, which vanishes at the nonzero point $(x,y)=(b_1,-a_1)$ (or at $(1,0)$ if $a_1=b_1=0$). But $x^2+2xy+3y^2=(x+y)^2+2y^2>0$ for every $(x,y)\ne(0,0)$, a contradiction.
