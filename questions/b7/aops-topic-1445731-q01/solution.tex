Put $u=2^x$ and $v=2^y$, so $u,v>0$ and $u+v=12$. Let $c=\log_2 3$, so $3^x=(2^x)^c=u^c$, and $c>1$ because $3>2$. The second equation becomes
\[
g(u)=u^c+(12-u)^c=36,\qquad 0<u<12 .
\]
The function $t\mapsto t^c$ is strictly convex on $t>0$ for $c>1$, so $g$ is strictly convex on $(0,12)$. It is symmetric about $u=6$, so it is strictly decreasing on $(0,6]$ and strictly increasing on $[6,12)$. Hence $g(u)=36$ has at most one solution in each of these two intervals.

Now $u=4$ (that is, $x=2$) gives $3^2+3^{\log_2 8}=9+27=36$, and $u=8$ gives the same by symmetry. So $u\in\{4,8\}$ and the solutions are
\[
\boxed{(x,y)=(2,3)\ \text{and}\ (3,2)} .
\]
Check: $4+8=12$ and $9+27=36$.
