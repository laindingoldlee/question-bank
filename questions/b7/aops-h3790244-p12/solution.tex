Scale the common length to $1$ and let $\theta=\angle ABC$, $c=\cos\theta$, $AB=BC=s$, $BM=t$, $BL=l$. In triangle $BKL$, $BK=KL=1$, so the cosine rule gives $l=2c$. Equal lengths $AM=LM=1$ give $s+l=2tc$, hence $s=2c(t-1)$. The equation $LM^2=1$ becomes $(t-1)(t+1-4c^2)=0$. Since $K$ is interior to $BM$, $t>1$, so $t=4c^2-1$ and $s=8c^3-4c$. In particular $0<\theta<45^\circ$. Now $AC=2s\sin\dfrac{\theta}{2}=1$ simplifies, using $s=\dfrac{\sin4\theta}{\sin\theta}$, to $\sin4\theta=\cos\dfrac{\theta}{2}$. In this angle range the possibilities are $\theta=20^\circ$ or $\theta=\dfrac{180^\circ}{7}$. The second gives $s=t$, since $\sin4\theta=\sin3\theta$, contradicting that $M$ is interior to $BC$. Thus $\theta=20^\circ$. Finally $KM=t-1=2\cos40^\circ$ and $KL=LM=1$, so the isosceles triangle $KLM$ has vertex angle $\angle MLK=2\arcsin(\cos40^\circ)=\boxed{100^\circ}$.
