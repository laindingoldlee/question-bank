Let $a=\sqrt x\ge0$ and $b=\sqrt y\ge0$. The system is linear in $a$ and $b$:
\[
a+b=7,\qquad 3a-4b=-14 .
\]
From the first, $b=7-a$; then $3a-4(7-a)=-14$, so $7a=14$, $a=2$ and $b=5$. Both are nonnegative, so $x=a^2=4$ and $y=b^2=25$. Check: $2+5=7$ and $6-20=-14$.

The system has exactly one solution, $\boxed{(x,y)=(4,25)}$. It cannot have infinitely many solutions: the two linear equations in $a,b$ are not proportional (their coefficient determinant is $1\cdot(-4)-1\cdot3=-7\ne0$). A relation such as $x=\dfrac{4y}{25}$ appears only if one equation is used twice; it holds at the solution, but it does not replace the system.
