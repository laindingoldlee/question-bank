Every digit except the last is followed by a digit at most $9$, so it satisfies $3a_i-1\le9$, i.e. $a_i\le3$.
\begin{itemize}
\item One digit: $1,\dots,9$, giving $9$ numbers.
\item Two digits: $a_1=1$ needs $a_2\ge2$ ($8$ choices); $a_1=2$ needs $a_2\ge5$ ($5$); $a_1=3$ needs $a_2\ge8$ ($2$). Total $15$.
\item Three digits: $a_2\le3$ and $a_2\ge3a_1-1$ force $a_1=1$, $a_2\in\{2,3\}$. Then $a_2=2$ needs $a_3\ge5$ ($5$ choices) and $a_2=3$ needs $a_3\ge8$ ($2$). Total $7$.
\item Four or more digits: $a_1\ge1$ forces $a_2\ge2$ and then $a_3\ge5>3$, but $a_3$ is not the last digit. Impossible.
\end{itemize}
So there are $9+15+7=\boxed{31}$ very uphill numbers.
