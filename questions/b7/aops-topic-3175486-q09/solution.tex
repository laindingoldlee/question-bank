Reserve one $7$-cent stamp. The remaining $7$-cent stamps come in pairs worth $14$, while the total number of $5$- and $6$-cent stamps is even and can be paired into values $10,11,12$. Thus allowable values are exactly $7$ plus nonnegative combinations of $10,11,12,14$. The value $29$ is impossible with these generators: one or two terms total at most $28$, while three or more total at least $30$. Every value $30$ through $39$ is possible, using $10+10+10$, $10+10+11$, $10+10+12$, $10+11+12$, $10+10+14$, $10+11+14$, $12+12+12$, $11+12+14$, $12+12+14$, $11+14+14$. Adding tens then gives all values at least $30$. Hence the greatest impossible postage is $7+29=\boxed{36}$.
