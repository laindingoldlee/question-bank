Write $P(x)=x^3+Ax^2+Bx+C$. For nonzero integer $a$, $\dfrac{P(a)}{a}$ is integral exactly when $a\mid C$. Thus $C>0$ has three positive divisors, so $C=p^2$ for a prime $p$.
The two numbers in the second condition are $U=A+C$ and $V=1+B$. The roots are nonzero integers whose product is $-p^2$, so their absolute values are either $\{1,1,p^2\}$ or $\{1,p,p\}$. Up to ordering, the possible sign patterns give
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{roots}&U&V\\ \hline
1,1,-p^2&2(p^2-1)&-2(p^2-1)\\
1,-1,p^2&0&0\\
-1,-1,-p^2&2(p^2+1)&2(p^2+1)\\
-1,p,p&(p-1)^2&(p-1)^2\\
1,p,-p&p^2-1&1-p^2\\
-1,-p,-p&(p+1)^2&(p+1)^2
\end{array}
\]
Rows with equal entries fail the distinctness requirement. In the fifth row, $(p-1)^2<p^2-1<p^2$, so neither entry is a square. Therefore only the first row can work, and it works precisely when $2(p^2-1)$ is a square.
For the primes $2,3,5,7,11,13,17$, these values are respectively $6,16,48,96,240,336,576$. The first two successful primes are $3$ and $17$. Hence the second-smallest possible constant term is $\boxed{17^2=289}$.
