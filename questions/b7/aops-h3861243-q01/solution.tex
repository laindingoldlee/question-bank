The nearest integer to $\sqrt n$ is $k$ exactly when
\[
(k-\dfrac{1}{2})^2<n<(k+\dfrac{1}{2})^2,
\]
where equality cannot occur for integer $n$. This gives the integer interval $k^2-k+1\le n\le k^2+k$, containing $2k$ terms. Each complete interval therefore contributes $2\dfrac{k}{k}=2$.

For $k=1,\ldots,44$ the intervals are complete, ending at $44^2+44=1980$. The remaining values $1981,\ldots,2025$ are $45$ terms with nearest square-root integer $45$, contributing one. Hence the sum is $\boxed{44\cdot2+1=89}$.
