For positive integers $n,k$, put $d=kn+1$. Since $\gcd(d,k)=1$, the condition is equivalent to $d\mid k^3+1$, because $k^2(n^2+k)\equiv1+k^3\pmod d$. Thus the permissible $n$ are precisely $\dfrac{d-1}{k}$ for divisors $d>1$ of $(k+1)(k^2-k+1)$ with $d\equiv1\pmod k$. Enumerating divisors of these two small factors for each $1\le k\le4500$ gives no qualifying sum for $k<4500$. At $k=4500$ the values are $1,4499,20250000$, summing to 20254500. Therefore $\boxed{4500}$. For $k=1$ the condition directly gives $n=1$. This divisor enumeration provides a reproducible finite method for the minimum.
