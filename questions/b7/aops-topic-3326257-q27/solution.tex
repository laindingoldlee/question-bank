Let $\phi=\dfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2}$. A standard regular icosahedron has vertices $(0,\pm1,\pm\phi)$, $(\pm1,\pm\phi,0)$ and $(\pm\phi,0,\pm1)$, with edge 2 and circumradius $\sqrt{\phi+2}$. Its 20 equilateral faces each have area $\sqrt3$. The face through $(0,1,\phi),(1,\phi,0),(\phi,0,1)$ lies on $x+y+z=1+\phi=\phi^2$, at distance $\dfrac{\phi^2}{\sqrt3}$ from the origin. Symmetry gives this same face distance throughout. Decomposing into 20 face pyramids gives volume $\dfrac{20\phi^2}{3}$. Scaling to circumradius 1 divides by $(\phi+2)^{\dfrac{3}{2}}$, resulting in $\dfrac{2}{3}\sqrt{10+2\sqrt5}$ after using $\phi^2=\phi+1$. Thus the required volume is $\boxed{\dfrac{4}{3}\pi-\dfrac{2}{3}\sqrt{10+2\sqrt5}}$, and the requested sum is $\boxed{29}$.
