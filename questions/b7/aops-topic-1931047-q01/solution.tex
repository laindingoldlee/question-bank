If $n=p_1^{e_1}p_2^{e_2}\cdots p_k^{e_k}$, then $n$ has $(e_1+1)(e_2+1)\cdots(e_k+1)$ divisors. We need this product to be $30$, and we write $30$ as a product of factors at least $2$:
\[
30,\qquad 15\cdot2,\qquad 10\cdot3,\qquad 6\cdot5,\qquad 5\cdot3\cdot2 .
\]
For each pattern the smallest $n$ puts the largest exponents on the smallest primes:
\[
2^{29},\qquad 2^{14}\cdot3=49152,\qquad 2^9\cdot3^2=4608,\qquad 2^5\cdot3^4=2592,\qquad 2^4\cdot3^2\cdot5=720 .
\]
The smallest is $\boxed{720}$, which indeed has $(4+1)(2+1)(1+1)=30$ divisors.
