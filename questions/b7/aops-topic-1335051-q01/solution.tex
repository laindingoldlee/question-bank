\textbf{(a)} Suppose every box contained at most $k$ objects. Then the $n$ boxes together would contain at most $kn$ objects, contradicting that there are more than $kn$. So some box has at least $k+1$ objects. (With $k=1$: if $n+1$ objects go into $n$ boxes, some box has two.)

\textbf{(b)} Put each person in the box of their birth month: $13>1\cdot12$ objects in $12$ boxes, so some month has at least two people.

\textbf{(c)} Put each integer in the box of its remainder on division by $4$ (the boxes $0,1,2,3$). Five integers in four boxes: two of them, say $x$ and $y$, have the same remainder, and then $4\mid x-y$. $\boxed{\text{Proved}}$
