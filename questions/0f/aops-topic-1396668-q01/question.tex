A student argues: ``Take a cubic equation $ax^3+bx^2+cx+d=0$ with $a\ne0$ and $d\ne0$. Dividing by $x$ gives $ax^2+bx+\left(c+\dfrac{d}{x}\right)=0$, and the quadratic formula gives
\[
x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4a\left(c+\dfrac{d}{x}\right)}}{2a}.
\]
So a cubic has at most two solutions.'' Find the error, and give a cubic equation with three distinct real solutions.
