\textbf{The error.} The quadratic formula solves $Ax^2+Bx+C=0$ when $A$, $B$, $C$ are \emph{constants}. Here the ``constant term'' $c+\dfrac{d}{x}$ depends on $x$ itself, so the formula does not give the solutions; it only rewrites the equation as
\[
x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4a\left(c+\dfrac{d}{x}\right)}}{2a},
\]
an equation in which $x$ still appears on both sides. Each choice of sign gives an equation that may have several solutions (or none), so nothing limits the number of solutions to two.

\textbf{A cubic with three solutions.} $x^3-6x^2+11x-6=(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)=0$ has the three solutions $x=1,2,3$. (Here $a=1$, $b=-6$, $c=11$, $d=-6$; for example $x=1$ satisfies the student's equation with the sign $-$ and $x=3$ with the sign $+$.)

In general a cubic has exactly three complex roots counted with multiplicity, and it can have three distinct real roots.
