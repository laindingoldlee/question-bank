Each of the $2^n$ win-or-loss patterns of $n$ games has probability $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^n$, and exactly $\dbinom nk$ of them have $k$ wins (choose which games are won). So
\[
\text{(c)}\quad P(k\text{ wins in }n\text{ games})=\boxed{\dfrac{\binom nk}{2^n}} .
\]
(a) $\dfrac{\binom42}{16}=\dfrac{6}{16}=\boxed{\dfrac{3}{8}}$. \quad (b) $\dfrac{\binom43}{16}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{4}}$.

If the probability of a win were $p$ instead of $\dfrac{1}{2}$, the formula would be $\dbinom nkp^k(1-p)^{n-k}$.
