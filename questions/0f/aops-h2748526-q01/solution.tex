Let the least element of $A$ be $a$, and translate by $-a$ to obtain $B$ with least element $0$, greatest element $M$, and least positive element $d$. Translation preserves the size of the sumset.

The sets $B$ and $M+B$ are contained in $B+B$ and overlap only at $M$. Their union already has nine elements, so
\[
B+B=B\cup(M+B).
\]
In particular, every sum at most $M$ belongs to $B$, and there are no sums strictly between $M$ and $M+d$.

Starting with any $b\in B$, repeatedly add $d$ while the result is at most $M$. Every such result remains in $B$. The last result must be exactly $M$: otherwise the next addition produces a sum strictly between $M$ and $M+d$, impossible. Taking $b=0$ shows $d\mid M$; taking any $b$ shows $d\mid b$. Repeated addition from $0$ also puts all $0,d,\ldots,M$ into $B$. Since $B$ has five elements, it is exactly $\{0,d,2d,3d,4d\}$. Thus
\[
\sum_{x\in A}x=5a+10d,
\]
which is divisible by $5$.
