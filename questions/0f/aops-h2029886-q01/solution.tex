Choose coordinates
\[
E=(0,0),\ A=(a,0),\ C=(-c,0),\ B=(0,b),\ D=(0,-d),
\qquad a,b,c,d>0.
\]
The power of $E$ with respect to the circle tangent at $P$ is $EP^2=c^2$. Its intersections with $AC$ are $A,R$, so the signed-coordinate product gives $aR_x=c^2$, hence $R=(\dfrac{c^2}{a},0)$.

Put $K=a^2b+c^2d>0$. Intersecting lines $AD$ and $BR$ gives
\[
Q=\left(\dfrac{ac^2(b+d)}{K},\ \dfrac{bd(c^2-a^2)}{K}\right).
\]
The circle through $B,C,D$ has equation
\[
x^2+y^2+\dfrac{c^2-bd}{c}x+(d-b)y-bd=0.
\]
Substituting the two coordinates of $Q$ and collecting terms yields
\[
-\dfrac{ac(a+c)(b+d)(ab+cd)(bd-c^2)}{K^2}=0.
\]
Every displayed factor apart from $bd-c^2$ is nonzero, so $bd=c^2$. Thus
\[
(B-C)\cdot(D-C)=(c,b)\cdot(c,-d)=c^2-bd=0.
\]
The two sides through $C$ are perpendicular, proving $\boxed{\angle BCD=90^\circ}$.
