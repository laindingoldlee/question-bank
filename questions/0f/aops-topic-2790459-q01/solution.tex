With $p$ the probability of heads and $q=1-p$: $10p^3q^2+5p^4q+p^5=31p^5$. Divide by $p^5$ and put $r=\dfrac qp$: $10r^2+5r+1=31$, i.e. $2r^2+r-6=(2r-3)(r+2)=0$, so $r=\dfrac32$ and $p=\dfrac1{1+r}=\boxed{\dfrac25}$. (Working with the ratio $r$ avoids expanding powers of $1-p$.)
