Count the ordered pairs $(a,b)$ with $a,b\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}$. There are $m^2$ of them. Split them into two kinds.

\emph{Pairs with $a<b$.} Each such pair is the same as a $2$-element subset $\{a,b\}$ of $\{1,\dots,m\}$, so there are $\dbinom m2$ of them.

\emph{Pairs with $a\ge b$.} Send $(a,b)$ to the set $\{b,\,a+1\}$. Since $b\le a<a+1\le m+1$, this is a $2$-element subset of $\{1,\dots,m+1\}$. Conversely, a subset $\{u,v\}$ with $u<v\le m+1$ comes from exactly one such pair, namely $(a,b)=(v-1,u)$, which has $b=u\le v-1=a$ and $a\le m$. So this is a bijection, and there are $\dbinom{m+1}2$ such pairs.

Adding the two counts gives $\dbinom m2+\dbinom{m+1}2=m^2$. $\blacksquare$
