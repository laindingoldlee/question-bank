\textbf{Upper bound.} The interior angles of a decagon add to $(10-2)\cdot180^\circ=1440^\circ$, and every interior angle of a simple polygon is less than $360^\circ$. If $k$ angles are acute (less than $90^\circ$), then
\[
1440^\circ<90^\circ k+360^\circ(10-k)=3600^\circ-270^\circ k,
\]
so $270k<2160$ and $k<8$. Hence $k\le7$.

\textbf{Construction.} The decagon with vertices, in order,
\[
(3,1),\ (13,5),\ (-2,10),\ (-2,4),\ (-12,18),\ (-21,6),\ (-2,-5),\ (0,-1),\ (4,-18),\ (14,-3)
\]
is simple (no two sides cross), and its interior angles, to the nearest tenth of a degree, are
\[
318.2^\circ,\ 40.2^\circ,\ 71.6^\circ,\ 324.5^\circ,\ 72.4^\circ,\ 83.2^\circ,\ 86.5^\circ,\ 320.2^\circ,\ 46.9^\circ,\ 76.3^\circ,
\]
seven of which are acute (red dots in the figure; the three reflex vertices are blue).

for(int i=0;i<10;++i){ if(ac[i]) dot(P[i],red+5); else dot(P[i],blue+5);}
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

So the greatest possible number is $\boxed{7}$.
