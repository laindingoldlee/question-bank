Since $54\%$ of $50$ is $27$ and $24\%$ of $50$ is $12$, the numbers $e$ of 8th graders and $s$ of 7th graders must satisfy
\[e+s=49,\qquad e\ge27,\qquad s\ge12.\]

\textbf{(a)} $e=49-s\le49-12=37$. The club with $37$ eighth graders and $12$ seventh graders satisfies all the conditions, so the greatest number of 8th graders is $\boxed{37}$.

\textbf{(b)} $s=49-e\le49-27=22$. The club with $27$ eighth graders and $22$ seventh graders satisfies all the conditions, so the greatest number of 7th graders is $\boxed{22}$.

The two maxima cannot happen at the same time: $(e,s)=(37,12)$ and $(27,22)$ are the two extreme clubs, and every club in between has $e+s=49$.
