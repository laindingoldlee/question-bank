From $f(1)=f(1\cdot1)=2f(1)$ we get $f(1)=0$, and from $f(q)+f\!\left(\dfrac1q\right)=f(1)=0$ we get $f\!\left(\dfrac1q\right)=-f(q)$. So $f\!\left(\dfrac ab\right)=f(a)-f(b)$ and $f(p^k)=kp$ for primes $p$:
\[f\!\left(\dfrac{17}{32}\right)=17-10=7,\quad f\!\left(\dfrac{11}{16}\right)=11-8=3,\quad f\!\left(\dfrac79\right)=7-6=1,\]
\[f\!\left(\dfrac76\right)=7-(2+3)=2,\quad f\!\left(\dfrac{25}{11}\right)=10-11=-1.\]
Only $\boxed{x=\dfrac{25}{11}}$ gives $f(x)<0$.
