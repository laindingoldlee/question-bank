Every integer gap at least $2$ is a sum of $2$s and $3$s, so transitivity gives $a_i<a_j$ whenever $j-i\ge2$. Only neighbouring positions may therefore be inverted. If $a_1=1$, remove it and solve the shorter problem. Otherwise the entry $1$ must be at position $2$; since $a_1$ is smaller than every later entry, it must equal $2$. Remove this initial pair. Conversely either construction preserves all required inequalities. Thus the counts satisfy $F_n=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}$ with $F_0=F_1=1$, giving $1,1,2,3,5,8,13,21,34$. The answer is $\boxed{34}$.
