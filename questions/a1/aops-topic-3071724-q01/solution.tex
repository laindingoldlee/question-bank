Let $a\in\{1,\ldots,9\}$ and $b,c\in\{0,\ldots,9\}$. Set $u=9-a$, $v=9-b$, $w=9-c$. Then $0\le u\le8$, $0\le v,w\le9$, and the condition becomes $u+v+w\le11$. Without upper bounds, stars and bars gives $\binom{14}{3}=364$ triples. Subtract those with $u\ge9$, counted by $\binom53=10$, and those with $v\ge10$ or $w\ge10$, each counted by $\binom43=4$. No two upper-bound violations can coexist when the sum is at most $11$. Thus the answer is $364-10-4-4=\boxed{346}$.
