Let $d=\gcd(r,s)$ and write $r=dr'$, $s=ds'$ with $\gcd(r',s')=1$ and $s'\neq0$. Dividing the equation by $d^2$ gives
\[
r'^2+pr's'+qs'^2=0,\qquad\text{so}\qquad r'^2=-s'(pr'+qs').
\]
Hence $s'$ divides $r'^2$.

Suppose $|s'|>1$ and let $\ell$ be a prime factor of $s'$. Then $\ell\mid r'^2$, and since $\ell$ is prime, $\ell\mid r'$. So $\ell$ divides both $r'$ and $s'$, contradicting $\gcd(r',s')=1$. Therefore $s'=\pm1$, so $s=\pm d$, and $d$ divides $r$. Thus $s\mid r$. $\blacksquare$

(Dividing the original equation by $s^2$ shows that $x=\dfrac{r}{s}$ is a root of $x^2+px+q$; the result says such a rational root is an integer.)
