If $d\mid x_k$ then $d\mid x_{k+1}$, so for each $d$ the terms divisible by $d$ form a final block. Hence the even terms are exactly $x_{51},\dots,x_{100}$ and the multiples of $10$ are $x_{91},\dots,x_{100}$. The multiples of $5$ form a final block whose intersection with $x_{51},\dots,x_{100}$ has $10$ terms, so they are exactly $x_{91},\dots,x_{100}$.

\emph{Lower bound.} Each step with $x_k\ne x_{k+1}$ raises the total exponent (of $2$, $3$ and $5$) by at least $1$. The term $x_{90}$ is not divisible by $5$ and divides $x_{100}$, so its total exponent is at most $33+33=66$. Hence at most $66$ of the $89$ steps from $x_1$ to $x_{90}$ are strict, and at least $89-66=23$ steps have $x_k=x_{k+1}$.

\emph{Construction.} Take $x_k=3^{k-1}$ for $1\le k\le34$ and $x_k=3^{33}$ for $34\le k\le50$ ($16$ equalities); then $x_k=2^{k-50}3^{33}$ for $50\le k\le83$ and $x_k=2^{33}3^{33}$ for $83\le k\le90$ ($7$ equalities); finally $x_k=2^{33}3^{33}5^{k-67}$ for $91\le k\le100$. All conditions hold, with exactly $23$ equalities.

The minimum is $\boxed{23}$.
