Remove the two $B$s: the four letters $A,A,C,C$ form one of $6$ words, and each place where an $A$ and a $C$ meet in this word (a \emph{switch}) must receive at least one $B$ in the full arrangement. A word of length $4$ has $5$ gaps (including the two ends) in which the $B$s can go, and the two $B$s are identical.
\begin{itemize}
\item $AACC$, $CCAA$ (one switch): put one $B$ in the switch gap; the other $B$ can go in any of the $5$ gaps. That is $5$ ways each, $10$ in all.
\item $ACCA$, $CAAC$ (two switches): one $B$ in each switch gap, $1$ way each, $2$ in all.
\item $ACAC$, $CACA$ (three switches): impossible with two $B$s.
\end{itemize}
The total is $10+2=\boxed{12}$, as the poster found.
