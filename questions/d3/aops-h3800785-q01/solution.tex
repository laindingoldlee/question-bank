Let $\angle A=\angle C=\theta$, using $AB=BC$. Then $\angle ACD=\dfrac{\theta}{2}$. The law of sines in $ACD$ gives
\[\dfrac{AD}{CD}=\dfrac{\sin(\dfrac{\theta}{2})}{\sin\theta}=\dfrac{1}{2\cos(\dfrac{\theta}{2})}.\]
In right triangle $CDE$, the hypotenuse is $CE$ and $\dfrac{CD}{CE}=\cos(\dfrac{\theta}{2})$. Multiplying yields $\boxed{\dfrac{AD}{CE}=\dfrac{1}{2}}$.

texpreamble("\usepackage{amsmath,amssymb}");

real h=1;real s=sqrt(1+h*h);pair A=(0,0),C=(2,0),B=(1,h),D=2/(s+2)*B,E=(2*(2-s)/(s+2),0);draw(A--B--C--cycle);draw(C--D);draw(D--E,blue);label("$A$",A,(-1,-1));label("$B$",B,(0,1));label("$C$",C,(1,-1));label("$D$",D,(-1,0));label("$E$",E,(0,-1));
\end{asy}
