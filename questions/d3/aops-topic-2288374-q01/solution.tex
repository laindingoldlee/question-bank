\textbf{(a)} $10+11+\cdots+15=75$ and $15+16+\cdots+20=105$, with difference $30=6\cdot5$. The student's sums are those of the first five numbers of each column: $10+11+12+13+14=60$ and $15+16+17+18+19=85$, with difference $25=5\cdot5$.

\textbf{(b)} If the numbers are $x_1,\dots,x_n$, then
\[(x_1+d)+(x_2+d)+\cdots+(x_n+d)=(x_1+\cdots+x_n)+nd.\]
So the difference of the column sums is always (number of rows)$\times$(shift): $6\cdot5=30$ here, and $5\cdot5=25$ for five rows. This is a general fact, not a special pattern of these numbers.

\textbf{(c)} The sum is the number of terms times the average of the first and last terms:
\[a+(a+1)+\cdots+(a+n-1)=na+\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}=\boxed{\dfrac{n(2a+n-1)}{2}}.\]
For example, $a=10$, $n=6$ gives $\dfrac{6\cdot25}{2}=75$.
