Call the sum $S$. For $n\ge1$ the ratio of the $(n+1)$st term to the $n$th term is $\dfrac{n+1}{6n}\le\dfrac{2}{6}\le\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the terms shrink at least as fast as a geometric series with ratio $\dfrac{1}{2}$; the series converges and we may manipulate it term by term.

Dividing by $6$ shifts every term one place to the right:
\[S=\dfrac{1}{6}+\dfrac{2}{36}+\dfrac{3}{216}+\cdots,\qquad \dfrac{S}{6}=\phantom{\dfrac{1}{6}+{}}\dfrac{1}{36}+\dfrac{2}{216}+\cdots.\]
Subtracting, each numerator drops by $1$:
\[S-\dfrac{S}{6}=\dfrac{1}{6}+\dfrac{1}{36}+\dfrac{1}{216}+\cdots=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{6}}{1-\dfrac{1}{6}}=\dfrac{1}{5}.\]
So $\dfrac{5}{6}S=\dfrac{1}{5}$ and
\[S=\dfrac{6}{5^2}=\boxed{\dfrac{6}{25}}.\]
