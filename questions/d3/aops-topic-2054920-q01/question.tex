The equation
\[C=\dfrac{5}{9}(F-32)\]
shows how a temperature $F$ in degrees Fahrenheit relates to a temperature $C$ in degrees Celsius. Based on the equation, which of the following must be true?
\begin{enumerate}
\item[I.] A temperature increase of $1$ degree Fahrenheit is equivalent to a temperature increase of $\dfrac{5}{9}$ degree Celsius.
\item[II.] A temperature increase of $1$ degree Celsius is equivalent to a temperature increase of $1.8$ degrees Fahrenheit.
\item[III.] A temperature increase of $\dfrac{5}{9}$ degree Fahrenheit is equivalent to a temperature increase of $1$ degree Celsius.
\end{enumerate}
$\textbf{(A)}$ I only\qquad $\textbf{(B)}$ II only\qquad $\textbf{(C)}$ III only\qquad $\textbf{(D)}$ I and II only
