(a) Substituting $x=\dfrac {p}{q}$ and multiplying by $q^n$,
\[
a_np^n+a_{n-1}p^{n-1}q+\dots+a_1pq^{n-1}+a_0q^n=0.
\]
Every term except $a_0q^n$ is divisible by $p$, so $p\mid a_0q^n$; since $\gcd(p,q)=1$, $p\mid a_0$. Similarly every term except $a_np^n$ is divisible by $q$, so $q\mid a_np^n$ and hence $q\mid a_n$. $\blacksquare$

(b) By (a), any rational root has the form $\pm\dfrac {p}{q}$ with $p\mid6$ and $q\mid2$. Trying $x=3$: $54-27-33+6=0$, so by the factor theorem $x-3$ is a factor. Dividing,
\[
2x^3-3x^2-11x+6=(x-3)(2x^2+3x-2)=(x-3)(2x-1)(x+2).
\]
So
\[\boxed{2x^3-3x^2-11x+6=(x-3)(2x-1)(x+2)}.\]
