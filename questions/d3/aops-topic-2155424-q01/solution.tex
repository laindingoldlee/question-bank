Let $K$ be the point where the circles touch.

\textbf{Step 1: $\angle AKB=90^\circ$.} The common tangent at $K$ meets $AB$ at a point $M$. Tangent segments from $M$ are equal: $MA=MK$ (to the circle with centre $S$) and $MK=MB$ (to the circle with centre $T$). So $K$ lies on the circle with diameter $AB$, and $\angle AKB=90^\circ$.

\textbf{Step 2: $P$, $K$, $B$ are collinear.} Since $AP$ is a diameter and $K$ is on that circle, $\angle AKP=90^\circ$. Both $KP$ and $KB$ are perpendicular to $AK$ at $K$, so $P$, $K$, $B$ lie on one line, the perpendicular to $AK$ at $K$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real a=2, c=1; real d=2*sqrt(a*c);
pair A=(0,0), B=(d,0), Sc=(0,a), Tc=(d,c);
pair K=Sc+(Tc-Sc)*a/(a+c);
pair P=(0,2a), M=(A+B)/2;
real L=abs(P-Tc), ang=degrees(P-Tc), phi=aCos(c/L);
pair Q1=Tc+c*dir(ang-phi), Q2=Tc+c*dir(ang+phi);
pair Q=(Q1.x>Q2.x)?Q1:Q2;
draw(circle(Sc,a)); draw(circle(Tc,c));
draw((-1,0)--(d+1.2,0));
draw(A--P,linewidth(0.9)); draw(P--B,blue); draw(P--Q,red+linewidth(1));
draw(A--K,dashed); draw(M--K,dashed);
dot(A); dot(B); dot(P); dot(Q); dot(K); dot(M); dot(Sc); dot(Tc);
label("$A$",A,S); label("$B$",B,S); label("$P$",P,N); label("$Q$",Q,NE); label("$K$",K,NE);
label("$M$",M,S); label("$S$",Sc,W); label("$T$",Tc,SE);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\textbf{Step 3: $PA^2=PK\cdot PB$.} The radius $SA$ is perpendicular to the tangent $AB$, so $\angle PAB=90^\circ$. In the right triangle $PAB$, $AK\perp PB$, so $AK$ is the altitude to the hypotenuse and $K$ lies between $P$ and $B$. The right triangles $PKA$ and $PAB$ share the angle at $P$, so they are similar and $\dfrac{PK}{PA}=\dfrac{PA}{PB}$, i.e. $PA^2=PK\cdot PB$.

\textbf{Step 4: $PQ^2=PK\cdot PB$.} The line $PB$ meets the circle with centre $T$ at $K$ and $B$, and $PQ$ is a tangent from $P$ to that circle. By the power of the point $P$, $PQ^2=PK\cdot PB$.

Hence $PQ^2=PA^2$, and $AP=PQ$. $\blacksquare$
