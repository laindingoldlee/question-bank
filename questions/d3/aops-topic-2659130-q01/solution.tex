By the binomial theorem,
\[(x+y)^p=x^p+y^p+\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\binom pk x^ky^{p-k}.\]
For $1\le k\le p-1$, $\dbinom pk=\dfrac{p!}{k!\,(p-k)!}$ is an integer, and $p$ divides the numerator $p!$ but not the denominator, whose factors are all smaller than $p$ (and $p$ is prime). Hence $p\mid\dbinom pk$, every term of the sum is divisible by $p$, and $(x+y)^p\equiv x^p+y^p\pmod p$. $\blacksquare$
