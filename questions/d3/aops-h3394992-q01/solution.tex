Factor the numerator as $(n^4-1)(n^4+4)$. Odd $n>1$ gives a factor $8$ after division by $2$, while $n=1$ gives zero, which is not squarefree. For even $n$, the expression factors as $2(n-1)(n+1)(n^2+1)\left(\dfrac{n^2-2n+2}{2}\right)\left(\dfrac{n^2+2n+2}{2}\right)$. Values $28,26,24,22$ fail because $n-1$ or $n+1$ has square factor $9$, $25$, $25$, $49$, respectively. At $n=20$, the factorization is $2\cdot19\cdot21\cdot401\cdot181\cdot221=2\cdot3\cdot7\cdot13\cdot17\cdot19\cdot181\cdot401$. These primes are distinct; trial division up to their square roots verifies $181$ and $401$. Hence $\boxed{20}$ is largest.
