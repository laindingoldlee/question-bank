A dog school reports that $10$ dogs have a blue ribbon, $15$ have a turquoise ribbon and $20$ have a yellow ribbon; $5$ have both a blue and a turquoise ribbon, $10$ have both a blue and a yellow ribbon, and $15$ have both a turquoise and a yellow ribbon; and $3$ have all three. Every dog has at least one ribbon.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Show that these numbers are impossible.
\item[(b)] Suppose the other six numbers are correct but the number of dogs with all three ribbons is unknown. Show that there is exactly one value of this number for which the data are possible, and find the number of dogs at the school in that case.
\end{enumerate}
