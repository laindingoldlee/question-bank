Let $x$ be the product of the primes that divide $c$ but not $a$ (with $x=1$ if there are none). Let $p$ be any prime divisor of $c$.
\begin{itemize}
\item If $p\mid a$, then $p\nmid b$ (as $\gcd(a,b)=1$) and $p\nmid x$, so $p\nmid bx$ and hence $p\nmid a+bx$.
\item If $p\nmid a$, then $p\mid x$, so $a+bx\equiv a\not\equiv0\pmod p$.
\end{itemize}
So no prime divisor of $c$ divides $a+bx$, i.e. $\gcd(a+bx,c)=1$. $\blacksquare$
