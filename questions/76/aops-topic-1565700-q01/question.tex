A student notices that
\[
x^2+y^2=\left(x+y-\sqrt{2xy}\right)\left(x+y+\sqrt{2xy}\right)
\]
and concludes that $x^2+y^2$ can be factored, contrary to the usual statement that it cannot.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For which real $x,y$ is the displayed identity valid? Prove it.
\item Prove that $x^2+y^2$ is not the product of two nonconstant polynomials in $x$ and $y$ with real coefficients, and explain why the identity does not contradict this.
\end{enumerate}
