Suppose the seals are $d$ apart when Gilly leaves Snap.
\begin{itemize}
\item Gilly and Snarl approach each other at $15+10=25$ km/h, so this leg takes time $\dfrac{d}{25}$ and Gilly swims $\dfrac{15d}{25}=\dfrac{3d}{5}$. The seals close at $20$ km/h, so they are now $d-\dfrac{20d}{25}=\dfrac d5$ apart.
\item On the way back Gilly and Snap approach at $20+10=30$ km/h: time $\dfrac{d}{150}$, distance $\dfrac{20d}{150}=\dfrac{2d}{15}$, and the seals end up $\dfrac d5-\dfrac{20d}{150}=\dfrac{d}{15}$ apart.
\end{itemize}
So each round trip Gilly swims $\dfrac{3d}{5}+\dfrac{2d}{15}=\dfrac{11d}{15}$, and the gap shrinks by a factor of $15$. In total
\[\dfrac{11d}{15}\left(1+\dfrac1{15}+\dfrac{1}{15^2}+\cdots\right)=\dfrac{11d}{15}\cdot\dfrac{15}{14}=\dfrac{11d}{14}=\dfrac{11\cdot210}{14}=\boxed{165\text{ m}}.\]

\emph{Check.} In every round trip the outbound and return times are in the ratio $\dfrac{d}{25}:\dfrac{d}{150}=6:1$, so Gilly spends $\dfrac67$ of the time at $15$ km/h and $\dfrac17$ at $20$ km/h, an average of $\dfrac{110}{7}$ km/h. The seals meet after $\dfrac{0.21}{20}=0.0105$ hours, and $\dfrac{110}{7}\cdot0.0105=0.165$ km.
