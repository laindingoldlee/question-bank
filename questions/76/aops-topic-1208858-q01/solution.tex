For fixed side lengths, the area is largest when the quadrilateral is cyclic, and then Brahmagupta's formula gives it: with $s=\dfrac{39+52+25+60}{2}=88$,
\[\sqrt{(88-39)(88-52)(88-25)(88-60)}=\sqrt{49\cdot36\cdot63\cdot28}=\sqrt{49\cdot36\cdot1764}=7\cdot6\cdot42=\boxed{1764}.\]
A pleasant check: $39^2+52^2=65^2=25^2+60^2$, so the cyclic quadrilateral is two right triangles on the common hypotenuse $AC=65$ (a diameter), with area $\dfrac{39\cdot52}{2}+\dfrac{25\cdot60}{2}=1014+750=1764$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),C=(65,0); pair B=(39*39/65,39*52/65); pair D=(25*25/65,-25*60/65);
draw(circle((32.5,0),32.5),gray); draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(0.9)); draw(A--C,dashed);
label("$A$",A,W); label("$B$",B,N); label("$C$",C,E); label("$D$",D,S);
label("$39$",(A+B)/2,NW); label("$52$",(B+C)/2,NE); label("$25$",(A+D)/2,SW); label("$60$",(C+D)/2,SE); label("$65$",(A+C)/2+(8,0),N);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}
