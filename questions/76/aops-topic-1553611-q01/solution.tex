\textbf{Chris and LeRoy.} If Chris is a toad, LeRoy is a frog, and LeRoy's statement ``Chris is a frog'' is indeed false. If Chris is a frog, LeRoy is not a frog, so LeRoy is a toad, and his statement ``Chris is a frog'' is indeed true. Either way exactly one of Chris and LeRoy is a frog and the other is a toad.

\textbf{Brian.} Suppose Brian is a toad. Then Mike is a different species, a frog, so Mike's statement is false: at most one of the four is a toad. But Brian and one of Chris, LeRoy are toads, a contradiction. So Brian is a frog.

\textbf{Mike.} Brian's statement is false, so Mike is the same species as Brian: a frog. Check Mike's statement: the toads are just one of Chris and LeRoy, so ``at least two are toads'' is false, as a frog's statement must be.

So Brian, Mike and one of Chris and LeRoy are frogs: $\boxed{3}$ frogs.
