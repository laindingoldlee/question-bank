\textbf{The conditions.} The digit sum is $45$, so every such number is divisible by $9$. Divisibility by $10$ means the last digit is $0$. Number the positions $1$ to $10$ from the left and let $S_o$ and $S_e$ be the sums of the digits in the odd and even positions. Then $S_o+S_e=45$ and $11\mid S_o-S_e$; since $S_o-S_e$ is odd and at most $45$ in size, $S_o-S_e=\pm11$ or $\pm33$, and $\pm33$ is impossible (five distinct digits sum to at least $10$, so $|S_o-S_e|\le35-10=25$). So $\{S_o,S_e\}=\{28,17\}$. Finally, divisibility by $8$ means the number formed by the last three digits is a multiple of $8$.

\textbf{Making it largest.} Try to start with $9876$, the largest possible start. Then positions $1,3$ hold $9,7$ and positions $2,4$ hold $8,6$, and the digits $1,2,3,4,5$ go in positions $5$ to $9$ ($0$ is in position $10$). If $S_o=17$, positions $5,7,9$ would sum to $1$, impossible. So $S_o=28$ and $S_e=17$: positions $5,7,9$ sum to $12$ and positions $6,8$ sum to $17-14=3$. Hence positions $6,8$ hold $\{1,2\}$ and positions $5,7,9$ hold $\{3,4,5\}$.

The last three digits are $\overline{xy0}$ with $x\in\{1,2\}$, $y\in\{3,4,5\}$, and $8\mid10\cdot\overline{xy}$ exactly when $4\mid\overline{xy}$. Among $13,14,15,23,24,25$ only $24$ works, so $x=2$, $y=4$. Then position $6$ is $1$ and positions $5,7$ hold $3,5$; the larger choice is $5$ then $3$. The number is
\[
\boxed{9876513240}.
\]
Check: $9876513240=3960\cdot2494069$, and $3960=\operatorname{lcm}(8,9,10,11)$. Since no ten-digit number with distinct digits starts with more than $9876$, and $9876513240$ is the largest admissible number starting with $9876$, it is the greatest.
