Since $7=2+2+1+1+1$, exactly two heirs get two cars and three heirs get one.

\emph{Without the restrictions:} choose the two heirs who get two cars in $\dbinom52=10$ ways, then distribute the $7$ cars into groups of sizes $2,2,1,1,1$ for the now-fixed heirs in $\dfrac{7!}{2!\,2!}=1260$ ways: $12600$ distributions.

\emph{Distributions in which one heir gets both the Maserati and the Bentley:} choose that heir ($5$ ways) and the other heir with two cars ($4$ ways), then give the other $5$ cars as $2,1,1,1$ in $\dfrac{5!}{2!}=60$ ways: $1200$.

The remaining $12600-1200=11400$ distributions give the two cars to different heirs, and by symmetry each of the $5\cdot4=20$ ordered pairs (heir of the Maserati, heir of the Bentley) occurs equally often, $\dfrac{11400}{20}=570$ times. The allowed pairs are a daughter and a son: $3\cdot2=6$ pairs. So the answer is $6\cdot570=\boxed{3420}$.
