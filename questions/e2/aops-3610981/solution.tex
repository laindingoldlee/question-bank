For the graph of matching-colour adjacencies, let $V=20$, let $E$ be its edge count, and let $F$ count $2\times2$ blocks all of one colour. Then the component count is $V-E+F$. To justify this, any cycle in a two-row grid encloses consecutive unit faces; its boundary being monochromatic forces every cell along those columns to have that colour, so every enclosed unit face and its internal edges are present. Thus the bounded faces are exactly the all-one-colour $2\times2$ blocks, and the planar graph formula gives the result. There are $28$ possible edges, each present with probability $\dfrac{1}{3}$, and $9$ unit faces, each present with probability $\dfrac{1}{27}$. Linearity of expectation gives $20 - \dfrac{28}{3} + \dfrac{9}{27} = \boxed{11}$.
