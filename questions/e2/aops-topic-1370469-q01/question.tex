A convex polyhedron has faces that are all quadrilaterals or pentagons, with equally many of each, and exactly three faces meet at each vertex. How many faces does it have?

(A student lets $a$ be the number of quadrilaterals, so there are $a$ pentagons, $2a$ faces and $\dfrac{4a+5a}{3}=3a$ vertices, but gets $\dfrac{4a+5a}{2}=4.5a$ edges, ``a fraction'', and thinks something is wrong. Is it?)
