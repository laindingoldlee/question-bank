On a test, each correct answer is worth $6$ points, each wrong answer $0$ points, and each unanswered question $1.5$ points. Every question has $5$ answer choices, exactly one of which is correct. For a question, you may eliminate some wrong choices and then guess at random among the remaining ones. What is the least number of choices you must eliminate so that guessing gives a strictly larger expected score than leaving the question blank?
