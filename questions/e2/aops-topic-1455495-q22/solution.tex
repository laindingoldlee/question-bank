The two walls meet at a right angle, and $1.5^2+2^2=2.5^2$ confirms that the walls and the $2.5$-inch edge of the box form a right triangle with legs $1.5$ and $2$. The coin is tangent to all three sides of this triangle, so it is the triangle's incircle.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair O=(0,0),Pp=(1.5,0),Q=(0,2); pair n=(0.8,0.6);
fill(Pp--Q--Q+1.3*n--Pp+1.3*n--cycle,rgb(0.88,0.88,0.88));
draw(Pp--Q--Q+1.3*n--Pp+1.3*n--cycle);
draw((0,0)--(3.2,0),linewidth(1.6)); draw((0,0)--(0,3.4),linewidth(1.6));
draw(circle((0.5,0.5),0.5),red+linewidth(0.9));
dot((0.5,0.5),red); draw((0.5,0.5)--(0.5,0),red+dashed); label("$r$",(0.27,0.22),red);
label("$1.5$",(0.75,0),S); label("$2$",(0,1),W); label("$2.5$",(Pp+Q)/2+0.45*n);
label("box",(Pp+Q)/2+1.0*n);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

For a right triangle with legs $p,q$ and hypotenuse $h$, the inradius is $r=\dfrac{p+q-h}{2}$: the tangent lengths from the right-angle vertex are both $r$, so the tangent lengths from the other two vertices are $p-r$ and $q-r$, and these add up to $h$. Here
\[
r=\dfrac{1.5+2-2.5}{2}=0.5 .
\]
(The same value comes from area over semiperimeter: $\dfrac{1.5}{3}=0.5$.) The diameter is $2r=\boxed{1}$ inch.
