Since $c^n=a^n+b^n>a^n$, we have $c>a$ and similarly $c>b$; so $c\ge3$ is an odd prime.

\textbf{One of $a$, $b$ is $2$.} If $a$ and $b$ were both odd, then $a^n+b^n$ would be even, so $c^n$ would be even and $c=2$, contradicting $c\ge3$. So, say, $a=2$, and then $b$ is an odd prime with $b<c$.

\textbf{The case $n\ge2$ is impossible.} Both $b$ and $c$ are odd and $c>b$, so $c\ge b+2$. By the binomial theorem, for $n\ge2$,
\[
c^n\ge(b+2)^n=b^n+n\,b^{n-1}\cdot2+\cdots+2^n>b^n+2^n ,
\]
because the expansion contains the positive middle term $2nb^{n-1}$ in addition to $b^n$ and $2^n$. Hence $c^n-b^n>2^n=a^n$, contradicting $a^n+b^n=c^n$.

Therefore $n=1$. $\blacksquare$
