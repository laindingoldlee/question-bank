Because the special block is unique, it provides a unique starting position for reading the circle clockwise. Its colour order can be chosen in $3!=6$ ways. By relabelling colours, fix that order as $RGB$. The remaining six positions contain two of each colour.
To avoid a second three-colour block, the first remaining letter cannot be $R$, and the last cannot be $B$. Also, whenever two adjacent letters differ, the next letter cannot be the third colour, except at the distinguished block. Applying these restrictions with the required letter counts gives the following exhaustive completions:
\[\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{first two remaining letters}&\text{possible six-letter completions}\\\hline
GG&GGBBRR\\GB&GBBRRG\\BG&\text{none}\\BB&BBGGRR,\ BBRRGG
\end{array}\]
For example, after $GG$ the two remaining $R$s cannot follow the preceding $BG$, and enforcing the same rule along the remaining positions forces $BBRR$. The other rows follow by the identical local rule and the two-of-each counts. Each listed circle has exactly one permitted three-colour block, including blocks wrapping around the end. Thus the total is $\boxed{6\cdot4=24}$.
