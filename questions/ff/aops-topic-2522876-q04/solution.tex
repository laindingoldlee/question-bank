If the side is $s$, the diagonal splits the square into two right isosceles triangles, so by the Pythagorean theorem $s^2+s^2=2^2$, giving $s^2=2$. The area is $s^2=\boxed{2}$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0), B=(1.4142,0), C=(1.4142,1.4142), D=(0,1.4142);
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(0.8));
draw(A--C,blue+linewidth(0.9));
label("$2$",(A+C)/2+(-0.12,0.12),NW,blue);
label("$s$",(A+B)/2,S); label("$s$",(B+C)/2,E);
real t=0.12; draw(B+(-t,0)--B+(-t,t)--B+(0,t));
\end{asy}

(In general a square with diagonal $d$ has area $\dfrac{d^2}{2}$.)
