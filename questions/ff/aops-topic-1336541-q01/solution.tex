\textbf{(a)} One of $N$ and $N+1$ is even, so $N(N+1)$ is even and $T(N)$ is a positive integer.

\textbf{(b)} $T(1)=1=1^2$, $T(8)=36=6^2$, $T(49)=\dfrac{49\cdot50}{2}=1225=35^2$.

\textbf{(c)} Suppose $T(N)=k^2$. Put $M=4N(N+1)=8T(N)$. Then $M+1=4N^2+4N+1=(2N+1)^2$, so
\[
T(M)=\dfrac{M(M+1)}{2}=\dfrac{8T(N)\,(2N+1)^2}{2}=4k^2(2N+1)^2=\bigl(2k(2N+1)\bigr)^2 ,
\]
a perfect square. Since $M>N$, starting from $N=1$ and repeating this step gives an infinite increasing sequence
\[
N=1,\ 8,\ 288,\ 332928,\ \dots
\]
for which $\sqrt{T(N)}$ is an integer ($T(288)=41616=204^2$). Hence there are $\boxed{\text{infinitely many}}$ such $N$.
