Number the positions $0,1,\dots,199$ around the second necklace. A placement of the first necklace puts one of its beads on each position.

\textbf{She can always get $100$.} Consider just the $200$ rotations of the first necklace (without turning it over). Fix a bead of the first necklace. As the necklace rotates, this bead lies once on each of the $200$ beads of the second necklace, and $100$ of these have its colour; so it is matched in exactly $100$ of the $200$ rotations. Adding over the $200$ beads, the total number of matches over all rotations is $200\cdot100$, so the average number of matches per rotation is $100$, and some rotation gives at least $100$ matches.

\textbf{She cannot be sure of more.} Let the second necklace be $WWBBWWBB\ldots WWBB$ (blocks of two), so that for each $j$ the positions $2j$ and $2j+1$ hold beads of the same colour. Let the first necklace alternate $WBWB\ldots WB$. However the first necklace is rotated or turned over, it still alternates, so the beads it puts on positions $2j$ and $2j+1$ have different colours. Exactly one of them matches, for each of the $100$ pairs of positions, so every placement gives exactly $100$ matches.

Hence the answer is $\boxed{100}$.
