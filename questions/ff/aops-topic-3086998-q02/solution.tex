The win probability is $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{6}+\dfrac{1}{4}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{1}{3}$. Thus the token change is $+4$ with probability $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $-1$ with probability $\dfrac{2}{3}$. Put $r=\dfrac{3}{4}$. Before ruin, the conditional expected multiplier of $r^{\text{tokens}}$ is $\dfrac{1}{3}r^4+\dfrac{2}{3}r^{-1}=\dfrac{27}{256}+\dfrac{8}{9}<1$. Freeze this quantity at 1 after ruin. Its expected value cannot increase, by repeated conditional expectation, and initially equals $r^3=\dfrac{27}{64}$. Therefore the probability of ruin by any finite time is at most $\dfrac{27}{64}$, because ruined paths contribute 1 and other paths contribute nonnegative amounts. Taking the increasing union over times shows a positive probability of never going broke. Consequently the ruin time, defined as infinity on such paths, has $\boxed{\text{infinite expectation}}$.
