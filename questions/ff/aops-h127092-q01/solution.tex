Any factor divisible by both $2$ and $5$ ends in zero. Thus a factorization in which neither factor has a zero must allocate all powers of $2$ to one factor and all powers of $5$ to the other. The only pair needing examination is therefore $\{2^n,5^n\}$.

For $n=1,\ldots,7$ these pairs are
\[
(2,5),(4,25),(8,125),(16,625),(32,3125),(64,15625),(128,78125),
\]
all zero-free. At $n=8$, the pair is $(256,390625)$, and the second contains zero. Every other factorization already has a factor ending in zero. Hence the minimum is $\boxed8$.
