\textbf{Proof 1 (four triangles in a square).} Place four copies of the triangle inside a square of side $a+b$ as shown: on each side of the big square the two legs $a$ and $b$ meet, and the four hypotenuses form a quadrilateral with all sides $c$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real a=3,bb=4,s=a+bb;
draw((0,0)--(s,0)--(s,s)--(0,s)--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
pair P1=(a,0),P2=(s,a),P3=(bb,s),P4=(0,bb);
fill(P1--P2--P3--P4--cycle,paleblue);draw(P1--P2--P3--P4--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
label("$a$",(a/2,0),S);label("$b$",(a+bb/2,0),S);
label("$a$",(s,a/2),E);label("$b$",(s,a+bb/2),E);
label("$b$",(bb/2,s),N);label("$a$",(bb+a/2,s),N);
label("$a$",(0,bb+a/2),W);label("$b$",(0,bb/2),W);
label("$c$",(P1+P2)/2,NW);label("$c$",(P2+P3)/2,SW);label("$c$",(P3+P4)/2,SE);label("$c$",(P4+P1)/2,NE);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

At each vertex of the inner quadrilateral, the two acute angles of the triangle and the angle of the quadrilateral make a straight angle; the acute angles add up to $90^\circ$, so every angle of the inner quadrilateral is $90^\circ$, and it is a square of area $c^2$. Counting the area of the big square in two ways,
\[
(a+b)^2=4\cdot\dfrac{ab}{2}+c^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a^2+2ab+b^2=2ab+c^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a^2+b^2=c^2 .
\]

\textbf{Proof 2 (similar triangles).} Let the right angle be at $C$, with $BC=a$, $AC=b$, $AB=c$, and let $D$ be the foot of the altitude from $C$; write $AD=x$ and $DB=y$, so $x+y=c$ ($D$ lies between $A$ and $B$ because the angles at $A$ and $B$ are acute).

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair C=(0,0),B=(4,0),A=(0,3);
pair D=B+dot(C-B,A-B)/dot(A-B,A-B)*(A-B);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));draw(C--D,dashed);
draw((0.3,0)--(0.3,0.3)--(0,0.3));
pair u=unit(B-D),v=unit(C-D);draw(D+0.3u--D+0.3u+0.3v--D+0.3v);
label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,E);label("$C$",C,SW);label("$D$",D,NE);
label("$a$",(B+C)/2,S);label("$b$",(A+C)/2,W);
label("$x$",(A+D)/2,NE);label("$y$",(B+D)/2,NE);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Triangles $ADC$ and $ACB$ are both right-angled (at $D$ and at $C$) and share the angle at $A$, so they are similar, and $\dfrac{AD}{AC}=\dfrac{AC}{AB}$, i.e.\ $b^2=cx$. In the same way triangles $BDC$ and $BCA$ are similar, giving $a^2=cy$. Adding,
\[
a^2+b^2=c(x+y)=c^2 .
\]

\textbf{Proof 3 (Garfield's trapezoid).} Put two copies of the triangle, $UPM$ with legs $UM=a$, $UP=b$ and $TMQ$ with legs $MT=b$, $TQ=a$, side by side along a line $UT$ as shown, so that $PU$ and $QT$ are both perpendicular to $UT$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real a=2.4,bb=3.6;
pair U=(0,0),P=(0,bb),M=(a,0),T=(a+bb,0),Q=(a+bb,a);
draw(U--P--Q--T--cycle,linewidth(0.9));draw(P--M--Q,linewidth(0.9));
draw((0.3,0)--(0.3,0.3)--(0,0.3));draw(T+(-0.3,0)--T+(-0.3,0.3)--T+(0,0.3));
pair u=unit(P-M),v=unit(Q-M);draw(M+0.3u--M+0.3u+0.3v--M+0.3v);
label("$U$",U,SW);label("$P$",P,NW);label("$M$",M,S);label("$T$",T,SE);label("$Q$",Q,NE);
label("$a$",(U+M)/2,S);label("$b$",(M+T)/2,S);label("$b$",(U+P)/2,W);label("$a$",(T+Q)/2,E);
label("$c$",(P+M)/2,NE);label("$c$",(M+Q)/2,NW);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The angles $\angle PMU$ and $\angle QMT$ are the two acute angles of the triangle, so they add up to $90^\circ$ and $\angle PMQ=90^\circ$. The quadrilateral $UPQT$ is a trapezoid with parallel sides $UP=b$, $TQ=a$ and height $UT=a+b$. Its area, computed directly and as the sum of three right triangles, gives
\[
\dfrac{(a+b)(a+b)}{2}=\dfrac{ab}{2}+\dfrac{ab}{2}+\dfrac{c^2}{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a^2+2ab+b^2=2ab+c^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a^2+b^2=c^2 . \qquad\blacksquare
\]
