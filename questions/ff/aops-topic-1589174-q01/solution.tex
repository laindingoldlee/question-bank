Let the sides be $a\le b\le c\le100$. The triangle exists when $a+b>c$, and the angle opposite $c$ is obtuse exactly when $a^2+b^2<c^2$ (law of cosines).

\textbf{The longest side.} If $c\le99$, then from $a^2+b^2<c^2$ and $(a+b)^2\le2\left(a^2+b^2\right)$ we get $a+b<\sqrt2\,c$, so the perimeter is less than $\left(1+\sqrt2\right)c\le2.415\cdot99<240$. We will find a triangle with $c=100$ and perimeter $241$, so we may take $c=100$.

\textbf{The other two sides, with $c=100$.} We need $a+b\ge101$ and $a^2+b^2\le9999$. For a fixed sum $s=a+b$, the sum of squares is smallest when $a$ and $b$ are as close as possible. For $s=142$ that smallest value is $71^2+71^2=10082>9999$, and for larger $s$ it is larger still, so $s\le141$. For $s=141$ the sides $a=70$, $b=71$ give $70^2+71^2=4900+5041=9941<10000$, and $70+71>100$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real a=70,bb=71,c=100;
real x=(c^2+bb^2-a^2)/(2c);
pair A=(0,0),B=(c,0),C=(x,sqrt(bb^2-x^2));
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
label("$A$",A,SW);label("$B$",B,SE);label("$C$",C,N);
label("$100$",(A+B)/2,S);label("$71$",(A+C)/2,NW);label("$70$",(B+C)/2,NE);
draw(arc(C,7,degrees(A-C),degrees(B-C)),red);
label("$\approx 90.3^\circ$",C+(0,-9),S,red);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

So the greatest perimeter is $70+71+100=\boxed{241}$ inches. (The triangle is only slightly obtuse: its largest angle is about $90.3^\circ$.)
