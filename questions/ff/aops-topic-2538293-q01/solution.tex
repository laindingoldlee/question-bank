\textbf{(a)} Write $(x+y+z)^n$ as a product of $n$ factors $(x+y+z)$. Expanding, we choose one of $x,y,z$ from each factor and multiply; every choice contributes one product. The product equals $x^ay^bz^c$ exactly when $x$ is chosen from $a$ factors, $y$ from $b$ factors and $z$ from $c$ factors. The number of such choices is the number of words of length $n$ made of $a$ letters $x$, $b$ letters $y$ and $c$ letters $z$:
\[\binom na\binom{n-a}{b}=\dfrac{n!}{a!\,(n-a)!}\cdot\dfrac{(n-a)!}{b!\,c!}=\dfrac{n!}{a!\,b!\,c!}.\]
Collecting equal products gives the formula. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{(b)} The monomials $x^ay^bz^c$ for different triples are different, and every coefficient is positive, so the number of terms equals the number of triples $(a,b,c)$ of nonnegative integers with $a+b+c=n$. By stars and bars (arrange $n$ stars and $2$ bars) this is
\[\boxed{\binom{n+2}{2}=\dfrac{(n+1)(n+2)}2}.\]
\textbf{(c)} With $n=6$ and $(a,b,c)=(2,3,1)$ the coefficient is $\dfrac{6!}{2!\,3!\,1!}=\dfrac{720}{12}=\boxed{60}$.
