With heads probability $p$, the probability of ending on an odd flip is $p\sum_{j\ge0}(1-p)^{2j}=\dfrac{1}{2-p}$. Increasing this chance increases the current player's eventual win probability, because failure merely passes the turn. Thus each chooses $p=\dfrac{3}{4}$, giving per-turn win probability $\dfrac{4}{5}$. The second player wins after one failed first turn, then possibly any number of pairs of failed turns. Her probability is $\dfrac{1}{5}\cdot\dfrac{4}{5}\sum_{j\ge0}(\dfrac{1}{25})^j=\dfrac{1}{6}$. Hence $\boxed{ab=6}$.
