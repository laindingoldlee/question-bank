Let circle $B$ have radius $3r$ and circle $A$ radius $r$. The centre of $A$ travels around a circle of radius $3r+r=4r$, a distance of $2\pi\cdot4r$. For rolling without slipping, the rotation of $A$ equals the distance travelled by its centre divided by its radius, so $A$ turns through $\dfrac{8\pi r}{r}=8\pi$ radians, i.e. $\boxed{4}$ full rotations.

label("$3r$",(1.5,0),N,fontsize(8pt));label("$r$",(4.5,0),N,fontsize(8pt));
draw(arc((0,0),4,20,70),dashed,Arrow(4));label("$B$",(0,-3),S);label("$A$",(4,-1),S);
\end{asy}

(Measuring only the arc length of $B$ in contact gives $3$; the extra rotation comes from going once around $B$.) None of the five choices is correct. This is the well-known flawed 1982 SAT question; the intended answer was (B), and it was rescored after students pointed out the error.
