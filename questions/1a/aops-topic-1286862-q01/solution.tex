Place $A=(0,0)$, $B=(1,0)$, $C=(1,2)$, $D=(0,2)$. Since $\angle PAB=\angle PBA$, $P$ is on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$, so $P=\left(\dfrac{1}{2},\dfrac{1}{2}\tan15^\circ\right)=\left(\dfrac{1}{2},\dfrac{2-\sqrt3}{2}\right)$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3.2cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(1,0),C=(1,2),D=(0,2); pair P=(0.5,0.5*tan(pi/12));
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle); draw(A--P--B,blue); draw(D--P--C,red);
label("$A$",A,SW); label("$B$",B,SE); label("$C$",C,NE); label("$D$",D,NW); label("$P$",P+(0,0.5)); dot(P);
draw(arc(A,0.3,0,15),gray); draw(arc(B,0.3,165,180),gray);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

By symmetry $PC=PD$, and the line $x=\dfrac{1}{2}$ bisects $\angle CPD$. The half-angle $\alpha$ satisfies
\[
\tan\alpha=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2}}{2-\dfrac{2-\sqrt3}{2}}=\dfrac{1}{2+\sqrt3}=2-\sqrt3=\tan15^\circ,
\]
so $\alpha=15^\circ$ and $\angle CPD=\boxed{30^\circ}$.
