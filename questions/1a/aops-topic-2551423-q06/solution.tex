The angles of a quadrilateral add up to $360^\circ$, so the other three add up to $360^\circ-138^\circ=222^\circ$. With common difference $d$,
\[70+(70+d)+(70+2d)=222\quad\Longrightarrow\quad3d=12\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d=4.\]
The angles are $70^\circ$, $74^\circ$, $78^\circ$, and the last one is $\boxed{\textbf{(A) }78}$. Such a quadrilateral exists (all four angles are less than $180^\circ$):

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=A+5*dir(0),C=B+2.5*dir(42);
pair D=extension(C,C+dir(148),A,A+dir(70));
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(0.8));
label("$70^\circ$",A+0.7*dir(35),fontsize(8pt));
label("$138^\circ$",B+0.75*dir(111),fontsize(8pt));
label("$74^\circ$",C+0.85*dir(185),fontsize(8pt));
label("$78^\circ$",D+0.8*dir(289),fontsize(8pt));
draw(arc(A,0.35,0,70));draw(arc(B,0.35,42,180));draw(arc(C,0.35,148,222));draw(arc(D,0.35,250,328));
\end{asy}
