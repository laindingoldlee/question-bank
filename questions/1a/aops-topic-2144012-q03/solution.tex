There are $64\cdot63=4032$ ways to place the two (distinguishable) pieces on different squares. We subtract the placements in which the queen attacks the knight, that is, in which the knight is on one of the squares the queen can reach (nothing can block the queen, since the board is otherwise empty).

Along its row and column the queen always reaches $7+7=14$ squares. Along its diagonals it reaches $7$, $9$, $11$ or $13$ squares, depending on whether it stands on the outer ring of the board ($28$ squares), the next ring ($20$ squares), the next ($12$ squares) or the central $2\times2$ block ($4$ squares). So the total number of (queen square, attacked square) pairs is
\[64\cdot14+\left(28\cdot7+20\cdot9+12\cdot11+4\cdot13\right)=896+(196+180+132+52)=896+560=1456 .\]
The answer is
\[4032-1456=\boxed{2576}.\]
