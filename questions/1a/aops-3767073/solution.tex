An on-light never turns off. A maximal run of exactly two off-lights survives forever, since each has one on-neighbour and one off-neighbour. An isolated off-light turns on immediately. In any longer run, all interior lights turn on at the first step and the endpoints turn on at the second. The all-off state turns entirely on in one step. Thus success occurs exactly when there is no maximal off-run of length two. For $k\ge1$ on-lights, encode the $k$ cyclic gaps of off-lights; each gap may have any nonnegative length other than 2, with total $10-k$. The number of labelled configurations is $\dfrac{10}{k}[x^{10-k}](1+x+x^3+x^4+\cdots)^k$. For $k=1,\ldots,10$, these counts are $10,35,70,95,112,85,60,35,10,1$, totaling 513. Add the all-off configuration to get 514 successful states out of 1024. The probability is $\boxed{\dfrac{257}{512}}$.
