An antiderivative is $-\cos t$, so the integral is $1-\cos x$. The identity $\sin (x - \dfrac{\pi}{2}) = - \cos x$ shows that $\boxed{\phi = - \dfrac{\pi}{2}}$ works; all equivalent phases are $- \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2 k \pi$. A shift of $11$ radians is only an approximation to one such phase, not an exact identity.
