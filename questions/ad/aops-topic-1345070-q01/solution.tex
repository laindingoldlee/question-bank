There are $\dfrac{6!}{3!\,2!\,1!}=60$ equally likely colour patterns.

\textbf{Red beads.} No two reds may be adjacent. Choosing $3$ pairwise non-adjacent positions out of $6$ can be done in $\dbinom43=4$ ways: $\{1,3,5\}$, $\{2,4,6\}$, $\{1,3,6\}$, $\{1,4,6\}$.

\textbf{White and blue beads.} The other three positions get W, W, B, and the two whites must not be adjacent.
\begin{itemize}
\item $\{1,3,5\}$ red: the free positions $2,4,6$ are pairwise non-adjacent, so all $3$ placements of B work. Same for $\{2,4,6\}$: $3$.
\item $\{1,3,6\}$ red: free positions $2,4,5$; positions $4,5$ are adjacent, so B must be at $4$ or $5$: $2$.
\item $\{1,4,6\}$ red: free positions $2,3,5$; B must be at $2$ or $3$: $2$.
\end{itemize}
That is $3+3+2+2=10$ good patterns, so the probability is $\dfrac{10}{60}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{6}}$.
