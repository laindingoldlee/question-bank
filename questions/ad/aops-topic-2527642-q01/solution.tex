\textbf{(a)} Both claims are wrong: $5-4-3^2+1=5-4-9+1=-7$ and $5+4+3^2+1=19$. Correct expressions are
\[12=5+4+3\times(2-1),\qquad 17=5+4\times3\times(2-1).\]

\textbf{(b)} One possible list:
\[\begin{array}{ll}
21=5+4\times(3+2-1) & 31=5\times4\times3\div2+1\\
22=5\times4+3-2+1 & 32=5\times(4+3)-2-1\\
23=5\times4+3\times(2-1) & 33=5\times(4+3)-2\times1\\
24=5+4\times(3+2)-1 & 34=5\times(4+3)-2+1\\
25=5\times4+3+2\times1 & 35=5\times(4+3)\times(2-1)\\
26=5\times4+3+2+1 & 36=(5+4)\times(3+2-1)\\
27=5\times4+3\times2+1 & 37=5\times(4+3)+2\times1\\
28=5+4\times3\times2-1 & 38=5\times(4+3)+2+1\\
29=5+4\times3\times2\times1 & 39=5\times4^{3\div2}-1\\
30=5+4\times3\times2+1 & 40=5\times(4+3+2-1)
\end{array}\]
For $39$: $4^{3\div2}=4^{\dfrac32}=8$, so $5\times8-1=39$. (A computer search shows that $39$ is the only integer from $1$ to $40$ that cannot be made with $+$, $-$, $\times$, $\div$ and parentheses alone, and that integer exponents do not help either; the fractional exponent is needed.)
