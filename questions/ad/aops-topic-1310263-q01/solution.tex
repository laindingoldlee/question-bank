Dividing by $abcd$:
\[
\dfrac{1}{a}+\dfrac{1}{b}+\dfrac{1}{c}+\dfrac{1}{d}+\dfrac{1}{abcd}=1 .
\]
The primes are distinct: reducing the original equation modulo $a$ gives $-bcd\equiv1\pmod a$, so $a\nmid bcd$, and similarly for the others. By symmetry assume $a<b<c<d$.
\begin{itemize}
\item All five terms are at most $\dfrac{1}{a}$, so $1<\dfrac{5}{a}$, $a<5$. If $a=3$, then $b\ge5$ and $\dfrac{1}{b}+\dfrac{1}{c}+\dfrac{1}{d}+\dfrac{1}{abcd}\le\dfrac{1}{5}+\dfrac{1}{7}+\dfrac{1}{11}+\dfrac{1}{1155}<\dfrac{2}{3}$, too small. So $a=2$, and $\dfrac{1}{b}+\dfrac{1}{c}+\dfrac{1}{d}+\dfrac{1}{2bcd}=\dfrac{1}{2}$.
\item Then $\dfrac{1}{2}<\dfrac{4}{b}$, so $b<8$. For $b=5$ or $7$ the left side is at most $\dfrac{1}{5}+\dfrac{1}{7}+\dfrac{1}{11}+\dfrac{1}{770}<\dfrac{1}{2}$. So $b=3$ and $\dfrac{1}{c}+\dfrac{1}{d}+\dfrac{1}{6cd}=\dfrac{1}{6}$, i.e. $cd-6c-6d=1$, i.e. $(c-6)(d-6)=37$.
\item As $37$ is prime and $c<d$, $c-6=1$ and $d-6=37$: $c=7$, $d=43$, both prime.
\end{itemize}
So $\{a,b,c,d\}=\boxed{\{2,3,7,43\}}$, in any order ($24$ ordered solutions). Check: $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{7}+\dfrac{1}{43}+\dfrac{1}{1806}=1$.
