Let the sequence be $a,a+2,\dots,a+2(k-1)$ with $a$ odd, $a\ge1$ and $k\ge2$ terms. Its sum is
\[
ka+k(k-1)=k(a+k-1)=240 .
\]
Put $m=a+k-1$ (the average of the terms). Then $km=240$ and $a=m-k+1$, which is odd exactly when $m-k$ is even, i.e.\ $m$ and $k$ have the same parity, and positive exactly when $m\ge k$. Both odd is impossible, since $240$ is even. So $k=2k'$ and $m=2m'$ with $k'm'=60$ and $k'\le m'$:
\[
(k',m')=(1,60),(2,30),(3,20),(4,15),(5,12),(6,10).
\]
These give $6$ sequences, with $k=2,4,6,8,10,12$ terms; for example $119+121=240$ ($k=2$) and $15+17+\cdots+33=10\cdot24=240$ ($k=10$, $m=24$, $a=m-k+1=15$). Six is not among the first four choices, so the answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(E)}}$.
