There is no need to multiply the series out. Expanding the product of $k$ factors, each term is $x^{a_1}x^{a_2}\cdots x^{a_k}$ where $a_i\ge0$ is the exponent chosen from the $i$th factor, and every such choice contributes $1$. So the coefficient of $x^n$ equals the number of ordered $k$-tuples $(a_1,\dots,a_k)$ of nonnegative integers with
\[
a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_k=n.
\]

\textbf{(a)} With $k=2$: the pairs $(a_1,a_2)$ with $a_1+a_2=6$ are $(0,6),(1,5),\dots,(6,0)$, so the coefficient is $\boxed{7}$.

\textbf{(b)} By stars and bars, the solutions of $a_1+\cdots+a_k=n$ correspond bijectively to rows of $n$ stars and $k-1$ bars (the $a_i$ are the numbers of stars between consecutive bars). Choosing the positions of the bars among the $n+k-1$ symbols gives $\dbinom{n+k-1}{k-1}$ solutions, which is the claimed coefficient. (For $k=2$ this gives $n+1$, matching part (a).)

For $k=4$ and $n=6$ the coefficient is
\[
\dbinom{9}{3}=\boxed{84}.
\]
