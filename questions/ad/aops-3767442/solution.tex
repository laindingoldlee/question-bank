For even $a$, every height-five tower is divisible by $2^{16}$: its exponent has value at least 16, as seen from the smallest tower with $a=2,n=0$, whose successive values begin $1,2,4,16,65536$. Thus height six is constant modulo 65537. For odd $a$, compare towers with arbitrary starting exponents. At height one their difference is divisible by $a-1$. Once two exponents are odd, their difference is even, and the elementary lifting formula $v_2(a^u-a^v)=v_2(u-v)+v_2(a-1)+v_2(a+1)-1$ applies. (It follows by repeated difference-of-squares factorisation.) If $a\equiv1\pmod4$, each level adds at least 2 to the initial valuation of at least 2. If $a\equiv3\pmod4$, the initial valuation is at least 1 and each later level adds at least 2. Thus all height-nine outputs agree modulo $2^{16}$, making height ten constant modulo the prime 65537. For sharpness take $a=3$ and compare $n=0,1$. At height $j$ the difference has exact valuation $2j-1$. Also repeated squaring gives $3^{32768}\equiv-1\pmod{65537}$, so 3 has order $65536$. Height nine therefore still distinguishes the two inputs, since height-eight exponents differ with valuation 15. Consequently $\boxed{\max g(a)=10}$.
