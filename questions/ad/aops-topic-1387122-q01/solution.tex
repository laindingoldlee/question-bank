By the definition (the numerator has $3$ factors, starting at $-10$ and going down by $1$),
\[
\dbinom{-10}{3}=\dfrac{(-10)(-11)(-12)}{3!}=\dfrac{-1320}{6}=\boxed{-220}.
\]
In general, taking a factor $-1$ out of each of the $k$ factors,
\[
\dbinom{-m}{k}=\dfrac{(-m)(-m-1)\cdots(-m-k+1)}{k!}=(-1)^k\dfrac{m(m+1)\cdots(m+k-1)}{k!}=(-1)^k\dbinom{m+k-1}{k},
\]
so $\dbinom{-10}{3}=(-1)^3\dbinom{12}{3}=-220$. (A calculator or a formula $\dfrac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}$ fails because $(-10)!$ is undefined; the product formula above is the definition that extends to negative and non-integer $r$, as used in the binomial series $(1+x)^{r}=\sum_k\dbinom rk x^k$.)
