Let $s(y)$ be the digit sum of $y$. Someone born in year $y$ is $Y-y$ years old on their birthday in year $Y$, so the claim is possible in year $Y$ exactly when $y+s(y)=Y$ for some year $y\le Y$. Recall that $y\equiv s(y)\pmod9$, so $y+s(y)\equiv2y\pmod9$.

\textbf{The year $1996$.} We need $2y\equiv1996\equiv7\pmod9$, i.e.\ $y\equiv8\pmod9$. Also $y\ge1000$ and $s(y)\le1+9+9+9=28$ for $y\le1999$, so $1968\le y\le1996$. The candidates are $1970$, $1979$, $1988$, and they give
\[
1970+17=1987,\qquad 1979+26=2005,\qquad 1988+26=2014,
\]
none equal to $1996$. So nobody could make the claim in $1996$.

\textbf{Earlier years.} Each of the years $1986$ to $1995$ is of the form $y+s(y)$:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
1965+21=1986, & 1970+17=1987,\\
1966+22=1988, & 1971+18=1989,\\
1967+23=1990, & 1972+19=1991,\\
1968+24=1992, & 1973+20=1993,\\
1969+25=1994, & 1974+21=1995.
\end{array}
\]
For $Y=1985$ we need $2y\equiv1985\equiv5\pmod9$, i.e.\ $y\equiv7\pmod9$, with $1957\le y\le1985$. The candidates $1960,1969,1978$ give $1976,1994,2003$, none equal to $1985$.

So the last such year before $1996$ is $\boxed{1985}$.
