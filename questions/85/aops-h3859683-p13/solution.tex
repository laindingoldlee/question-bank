For a target $j$, let $p_k$ be the chance of hitting $j$ from $k<j$. A direct jump from $j-1$ gives $p_{j-1}=\dfrac{1}{101-j}$. Inductively, if all values from $k+1$ through $j-1$ equal $c=\dfrac{1}{101-j}$, then $p_k=\dfrac{[1+(j-k-1)c]}{100-k}=c$. Starting at zero therefore has probability $\dfrac{1}{101-j}$. At $j=75$ this is $\dfrac{1}{26}$, so $\boxed{m+n=27}$.
