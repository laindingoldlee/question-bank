Count by the number of rooms holding two friends.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{No double room:} each friend gets a different room: $5!=120$ ways.
\item \emph{One double room:} choose the double room ($5$), the empty room ($4$), the two friends sharing ($\dbinom52=10$), and place the other three friends in the three remaining rooms ($3!=6$): $5\cdot4\cdot10\cdot6=1200$ ways.
\item \emph{Two double rooms:} choose the two double rooms ($\dbinom52=10$) and the single room ($3$), the friend in the single room ($5$), and split the other four friends between the two double rooms ($\dbinom42=6$): $10\cdot3\cdot5\cdot6=900$ ways.
\end{itemize}
Three double rooms would need $6$ friends. The total is $120+1200+900=\boxed{2220}$.
