\emph{When does the game end?} The rounds $1$ to $7$ are worth $254$ points in total. A player reaches $250$ by round $7$ exactly when he has won rounds $3$--$7$ and at least one of rounds $1$, $2$ (totals $250$, $252$, $254$; missing round $3$ gives only $246$). Otherwise round $8$ is played and its winner gets $256$ points and ends the game.

\emph{Ending in round 7} (probability $2\cdot\dfrac{1}{32}\cdot\dfrac{3}{4}$). The winner has $W\in\{250,252,254\}$ and the loser $254-W$, so the difference is $2W-254\in\{246,250,254\}$; only $246$ is a multiple of $3$, which needs the winner to have lost round $2$ only: probability $2\cdot\dfrac{1}{32}\cdot\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{2}{128}$.

\emph{Ending in round 8.} If the round-$8$ winner had $x$ points after $7$ rounds, the totals are $x+256$ and $254-x$, with difference $2x+2$, a multiple of $3$ iff $x\equiv2\pmod3$. By symmetry we may assume Gary wins round $8$ (probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$, and the other case is the same). Gary's $7$-round total is the sum of $2^j$ over the set $S$ of rounds he won; $2^j\equiv2$ for odd $j$ and $\equiv1$ for even $j$ (mod $3$). With $\omega$ a primitive cube root of unity, the number of the $128$ sets with sum $\equiv2\pmod3$ is
\[\dfrac{1}{3}\left(128+\omega^{-2}(1+\omega^2)^4(1+\omega)^3+\omega^{2}(1+\omega)^4(1+\omega^2)^3\right)=\dfrac{1}{3}\left(128-\omega-\omega^2\right)=43.\]
Two of these sets, $\{1,\dots,7\}$ and $\{1\}$, end the game in round $7$, leaving $41$ sets that reach round $8$ with $x\equiv2$. So this case contributes $\dfrac{41}{128}$.

Total: $\dfrac{2}{128}+\dfrac{41}{128}=\boxed{\dfrac{43}{128}}$.
