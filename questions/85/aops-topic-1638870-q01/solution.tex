The first line has slope $3$ and $y$-intercept $-1$; it passes through $(0,-1)$ and $(1,2)$. The second can be written $y=\dfrac{x+1}{3}$: slope $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $y$-intercept $\dfrac{1}{3}$, through $(-1,0)$ and $(2,1)$. The two lines are reflections of each other in the line $y=x$, so they meet on it.

The graphs cross at $\left(\dfrac{1}{2},\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$. Algebraic check: on $y=x$ the first equation gives $x=3x-1$, so $x=\dfrac{1}{2}$; and indeed $3\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}-1=\dfrac{1}{2}$ in both equations. The solution is $\boxed{(x,y)=\left(\dfrac{1}{2},\dfrac{1}{2}\right)}$.
