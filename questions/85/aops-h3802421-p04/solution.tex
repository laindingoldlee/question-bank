Divisibility by $12$ requires a digit sum divisible by $3$ and a last-two-digit number divisible by $4$. Thus the digit sum is $30,33,$ or $36$. One finite way to enumerate is to write the digits as $9-u,9-v,9-w,9-z$. Their deficits are nonnegative and sum to $6,3,$ or $0$, respectively; the leading digit must remain nonzero. Checking these small deficit lists for a last-two-digit multiple of four, and then removing multiples of seven, gives
\[\boxed{\begin{gathered}5988,6996,7788,7968,8688,8796,8868,8976,\\9588,9696,9768,9876,9888,9948,9984.\end{gathered}}\]
For reproducibility the check is: for each $k\in\{0,3,6\}$ and $u,v,w\ge0$ with $u+v+w\le k$, set $z=k-u-v-w$; form the four digits, and retain the number exactly when its final two digits are divisible by four and its remainder modulo seven is nonzero. This covers each eligible number once.
