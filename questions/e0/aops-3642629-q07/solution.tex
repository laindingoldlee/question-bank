Assume at least one new road is legally addable. If the original directed graph is not strongly connected, contract each strongly connected component. The resulting graph is acyclic, so give its vertices a topological order. Choose any pair of cities with no road in either direction. If they lie in one component, adding a road between them cannot connect the different components strongly. If they lie in different components, add the road forward in the topological order; the component graph remains acyclic and cannot become strongly connected. This supplies a legal addition that fails the claimed property, a contradiction. Therefore the original graph was strongly connected.
