Every set of three seats is equally likely to be the boys' set, so we may simply list the sets $\{x,y,z\}$ of three different numbers from $1$ to $9$ with sum $12$:
\[
\{1,2,9\},\ \{1,3,8\},\ \{1,4,7\},\ \{1,5,6\},\ \{2,3,7\},\ \{2,4,6\},\ \{3,4,5\}.
\]
(For smallest number $1$ the other two add up to $11$; for $2$ they add up to $10$ with both at least $3$; for $3$ they add up to $9$ with both at least $4$; a smallest number $4$ would need $4+5+6=15>12$.)

These $7$ sets are equally likely. No two boys are adjacent exactly when no two of the numbers are consecutive, which leaves $\{1,3,8\}$, $\{1,4,7\}$ and $\{2,4,6\}$. So the probability is
\[
\boxed{\dfrac{3}{7}}.
\]
