Let $17p+625=n^2$ with $n$ a positive integer. Then $n>25$ and
\[17p=n^2-625=(n-25)(n+25).\]
The two factors are positive and their product is $17p$, whose positive divisors are $1,17,p,17p$ (if $p\ne17$; for $p=17$ they are $1,17,289$). Since $n-25<n+25$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $n-25=1$, $n+25=17p$: $n=26$, $17p=51$, $p=3$.
\item $n-25=17$, $n+25=p$: $n=42$, $p=67$, which is prime.
\item $n-25=p$, $n+25=17$: impossible, since then $n=-8$.
\item $p=17$: $(n-25)(n+25)=289$ would need $n-25=1$, $n+25=289$, impossible.
\end{itemize}
Check: $17\cdot3+625=676=26^2$ and $17\cdot67+625=1764=42^2$. So $\boxed{p=3\text{ or }p=67}$.
