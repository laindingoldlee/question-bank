The coin lies within a tile exactly when its centre is at distance at least $\dfrac{1}{2}$ from every side of that tile. In a $1\times1$ tile that leaves only the single centre point. So the favourable centres are $64$ points, a set of total area $0$, while the possible centres fill a region of area $64$. For a uniformly random point, probability is area ratio, so the probability is $\dfrac{0}{64}=\boxed{0}$.

Probability $0$ does not mean impossible: in a continuous model every single point has probability $0$, yet the coin does land somewhere. (Likewise, adding up infinitely many ``infinitely small'' quantities need not give infinity; areas and probabilities add over countably many pieces, but a region is not a sum of its points.)
