\textbf{(a)} The flips are independent: the coin has no memory of earlier results. So the $14$th flip is tails with probability $\boxed{\dfrac12}$, whatever happened before. Jack's mistake is to treat ``about half heads'' as a law that the coin must obey in every finite run (the gambler's fallacy). In fact even before any flips, an exact $7$--$7$ split is fairly unlikely, as (b) shows.

\textbf{(b)} All $2^{14}=16384$ sequences of $14$ flips are equally likely, and $\binom{14}{7}=3432$ of them have exactly $7$ heads. The probability is
\[\dfrac{3432}{16384}=\boxed{\dfrac{429}{2048}}\approx0.21.\]
