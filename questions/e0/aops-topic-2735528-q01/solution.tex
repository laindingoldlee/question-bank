Use vectors with $\vec{DC}=2\vec{AB}$ (the bases are parallel, same direction, and $CD=2AB$). Then
\[\vec{PQ}=\dfrac{\vec B+\vec D}{2}-\dfrac{\vec A+\vec C}{2}=\dfrac{\vec{AB}-\vec{DC}}{2}=-\dfrac{\vec{AB}}{2}.\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair D=(0,0),C=(20.2,0),A=(5,8),B=(15.1,8);
pair P=(A+C)/2,Q=(B+D)/2;
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(0.8));draw(A--C,gray);draw(B--D,gray);
draw(P--Q,linewidth(1));dot(P);dot(Q);
label("$A$",A,NW);label("$B$",B,NE);label("$C$",C,SE);label("$D$",D,SW);label("$P$",P,S);label("$Q$",Q,S);
label("$1010$",(A+B)/2,N,fontsize(8pt));label("$2020$",(C+D)/2,S,fontsize(8pt));
\end{asy}

So $PQ=\dfrac{CD-AB}{2}=\dfrac{2020-1010}{2}=\boxed{505}$, and $PQ$ is parallel to the bases.
