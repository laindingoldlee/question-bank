Euler's theorem requires $\gcd(a,n)=1$, and $\gcd(2,10)=2$, so it does not apply. Splitting $10=2\cdot5$: mod $5$, $\gcd(2,5)=1$ and Euler gives $2^4\equiv1$; mod $2$, $2^4\equiv0$. The unique residue mod $10$ that is $\equiv1\pmod5$ and $\equiv0\pmod2$ is $\boxed{6}$, as observed.
