\textbf{The expansion.} Multiplying out, each term comes from choosing, for each $n$, either $1$ or $nx^{3^n}$. A choice is a subset $T\subseteq\{1,2,\dots,1996\}$ (the $n$ for which $nx^{3^n}$ is taken), and it contributes
\[
\Bigl(\prod_{n\in T}n\Bigr)x^{e(T)},\qquad e(T)=\sum_{n\in T}3^n .
\]
Different subsets give different exponents, because a base-$3$ representation using only the digits $0$ and $1$ is unique. So no terms combine; the nonempty subsets give exactly the terms $a_ix^{k_i}$, with nonzero coefficients.

\textbf{The order of the exponents.} Compare two different subsets $T$, $T'$ and let $N$ be the largest number in exactly one of them, say in $T$. The common elements above $N$ contribute equally, and
\[
3^N>\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}3^n=\dfrac{3^N-3}{2},
\]
so $e(T)>e(T')$. This is exactly how the binary numbers $\beta(T)=\sum_{n\in T}2^{n-1}$ compare. Since $\beta$ is a bijection from the subsets of $\{1,\dots,1996\}$ onto $\{0,1,\dots,2^{1996}-1\}$, the $i$-th smallest exponent $k_i$ comes from the subset $T$ with $\beta(T)=i$.

\textbf{Apply to $i=1234$.} In binary,
\[
1234=1024+128+64+16+2=2^{10}+2^7+2^6+2^4+2^1,
\]
so $T=\{11,8,7,5,2\}$, $k_{1234}=3^{11}+3^8+3^7+3^5+3^2$, and
\[
a_{1234}=11\cdot8\cdot7\cdot5\cdot2=\boxed{6160}.
\]
