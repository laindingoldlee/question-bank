We use the standard constructions of a perpendicular to a line at a given point and of copying a length with the compass.

\textbf{(a)} Draw a segment $PQ$ of length $1$, erect the perpendicular at $Q$, and mark $QR=1$ on it. By the Pythagorean theorem $PR=\sqrt{1^2+1^2}=\sqrt2$. (Equivalently, $\sqrt2$ is the diagonal of a unit square.)

\textbf{(b)} Draw $PQ=1$, erect the perpendicular at $Q$, and mark $QR=\dfrac{1}{2}$ on it (bisect a unit segment with the perpendicular bisector construction). Then
\[
PR=\sqrt{1+\dfrac{1}{4}}=\dfrac{\sqrt5}{2}.
\]
Extend $PR$ beyond $R$ and draw the circle with centre $R$ and radius $\dfrac{1}{2}$; it meets the extension at $T$ with $RT=\dfrac{1}{2}$. So
\[
PT=\dfrac{\sqrt5}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2}.
\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair ft(pair P,pair U,pair V){return U+dot(P-U,V-U)/dot(V-U,V-U)*(V-U);}
path ra(pair P,pair u,pair v,real s=0.2){return P+s*unit(u)--P+s*unit(u)+s*unit(v)--P+s*unit(v);}
real s=3; pair P=(0,0),Q=(s,0),R=(s,s/2); pair T=R+(s/2)*unit(R-P);
draw(arc(R,s/2,-90,degrees(R-P)),gray+dashed);
draw(P--Q--R--cycle,linewidth(0.9)); draw(R--T,red+linewidth(0.9));
draw(ra(Q,P-Q,R-Q,0.2));
dot(P);dot(Q);dot(R);dot(T,red);
label("$P$",P,SW); label("$Q$",Q,SE); label("$R$",R,E); label("$T$",T,NE,red);
label("$1$",(P+Q)/2,S); label("$\dfrac{1}{2}$",(Q+R)/2,E); label("$\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{2}$",(P+R)/2,NW); label("$\dfrac{1}{2}$",(R+T)/2,NW,red);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\textbf{(c)} On a line mark $AB=1$ and then $BC=a$ beyond $B$, so $AC=1+a$. Draw the semicircle with diameter $AC$ (its centre $O$ is the midpoint of $AC$, found with the perpendicular bisector). Erect the perpendicular to $AC$ at $B$, meeting the semicircle at $H$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair ft(pair P,pair U,pair V){return U+dot(P-U,V-U)/dot(V-U,V-U)*(V-U);}
path ra(pair P,pair u,pair v,real s=0.2){return P+s*unit(u)--P+s*unit(u)+s*unit(v)--P+s*unit(v);}
real a=2.5; pair A=(0,0),B=(1,0),C=(1+a,0),O=((1+a)/2,0); pair H=(1,sqrt(a));
draw(arc(O,(1+a)/2,0,180),gray);
draw(A--C,linewidth(0.9)); draw(B--H,red+linewidth(0.9)); draw(A--H--C,blue+dashed);
draw(ra(B,C-B,H-B,0.15)); draw(ra(H,A-H,C-H,0.15),blue);
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);dot(H);dot(O);
label("$A$",A,SW); label("$B$",B,S); label("$C$",C,SE); label("$H$",H,N); label("$O$",O,S);
label("$1$",(A+B)/2,S); label("$a$",(B+C)/2+(0.35,0),S); label("$\sqrt{a}$",(B+H)/2,W,red);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The angle $AHC$ is a right angle, since it is inscribed in a semicircle, and $HB$ is the altitude to the hypotenuse of right triangle $AHC$. Triangles $ABH$ and $HBC$ are similar (each has a right angle at $B$, and $\angle BAH=90^\circ-\angle AHB=\angle BHC$). Hence
\[
\dfrac{BH}{AB}=\dfrac{BC}{BH},\qquad BH^2=AB\cdot BC=a,\qquad BH=\sqrt a .
\]
$\blacksquare$
