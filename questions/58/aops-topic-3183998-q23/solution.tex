All $\dfrac{12!}{(4!)^3}=34650$ assignments of star colours to the twelve distinct sheets are equally likely. In a valid assignment, let $k$ be the number of blue stars on red sheets. The row and column totals of four force all six off-diagonal counts: three are $k$ and three are $4-k$. Choosing which positions on each colour of sheet get each permitted star colour gives $\binom4k^3$ assignments. Summing for $k=0,1,2,3,4$ gives $1+64+216+64+1=346$. Thus the probability is $\dfrac{173}{17325}$ and $m+n=\boxed{17498}$.
