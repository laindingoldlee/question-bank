Since $a^3+b^3=(a+b)\left(a^2-ab+b^2\right)=c\left(a^2-ab+b^2\right)$ and $c\ne0$, the second equation becomes
\[a^2-ab+b^2=c=a+b .\]
Regard this as a quadratic in $a$: $a^2-(b+1)a+\left(b^2-b\right)=0$. For an integer (indeed a real) solution, the discriminant must be nonnegative:
\[(b+1)^2-4\left(b^2-b\right)=-3b^2+6b+1=4-3(b-1)^2\ge0 ,\]
so $(b-1)^2\le1$ and $b\in\{0,1,2\}$. As $b\ne0$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $b=1$: $a^2-2a=0$, so $a=2$ (since $a\ne0$), and $c=3$;
\item $b=2$: $a^2-3a+2=0$, so $a=1$ or $a=2$, and $c=3$ or $c=4$.
\end{itemize}
The solutions are
\[(a,b,c)=\boxed{(2,1,3),\ (1,2,3),\ (2,2,4)} .\]
Check: $8+1=9=3^2$ and $8+8=16=4^2$.
