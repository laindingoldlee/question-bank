Let the two-digit year be $y$ (with $y=00$ for 2100). Month $m$ has a sum date exactly when $d=y-m$ is a valid day of month $m$, that is, $1\le y-m\le(\text{length of month }m)$.
\begin{itemize}
\item December ($m=12$) needs $y-12\ge1$, so $y\ge13$. (This also rules out 2100.)
\item January ($m=1$) needs $y-1\le31$, so $y\le32$; February ($m=2$) needs $y-2\le28$ (or $29$ in a leap year), so $y\le30$, or $y\le31$ if the year is a leap year. But $2031$ is not a leap year, so $y\le30$.
\item For every other month $m\ge3$ the condition $y-m\le28$ already holds for $y\le30$, and $y-m\ge1$ holds for $y\ge13$.
\end{itemize}
So the years are $2013,2014,\dots,2030$, and there are $\boxed{18}$ of them.
