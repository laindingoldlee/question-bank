On the line $x=y$ the equation becomes $|a|x^2=|b|$.

\textbf{Case $a\ne0$.} Then $x^2=\dfrac{|b|}{|a|}\ge0$ has the solutions $x=\pm\sqrt{\dfrac{|b|}{|a|}}$, so the point $\left(\sqrt{\dfrac{|b|}{|a|}},\sqrt{\dfrac{|b|}{|a|}}\right)$ is on both graphs.

\textbf{Case $a=0$, $b=0$.} The equation $0=0$ holds everywhere, so the ``graph'' is the whole plane and certainly meets the line.

\textbf{Case $a=0$, $b\ne0$.} The equation reads $0=|b|$, which no point satisfies; the graph is empty and does not meet the line.

So the statement is \textbf{false}: $a=0$, $b=1$ is a counterexample. The graphs intersect exactly when $\boxed{a\ne0\text{ or }b=0}$.
