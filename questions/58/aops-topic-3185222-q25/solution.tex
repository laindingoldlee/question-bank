Modulo $3$, $x^2+x+1\equiv(x-1)^2$, so the polynomial is congruent to $(x-1)^{4036}$. The base-$3$ expansion is $4036=(12112111)_3$; its eight digits $d_j$ consist of six $1$s and two $2$s. In characteristic $3$, repeated use of $(a+b)^3=a^3+b^3$ gives $(x-1)^{4036}\equiv\prod_{j=0}^7(x^{3^j}-1)^{d_j}$. In a factor with $d_j=1$ or $2$, all $d_j+1$ coefficients are nonzero modulo $3$. Choosing an exponent $e_j3^j$ with $0\le e_j\le d_j$ produces a unique exponent $\sum e_j3^j$, since these are valid base-$3$ digits. Thus no contributions collide or cancel, and the number of nonzero coefficients modulo $3$ is $\prod(d_j+1)=2^6\cdot3^2=\boxed{576}$.
