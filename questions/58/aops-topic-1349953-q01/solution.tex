The first five conditions say that $x-1$ is divisible by $2,3,4,5,6$, i.e. by $\operatorname{lcm}(2,3,4,5,6)=60$. So $x=60m+1$ for some integer $m\ge0$. We also need $7\mid 60m+1$. Since $60\equiv4\pmod7$, this is $4m+1\equiv0$, i.e. $4m\equiv6\pmod7$; multiplying by $2$ (because $2\cdot4=8\equiv1$) gives $m\equiv12\equiv5\pmod7$. The least choice is $m=5$, so $k=301=7\cdot43$. Then
\[
2k+10=\boxed{612}.
\]
(The poster's $864$ comes from a wrong value of $k$; $k=301$ is the least, as $m=0,1,2,3,4$ give $1,61,121,181,241$, none divisible by $7$.)
