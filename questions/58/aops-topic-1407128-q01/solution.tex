Let the bisector of angle $A$ meet $BC$ at $D$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(1,3.2),B=(0,0),C=(5,0);
real a=abs(B-C),b=abs(C-A),c=abs(A-B);
pair I=(a*A+b*B+c*C)/(a+b+c);
pair D=(b*B+c*C)/(b+c);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(A--D,red+dashed); draw(B--I,blue+dashed);
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);dot(D,red);dot(I);
label("$A$",A,N); label("$B$",B,SW); label("$C$",C,SE); label("$D$",D,S,red); label("$I$",I,E);
label("$a$",(B+C)/2+(0.6,0),S); label("$b$",(A+C)/2,NE); label("$c$",(A+B)/2,W);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

By the angle bisector theorem, $\dfrac{BD}{DC}=\dfrac{AB}{AC}=\dfrac{c}{b}$. So $D$ divides $BC$ in the ratio $c:b$, and
\[
D=\dfrac{bB+cC}{b+c},\qquad BD=\dfrac{c}{b+c}\,a .
\]
The incentre $I$ lies on $AD$, and $BI$ bisects angle $ABD$ of triangle $ABD$. Applying the angle bisector theorem in triangle $ABD$:
\[
\dfrac{AI}{ID}=\dfrac{BA}{BD}=\dfrac{c}{\dfrac{ac}{b+c}}=\dfrac{b+c}{a}.
\]
So $I$ divides $AD$ in the ratio $(b+c):a$, which means
\[
I=\dfrac{a\,A+(b+c)\,D}{a+b+c}=\dfrac{aA+bB+cC}{a+b+c}.
\]
This is the incentre formula: the vertices are weighted by the lengths of the opposite sides.
