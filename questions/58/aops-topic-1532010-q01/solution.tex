By the quadratic formula with $a=1$, $b=-1$, $c=1$, the discriminant is $b^2-4ac=1-4=-3<0$, so there are no real roots and the roots are the complex numbers
\[
x=\dfrac{1\pm\sqrt{-3}}{2}=\boxed{\dfrac{1\pm i\sqrt3}{2}}.
\]
Equivalently, completing the square: $\left(x-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2=-\dfrac{3}{4}$, so $x-\dfrac{1}{2}=\pm\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i$.

(Check: the roots have sum $1$ and product $\dfrac{1+3}{4}=1$, as Vieta's formulas require. Since $x^3+1=(x+1)(x^2-x+1)$, they are the two non-real cube roots of $-1$.)
