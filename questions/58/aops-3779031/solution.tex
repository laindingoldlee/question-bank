For a polynomial with negative leading coefficient, only finitely many positive integer inputs have positive outputs. For one with positive leading coefficient $a$, there is a constant $M$ beyond which $f_i (m) \ge (\dfrac{a}{2}) m^2$. Therefore at most $M + \sqrt{\dfrac{2 X}{a}}$ positive integer inputs can give values in $[1,X]$. Taking the union over finitely many polynomials yields at most $C\sqrt X+D$ represented positive integers up to $X$, for constants $C,D$. Since $X-C\sqrt X-D\to\infty$, the number of unrepresented positive integers is unbounded.
