The claim is false in general. $P=2$ is a palindrome and $12$ is divisible by $2$, but its reversal $21$ is odd. With a two-digit palindrome: $132=22\cdot6$, but $231$ is odd, so $22\nmid231$.

For $P=11$ the statement is true: a number and its reversal are congruent modulo $11$ up to sign, because $10\equiv-1\pmod{11}$ (if the number has digits $d_k\ldots d_0$, its reversal is $\equiv(-1)^k$ times the number). So the claim is $\boxed{\text{false in general, true for }P=11}$.
