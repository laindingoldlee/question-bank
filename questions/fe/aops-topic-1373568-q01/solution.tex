For the cubic $2y^3-3y^2+5y+21$, the rational root theorem allows roots $\pm\dfrac{p}{q}$ with $p\mid21$ and $q\mid2$. Trying $y=-\dfrac{3}{2}$:
\[
2\left(-\dfrac{27}{8}\right)-3\cdot\dfrac{9}{4}+5\left(-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)+21=-\dfrac{27}{4}-\dfrac{27}{4}-\dfrac{15}{2}+21=0 .
\]
So $2y+3$ is a factor, and dividing,
\[
2y^3-3y^2+5y+21=(2y+3)(y^2-3y+7).
\]
The quadratic has discriminant $9-28=-19<0$, so its roots are $\dfrac{3\pm i\sqrt{19}}{2}$. Altogether
\[
2y^4-9y^3+14y^2+6y-63=(y-3)(2y+3)(y^2-3y+7),
\]
with roots $\boxed{y=3,\ y=-\dfrac{3}{2},\ y=\dfrac{3+i\sqrt{19}}{2},\ y=\dfrac{3-i\sqrt{19}}{2}}$. The only real roots are $3$ and $-\dfrac{3}{2}$.
