\textbf{(a)} Substituting $a_n=c^n$ gives $c^n=3c^{n-1}c^{n-3}+c^{n-4}=3c^{2n-4}+c^{n-4}$. Dividing by $c^{n-4}\ne0$,
\[c^4=3c^n+1\qquad\text{for all }n\ge5.\]
Comparing $n=5$ and $n=6$ gives $3c^5=3c^6$, so $c=1$; but then $1=3+1$ is false. So there is no such geometric sequence. (The student's equation $c^4=3c^4+1$ is not what substitution gives; it does not even have a real solution.)

\textbf{(b)} A solution is determined by $a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4$, and $a_5=3a_4a_2+a_1$. Take $x$ with initial terms $0,1,0,0$ and $y$ with initial terms $0,0,0,1$. Then
\[x_5=3\cdot0\cdot1+0=0,\qquad y_5=3\cdot1\cdot0+0=0.\]
The sum $s=x+y$ has initial terms $0,1,0,1$, and if it satisfied the recurrence we would need $s_5=3\cdot1\cdot1+0=3$; but $x_5+y_5=0$. So $x+y$ is $\boxed{\text{not a solution}}$.

\textbf{(c)} For a linear recurrence $a_n=k_1a_{n-1}+k_2a_{n-2}$, two facts make the method work: if $r$ is a root of $c^2=k_1c+k_2$, then $a_n=r^n$ is a solution; and sums and constant multiples of solutions are again solutions. Combining the geometric solutions, $a_n=Ar_1^n+Br_2^n$ (when $r_1\ne r_2$), and choosing $A,B$ to match $a_1,a_2$ gives every solution. The recurrence $a_n=3a_{n-1}a_{n-3}+a_{n-4}$ is not linear, because of the product $a_{n-1}a_{n-3}$: by (a) it has no nonzero geometric solutions, and by (b) solutions cannot be added. So there is no characteristic polynomial for it, and in general such a recurrence has no simple closed form.
