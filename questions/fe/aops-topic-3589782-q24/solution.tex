Initially every pile is divisible by $7$. Pair operations preserve this property. An averaging operation also preserves it: if $7\mid kn$ and $1\le k\le5$, then $\gcd(k,7)=1$ implies $7\mid n$. Since $2008$ is not divisible by $7$, that target is impossible. For $2009$, repeated subtraction implements the Euclidean algorithm on pairs. Since $\gcd(42,70,105,462,2009)=7$, the piles can be reduced to $(7,0,0,0,0)$. Keep the first pile fixed and add it repeatedly to the second, obtaining $(7,10038,0,0,0)$. Their total is $10045=5\cdot2009$, so averaging all five piles produces the requested target. Thus the answers are $\boxed{\text{no for }2008,\ \text{yes for }2009}$.
