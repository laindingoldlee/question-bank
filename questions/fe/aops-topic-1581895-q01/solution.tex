The triangular numbers are $T_n=\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}$: $1,3,6,10,15,21,28,36,\dots$

We need $\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}=m^2$. Since $n$ and $n+1$ are coprime, one of them is a square and the other is twice a square:
\begin{itemize}
\item $n$ odd: $n=a^2$, $n+1=2b^2$, so $a^2-2b^2=-1$, and $m=ab$;
\item $n$ even: $n=2b^2$, $n+1=a^2$, so $a^2-2b^2=1$, and $m=ab$.
\end{itemize}
The solutions of $a^2-2b^2=\pm1$ in positive integers, in increasing order, are
\[
(a,b)=(1,1),\ (3,2),\ (7,5),\ (17,12),\dots
\]
(each is obtained from the previous one by $(a,b)\mapsto(a+2b,\,a+b)$; for the small ones simply check $b=1,2,\dots,5$). They give
\begin{itemize}
\item $(1,1)$: $n=1$, $T_1=1=1^2$;
\item $(3,2)$: $n=8$, $T_8=36=6^2$;
\item $(7,5)$: $n=49$, $T_{49}=1225=35^2$;
\item $(17,12)$: $n=288$, $T_{288}=41616=204^2$, the next one.
\end{itemize}
Every square triangular number arises this way, with $m=ab$ increasing along the list, so the first three are $1$, $36$ and $1225$, and their sum is
\[
1+36+1225=\boxed{1262}.
\]
