For $2\le k\le n$, $k\mid n!$, so $k\mid n!+k$, and $n!+k>k$. Thus $n!+k$ has a divisor strictly between $1$ and itself and is composite. $\blacksquare$ (This gives $n-1$ consecutive composite numbers.)
