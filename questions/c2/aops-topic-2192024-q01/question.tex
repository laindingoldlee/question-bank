\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] A game of Four Square has four positions $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$; the players who are not in a position wait in an ordered line. If $6$ people play, how many arrangements are possible (who is in each of $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$, and who is first and who is second in line)?
\item[(b)] Frank and his $9$ friends play a variant: when a player gets out, that player chooses who comes in from the line, so the order of the line no longer matters. How many arrangements are possible now (an arrangement says who is in each of the positions $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$)?
\item[(c)] In this variant, the only people who ever choose Frank are his two best friends, and they always choose Frank. Frank is waiting in the line, talking on the phone, and does not know where anyone is: all arrangements of the other $9$ people are equally likely. Each of the four people in the positions is equally likely to be the next to get out. What is the probability that Frank is chosen to come in next?
\end{enumerate}
