Let the fixed adjacent vertices be $A,B$ and the polygon area be $K$. Almost surely the four points are in general position. They fail to form a convex quadrilateral precisely when $X$ lies inside triangle $ABY$ or $Y$ lies inside triangle $ABX$; the fixed boundary vertices cannot be interior points. The first event has probability $\dfrac{\mathbb E[ABY]}{K}$. Triangle area is proportional to the height of $Y$ above side $AB$, so its expected area equals the area of triangle $ABO$, where $O$ is the polygon centre, the mean position of a uniform point. This area is $\dfrac{K}{9}$, so each nonconvex event has probability $\dfrac{1}{9}$, and convex position has probability $\dfrac{7}{9}$. In convex position, exactly one of the pairings $(AX,BY)$ and $(AY,BX)$ crosses: pairing $AB$ with $XY$ cannot cross because $AB$ is a boundary side. Exchanging $X,Y$ makes the two remaining pairings equally likely. The required probability is therefore $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{7}{9}=\boxed{\dfrac{7}{18}}$.
