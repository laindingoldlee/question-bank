Ten people, including $x$, $y$ and $z$, sit around a circular table with ten seats; seatings that differ by a rotation are the same.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] In how many seatings are $x$, $y$, $z$ \emph{not} all three sitting together (in three consecutive seats)?
\item[(b)] In how many seatings are no two of $x$, $y$, $z$ next to each other?
\item[(c)] A student answers (a) with $1-\dfrac{6!\cdot3!}{9!}$. What is wrong with this?
\end{enumerate}
