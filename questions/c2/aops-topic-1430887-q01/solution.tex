There are $9!=362880$ circular seatings of $10$ people.

\textbf{(a)} Count the complement. Glue $x,y,z$ into a block ($3!$ internal orders). The block and the other $7$ people are $8$ units around a circle: $7!$ seatings. So $7!\cdot3!=30240$ seatings have all three together, and
\[
9!-7!\cdot3!=362880-30240=\boxed{332640}
\]
do not.

\textbf{(b)} Seat the $7$ others first: $6!=720$ circular seatings. They leave $7$ gaps, and $x$, $y$, $z$ go into three different gaps in $7\cdot6\cdot5=210$ ways. So there are $720\cdot210=\boxed{151200}$ seatings.

\textbf{(c)} The expression is a probability, not a number of seatings, and its count is wrong: with $x,y,z$ glued there are $8$ units, which give $7!$ (not $6!$) circular seatings. The correct probability that they are not all together is $1-\dfrac{7!\cdot3!}{9!}=1-\dfrac{1}{12}=\dfrac{11}{12}$, matching $\dfrac{332640}{362880}$.
