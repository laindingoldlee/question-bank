Let $S$ and $T$ be Bob's and Tom's sums; each is at most $12$. The number of ways to roll a sum of $9,10,11,12$ with two dice is $4,3,2,1$.

If $ST>100$ then both sums are at least $9$ (otherwise $ST\le8\cdot12=96$). Among sums from $9$ to $12$:
\[
9\cdot11=99,\quad 10\cdot10=100,\quad 9\cdot12=108,\quad 10\cdot11=110,
\]
so the pairs with $ST>100$ are $(9,12)$, $(10,11)$, $(10,12)$, $(11,11)$, $(11,12)$, $(12,12)$ and the reverses of the unequal ones. Counting outcomes of the four dice:
\[
2\cdot4\cdot1+2\cdot3\cdot2+2\cdot3\cdot1+2\cdot2+2\cdot2\cdot1+1\cdot1=8+12+6+4+4+1=35 .
\]
There are $6^4=1296$ equally likely outcomes, so the probability is $\boxed{\dfrac{35}{1296}}$, as stated in the post.
