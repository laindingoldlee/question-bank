A chick is pecked only by its two neighbours. It is unpecked exactly when its left neighbour pecks to the left (away from it) and its right neighbour pecks to the right (away from it). These two independent events each have probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so each chick is unpecked with probability $\dfrac{1}{4}$.

By linearity of expectation (the number of unpecked chicks is the sum of $100$ indicator variables, one per chick), the expected number of unpecked chicks is
\[
100\cdot\dfrac{1}{4}=\boxed{25}.
\]
