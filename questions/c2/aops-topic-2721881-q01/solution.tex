Triangles $ACD$ and $ABC$ have the same apex $A$ and bases $CD$ and $CB$ on the same line, so their areas are in the ratio $CD:CB$. Hence $CD=\dfrac12CB$, and $D$ is the midpoint of $BC$:
\[D=\left(\dfrac{5+(-1)}{2},\dfrac{8+(-7)}{2}\right)=\left(2,\dfrac12\right),\qquad x+y=\boxed{\dfrac52}.\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(-1,2),B=(5,8),C=(-1,-7),D=(B+C)/2;
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.8));draw(A--D,dashed);
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);dot(D);
label("$A(-1,2)$",A,W,fontsize(8pt));label("$B(5,8)$",B,NE,fontsize(8pt));label("$C(-1,-7)$",C,SW,fontsize(8pt));label("$D$",D,E,fontsize(8pt));
\end{asy}

(The handbook's answer $D=(2,0)$, $x+y=2$, would be correct for $B=(5,7)$, not for the stated $B=(5,8)$.)
