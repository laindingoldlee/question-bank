\textbf{Counting argument.} Consider a group of $n+1$ people, one of whom is called Pat. The left side $\dbinom{n+1}{k}$ counts the committees of $k$ people chosen from the group. Split these committees according to Pat:
\begin{itemize}
\item committees containing Pat: the other $k-1$ members are chosen from the remaining $n$ people, in $\dbinom{n}{k-1}$ ways;
\item committees not containing Pat: all $k$ members are chosen from the remaining $n$ people, in $\dbinom{n}{k}$ ways.
\end{itemize}
Every committee is in exactly one of the two groups, so $\dbinom{n+1}{k}=\dbinom{n}{k-1}+\dbinom nk$. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{The example.} $\dbinom72=21$ counts the pairs chosen from $7$ people. The pairs containing Pat are Pat together with one of the other $6$ people: $\dbinom61=6$ pairs. The pairs not containing Pat are chosen from the other $6$ people: $\dbinom62=15$ pairs. Indeed $6+15=21$.
