By the definition of squaring and the distributive law (used twice),
\[
(a+b)^2=(a+b)(a+b)=a(a+b)+b(a+b)=a^2+ab+ba+b^2 .
\]
Multiplication is commutative, so $ba=ab$, and therefore
\[
(a+b)^2=a^2+ab+ab+b^2=a^2+b^2+2ab. \qquad\blacksquare
\]
(For positive $a$ and $b$ this is the familiar picture of a square of side $a+b$ cut into a square of area $a^2$, a square of area $b^2$ and two rectangles of area $ab$.)
