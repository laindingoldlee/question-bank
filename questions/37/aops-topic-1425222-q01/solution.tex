List the numbers as $a_1<a_2<\dots<a_k$. Since the two products differ, the smallest two and the largest two are not the same pair, so $k\ge3$. If $k=3$, then $a_2$ divides both $16$ and $225$, so $a_2=1$, which is impossible because $a_1<a_2$. So $k\ge4$, and the pairs $\{a_1,a_2\}$ and $\{a_{k-1},a_k\}$ are disjoint, with $a_2<a_{k-1}$.

From $a_1a_2=16$ with $a_1<a_2$: $(a_1,a_2)=(1,16)$ or $(2,8)$. From $a_{k-1}a_k=225$ with $a_{k-1}<a_k$: $(a_{k-1},a_k)=(1,225),(3,75),(5,45)$ or $(9,25)$. The condition $a_{k-1}>a_2\ge8$ leaves only $(a_{k-1},a_k)=(9,25)$, and then $a_2<9$ forces $(a_1,a_2)=(2,8)$. No integer lies strictly between $8$ and $9$, so the board shows exactly
\[
2,\ 8,\ 9,\ 25,
\]
and the sum is $\boxed{44}$.
