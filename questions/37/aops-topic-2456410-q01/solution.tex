\textbf{(a)} Expanding,
\[(ax+m)(ax+n)=a^2x^2+a(m+n)x+mn=a^2x^2+abx+ac=a\left(ax^2+bx+c\right).\ \blacksquare\]
So once two numbers with sum $b$ and product $ac$ are found, the quadratic factors; in practice we look for them among the factor pairs of $ac$.

\textbf{(b)} For $6x^2+7x-20$: $ac=6\cdot(-20)=-120$, and we need two numbers with product $-120$ and sum $7$. Among the factor pairs of $120$, the pair $15,8$ has difference $7$, so $m=15$, $n=-8$. By (a),
\[6\left(6x^2+7x-20\right)=(6x+15)(6x-8)=3(2x+5)\cdot2(3x-4),\]
and dividing by $6$ gives
\[6x^2+7x-20=\boxed{(2x+5)(3x-4)}.\]

For $12x^2-17x+6$: $ac=72$, and we need product $72$ and sum $-17$, so both numbers are negative and their absolute values are a factor pair of $72$ with sum $17$: that is $8$ and $9$. With $m=-8$, $n=-9$,
\[12\left(12x^2-17x+6\right)=(12x-8)(12x-9)=4(3x-2)\cdot3(4x-3),\]
so
\[12x^2-17x+6=\boxed{(3x-2)(4x-3)}.\]
(Check by expanding: $(2x+5)(3x-4)=6x^2-8x+15x-20$ and $(3x-2)(4x-3)=12x^2-9x-8x+6$.)
