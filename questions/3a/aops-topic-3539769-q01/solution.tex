For digits a,b,c,d,e,f, put $u=a+d$, $v=b+e$, $w=c+f$. Since $1000\equiv 1\pmod{111}$, divisibility requires $100 u+10 v+w\equiv 0$. Also $u+v+w=27$, so $37$ divides $11 u+v+3$. Each pair sum lies between 5 and 13. The possible multiples of 37 in this range are 74,111,148. For 74, the only feasible u is 6 giving v 5, but then w 16 is impossible. For 148, u 12 gives v 13 and w 2, also impossible. The middle multiple forces $u=v=w=9$. Thus the opposite-position pairs must be $(2,7),(3,6),(4,5)$. Assign them in $3!$ ways and order each in 2 ways, giving $3!2^3=\boxed{48}$.
