Group the binary digits of $Q$ in pairs from the right: each pair is one base-$4$ digit, and the base-$4$ digit is $1$ exactly when the pair is $01$. So three pairs are $01$, contributing three of the five binary $1$s; the other two $1$s lie in pairs $11$ (one pair) or $10$ (two pairs).

\textbf{Smallest candidate.} We need at least $4$ pairs, so $Q\ge4^3=64$, and with exactly $4$ pairs they are three $01$s and one $11$, the leading pair being $01$ or $11$. The smallest arrangement puts $01$ first and $11$ last:
\[
01\,01\,01\,11_2=64+16+4+3=87 .
\]
The other $4$-pair arrangements are $93$, $117$ and $213$, and $5$ or more pairs give $Q\ge256$. So $87$ is the smallest number satisfying the first two conditions.

\textbf{Base $8$.} $87=127_8$, which has exactly one digit $1$. So $Q=87$, and its digit sum is $8+7=\boxed{\textbf{(c)}\ 15}$.
