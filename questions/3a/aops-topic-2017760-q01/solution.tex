Try to write $1.75+\sqrt3$ as a perfect square $(p+q\sqrt3)^2=p^2+3q^2+2pq\sqrt3$. We need $2pq=1$ and $p^2+3q^2=\dfrac{7}{4}$; $p=1$, $q=\dfrac{1}{2}$ works:
\[
\left(1+\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)^2=1+\sqrt3+\dfrac{3}{4}=1.75+\sqrt3 .
\]
Let $t=1+\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}>0$, so $\sqrt{1.75+\sqrt3}=t$ and the expression equals
\[
2t-t^2=2+\sqrt3-\left(1.75+\sqrt3\right)=0.25 .
\]
The answer is $\boxed{0.25}$.
