The statement ``each cube lands with a prime number on top'' is a condition: we work in the reduced sample space where both cubes show one of the primes $2$, $3$, $5$. Its $3\cdot3=9$ ordered outcomes are equally likely (each face is equally likely, so each pair of prime faces is too).

The sum is at least $9$ only for $(5,5)$, whose sum is $10$; every other pair has sum at most $5+3=8$. So the probability is
\[
\boxed{\dfrac{8}{9}}.
\]
(This is the conditional probability $P(\text{sum}<9\mid\text{both prime})=\dfrac{\dfrac{8}{36}}{\dfrac{9}{36}}$; it is not the probability that both cubes show primes and the sum is less than $9$, which would be $\dfrac{8}{36}=\dfrac{2}{9}$.)
