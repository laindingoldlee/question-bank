Alice and Bob play the following game. Alice fixes a positive integer $n\ge2$ and a table that says, for each pair $(x,y)$ of different numbers from $1$ to $n$, which player gets a point when Alice chooses $x$ and Bob chooses $y$. When both choose the same number, nobody gets a point. Bob then announces a strategy, that is, probabilities $p_1,\dots,p_n$ (adding to $1$) with which he will choose each number in every round, independently. Knowing these, Alice chooses her number in each round as she likes. Bob ``wins'' if his expected number of points per round is at least Alice's.

A student claims that, whatever table Alice sets up, Bob has a strategy with which he wins. Show that the claim is false.
