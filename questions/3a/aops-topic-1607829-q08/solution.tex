Divide: $-2x-9=-2(x+5)+1$, so
\[
f(x)=-2+\dfrac{1}{x+5},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad (x+5)(y+2)=1 .
\]
This is the hyperbola $XY=1$ (with $X=x+5$, $Y=y+2$) moved so that its centre is $(-5,-2)$; its asymptotes are $x=-5$ and $y=-2$. The hyperbola $XY=1$ is symmetric about the line $Y=X$, and its vertices are the points of the curve on that line, $(X,Y)=(1,1)$ and $(-1,-1)$. Hence the vertices of the graph of $f$ are $(-4,-1)$ and $(-6,-3)$, and the line through them is $Y=X$, that is $y+2=x+5$:
\[
\boxed{\textbf{(d)}\ y=x+3}.
\]
