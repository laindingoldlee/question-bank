\textbf{Correct count.} Line up the $10$ toys as stars and insert $2$ bars to split them into $3$ groups (dog $1$, dog $2$, dog $3$):
\[\star\star\star\,|\,\star\star\star\star\star\,|\,\star\star\quad\text{means }3,5,2 .\]
Every distribution corresponds to exactly one arrangement of $10$ stars and $2$ bars in a row of $12$ symbols, and the arrangement is fixed by choosing the positions of the $2$ bars. So the number of ways is
\[\dbinom{12}{2}=\boxed{66}.\]

\textbf{What is wrong.} There are two mistakes.
\begin{itemize}
\item Three groups need only \emph{two} dividers, not three.
\item Choosing different gaps among the $11$ gaps forbids two bars in the same gap. But two adjacent bars are exactly what happens when the middle dog gets nothing. Choosing $2$ different gaps out of $11$ gives $\dbinom{11}{2}=55$ distributions; the $11$ ways of putting both bars in the same gap give the remaining ones, and $55+11=66$.
\end{itemize}
