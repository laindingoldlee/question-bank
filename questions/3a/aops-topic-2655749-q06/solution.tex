In the face-adjacency diagram below (the dodecahedron flattened, with the bottom face as the outer region), $T_1,\dots,T_5$ form the top ring and $U_1,\dots,U_5$ the bottom ring.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real r1=1,r2=1.85,r3=2.55,r4=3.55;
pair[] A,B,C,D;
for(int i=0;i<5;++i){A[i]=r1*dir(90+72i);B[i]=r2*dir(90+72i);C[i]=r3*dir(126+72i);D[i]=r4*dir(126+72i);}
for(int i=0;i<5;++i){
 filldraw(A[i]--A[(i+1)%5]--B[(i+1)%5]--C[i]--B[i]--cycle,mediumgray,linewidth(0.7));
}
for(int i=0;i<5;++i){
 filldraw(B[(i+1)%5]--C[i]--D[i]--D[(i+1)%5]--C[(i+1)%5]--cycle,palegray,linewidth(0.7));
}
filldraw(A[0]--A[1]--A[2]--A[3]--A[4]--cycle,white,linewidth(0.7));
label("top",(0,0));
for(int i=0;i<5;++i){label("$T_"+string(i+1)+"$",1.45*dir(126+72i));label("$U_"+string(i+1)+"$",2.45*dir(162+72i));}
label("bottom face = outer region",(0,-4.2));
\end{asy}

A route goes: top face $\to$ some faces of the top ring $\to$ some faces of the bottom ring $\to$ bottom face. It never returns to the top face, and it cannot go back up from the bottom ring.
\begin{itemize}
\item Enter the top ring at any of its $5$ faces. Since faces cannot be revisited, the route then stays put or walks $1,2,3$ or $4$ steps around the ring in one of the two directions: $1+2\cdot4=9$ possibilities.
\item From the last top-ring face, go down to one of its $2$ neighbours in the bottom ring.
\item In the bottom ring there are again $9$ possibilities (stay, or walk $1$ to $4$ steps in either direction), and then the move to the bottom face.
\end{itemize}
Hence the number of routes is $5\cdot9\cdot2\cdot9=\boxed{810}$.
