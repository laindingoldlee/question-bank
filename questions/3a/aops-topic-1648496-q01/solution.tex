$G$ has exactly three elements: $\varnothing$, $\{1\}$ and $\{\varnothing,1\}$. These elements happen to be sets themselves, but for the power set they are simply three different objects (they are different sets, since they have $0$, $1$ and $2$ elements). A three-element set has $2^3=8$ subsets: one with no elements, three with one, three with two and one with three. So
\[
\mathcal P(G)=\Bigl\{\ \varnothing,\ \{\varnothing\},\ \{\{1\}\},\ \{\{\varnothing,1\}\},\ \{\varnothing,\{1\}\},\ \{\varnothing,\{\varnothing,1\}\},\ \{\{1\},\{\varnothing,1\}\},\ G\ \Bigr\}.
\]
Note that $\varnothing$ (the empty subset) and $\{\varnothing\}$ (the subset containing the element $\varnothing$) are different members of $\mathcal P(G)$.
