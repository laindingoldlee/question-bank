We need $7k=\dfrac{m(m+1)}{2}$ for a positive integer $m$, i.e.\ $14k=m(m+1)$. Since $7$ is prime, it divides $m$ or $m+1$, i.e.\ $m\equiv0$ or $6\pmod7$; then $m(m+1)$ is automatically even, and $k=\dfrac{m(m+1)}{14}$ is an integer. Different $m$ give different $k$, increasing with $m$.

The admissible $m$ in increasing order are $6,7,13,14,20,21,\dots$, i.e.\ the pairs $7j-1,7j$ for $j=1,2,\dots$; they give the terms $3,4,13,15,\dots$ in order. The $100$th term is the second of the $50$th pair, $m=350$:
\[
k=\dfrac{350\cdot351}{14}=25\cdot351=\boxed{8775}.
\]
