Dividing by the positive quantity $2^{x^2+x}$ reduces the equation to $h(x)=2^{x^2-x+1}+2^{2-x}=9$. Each summand has positive second derivative: for the first it is $2^{x^2-x+1}((2x-1)^2(\ln2)^2+2\ln2)$, and the second contributes $2^{2-x}(\ln2)^2$. Thus $h$ is strictly convex, so a horizontal line meets it at most twice. Since $h(-1)=16,h(0)=6,h(2)=9$, continuity gives one solution in $(-1,0)$ and another at $2$. These are exactly the two real solutions.
