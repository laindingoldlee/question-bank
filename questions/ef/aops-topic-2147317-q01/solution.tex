\textbf{Repeated squaring.} $3^{10}=59049\equiv49\pmod{1000}$. Then
\[3^{20}\equiv49^2=2401\equiv401,\qquad 3^{40}\equiv401^2=160801\equiv801,\qquad 3^{50}=3^{40}\cdot3^{10}\equiv801\cdot49=39249\equiv249 .\]

\textbf{Binomial theorem.} Since $3^{10}\equiv49=50-1$,
\[3^{50}\equiv(50-1)^5=\sum_{k=0}^{5}\dbinom5k50^k(-1)^{5-k}\equiv-1+5\cdot50=249\pmod{1000},\]
because the term with $k=2$ is $\dbinom52\cdot50^2\cdot(-1)^3=-25000$ and every term with $k\ge3$ contains $50^3=125000$, so all of them are multiples of $1000$.

So the remainder is $\boxed{249}$.

\emph{In general}, $a^b\bmod m$ is found by repeated squaring (write $b$ in binary and multiply the needed squares), reducing modulo $m$ at every step; when $\gcd(a,m)=1$, Euler's theorem $a^{\varphi(m)}\equiv1\pmod m$ lets one first reduce the exponent modulo $\varphi(m)$ (here $\varphi(1000)=400$).
