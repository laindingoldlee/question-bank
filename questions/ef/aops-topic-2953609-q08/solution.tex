Use Burnside's lemma with the $8$ symmetries of the square.

\emph{Identity.} It fixes all $\dbinom{9}{3,3,3}=1680$ colourings.

\emph{Rotations by $90^\circ$ and $270^\circ$.} Their cycles are the centre, one $4$-cycle of corners and one $4$-cycle of edges. Each colour class would be a union of cycles of size $3$, which is impossible, so they fix $0$.

\emph{Rotation by $180^\circ$.} Its cycles are the centre and four $2$-cycles. Sizes $1+2+2+2+2$ cannot be split into $3,3,3$: the two colours not at the centre would need odd sizes from $2$-cycles. So it fixes $0$.

\emph{Each of the $4$ reflections.} Its cycles are $3$ fixed cells and three $2$-cycles. Each colour takes one fixed cell and one $2$-cycle, giving $3!\cdot3!=36$ colourings.

Total $\dfrac{1680+0+0+0+4\cdot36}{8}=\dfrac{1824}8=\boxed{228}$.
