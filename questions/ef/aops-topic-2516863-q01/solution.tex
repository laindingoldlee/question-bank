For a positive integer $k$, $k!=1\cdot2\cdots k$ is a product of positive integers, so it is a positive integer. Hence $\dfrac{m!}{n!}$ is a quotient of two positive numbers, which is positive.

If $m\ge n$, then $m!=n!\cdot(n+1)(n+2)\cdots m$, so
\[\dfrac{m!}{n!}=(n+1)(n+2)\cdots m,\]
a product of positive integers (the empty product $1$ when $m=n$), hence a positive integer. $\blacksquare$
