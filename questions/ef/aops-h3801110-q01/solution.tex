The domain is $x>-7$. Put $F(x)=5^x-2^x-\log_2(x+7)$.
For $-6\le x<0$, the first difference is negative and the logarithm is nonnegative, so no solution occurs; at zero $F(0)<0$ as well. For $-7<x<-6$, the difference $5^x-2^x$ is strictly decreasing: indeed $(\dfrac{5}{2})^x\ln5<\ln2$ there. The logarithm is strictly increasing, so $F$ is strictly decreasing. Since $F(x)\to+\infty$ as $x\to-7^+$ and $F(-6)<0$, there is exactly one root in this interval. Bisection gives
\[
-6.0106501<x<-6.0106500.
\]
For $x\ge0$,
\[
F'(x)=5^x\ln5-2^x\ln2-\dfrac{1}{(x+7)\ln2}
\ge\ln(\dfrac{5}{2})-\dfrac{1}{7\ln2}>0.
\]
Hence there is at most one nonnegative root, and $x=1$ works. The complete answer is therefore $\boxed{x=1\quad\text{or}\quad x\approx-6.010650}$.
