Multiply the equation by $8$ and add $1$:
\[4n^2+4n+1=2^{x+3}-8+1,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad(2n+1)^2=2^{x+3}-7.\]
So $(k,m)=(x+3,\,2n+1)$ is a solution of $2^k-7=m^2$ in positive integers, with $k=x+3\ge8$ and $m=2n+1\ge13$. Conversely, every such solution with $m$ odd gives a pair $(n,x)$.

By the Ramanujan--Nagell theorem the only solution with $k\ge8$ is $(k,m)=(15,181)$, which gives $x=12$ and $n=90$. Check:
\[\dfrac{90\cdot91}{2}=4095=2^{12}-1.\]
So there is exactly one pair: $\boxed{(n,x)=(90,12)}$.

(The other four solutions of the Ramanujan--Nagell equation give $(n,x)=(0,0),(1,1),(2,2),(5,4)$, i.e. $0=2^0-1$, $1=2^1-1$, $3=2^2-1$ and $15=2^4-1$; they are excluded by $n>5$ and $x>4$.)
