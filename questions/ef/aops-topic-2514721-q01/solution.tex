\textbf{(a)} Choose which $2$ of the $4$ students voted ($\dbinom42=6$ ways); each such pattern has probability $\left(\dfrac15\right)^2\left(\dfrac45\right)^2$. So
\[P=6\cdot\dfrac{1}{25}\cdot\dfrac{16}{25}=\boxed{\dfrac{96}{625}}=0.1536.\]

\textbf{(b)} If the school has exactly $x$ students, of whom $0.2x$ voted, and $4$ different students are chosen uniformly at random, then the number of favourable groups is $\dbinom{0.2x}{2}\dbinom{0.8x}{2}$ out of $\dbinom x4$. So the formula is the exact answer for sampling \emph{without replacement} from a school of known size $x$, which the problem does not give. For $x=100$:
\[\dfrac{\dbinom{20}{2}\dbinom{80}{2}}{\dbinom{100}{4}}=\dfrac{190\cdot3160}{3921225}=\dfrac{600400}{3921225}\approx0.1531.\]
As $x\to\infty$,
\[\dfrac{\dfrac{0.2x(0.2x-1)}{2}\cdot\dfrac{0.8x(0.8x-1)}{2}}{\dfrac{x(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)}{24}}=6\cdot\dfrac{0.2x(0.2x-1)\cdot0.8x(0.8x-1)}{x(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)}\to6\cdot0.2^2\cdot0.8^2=\dfrac{96}{625}.\]
So for a large school the two answers agree; the intended answer is $\dfrac{96}{625}$.
