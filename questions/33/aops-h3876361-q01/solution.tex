The left side is positive, so write $x=t+k\pi$ with $k\in\mathbb Z$ and $0<t<\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. Taking logarithms, define
\[
H_k(t)=(-1)^k(\log3)(\sin t-\cos t)-\log(\tan t).
\]
For odd $k$, its derivative is negative. For even $k$,
\[
H_k'(t)=(\log3)(\cos t+\sin t)-\dfrac{1}{\sin t\cos t}
\le\sqrt2\log3-2<0.
\]
Here $\log3<\sqrt2$, for instance because $e^{\sqrt2}>1+\sqrt2+\dfrac{(\sqrt2)^2}{2}>3$. Hence $H_k$ is strictly decreasing. Since $H_k(\dfrac{\pi}{4})=0$, that is its only zero. All solutions are
\[
\boxed{x=\dfrac{\pi}{4}+k\pi,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z}.
\]
