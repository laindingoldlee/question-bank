Two numbers differ by $205$ only if they leave the same remainder on division by $205$. Group the integers into the $205$ chains
\[r,\ r+205,\ r+410,\ \dots\qquad(r=1,2,\dots,205),\]
within which numbers differing by $205$ are exactly the neighbouring terms. Since $2020=9\cdot205+175$, the chains with $r\le175$ have $10$ terms (the last is $r+1845\le2020$) and the other $30$ chains have $9$ terms.

In a chain of $10$ terms, the five pairs (1st, 2nd), (3rd, 4th), $\dots$, (9th, 10th) of neighbours each contain at most one drawn number if no two drawn numbers differ by $205$; so at most $5$ numbers come from that chain. In a chain of $9$ terms there are four such pairs plus the last term, so again at most $5$. Hence any set of numbers with no difference $205$ has at most $205\cdot5=1025$ elements.

This bound is reached: take the 1st, 3rd, 5th, 7th and 9th terms of every chain. So $1025$ draws do not suffice, while any $1026$ numbers must contain two that differ by $205$. The answer is $\boxed{1026}$.
