Let $L=\operatorname{lcm}(s,t)$ and $M=\operatorname{lcm}(ns,nt)$.

$nL$ is a common multiple of $ns$ and $nt$ (since $s\mid L$ gives $ns\mid nL$, and similarly for $t$), so $M\le nL$, and in fact $M\mid nL$.

Conversely $n\mid ns\mid M$, so $M=nK$ for an integer $K$. From $ns\mid nK$ we get $s\mid K$, and similarly $t\mid K$, so $K$ is a common multiple of $s$ and $t$ and $L\le K$. Hence $nL\le nK=M$.

Together, $M=nL$. $\blacksquare$
