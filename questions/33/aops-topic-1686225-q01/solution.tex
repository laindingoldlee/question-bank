Suppose $\sqrt{100-48\sqrt3}=\sqrt p-\sqrt q$ with rational $p,q\ge0$. Squaring,
\[
p+q-2\sqrt{pq}=100-48\sqrt3 .
\]
Let $s=p+q-100$, a rational number. Then $2\sqrt{pq}=s+48\sqrt3$, and squaring again,
\[
4pq=s^2+96s\sqrt3+6912 .
\]
Since $\sqrt3$ is irrational while $4pq$, $s^2$ and $6912$ are rational, we must have $96s=0$, so $s=0$: $p+q=100$ and $4pq=6912$, i.e.\ $pq=1728$. Then $p$ and $q$ are the roots of
\[
t^2-100t+1728=0,\qquad t=\dfrac{100\pm\sqrt{10000-6912}}{2}=50\pm2\sqrt{193},
\]
and $\sqrt{193}$ is irrational ($193$ is prime). So $p$ and $q$ are not rational, a contradiction. $\blacksquare$

(For comparison, a radical such as $\sqrt{28-10\sqrt3}$ does denest: $28-10\sqrt3=(5-\sqrt3)^2$. The test is whether $a^2-b^2c$ is a perfect square for $\sqrt{a-b\sqrt c}$; here $100^2-48^2\cdot3=3088$ is not.)
