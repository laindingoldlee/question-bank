The statement is \textbf{false} in general; it holds only for equilateral triangles.

Let $a=BC$, $b=CA$, $c=AB$, let $s=\dfrac{a+b+c}{2}$ and let $r$ be the inradius. Tangent segments from a point to a circle are equal, so $AX=AZ$, $BX=BY$, $CY=CZ$; solving $AX+BX=c$, $BY+CY=a$, $CZ+AZ=b$ gives
\[
AX=AZ=s-a,\qquad BX=BY=s-b,\qquad CY=CZ=s-c .
\]
The radii $IX$, $IZ$ are perpendicular to the sides, so the kite $IXAZ$ consists of two right triangles with legs $r$ and $s-a$:
\[
[IXAZ]=2\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}\,r(s-a)=r(s-a),
\]
and likewise $[IXBY]=r(s-b)$, $[IYCZ]=r(s-c)$. These three areas are equal exactly when $s-a=s-b=s-c$, that is, when $a=b=c$. (They always add up to $r\bigl(3s-(a+b+c)\bigr)=rs=[ABC]$, so if they are equal each is $\dfrac{[ABC]}{3}$.)

\textbf{Counterexample.} Take the right triangle with $AB=4$, $AC=3$, $BC=5$. Then $s=6$, $[ABC]=6$ and $r=\dfrac{[ABC]}{s}=1$, so
\[
[IXAZ]=1\cdot(6-5)=1,\qquad [IXBY]=1\cdot(6-3)=3,\qquad [IYCZ]=1\cdot(6-4)=2,
\]
which are not all equal to $\dfrac{6}{3}=2$.

fill(A--X--I--Z--cycle,rgb(1,0.85,0.85));
fill(X--B--Y--I--cycle,rgb(0.85,0.9,1));
fill(Y--C--Z--I--cycle,rgb(0.85,1,0.85));
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(circle(I,1),gray);
draw(I--X^^I--Y^^I--Z,dashed);
dot(I);dot(X);dot(Y);dot(Z);
label("$A$",A,SW);label("$B$",B,SE);label("$C$",C,N);
label("$I$",I,NW);label("$X$",X,S);label("$Y$",Y,NE);label("$Z$",Z,W);
label("$1$",(0.42,0.45));label("$3$",(2.35,0.55));label("$2$",(0.45,1.75));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

So the claimed equality holds if and only if the triangle is equilateral; trying an equilateral triangle (or any numbers that happen to give equal tangent lengths) makes it look true.
