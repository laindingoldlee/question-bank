We need $3^n=m^3+1=(m+1)(m^2-m+1)$. Both factors are positive divisors of $3^n$, so both are powers of $3$. Since $m\ge1$, we have $m+1\ge2$, so $m+1=3^a$ with $a\ge1$.

Now write $m^2-m+1$ in terms of $m+1$:
\[
m^2-m+1=(m+1)^2-3(m+1)+3 .
\]
Because $3\mid m+1$, the first two terms are divisible by $9$ when $a\ge1$ (indeed $(m+1)^2$ is divisible by $9$ and $3(m+1)$ is divisible by $9$), so $m^2-m+1\equiv3\pmod 9$. A power of $3$ that is $3$ modulo $9$ must equal $3$ itself. Hence
\[
m^2-m+1=3\iff m^2-m-2=0\iff (m-2)(m+1)=0\iff m=2 .
\]
Then $3^n=2^3+1=9$, so $n=2$. The only pair is $\boxed{(m,n)=(2,2)}$.

(If $0$ is counted as a natural number, $(m,n)=(0,0)$ also works, since $3^0-0^3=1$.)
