Write $n=10a+b$ with $b$ the units digit. Then
\[
n^2=100a^2+20ab+b^2 .
\]
The term $100a^2$ does not affect the tens digit, and $20ab$ adds an even number of tens. So the tens digit of $n^2$ has the same parity as the tens digit of $b^2$. Among $b^2=0,1,4,9,16,25,36,49,64,81$, the tens digit is odd only for $16$ and $36$, that is, for $b=4$ and $b=6$.

So the tens digit of $n^2$ is odd exactly when $n$ ends in $4$ or $6$. In $\{1,\dots,20\}$ these are $4,6,14,16$ (with squares $16,36,196,256$), giving $\boxed{4}$ integers.
