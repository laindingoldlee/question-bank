\textbf{(a)} Let $B_k$ be the balance after $k$ periods, so $B_0=P$. At the end of period $k+1$ the interest $\dfrac {r}{n} B_k$ is added, so
\[B_{k+1}=B_k+\dfrac {r}{n}B_k=\left(1+\dfrac {r}{n}\right)B_k .\]
We prove by induction on $k$ that
\[B_k=P\left(1+\dfrac {r}{n}\right)^k .\]
It holds for $k=0$. If it holds for $k$, then $B_{k+1}=\left(1+\dfrac {r}{n}\right)P\left(1+\dfrac {r}{n}\right)^k=P\left(1+\dfrac {r}{n}\right)^{k+1}$. So it holds for all $k\ge0$; in other words, the balances form a geometric sequence with ratio $1+\dfrac {r}{n}$. After $t$ years there have been $nt$ periods, so $A=B_{nt}=P\left(1+\dfrac {r}{n}\right)^{nt}$.

\textbf{(b)} Here $P=1000$, $r=0.06$, $n=4$, $t=2$:
\[A=1000\left(1+\dfrac{0.06}{4}\right)^{8}=1000\cdot1.015^8\approx\boxed{\$1126.49}.\]
