Since $8^2+15^2=64+225=289=17^2$, triangles $ABD$ and $ACD$ are right-angled at $A$ (converse of Pythagoras). So $AD\perp AB$ and $AD\perp AC$, and $AD$ is perpendicular to the plane $ABC$: it is the height of the tetrahedron over the base $ABC$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0), B=(-2.2,-1.6), C=(3.2,-0.9), D=(0,3.2);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.8)); draw(A--D,red+linewidth(0.9)); draw(B--D--C,linewidth(0.8));
pair M=(B+C)/2; draw(A--M,blue+dashed);
real m=0.22; draw(A+m*unit(D-A)--A+m*unit(D-A)+m*unit(B-A)--A+m*unit(B-A));
draw(A+m*unit(D-A)--A+m*unit(D-A)+m*unit(C-A)--A+m*unit(C-A));
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);dot(D);dot(M);
label("$A$",A,1.3*dir(-40));label("$B$",B,SW);label("$C$",C,SE);label("$D$",D,N);label("$M$",M,S);
label("$8$",(A+D)/2,W,red);label("$15$",(A+B)/2,NW);label("$15$",(A+C)/2,NE);
label("$17$",(B+D)/2,W);label("$17$",(C+D)/2,NE);label("$12$",(A+M)/2,W,blue);
\end{asy}

In the isosceles base triangle, the median $AM$ to $BC$ is an altitude, with $AM=\sqrt{15^2-9^2}=12$, so $[ABC]=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot18\cdot12=108$. Therefore
\[V=\dfrac{1}{3}\cdot108\cdot8=\boxed{288}\ \text{cm}^3.\]
