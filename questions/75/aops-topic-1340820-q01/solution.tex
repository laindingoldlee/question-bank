\textbf{The claim is false.} For $n=2$, $z=1$ the left side is $2$ and the right side is $\dfrac{4-1}{2}=\dfrac{3}{2}$. (In fact the left side is a multiple of $n$, and for $n=3$ the right side is not even always an integer: $z=2$ gives $12$ on the left and $13$ on the right.)

\textbf{Correct formula.} Let $S=n+n^2+\dots+n^z$. Then $nS=n^2+\dots+n^z+n^{z+1}$, and subtracting,
\[
nS-S=n^{z+1}-n\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{S=\dfrac{n^{z+1}-n}{n-1}}\qquad(n\ne1).
\]
For $n=3$ this is $\dfrac{3^{z+1}-3}{2}$, which differs from the student's $\dfrac{3^{z+1}-1}{2}$ by exactly $1$: the student's formula is the sum $1+3+\dots+3^z$ including the term $3^0$, and only for $n=3$.
