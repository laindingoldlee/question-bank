Completing the squares, the condition is
\[
(x-2)^2+(y-3)^2=1,
\]
the circle with centre $(2,3)$ and radius $1$. On it $1\le x\le3$, so $\dfrac {y}{x}$ is defined, and $\dfrac {y}{x}=k$ means that $(x,y)$ lies on the line $y=kx$ through the origin.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real k1=2-2*sqrt(3)/3,k2=2+2*sqrt(3)/3;pair Z=(2,3);
draw((-0.3,0)--(4.3,0),gray,Arrow(TeXHead));draw((0,-0.3)--(0,5.3),gray,Arrow(TeXHead));
label("$x$",(4.3,0),E,gray);label("$y$",(0,5.3),N,gray);
draw(circle(Z,1),linewidth(0.9));dot(Z);label("$(2,3)$",Z,N);
draw((0,0)--(4.3,4.3*k1),red);draw((0,0)--(5.1/k2,5.1),blue+dashed);
pair T=(2+3*k1)/(1+k1^2)*(1,k1);dot(T,red);draw(Z--T,red+dashed);
label("$y=kx$",(4.3,4.3*k1),E,red);label("$T$",T,SE,red);label("$O$",(0,0),SW);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

So we need the least slope $k$ of a line through $O$ that meets the circle. As $k$ decreases the line turns clockwise, and the extreme lines are tangent to the circle: their distance from $(2,3)$ equals $1$:
\[
\dfrac{|2k-3|}{\sqrt{k^2+1}}=1\iff4k^2-12k+9=k^2+1\iff3k^2-12k+8=0\iff k=2\pm\dfrac{2\sqrt3}{3}.
\]
The line meets the circle exactly for $k$ between these values (distance at most $1$ means $3k^2-12k+8\le0$). The minimum is at the lower tangent (solid red, touching at $T$; the upper tangent, dashed, gives the maximum):
\[
\left(\dfrac {y}{x}\right)_{\min}=2-\dfrac{2\sqrt3}{3}=\boxed{\dfrac{6-2\sqrt3}{3}}.
\]
