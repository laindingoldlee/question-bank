The product of three consecutive integers is a binomial coefficient:
\[(k-1)k(k+1)=6\cdot\dfrac{(k+1)k(k-1)}{6}=6\dbinom{k+1}{3},\]
and $(k-1)k(k+1)=k^3-k$. Hence $k^3=6\dbinom{k+1}3+k$ for every $k\ge1$ (for $k=1$ both terms check: $1=0+1$). Summing,
\[\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^3=6\sum_{k=1}^{n}\dbinom{k+1}{3}+\sum_{k=1}^nk .\]
In the first sum, $\dbinom{k+1}3=0$ for $k=1$, so by the hockey stick identity with $r=3$,
\[\sum_{k=1}^{n}\dbinom{k+1}{3}=\dbinom33+\dbinom43+\cdots+\dbinom{n+1}3=\dbinom{n+2}{4}\qquad(n\ge2),\]
and this also holds for $n=1$ (both sides are $0$). Therefore
\[\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^3=6\cdot\dfrac{(n+2)(n+1)n(n-1)}{24}+\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}=\dfrac{n(n+1)}{4}\left((n+2)(n-1)+2\right)=\dfrac{n(n+1)}{4}\left(n^2+n\right),\]
that is,
\[1^3+2^3+\cdots+n^3=\boxed{\left(\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}\right)^2}.\]
