\textbf{(a)} A ratio $a:b$ compares two quantities of the same kind measured in the same unit, and is a pure number. Kilograms and dollars are different kinds of quantity, so ``$32$ kg $:$ \$$36$'' is not a ratio in this sense (it is a rate, a price of \$$36$ for $32$ kg). With this convention the statement as written is not a valid proportion, which is why the book marks it false. The numbers themselves agree: $\dfrac{32}{36}=\dfrac{8}{9}$.

\textbf{(b)} Compare like with like:
\[
32\text{ kg}:8\text{ kg}=\$36:\$9,
\]
since both ratios equal $4:1$. So $32$ kg, $8$ kg, \$$36$ and \$$9$ are in proportion: $8$ kg costs \$$9$ at the same price per kilogram as $32$ kg for \$$36$.
