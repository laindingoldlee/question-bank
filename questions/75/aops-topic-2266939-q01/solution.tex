The divisors of $n$ come in pairs $d$ and $\dfrac {n}{d}$. The two members of a pair are different unless $d=\dfrac {n}{d}$, that is, $d^2=n$. So $n$ has an odd number of divisors exactly when $n$ is a perfect square (then $\sqrt n$ is the one unpaired divisor).

The perfect squares less than $1000$ are $1^2,2^2,\dots,31^2=961$ (since $32^2=1024$). The answer is $\boxed{31}$.
