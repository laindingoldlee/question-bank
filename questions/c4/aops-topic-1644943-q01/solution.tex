\textbf{Basic values.} With $x=y$: $f(1)=1$. With $x=1$: $f\!\left(\dfrac{1}{y}\right)=\dfrac{1}{f(y)}$. Applying the equation to $x$ and $\dfrac{1}{y}$ (note $x$ divided by $\dfrac{1}{y}$ is $xy$),
\[
f(xy)=\dfrac{f(x)}{f\left(\dfrac{1}{y}\right)}=f(x)f(y)\qquad\text{for all }x,y>0 .
\]

\textbf{A differential equation.} Fix $x>0$ and differentiate $f(xy)=f(x)f(y)$ with respect to $y$: $x\,f'(xy)=f(x)f'(y)$. At $y=1$ this gives $x\,f'(x)=f(x)f'(1)=f(x)$. Therefore
\[
\left(\dfrac{f(x)}{x}\right)'=\dfrac{x f'(x)-f(x)}{x^2}=0,
\]
so $\dfrac{f(x)}{x}$ is constant on $(0,\infty)$; since $f(1)=1$, the constant is $1$. Hence $\boxed{f(x)=x}$.

\textbf{Check.} $f\!\left(\dfrac {x}{y}\right)=\dfrac {x}{y}=\dfrac{f(x)}{f(y)}$, $f(y)\ne0$ and $f'(1)=1$.
