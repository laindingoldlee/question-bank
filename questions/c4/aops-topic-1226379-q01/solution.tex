Let $r_1,\dots,r_k$ ($k=\varphi(n)$) be the residues in $\{1,\dots,n\}$ coprime to $n$. Consider $ar_1,\dots,ar_k$ modulo $n$. Each is coprime to $n$, and they are distinct: $ar_i\equiv ar_j$ gives $n\mid a(r_i-r_j)$, hence $n\mid r_i-r_j$ since $\gcd(a,n)=1$, so $i=j$. Therefore $ar_1,\dots,ar_k$ is a rearrangement of $r_1,\dots,r_k$ modulo $n$, and
\[a^kr_1r_2\cdots r_k\equiv r_1r_2\cdots r_k\pmod n.\]
The product $r_1\cdots r_k$ is coprime to $n$, so it can be cancelled: $a^{\varphi(n)}\equiv1\pmod n$. $\blacksquare$
