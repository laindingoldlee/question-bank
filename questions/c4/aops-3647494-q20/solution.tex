View the integers as a 12-cycle. The five cyclic gaps between selected vertices must be at least two; their sum is 12. The excess above two is therefore two. One gap four and four gaps two necessarily produce a pair at distance six. With two gaps three, they cannot be adjacent in the five-gap cycle, again because of a distance-six pair. The only remaining cyclic pattern is $(3,2,3,2,2)$, and checking its partial sums shows it has no distance-six pair. Its rotations yield 12 distinct subsets, for example the rotations of $\{1,3,5,8,10\}$. Thus the answer is $\boxed{12}$.
