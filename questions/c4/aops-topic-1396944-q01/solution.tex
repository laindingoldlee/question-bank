\textbf{(a)} She needs $\dfrac{300}{4}=75$ of each colour, so she must buy $75-32=43$ red, $75-26=49$ green, $75-9=66$ yellow and $75$ blue pickets.

\textbf{(b)} Red and green are fewer than $50$, so they cost full price; yellow and blue are at least $60$, so they cost $0.25\cdot0.7=\$0.175$ each:
\[
43(0.25)+49(0.25)+66(0.175)+75(0.175)=10.75+12.25+11.55+13.125=\$47.675,
\]
which is $\boxed{\$47.675}$, about $\$47.68$.

\textbf{(c)} Buying $50$ of a colour costs $50\cdot0.25\cdot0.8=\$10.00$, which is less than $43$ red at full price ($\$10.75$) and $49$ green at full price ($\$12.25$); buying $60$ costs $\$10.50$, more than $\$10.00$. For yellow and blue she already gets the best discount, and buying more only costs more. So the cheapest plan is $50$ red, $50$ green, $66$ yellow and $75$ blue:
\[
10.00+10.00+11.55+13.125=\boxed{\$44.675}\approx\$44.68 .
\]
