Let the roots be $\alpha$ and $m\alpha$, with $\alpha\ne0$. By Vieta's formulas,
\[\alpha(1+m)=p,\qquad m\alpha^2=q .\]
Then
\[p^2-2q=\alpha^2(1+m)^2-2m\alpha^2=\alpha^2\left(1+m^2\right),\]
which is nonzero, and
\[\dfrac{q}{p^2-2q}=\dfrac{m\alpha^2}{\alpha^2\left(1+m^2\right)}=\dfrac{m}{1+m^2}.\]
The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(A)}}$. (Check with $m=2$: the roots $1,2$ give $p=3$, $q=2$, and $\dfrac{2}{9-4}=\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{2}{1+4}$.)
