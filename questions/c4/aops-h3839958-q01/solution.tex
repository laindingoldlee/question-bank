The losing positions, up to exchanging piles, are $(3k,3k)$ and $(3k+1,3k+2)$ for $k\ge0$. No legal move connects two such positions: changing one coordinate cannot do so because each fixed coordinate has a unique partner in the list, and reducing both by one leaves the list. Conversely let $a\le b$ be outside the list. If $a\equiv0\pmod3$, reduce $b$ to $a$. If $a\equiv1\pmod3$ and $b>a$, reduce $b$ to $a+1$; if $a=b$, remove one from each. If $a\equiv2\pmod3$, reduce the larger pile to $a-1$. Each move reaches a listed position. This proves the classification by induction on total coins. Since $2010$ is divisible by three, the initial position is losing; the second player always responds by restoring the list and eventually takes the last coin.
