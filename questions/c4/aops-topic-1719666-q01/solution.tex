Let the sides be $a<b<11$ (scalene, and $11$ is the longest). The triangle inequality reduces to $a+b>11$ (the other two inequalities hold automatically since $11$ is the longest side).

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(11,0),C=(3.4,5.2);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
label("$11$",(A+B)/2,S);label("$a$",(A+C)/2,NW);label("$b$",(B+C)/2,NE);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

For each $b$, $a$ runs over $12-b\le a\le b-1$:
\[
b=10:\ a=2,\dots,9\ (8);\quad b=9:\ 3,\dots,8\ (6);\quad b=8:\ 4,\dots,7\ (4);\quad b=7:\ 5,6\ (2);\quad b\le6:\ \text{none}.
\]
Total $8+6+4+2=\boxed{20}$.
