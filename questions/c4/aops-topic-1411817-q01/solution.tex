The probabilities of being unlucky at 6, 7, 8 and 9 PM are
\[
\dfrac{1}{4},\qquad \dfrac{1}{4}\cdot\dfrac{3}{2}=\dfrac{3}{8},\qquad \dfrac{3}{8}\cdot\dfrac{3}{2}=\dfrac{9}{16},\qquad \dfrac{9}{16}\cdot\dfrac{3}{2}=\dfrac{27}{32}.
\]

\textbf{(a)} John is still awake after 9:00 PM only if he is lucky at all four of these hours. Each probability is conditional on the earlier hours, so we multiply:
\[
\dfrac{3}{4}\cdot\dfrac{5}{8}\cdot\dfrac{7}{16}\cdot\dfrac{5}{32}=\dfrac{3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot5}{4\cdot8\cdot16\cdot32}=\boxed{\dfrac{525}{16384}}\approx0.032 .
\]
So he falls asleep by 9:00 PM with probability $1-\dfrac{525}{16384}=\dfrac{15859}{16384}$.

\textbf{(b)} At 10:00 PM the rule would give $\dfrac{27}{32}\cdot\dfrac{3}{2}=\dfrac{81}{64}>1$, which is not a probability. The only sensible reading is that from then on he is unlucky with certainty: if he is still awake at 10:00 PM, he falls asleep then. Therefore John is certain to fall asleep during the night; the probability is $\boxed{1}$, and the night never gets past 10:00 PM.
