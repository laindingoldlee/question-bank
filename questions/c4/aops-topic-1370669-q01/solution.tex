Write $n=2^e m$ with $m$ odd. The odd divisors of $n$ are the divisors of $m$, so there are $d(m)$ of them. The even divisors are $2^j d'$ with $1\le j\le e$ and $d'\mid m$, so there are $e\,d(m)$ of them. They are equal exactly when $e=1$, i.e.\ when $n$ is twice an odd number, $n\equiv2\pmod4$.

The two-digit numbers $\equiv2\pmod4$ are $10,14,18,\dots,98$, and there are $\dfrac{98-10}{4}+1=\boxed{23}$ of them.
