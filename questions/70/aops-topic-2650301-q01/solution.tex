Number the coins $1,\dots,n$ from left to right.

\emph{$n$ even: Peter wins.} Call the odd-numbered coins class $O$ and the even-numbered coins class $E$. When an even number of coins remain and the end coins have positions of opposite parity, the player to move can take an end coin of either class. Suppose Peter has just taken a coin of his chosen class. Then both end coins belong to the other class, so Dave must take one of them, and Peter again has an end coin of his class. By induction, Peter can take exactly all the coins of one class. He chooses the class with the smaller (or equal) total, so his total is at most Dave's.

\emph{$n$ odd: Dave wins.} After Peter's first coin, of value $v>0$, an even number of coins remains and Dave moves first. By the same strategy, Dave takes exactly the class with the smaller (or equal) total, $T_1$, and Peter gets the other class, $T_2\ge T_1$. Then Peter's total $v+T_2>T_1$, so Dave wins whatever the values.

So Peter has a winning strategy for every assignment exactly when $n$ is even. There are $\boxed{1010}$ even $n$ with $2\le n\le2020$.
