The smallest sum of $N$ distinct elements is $1+2+\dots+N=\dfrac{N(N+1)}{2}$ and the largest is $(14-N)+\dots+13=\dfrac{N(27-N)}{2}$.

\textbf{Every integer in between occurs.} Start from $\{1,\dots,N\}$. As long as the chosen set is not $\{14-N,\dots,13\}$, some chosen element $a$ has $a+1$ not chosen and $a+1\le13$ (take the largest chosen element that is not part of the top block). Replacing $a$ by $a+1$ raises the sum by exactly $1$. Repeating, we pass through every integer from the minimum to the maximum.

Hence
\[
S(N)=\dfrac{N(27-N)}{2}-\dfrac{N(N+1)}{2}+1=N(13-N)+1 .
\]
The product $N(13-N)$ is largest when $N$ is as close as possible to $\dfrac{13}{2}$, i.e.\ for $N=6$ and $N=7$, both giving $S=43$. The requested sum is $6+7=\boxed{13}$.
