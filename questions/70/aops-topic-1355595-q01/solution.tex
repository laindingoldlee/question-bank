For $x>0$, $2^x>x^8\iff g(x)=x\ln2-8\ln x>0$. Since $g'(x)=\ln2-\dfrac{8}{x}$, $g$ decreases on $\left(0,\dfrac{8}{\ln2}\right)\approx(0,11.5)$ and increases afterwards, so $g$ has at most one zero beyond $11.5$, and $g>0$ after it.

Now $2^{43}=8\,796\,093\,022\,208<43^8=11\,688\,200\,277\,601$ (so $g(43)<0$) and $2^{44}=17\,592\,186\,044\,416>44^8=14\,048\,223\,625\,216$ (so $g(44)>0$). So the zero $x_0$ lies between $43$ and $44$ (in fact $x_0\approx43.56$), and for $x\ge12$ we have $2^x>x^8$ exactly when $x>x_0$.

The condition ``for every real $x>n$'' needs $n\ge x_0$, so the least natural $n$ is $\boxed{44}$. ($n=43$ fails, e.g.\ at $x=43.5$.) If only integer $x$ were meant, $n=43$ would work, since $2^{x}>x^8$ for all integers $x\ge44$.
