\textbf{(a)} By the quadratic formula, $x^2-x-1=0$ has roots $\dfrac{1\pm\sqrt5}{2}$, which are $\varphi$ and $\psi$; the $\sqrt5$ is the square root of the discriminant $1+4=5$.

Where the formula comes from: a geometric sequence $x^n$ ($x\ne0$) satisfies $x^{n+1}=x^n+x^{n-1}$ exactly when $x^2=x+1$, i.e. $x=\varphi$ or $x=\psi$. Any combination $A\varphi^n+B\psi^n$ then satisfies the Fibonacci recurrence too. To match $F_0=0$ and $F_1=1$ we need $A+B=0$ and $A\varphi+B\psi=1$, so $A(\varphi-\psi)=1$. Since $\varphi-\psi=\sqrt5$, this gives $A=\dfrac1{\sqrt5}$ and $B=-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt5}$. So the $\sqrt5$ is $\varphi-\psi$, the difference of the two roots.

\textbf{(b)} Let $G_n=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt5}\left(\varphi^n-\psi^n\right)$. Then $G_0=0$ and $G_1=\dfrac{\varphi-\psi}{\sqrt5}=1$. For $n\ge1$, using $\varphi^2=\varphi+1$ and $\psi^2=\psi+1$,
\[G_{n+1}-G_n-G_{n-1}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt5}\left(\varphi^{n-1}\left(\varphi^2-\varphi-1\right)-\psi^{n-1}\left(\psi^2-\psi-1\right)\right)=0.\]
So $(G_n)$ satisfies the same recurrence with the same first two terms as $(F_n)$. By strong induction, $G_n=F_n$ for all $n\ge0$: if $G_{n-1}=F_{n-1}$ and $G_n=F_n$, then $G_{n+1}=G_n+G_{n-1}=F_n+F_{n-1}=F_{n+1}$. $\blacksquare$
