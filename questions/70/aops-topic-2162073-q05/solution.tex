List the children from older to younger. The four equally likely possibilities are BB, BG, GB, GG. The information ``at least one is a girl'' rules out BB and leaves three equally likely cases, BG, GB and GG. In two of them there is a boy, so the probability is
\[\boxed{\dfrac{2}{3}}.\]
\emph{Remark.} If instead a \emph{particular} child (say the older one) were known to be a girl, the other child would be a boy with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$. That is why the information must be stated carefully.
