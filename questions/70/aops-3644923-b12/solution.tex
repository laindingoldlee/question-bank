Rotate an equilateral triangle through an angle between zero and $30^\circ$ and consider its horizontal and vertical spans. Scaling into the square is limited by the larger span. That maximum is minimised when the two spans are equal, which occurs with vertices $(0,0),(1,2-\sqrt3),(2-\sqrt3,1)$. The side squared is $1+(2-\sqrt3)^2=8-4\sqrt3$, so its area is $(\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4})(8-4\sqrt3)=\boxed{2\sqrt3-3}$. This span comparison also proves maximality rather than merely exhibiting a candidate.

import graph;import geometry;usepackage("amsmath");size(7cm);
real t=2-sqrt(3);draw(box((0,0),(1,1)));draw((0,0)--(1,t)--(t,1)--cycle,blue);
\end{asy}
