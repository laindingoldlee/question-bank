The cosine series converges absolutely and uniformly, so its cube may be expanded and integrated term by term. Product-to-sum shows that for positive integers $a,b,c$,
\[\int_0^{2\pi}\cos(ax)\cos(bx)\cos(cx)\,dx\]
is $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ when one of $a,b,c$ is the sum of the other two, and is zero otherwise. There are three choices for which frequency is the largest. Consequently
\[\int_0^{2\pi}F(x)^3\,dx=\dfrac{3\pi}{2}
\sum_{a,b\ge1}\dfrac{1}{a^2b^2(a+b)^2}=\dfrac{3\pi}{2}\Omega.\]
Using the supplied quadratic formula and substituting $x=2\pi t$,
\[\int_0^{2\pi}F(x)^3\,dx
=2\pi^7\int_0^1\left(t^2-t+\dfrac{1}{6}\right)^3dt
=2\pi^7\cdot\dfrac{1}{3780}.\]
The last integral follows by expanding the cubic polynomial (or centering with $u=t-\dfrac{1}{2}$). Thus
\[\Omega=\dfrac{2}{3\pi}\cdot\dfrac{\pi^7}{1890}
=\boxed{\dfrac{\pi^6}{2835}}.\]
