Clearing denominators, the equation is equivalent to
\[
kb+ka=ab\iff ab-ka-kb+k^2=k^2\iff(a-k)(b-k)=k^2 .
\]
For a solution in positive integers, $\dfrac{1}{a}<\dfrac{1}{a}+\dfrac{1}{b}=\dfrac{1}{k}$, so $a>k$, and similarly $b>k$. Hence $u=a-k$ and $v=b-k$ are positive integers with $uv=k^2$; that is, $u$ is a positive divisor of $k^2$ and $v=\dfrac{k^2}{u}$.

Conversely, for every positive divisor $u$ of $k^2$, the pair $a=k+u$, $b=k+\dfrac{k^2}{u}$ consists of positive integers satisfying $(a-k)(b-k)=k^2$, hence the equation. Different divisors $u$ give different pairs (different $a$).

So $(a,b)\mapsto a-k$ is a bijection between the solutions and the positive divisors of $k^2$, and the number of solutions is $\boxed{d(k^2)}$. For example, $k=2$: $d(4)=3$, and the solutions are $(3,6)$, $(4,4)$, $(6,3)$.
