Extend the slanted sides to the apex $P$ of the full cone. The figure shows the cross-section through the axis: the frustum appears as the trapezoid $ABCD$, with bottom radius $OB=8$, top radius $TC=6$ and height $OT=2$. The removed top cone is similar to the full cone with ratio $TC:OB=6:8=3:4$, so if the small cone has height $h=PT$, then $\dfrac{h}{h+2}=\dfrac{3}{4}$, giving $h=6$ and a full height $PO=8$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair P=(0,8), A=(-8,0), B=(8,0), C=(6,2), D=(-6,2), O=(0,0), T=(0,2);
draw(D--P--C,gray+dashed);
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(O--T,blue+dashed); draw(T--P,gray+dashed);
dot(O);dot(T);dot(P);
label("$8$",(4,0),S); label("$6$",(3,2),N); label("$2$",(0,1),W,blue);
label("$6$",(0,5),W,gray);
label("$P$",P,N); label("$A$",A,SW); label("$B$",B,SE); label("$C$",C,NE); label("$D$",D,NW);
label("$O$",O,S); label("$T$",T,NW);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The frustum is the full cone minus the small cone:
\[
V=\dfrac{1}{3}\pi\cdot8^2\cdot8-\dfrac{1}{3}\pi\cdot6^2\cdot6=\dfrac{512\pi-216\pi}{3}=\boxed{\dfrac{296\pi}{3}} .
\]
This agrees with the frustum formula $V=\dfrac{\pi h}{3}\left(R^2+Rr+r^2\right)=\dfrac{2\pi}{3}(64+48+36)=\dfrac{296\pi}{3}$.
