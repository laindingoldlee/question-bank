Let $s=x+y+z$. Subtracting the equations in pairs and factoring:
\[
(z^2-y^2)+x(z-y)=(z-y)s=9,\qquad (z^2-x^2)+y(z-x)=(z-x)s=18,\qquad (y^2-x^2)+z(y-x)=(y-x)s=9 .
\]
In particular $s\ne0$. Put $u=\dfrac{9}{s}$; then $z-y=u$ and $y-x=u$, so $x=y-u$, $z=y+u$ and $s=3y$, that is $y=\dfrac {s}{3}$ and $yu=\dfrac {s}{3}\cdot\dfrac{9}{s}=3$.

Substituting $x=y-u$ into the first equation:
\[
(y-u)^2+y^2+y(y-u)=3y^2-3yu+u^2=3y^2-9+u^2=19 ,
\]
so $3y^2+u^2=28$ with $u=\dfrac{3}{y}$:
\[
3y^2+\dfrac{9}{y^2}=28\iff3y^4-28y^2+9=0\iff(y^2-9)(3y^2-1)=0 .
\]
\begin{itemize}
\item $y^2=9$: $y=3$, $u=1$ gives $(x,y,z)=(2,3,4)$, and $y=-3$, $u=-1$ gives $(-2,-3,-4)$.
\item $y^2=\dfrac{1}{3}$: $y=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{3}$, $u=3\sqrt3$ gives $\left(-\dfrac{8\sqrt3}{3},\dfrac{\sqrt3}{3},\dfrac{10\sqrt3}{3}\right)$, and the negatives.
\end{itemize}
Each of these satisfies the first equation and the three difference equations, hence all three equations; for example $\dfrac{64}{3}+\dfrac{100}{3}-\dfrac{80}{3}=28$ and $\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{100}{3}+\dfrac{10}{3}=37$. So the solutions are
\[
\boxed{(x,y,z)=\pm(2,3,4)\quad\text{and}\quad(x,y,z)=\pm\left(-\dfrac{8\sqrt3}{3},\dfrac{\sqrt3}{3},\dfrac{10\sqrt3}{3}\right)}.
\]
