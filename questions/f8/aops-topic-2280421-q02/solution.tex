Divide both sides by $n!\,k!$. The inequality becomes
\[\dfrac{n!}{k!}<\dfrac{(2n-k)!}{n!},\qquad\text{that is,}\qquad (k+1)(k+2)\cdots n<(n+1)(n+2)\cdots(2n-k).\]
Both sides are products of $n-k\ge1$ positive integers. Every factor on the left is at most $n$, and every factor on the right is at least $n+1$. Hence the left product is strictly smaller than the right one, which proves the inequality.
