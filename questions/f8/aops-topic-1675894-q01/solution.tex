\textbf{The error.} The square root sign means the \emph{nonnegative} square root. For every real $t$,
\[
\sqrt{t^2}=|t| ,
\]
which equals $t$ only when $t\ge0$. Here $t=1-2=-1<0$, so $\sqrt{(1-2)^2}=|-1|=1$, not $1-2$. The rule ``square, then take the square root, and you get $x$ back'' holds only for $x\ge0$; in general one gets $|x|$. (With $x+1$ and $x$ in place of $2$ and $1$, the same mistake gives $\sqrt{(-x)^2}=|-x|=x$, not $-x$.)

\textbf{No positive number equals its negation.} If $x=-x$, then adding $x$ to both sides gives $2x=0$, so $x=0$. Hence for $x>0$ we always have $-x\ne x$. $\blacksquare$
