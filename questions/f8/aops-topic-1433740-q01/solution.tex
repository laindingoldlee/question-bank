Factor:
\[
m^5-m=m(m^4-1)=m(m-1)(m+1)(m^2+1)=(m-1)m(m+1)(m^2+1).
\]
Consider $m$ modulo $5$.
\begin{itemize}
\item If $m\equiv0$, $1$ or $4\pmod5$, then one of $m$, $m-1$, $m+1$ is divisible by $5$.
\item If $m\equiv2$ or $3\pmod5$, then $m^2\equiv4\pmod5$, so $m^2+1\equiv0\pmod5$.
\end{itemize}
In every case one factor is divisible by $5$, so $5\mid m^5-m$. $\blacksquare$

(This is the case $p=5$ of Fermat's little theorem, $m^p\equiv m\pmod p$.)
