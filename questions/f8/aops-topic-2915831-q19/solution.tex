No nonzero digit can be green: the two-digit repdigit $dd=11d$ is composite for $d\ge2$, and 11 excludes green 1. Digits 2,3,5,7 cannot be blue by their one-digit primality, so they are red. Digit 1 cannot be blue because 11 is prime, so it is red too. The only blue candidates are 0,4,6,8,9. If 9 is red, any subset of 4,6,8 may be blue; all all-blue positive numbers are then even and greater than 2, so valid. This gives $2^3$ choices. If 9 is blue,8 cannot be blue because 89 is prime, and 4 cannot be blue because 449 is prime (trial division by primes through 19 verifies it). Digit 6 may be red or blue; every all-blue positive number then uses only 0,6,9 and is a multiple of 3 greater than 3. This gives 2 choices. Digit 0 independently has three choices in both cases: an all-green numeral could then only denote 0, which is not composite. Therefore the count is $3(8+2)=\boxed{30}$.
