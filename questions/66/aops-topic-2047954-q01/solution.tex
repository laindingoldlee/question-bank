The sets $A-B$ and $B-C$ are disjoint, since the first has no element of $B$ and the second lies inside $B$. The symmetric difference of disjoint sets is their union, so the hypothesis says
\[(A-B)\cup(B-C)=(A-B)\cup(B-A).\]
Intersect both sides with $B$. Since $(A-B)\cap B=\varnothing$, $(B-C)\cap B=B-C$ and $(B-A)\cap B=B-A$, we get
\[B-C=B-A .\]
Within $B$, these are the complements of $B\cap C$ and of $B\cap A$, respectively: $B\cap C=B-(B-C)$ and $B\cap A=B-(B-A)$. Hence
\[A\cap B=B-(B-A)=B-(B-C)=B\cap C .\qquad\blacksquare\]
