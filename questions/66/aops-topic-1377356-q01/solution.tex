The water inside the hose fills a cylinder of diameter $2$ inches, i.e. radius $1$ inch $=\dfrac{1}{12}$ foot and length $100$ feet.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(7cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real L=6, r=0.45;
draw(ellipse((0,0),0.18,r),linewidth(0.9));
draw((0,r)--(L,r),linewidth(0.9)); draw((0,-r)--(L,-r),linewidth(0.9));
draw(shift((L,0))*scale(0.18,r)*arc((0,0),1,-90,90),linewidth(0.9));
draw(shift((L,0))*scale(0.18,r)*arc((0,0),1,90,270),dashed);
draw((-0.55,-r)--(-0.55,r),blue,Arrows(4)); label("$2$ in",(-0.55,0),W,blue);
draw((0,-r-0.35)--(L,-r-0.35),Arrows(5)); label("$100$ ft $=1200$ in",(L/2,-r-0.35),S);
label("(not to scale)",(L/2,r+0.05),N,fontsize(8pt));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Working in feet,
\[
V=\pi r^2h=\pi\left(\dfrac{1}{12}\right)^2\cdot100=\dfrac{100\pi}{144}=\dfrac{25\pi}{36}\approx2.18\text{ cubic feet}.
\]
To the nearest whole number the hose holds $\boxed{2}$ cubic feet. (Mixing units is the usual trap: the radius must be converted to feet before it is squared.)
