\textbf{A lemma.} For positive integers $u,v$ we have $(u-1)(v-1)\ge0$, that is, $u+v\le uv+1$. Applying this repeatedly, the sum of $k$ positive integers is at most their product plus $k-1$: if $x_1+\cdots+x_k\le x_1\cdots x_k+(k-1)$, then
\[x_1+\cdots+x_k+x_{k+1}\le\bigl(x_1\cdots x_k+x_{k+1}\bigr)+(k-1)\le x_1\cdots x_kx_{k+1}+1+(k-1).\]

\textbf{The bound.} Let $P=S\cdot E\cdot C\cdot T\cdot I\cdot O$ and $s=S+E+C+T+I+O$. The condition is $NP=N+s$, that is,
\[N(P-1)=s.\]
If $P=1$, all six numbers are $1$ and the condition reads $N=N+6$, which is impossible. So $P\ge2$, and by the lemma $s\le P+5$. Hence
\[N=\dfrac{s}{P-1}\le\dfrac{P+5}{P-1}=1+\dfrac{6}{P-1}\le1+6=7.\]

\textbf{Equality.} Take $S=2$, $E=C=T=I=O=1$ and $N=7$: the product is $14$ and the sum is $2+5+7=14$. So the maximum is $\boxed{7}$.
