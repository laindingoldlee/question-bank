John gets the candy exactly when none of the stopping events happens.

\textbf{Fear.} The stopping probabilities are $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\dfrac{1}{3}\cdot\dfrac{3}{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}$, so he gets through both $250$-ft halves without fear with probability
\[\left(1-\dfrac{1}{3}\right)\left(1-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)=\dfrac{2}{3}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{1}{3} .\]

\textbf{Obstacles.} The stopping probabilities in the five $100$-ft stretches are
\[\dfrac{1}{8},\quad\dfrac{1}{20},\quad\dfrac{1}{50},\quad\dfrac{1}{125},\quad\dfrac{2}{625},\]
so he avoids all obstacles with probability
\[\dfrac{7}{8}\cdot\dfrac{19}{20}\cdot\dfrac{49}{50}\cdot\dfrac{124}{125}\cdot\dfrac{623}{625}=\dfrac{503451284}{625000000}\approx0.8055 .\]

By independence, the probability that he gets the candy is
\[\dfrac{1}{3}\cdot\dfrac{503451284}{625000000}=\boxed{\dfrac{125862821}{468750000}}\approx0.2685 .\]
