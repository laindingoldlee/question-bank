We need divisibility by $4$ and by $11$.

\emph{By $11$:} the alternating sum of the digits, starting from the units digit, is
\[b-a+1-2+0-2+0-2+0-2=b-a-7.\]
It must be a multiple of $11$. Since $-16\le b-a-7\le2$, we need $b-a-7=0$ or $b-a-7=-11$, that is $b=a+7$ or $b=a-4$.

\emph{By $4$:} the two-digit number $\overline{ab}$ must be divisible by $4$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $b=a+7$: the candidates $07,18,29$ are not divisible by $4$.
\item $b=a-4$: the candidates are $40,51,62,73,84,95$, and only $40$ and $84$ are divisible by $4$.
\end{itemize}
So $(a,b)=(4,0)$ or $(8,4)$, and the largest value of $ab$ is $8\cdot4=\boxed{32}$.
