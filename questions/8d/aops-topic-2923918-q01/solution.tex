Take $P=(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)$ and $Q=(\cos\beta,\sin\beta)$ on the unit circle; the angle $POQ$ is $\alpha-\beta$.
\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair O=(0,0),A=dir(20),B=dir(130);draw(circle(O,1));draw((-1.3,0)--(1.3,0),Arrow);draw((0,-1.3)--(0,1.3),Arrow);draw(O--A);draw(O--B);draw(A--B,dashed);
label("$P$",A,E);label("$Q$",B,NW);label("$O$",O,SW);
\end{asy}

By the distance formula, $PQ^2=(\cos\alpha-\cos\beta)^2+(\sin\alpha-\sin\beta)^2=2-2(\cos\alpha\cos\beta+\sin\alpha\sin\beta)$. By the law of cosines in triangle $POQ$, $PQ^2=2-2\cos(\alpha-\beta)$. Comparing gives the first identity. Replacing $\beta$ by $-\beta$ (with $\cos(-\beta)=\cos\beta$ and $\sin(-\beta)=-\sin\beta$) gives the second. $\blacksquare$
