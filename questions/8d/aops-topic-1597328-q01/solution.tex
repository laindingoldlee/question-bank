Take the edge length to be $1$; let $\theta=\angle C_1AC$. Here $AC$ is a diagonal of the face $ABCD$, and $AC_1$ is a space diagonal (blue and red in the sketch):

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real s=3; pair d=(0.8,1.6);
pair A=(0,0),B=(s,0),C=B+d,D=d,A1=A+(0,s),B1=B+(0,s),C1=C+(0,s),D1=D+(0,s);
draw(A--B--C--C1--D1--A1--cycle,linewidth(0.9));draw(B--B1,linewidth(0.9));draw(A1--B1--C1,linewidth(0.9));
draw(A--D--C,dashed);draw(D--D1,dashed);
draw(A--C,blue+linewidth(1));draw(A--C1,red+linewidth(1));
draw(arc(A,0.9,degrees(C),degrees(C1)),linewidth(0.8));
label("$\theta$",A+1.25*dir((degrees(C)+degrees(C1))/2));
label("$A$",A,SW);label("$B$",B,SE);label("$C$",C,E);label("$D$",D,NW);
label("$A_1$",A1,W);label("$B_1$",B1,SE);label("$C_1$",C1,NE);label("$D_1$",D1,N);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The edge $CC_1$ is perpendicular to the whole face $ABCD$, so it is perpendicular to $AC$, and triangle $ACC_1$ has a right angle at $C$. Its legs are
\[
AC=\sqrt{1^2+1^2}=\sqrt2,\qquad CC_1=1,
\]
and its hypotenuse is $AC_1=\sqrt{2+1}=\sqrt3$. Drawn in its true shape:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real s=2; pair A=(0,0),C=(s*sqrt(2),0),C1=(s*sqrt(2),s);
draw(A--C--C1--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(C+(-0.22,0)--C+(-0.22,0.22)--C+(0,0.22),linewidth(0.6));
draw(arc(A,0.75,0,degrees(C1)),linewidth(0.8));
label("$\theta$",A+1.05*dir(degrees(C1)/2));
label("$A$",A,W);label("$C$",C,SE);label("$C_1$",C1,NE);
label("$\sqrt2$",(A+C)/2,S);label("$1$",(C+C1)/2,E);label("$\sqrt3$",(A+C1)/2,NW);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Therefore
\[
\tan\theta=\dfrac{CC_1}{AC}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \cos\theta=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{\sqrt6}{3},\quad \sin\theta=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{3}.
\]
So
\[
\angle C_1AC=\boxed{\arctan\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}}\approx35.26^\circ .
\]
It is not a "nice" angle such as $30^\circ$ or $45^\circ$: it lies strictly between them, since $\tan30^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{3}<\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}<1=\tan45^\circ$.
