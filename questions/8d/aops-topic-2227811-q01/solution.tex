A perfect power $a^b$ with $b\ge2$ is a square, a cube, or a fifth power, sixth power, and so on; an exponent $b$ that is even gives a square, and $b$ a multiple of $3$ gives a cube, so it is enough to look at squares, cubes, fifth powers and seventh powers ($2^7=128>125$ already).
\begin{itemize}
\item Squares: $2^2,3^2,\ldots,11^2$, that is $4,9,\ldots,121$: $10$ numbers.
\item Cubes: $8,27,64,125$: $4$ numbers, of which $64=8^2$ is already counted: $3$ new.
\item Fifth powers: $32$ ($3^5=243$ is too large): $1$ new.
\end{itemize}
In total $10+3+1=\boxed{14}$ perfect powers.
