Let a chord through $P$ be at distance $h$ from $O$; then its length is $2\sqrt{100-h^2}$. Since the chord passes through $P$, $0\le h\le OP=6$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair O=(0,0), Q=(10,0), P=(6,0);
draw(circle(O,10),linewidth(0.9));
draw((-10,0)--Q,blue);
draw((6,-8)--(6,8),red+linewidth(0.9));
pair d=dir(60); real t=-dot(P,d)+sqrt(dot(P,d)^2-36+100);
real t2=-dot(P,d)-sqrt(dot(P,d)^2-36+100);
draw((P+t2*d)--(P+t*d),heavygreen+dashed);
draw(O--(6,8),gray(0.5));
draw((6-0.6,0)--(6-0.6,0.6)--(6,0.6));
dot(O); dot(P); dot(Q);
label("$O$",O,SW); label("$P$",P,SE); label("$Q$",Q,E);
label("$6$",(3,0),S,blue); label("$8$",(6,4),E,red); label("$10$",(3,4),NW,gray(0.4));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\begin{itemize}
\item The longest chord through $P$ is the diameter ($h=0$), of length $20$.
\item The shortest is the chord perpendicular to $OP$ ($h=6$), of length $2\sqrt{100-36}=16$.
\end{itemize}
As the chord turns about $P$, its length varies continuously between $16$ and $20$. Each length strictly between $16$ and $20$ is achieved by exactly two chords, symmetric about line $OP$, while lengths $16$ and $20$ are each achieved by exactly one chord. The integer lengths are $16,17,18,19,20$, giving
\[
1+2+2+2+1=\boxed{8}
\]
chords.
