Let $d=\gcd(a,b)\ge1$ and write $a=da'$, $b=db'$ with integers $a',b'$. Dividing $ax+by=d$ by $d$ gives
\[
a'x+b'y=1 .
\]
Let $g$ be any positive common divisor of $x$ and $y$. Then $g$ divides $a'x+b'y=1$, so $g=1$. Hence $\gcd(x,y)=1$. $\blacksquare$

(The same argument shows $\gcd(a',b')=1$, i.e. $\gcd\left(\dfrac{a}{d},\dfrac{b}{d}\right)=1$. The converse fails: $\gcd(x,y)=1$ does not force $ax+by=\gcd(a,b)$, e.g. $a=b=1$, $x=y=1$.)
