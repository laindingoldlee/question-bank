Call the sum $S$. For $n\ge1$ the ratio of the $(n+1)$st term to the $n$th term is $\dfrac{n+1}{8n}\le\dfrac{2}{8}\le\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the terms shrink at least as fast as a geometric series with ratio $\dfrac{1}{2}$; the series converges and we may manipulate it term by term.

Dividing by $8$ shifts every term one place to the right:
\[S=\dfrac{1}{8}+\dfrac{2}{64}+\dfrac{3}{512}+\cdots,\qquad \dfrac{S}{8}=\phantom{\dfrac{1}{8}+{}}\dfrac{1}{64}+\dfrac{2}{512}+\cdots.\]
Subtracting, each numerator drops by $1$:
\[S-\dfrac{S}{8}=\dfrac{1}{8}+\dfrac{1}{64}+\dfrac{1}{512}+\cdots=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{8}}{1-\dfrac{1}{8}}=\dfrac{1}{7}.\]
So $\dfrac{7}{8}S=\dfrac{1}{7}$ and
\[S=\dfrac{8}{7^2}=\boxed{\dfrac{8}{49}}.\]
