\emph{Counterexample.} $N=4$, $K=2$: $\dbinom42=6$, but $\dbinom41+\dbinom31=4+3=7$.

\emph{Pascal's rule.} Count the $K$-element subsets of $\{1,\dots,N\}$ according to whether they contain $N$. Those not containing $N$ are $K$-subsets of $\{1,\dots,N-1\}$: $\dbinom{N-1}K$ of them. Those containing $N$ are determined by their other $K-1$ elements, chosen from $\{1,\dots,N-1\}$: $\dbinom{N-1}{K-1}$. Adding gives $\dbinom NK$. $\blacksquare$
