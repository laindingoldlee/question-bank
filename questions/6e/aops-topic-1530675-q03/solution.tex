\textbf{(a)} Glitch $n$ affects cell $m$ exactly when $m$ is a multiple of $n$. So cell $m$ is affected by the glitches $n$ that are the $\boxed{\text{positive divisors of }m}$ (all of them are at most $m\le2000$).

\textbf{(b)} Cell $m$ starts locked and its lock is turned $d(m)$ times, where $d(m)$ is the number of positive divisors of $m$. It ends unlocked exactly when $d(m)$ is odd. The divisors of $m$ pair up as $k\leftrightarrow\dfrac {m}{k}$, and a divisor is paired with itself only when $k^2=m$; so $d(m)$ is odd exactly when $m$ is a perfect square. The perfect squares from $1$ to $2000$ are $1^2,2^2,\dots,44^2$ (as $44^2=1936\le2000<2025=45^2$), so $\boxed{44}$ prisoners awaken to unlocked cells.
