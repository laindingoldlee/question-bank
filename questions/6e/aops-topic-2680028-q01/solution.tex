When the $j$-th line is drawn, it meets each earlier line at most once inside the circle, so it is cut into at most $j$ pieces, and each piece splits one region into two. Hence $k$ lines give at most
\[1+(1+2+\cdots+k)=1+\dfrac{k(k+1)}{2}\]
regions. For $k=27$ this is $379<407$, so $27$ lines are not enough. For $k=28$ it is $1+406=407$, and this is attained: take $28$ lines through points very close to the centre with $28$ different directions and no three concurrent, so that every pair crosses inside the circle. So the answer is $\boxed{28}$.
