The centres are pairwise $6+6=12$ apart, so they form an equilateral triangle of side $12$. The fourth circle is its circumcircle, with radius $\dfrac{12}{\sqrt3}=4\sqrt3$ (two thirds of the altitude $6\sqrt3$).

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0), B=(12,0), C=(6,6*sqrt(3)); pair G=(A+B+C)/3;
draw(circle(A,6)); draw(circle(B,6)); draw(circle(C,6));
draw(circle(G,4*sqrt(3)),red+linewidth(0.9));
draw(A--B--C--cycle,dashed); draw(G--A,red);
dot(A); dot(B); dot(C); dot(G,red);
label("$12$",(3,0),S); label("radius $4\sqrt3$",(-4.8,13.5),red+fontsize(9pt));
\end{asy}

Its area is $\pi(4\sqrt3)^2=48\pi$, and the three circles have total area $3\cdot36\pi=108\pi$. The ratio is
\[\dfrac{48\pi}{108\pi}=\boxed{\dfrac49}.\]
