With this numbering, row $n$ consists of $\dbinom{n-1}{0},\dbinom{n-1}{1},\dots,\dbinom{n-1}{n-1}$. So the third number in row $20$ is
\[
\dbinom{19}{2}=\dfrac{19\cdot18}{2}=\boxed{171}.
\]
Why $\dfrac{(1+18)\cdot18}{2}$: every entry is the sum of the two entries above it. The second entries of rows $2,3,4,\dots$ are $1,2,3,\dots$ (row $n$ has second entry $n-1$), so the third entry grows by $1,2,3,\dots$ from row to row:
\[
\text{row }3:\ 1,\quad\text{row }4:\ 1+2,\quad\text{row }5:\ 1+2+3,\quad\dots,\quad\text{row }20:\ 1+2+\cdots+18 .
\]
And $1+2+\cdots+18=\dfrac{(1+18)\cdot18}{2}$ by pairing the first and last terms. (If the top row were called row $0$, row $20$ would give $\dbinom{20}{2}=190$ instead.)
