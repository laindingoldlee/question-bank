Write $T=(a\cos\theta,b\sin\theta)$, with both sine and cosine nonzero. A normal direction to the tangent is $(\dfrac{\cos\theta}{a},\dfrac{\sin\theta}{b})$. If the intersection is $\lambda T$, then for some $t$,
\[
\lambda(a\cos\theta,b\sin\theta)
=(c,0)+t(\dfrac{\cos\theta}{a},\dfrac{\sin\theta}{b}).
\]
The second coordinate gives $t=\lambda b^2$. The first then gives
\[
\lambda a\cos\theta=c+\lambda\dfrac{b^2}{a}\cos\theta,
\]
so $\lambda c^2\dfrac{\cos\theta}{a}=c$ and $\lambda a\cos\theta=\dfrac{a^2}{c}$. This is precisely the $x$-coordinate of the corresponding directrix, proving the claim.
