\textbf{(a)} The product is odd exactly when all three numbers are odd, which has probability $\left(\dfrac12\right)^3=\dfrac18$. So the product is even with probability $\boxed{\dfrac78}$.

\textbf{(b)} The product is a multiple of $10$ exactly when at least one number is $5$ and at least one is even. Let $A$ be ``no $5$'' and $B$ ``no even number''. Then $P(A)=\left(\dfrac56\right)^3=\dfrac{125}{216}$, $P(B)=\dfrac{27}{216}$, and $A\cap B$ means every number is $1$ or $3$: $P(A\cap B)=\left(\dfrac26\right)^3=\dfrac{8}{216}$. By inclusion--exclusion,
\[P(\text{multiple of }10)=1-P(A\cup B)=1-\dfrac{125+27-8}{216}=\dfrac{72}{216}=\boxed{\dfrac13}.\]
