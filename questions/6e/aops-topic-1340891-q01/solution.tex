Factor: $16=2^4$, $34=2\cdot17$, $75=3\cdot5^2$, $21=3\cdot7$, $13$. So the product without $n$ is $2^5\cdot5^2\cdot m$ with $m=3^2\cdot7\cdot13\cdot17$ coprime to $10$. Ending in (at least) six zeros means divisibility by $10^6=2^6\cdot5^6$, so $n$ must supply at least one more factor $2$ and four more factors $5$: $n$ is a multiple of $2\cdot5^4=1250$. The smallest is $\boxed{1250}$, and then the product $2^6\cdot5^6\cdot m$ ends in exactly six zeros.
