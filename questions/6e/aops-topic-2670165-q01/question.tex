A circle is determined by two points (its centre and a point on it), and an ellipse by three points (its two foci and a point on it). A hyperbola with foci $F_1\ne F_2$ is the set of points $X$ with $\left|XF_1-XF_2\right|=2a$, for a constant $a$ with $0<2a<F_1F_2$. Prove that for every point $P$ that is not on the perpendicular bisector of $F_1F_2$, and not on line $F_1F_2$ unless it lies strictly between $F_1$ and $F_2$, there is exactly one hyperbola with foci $F_1$, $F_2$ passing through $P$. (So a hyperbola is also determined by three points: its foci and one point on it.)
