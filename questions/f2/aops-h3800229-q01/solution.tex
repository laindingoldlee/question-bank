An upright triangle of side $k$ has $\binom{12-k}{2}$ possible positions, for $1\le k\le10$. Summing gives $\binom{12}{3}=220$ upright triangles: equivalently, each placement leaves three nonnegative boundary gaps totalling $10-k$.
An inverted triangle of side $k$ requires a supporting upright triangle of side at least $2k$. It therefore has $\binom{12-2k}{2}$ positions, for $1\le k\le5$. These counts are $45,28,15,6,1$, totalling $95$. No other orientations can have all sides on the three grid-line directions. Thus the total is $\boxed{220+95=315}$.
