Going once around a closed fence, the number of gaps equals the number of posts, so there are $36$ gaps of $5$ m and the perimeter is $180$ m. Since there are posts at the corners, each side is a whole number of gaps: if the sides have $p$ and $q$ gaps ($p,q\ge1$), then $2(p+q)=36$, so $p+q=18$, and the area is
\[
5p\cdot5q=25pq .
\]
With $p+q=18$, the product $pq=81-(p-9)^2$ is largest for $p=q=9$. So the best plot is a $45$ m by $45$ m square:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real s=45;
draw((0,0)--(s,0)--(s,s)--(0,s)--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
for(int i=0;i<9;++i){ dot((5*i,0)); dot((s,5*i)); dot((s-5*i,s)); dot((0,s-5*i)); }
label("$45$ m",(s/2,0),2*S);label("$45$ m",(0,s/2),2*W);
draw((0,-6)--(5,-6),Arrows(TeXHead));label("$5$ m",(2.5,-6),S);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The greatest possible area is $25\cdot81=\boxed{2025}$ square metres. (If the plot had to be a non-square rectangle, the best would be $40$ m by $50$ m, area $2000$ square metres.)
