Let the numbers be $C$, $D$, $E$. Then $C+D=6$ and $DE=10$. With distinct numbers from $1$ to $9$, $DE=10$ gives $(D,E)=(2,5)$ or $(5,2)$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $D=2,E=5$: then $C=4$, and $4,2,5$ are distinct.
\item $D=5,E=2$: then $C=1$, and $1,5,2$ are distinct.
\end{itemize}
Everett's number is $5$ or $2$, and the sum of the possibilities is $\boxed{7}$.
