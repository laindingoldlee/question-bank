Let $T(q)$ be the largest number of ordered candidates searchable in $q$ questions with a required equality answer. The first guess covers itself and leaves two intervals with at most $T(q-1)$ candidates, so $T(q)=1+2T(q-1)$ and $T(0)=0$. Thus $T(q)=2^q-1$. Nine questions cover only $511$ numbers, but ten cover $1023$. Repeatedly guessing a middle remaining candidate therefore guarantees equality within $\boxed{10}$ questions; nine cannot suffice.
