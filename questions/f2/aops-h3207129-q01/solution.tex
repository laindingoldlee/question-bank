For distinct divisors $d_1,d_2,d_3$, put $u=\dfrac{n}{d_1}$, $v=\dfrac{n}{d_2}$, $w=\dfrac{n}{d_3}$ and reorder so $u<v<w$. They are positive integers and
\[
\dfrac{1}{u}+\dfrac{1}{v}+\dfrac{1}{w}=1.
\]
We must have $u\ge2$. If $u\ge3$, the sum is at most $\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{1}{5}<1$, so $u=2$. Then $\dfrac{1}{v}+\dfrac{1}{w}=\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $v\ge3$. If $v\ge4$, the sum is at most $\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{1}{5}<\dfrac{1}{2}$. Thus $v=3,w=6$.
Consequently $n$ is nice exactly when $6\mid n$, in which case $\dfrac{n}{2},\dfrac{n}{3},\dfrac{n}{6}$ work. The count is $\boxed{\lfloor\dfrac{999}{6}\rfloor=166}$.
