Write \(x=10a+b\) and \(y=10b+a\), where \(a,b\) are digits with \(b\ne0\) (so that \(y\) has two digits) and \(a>b\) (since \(m>0\)). Then
\[
x^2-y^2=(x+y)(x-y)=11(a+b)\cdot9(a-b)=99(a+b)(a-b)=m^2 .
\]
Since \(99=9\cdot11\), the factor \(11\) must divide \((a+b)(a-b)\). As \(1\le a-b\le8\), we need \(11\mid a+b\), and \(a+b\le17\) forces \(a+b=11\). Then \(m^2=9\cdot121\cdot(a-b)\), so \(a-b\) is a perfect square. Since \(a+b=11\) is odd, \(a-b\) is odd, so \(a-b\in\{1,9\}\); but \(a-b=9\) together with \(a+b=11\) gives \(a=10\), impossible. So \(a-b=1\), \(a=6\), \(b=5\), giving \(x=65\), \(y=56\), \(m=\sqrt{99\cdot11}=33\). Therefore
\[
x+y+m=65+56+33=\boxed{154}.
\]
