The sequence of flavours taken is a uniformly random arrangement of $AAABBBCCC$; there are $\dfrac{9!}{3!\,3!\,3!}=1680$ arrangements, all equally likely.

The event happens exactly when the third flavour first appears before any flavour has been taken three times. Then, before that moment, two flavours have appeared, each once or twice. Let $Z$ be the third flavour ($3$ choices) and $X,Y$ the other two.
\begin{itemize}
\item One $X$ and one $Y$ first ($2$ orders), then $Z$; the remaining $2,2,2$ muffins in $\dfrac{6!}{2!\,2!\,2!}=90$ orders: $180$.
\item One of $X,Y$ once and the other twice ($2\cdot3=6$ ways), then $Z$; the remaining $2,1,2$ in $\dfrac{5!}{2!\,1!\,2!}=30$ orders: $180$.
\item $X$ twice and $Y$ twice ($\dfrac{4!}{2!\,2!}=6$ orders), then $Z$; the remaining $1,1,2$ in $\dfrac{4!}{2!}=12$ orders: $72$.
\end{itemize}
This gives $3(180+180+72)=1296$ favourable arrangements, so the probability is
\[\dfrac{1296}{1680}=\boxed{\dfrac{27}{35}},\]
which agrees with the poster's answer.
