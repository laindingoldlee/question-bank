Let $k=\dfrac{u}{v}$ in lowest terms and $N=\lfloor k\rfloor$. Since $N(N+1)>58$, we have $N\ge8$. Put $w=u-Nv$, so $0<w<v$. The equation becomes
\[
Nu w=58v^2.
\]
Both $u$ and $w$ are coprime to $v$, hence $v^2\mid N$. Write $N=t v^2$; then $tuw=58$ and $u=tv^3+w$. Since $u>8$, the only possible divisors $u$ of $58$ are $29$ and $58$.

If $u=29$, then $(t,w)=(1,2)$ or $(2,1)$. The former gives $v^3=27$, so $v=3$; the latter would give $v^3=14$, impossible. If $u=58$, then $t=w=1$ gives $v^3=57$, also impossible. Therefore $k=\dfrac{29}{3}$, which indeed has floor $9$ and satisfies the equation. The answer is $\boxed{32}$.
