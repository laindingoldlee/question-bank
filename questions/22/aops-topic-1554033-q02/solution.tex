In one revolution the wheel rolls without slipping through a distance equal to its circumference, $\pi d=19.1\pi$ inches:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(8cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real d=19.1, L=pi*d;
draw((-d/2-3,0)--(L+d/2+3,0),linewidth(0.8));
draw(circle((0,d/2),d/2),linewidth(1.2));draw(circle((L,d/2),d/2),dashed);
draw((-d/2,d/2)--(d/2,d/2),Arrows(TeXHead));label("$19.1$ in",(0,d/2),N);
dot((0,0));dot((L,0));
draw((0,-3)--(L,-3),Arrows(TeXHead));
label("one revolution: $19.1\pi$ in",(L/2,-3),S);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Ten miles is $10\cdot5280\cdot12=633600$ inches. The number of revolutions is
\[
\dfrac{633600}{19.1\pi}\approx\dfrac{633600}{60.0044}\approx10559.2,
\]
which is $\boxed{10559}$ to the nearest whole number.
