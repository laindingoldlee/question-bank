Let $t=\sqrt{x^2+1}$. Then $t\ge1>0$ and $x^2+2=t^2+1$, so
\[\dfrac{x^2+2}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}=\dfrac{t^2+1}{t}=t+\dfrac{1}{t} .\]
Now
\[t+\dfrac{1}{t}-2=\dfrac{t^2-2t+1}{t}=\dfrac{(t-1)^2}{t}\ge0,\]
since $t>0$. (This is also AM-GM for $t$ and $\dfrac{1}{t}$.) Hence the expression is at least $2$, with equality exactly when $t=1$, that is, $x^2+1=1$, so $\boxed{x=0}$. $\blacksquare$
