Each factorial sum is divisible by $1320$, hence by $11$. If one index is at least $11$ and the other below $11$, the sum is not divisible by $11$. Thus each pair has either both indices below $11$ or both at least $11$. For indices $1$ through $10$, checking the factorial residues and sums gives exactly two unordered pairs divisible by $1320$: $(5,9)$, with sum $363000=1320\cdot275$, and $(9,10)$, with sum $3991680=1320\cdot3024$. A pair with both indices at least $11$ has sum divisible by $11!$. Two such sums have gcd larger than $1320$. Paired with the $(5,9)$ sum, the gcd would be divisible by $25$; paired with the $(9,10)$ sum, it would be divisible by $16$. Both contradict the prime exponents in $1320$. Therefore the two sums must come from the two different small pairs. Their gcd is indeed $1320$ because $\gcd(275,3024)=1$. Hence the product is $5\cdot9\cdot9\cdot10=\boxed{4050}$.
