\textbf{(a)} An expression is undefined exactly where its denominator is $0$; the numerator plays no role in the restrictions. Here $(2x+1)(3+x)=0$ when $x=-\dfrac{1}{2}$ or $x=-3$. So the restrictions are $x\ne-\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $x\ne-3$. (Both come from the denominator: the factor $3+x$ is zero at $x=-3$.)

\textbf{(b)} Since $x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3)$,
\[\dfrac{x^2-9}{(2x+1)(3+x)}=\dfrac{x-3}{2x+1},\qquad x\ne-3,\ x\ne-\dfrac{1}{2}.\]
The original expression at $x=-3$ is $\dfrac{0}{0}$, which is undefined; cancelling the common factor $x+3$ is valid only where that factor is nonzero, so the restriction $x\ne-3$ must be carried along with the simplified form.
