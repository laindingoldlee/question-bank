Let $S=a+b+c$. Subtracting pairs of equations gives
\[
S(x-y)=(a-b)t,\quad S(y-z)=(b-c)t,\quad S(z-x)=(c-a)t.
\]
(a) Here $S=3$ and all differences on the right vanish, so $x=y=z=1$. Substitution gives $t=0$. The unique solution is $\boxed{(x,y,z,t)=(1,1,1,0)}$.

(b) Put $D=a^2+b^2+c^2-ab-bc-ca=\dfrac{1}{2}((a-b)^2+(b-c)^2+(c-a)^2)>0$ and $e=ab+bc+ca$.
If $S=0$, pairwise distinctness in the displayed differences forces $t=0$. Summing the first three equations then gives $x^2+y^2+z^2-xy-yz-zx=0$, so $x=y=z$; their sum is zero. Thus only $(0,0,0,0)$ occurs.

For $S\ne0$, the differences and sum determine
\[
x=\dfrac{S}{3}+\dfrac{t(3a-S)}{3S},
\]
and the analogous formulas for $y,z$. Summing the original three equations and using the differences gives
\[
tS=x^2+y^2+z^2-xy-yz-zx=\dfrac{t^2D}{S^2}.
\]
Thus $t=0$ or $t=\dfrac{S^3}{D}$. The corresponding solutions are
\[
\boxed{\left(\dfrac{S}{3},\dfrac{S}{3},\dfrac{S}{3},0\right)}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{\left(\dfrac{S(Sa-e)}{D},\dfrac{S(Sb-e)}{D},\dfrac{S(Sc-e)}{D},\dfrac{S^3}{D}\right)}.
\]
Both satisfy the system: their three equation residuals have zero pairwise differences and zero sum, so each residual vanishes. These formulas coincide with the zero solution when $S=0$.
