Put $f(n)=k$. Since $4^{3k-1}\ge10^k$ for every $k\ge1$, we have $g(n)<3k$. Also $n\ge10^{k-1}\ge4^{k-1}$ gives $g(n)\ge k$. Thus only $g=k$ or $g=2k$ are possible. For $g=k$, intersect $[10^{k-1},10^k-1]$ with $[4^{k-1},4^k-1]$: the nonempty intervals are 1 through 3 and 10 through 15, giving 9 numbers. For $g=2k$, the corresponding intersections with $[4^{2k-1},4^{2k}-1]$ are 4 through 9 and 64 through 99, giving 42 numbers. For $k\ge3$ both intersections are empty: $10^{k-1}>4^k$ and $4^{2k-1}>10^k$, inequalities that only strengthen as $k$ grows. Total $\boxed{51}$.
