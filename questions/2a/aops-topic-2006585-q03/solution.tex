\textbf{Upper bound.} Divide the board into $16$ blocks of $2\times2$ squares. Any two squares in the same $2\times2$ block share an edge or a vertex, so each block contains at most one king. Hence $n\le16$.

\textbf{Construction.} Number the rows and columns $1$ to $8$ and place a king on every square whose row and column numbers are both odd. This gives $4\cdot4=16$ kings. Two different such squares differ by at least $2$ in their row number or in their column number, so they share neither an edge nor a vertex, and no two kings attack each other.

Therefore the maximum is $\boxed{16}$.
