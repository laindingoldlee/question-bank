By the order of operations, exponents are applied before the negation (which is multiplication by $-1$). The base of the power in $-5^2$ is $5$, not $-5$:
\[
-5^2=-(5^2)=-25 .
\]
In $(-5)^2$ the parentheses make $-5$ the base:
\[
(-5)^2=(-5)(-5)=25 .
\]
So $-5^2=\boxed{-25}$ and $(-5)^2=\boxed{25}$. The rule ``negative times negative is positive'' is still true; it applies to $(-5)(-5)$, but $-5^2$ means $-1\cdot5\cdot5$, which has only one negative factor.
