Let B(n) count partitions of n using powers of two; denominations above n are irrelevant. An odd n must use an odd number of one-cent coins, so removing one gives $B(2m+1)=B(2m)$. For even n, separate representations with at least two ones from those with no ones: $B(2m)=B(2m-2)+B(m)$, the latter obtained by halving every coin. Since $B(2)=2$ and $B(3)=2$, induction shows B(n) is even for every $n\ge2$. Thus $B(99)$ is $\boxed{\text{even}}$. All denominations needed are at most 64, as required.
