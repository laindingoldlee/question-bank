Let $b=p$ be the least prime divisor. The third divisor is either $p^2$ or a distinct prime $q>p$. Since $d\mid N$, the equation implies $d\mid bc$.
If $c=p^2$, the only divisor of $p^3$ larger than $p^2$ is $p^3$, so $d=p^3$ and $N=2p^3$. The least prime divisor must then be $p=2$, giving $N=16$, which works.
If $c=q$ is prime, the only divisor of $pq$ larger than $q$ is $pq$, so $d=pq$ and $N=2pq$. Again $p=2$. Since $4$ is a divisor and $q$ is the third divisor, $q<4$, hence $q=3$. But $N=12$ has fourth divisor four, not $pq=6$, a contradiction. Thus $\boxed{N=16}$ is the only possibility.
