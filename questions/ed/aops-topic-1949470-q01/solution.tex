There are $\binom83=56$ ways to choose three of the points, but a choice gives a triangle only if the three points are not collinear. So $56$ overcounts.

Which triples are collinear? Each side of the square contains three marked points (two corners and the midpoint), giving $4$ collinear triples. No other triple is collinear: every marked point lies on the boundary of the square, and a line that does not contain a side of the square meets the boundary of this convex figure in at most two points.

Hence the number of triangles is $56-4=\boxed{52}$. The student forgot that three points on the same side of the square do not form a triangle.
