Take $A=(-2,0)$, $B=(0,-1)$, $C=(2,0)$, $D=(0,3)$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(7cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(-3,0), B=(0,-1.5), C=(3,0), D=(0,4.5);
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(A--C,red+dashed); draw(B--D,blue+dashed);
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);dot(D);
label("$A(-2,0)$",A,W); label("$B(0,-1)$",B,S); label("$C(2,0)$",C,E); label("$D(0,3)$",D,N);
label("$AC=4$",(-1.6,0),N,red); label("$BD=4$",(0,1.3),E,blue);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\textbf{Equal diagonals.} $AC$ runs from $(-2,0)$ to $(2,0)$ and $BD$ from $(0,-1)$ to $(0,3)$, so $AC=BD=4$. The diagonals cross at the origin, which lies inside both of them, so $ABCD$ is convex.

\textbf{No parallel sides.} The slopes are
\[
AB:\ -\dfrac{1}{2},\qquad BC:\ \dfrac{1}{2},\qquad CD:\ -\dfrac{3}{2},\qquad DA:\ \dfrac{3}{2} ,
\]
so no two sides are parallel. Hence $ABCD$ is not a trapezoid (isosceles or otherwise), not a rectangle and not a square. (It is in fact a kite: $AB=CB=\sqrt5$ and $AD=CD=\sqrt{13}$.)

So congruent diagonals do not force a square, rectangle or isosceles trapezoid; the claim is \textbf{false}. In fact any two equal segments that cross at an interior point of each, at any angle, are the diagonals of such a quadrilateral; the claim becomes true only with an extra condition, for example that the diagonals bisect each other (then the quadrilateral is a rectangle).
