Fermat's Last Theorem says that \(x^n+y^n=z^n\) has no solutions in positive integers when \(n\ge 3\); it does not claim that every choice such as \(x=1,y=2,z=3\) must be a solution. Prove the following special case directly: if \(x,y,z,n\) are positive integers with \(n\ge z\), then \(x^n+y^n\ne z^n\).
