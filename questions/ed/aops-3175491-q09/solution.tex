Let half of angle $ABC$ be $\theta$. Put $B=(0,0),A=(6,0),C=(5\cos2\theta,5\sin2\theta)$. The angle-bisector theorem gives $AD:DC=6:5$, so $D=\dfrac{5A+6C}{11}$. Its projection on $AB$ is therefore $BE=\dfrac{30(1+\cos2\theta)}{11}=4$. Hence $\cos2\theta=\dfrac{7}{15}$. The cosine rule gives $AC^2=6^2+5^2-60(\dfrac{7}{15})=\boxed{33}$.
