Let $p,q,r$ be the roots of $x^3+ax^2+bx+c$, where $c\ne0$, and let
\[S=\dfrac pq+\dfrac qr+\dfrac rp,\qquad T=\dfrac qp+\dfrac rq+\dfrac pr.\]
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Show by an example that $S$ is not determined by $a,b,c$ alone (it depends on how the roots are labelled).
\item[(b)] Express $S+T$ and $ST$ in terms of $a,b,c$, and hence find a quadratic equation, with coefficients expressed in $a,b,c$, of which $S$ is a root.
\end{enumerate}
