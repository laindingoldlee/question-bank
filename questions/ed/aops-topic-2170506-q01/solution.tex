\textbf{Infinitely many triples.} Since $3^2+4^2=9+16=25=5^2$, multiplying by $k^2$ gives $(3k)^2+(4k)^2=(5k)^2$ for every positive integer $k$, and different $k$ give different triples.

\textbf{Infinitely many primitive triples.} For every positive integer $n$ let
\[a=2n+1,\qquad b=2n^2+2n,\qquad c=2n^2+2n+1 .\]
Then
\[a^2+b^2=\left(4n^2+4n+1\right)+\left(4n^4+8n^3+4n^2\right)=4n^4+8n^3+8n^2+4n+1=\left(2n^2+2n+1\right)^2=c^2 .\]
Since $c-b=1$, any common divisor of $b$ and $c$ divides $1$, so $\gcd(a,b,c)=1$. Different $n$ give different values of $a$, so there are infinitely many such triples: $(3,4,5)$, $(5,12,13)$, $(7,24,25)$, $(9,40,41)$, \ldots{} $\blacksquare$
