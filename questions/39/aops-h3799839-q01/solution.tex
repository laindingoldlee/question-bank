Let the base angles of isosceles triangle $ABC$ be $\alpha$, and let $\theta=\angle ABC=180^\circ-2\alpha$. Triangle $BCD$ is congruent to $ABC$, so $\angle CBD=\angle BDC=\alpha$. Triangles $ABD$ and $ACD$ both have two sides of the longer common length and a base of the shorter length; they are congruent. Write their base angles as $\delta$ and apex angles as $\gamma$, so $2\delta+\gamma=180^\circ$.
At $B$, $\theta=\delta+\alpha$, yielding $\delta=180^\circ-3\alpha$. At $A$, $\delta=\alpha+\gamma$, yielding $3\delta=180^\circ+\alpha$. Combining gives $3(180^\circ-3\alpha)=180^\circ+\alpha$, hence $\alpha=36^\circ$. Therefore $\boxed{\angle ABC=108^\circ}$.
