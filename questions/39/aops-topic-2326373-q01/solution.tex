\textbf{(b)} We prove the general identity
\[n^m-k^m=(n-k)\left(n^{m-1}+n^{m-2}k+\cdots+nk^{m-2}+k^{m-1}\right).\]
Multiplying out the right side, $n\cdot\left(n^{m-1}+\cdots+k^{m-1}\right)=n^m+n^{m-1}k+\cdots+nk^{m-1}$ and $k\cdot\left(n^{m-1}+\cdots+k^{m-1}\right)=n^{m-1}k+\cdots+nk^{m-1}+k^m$; subtracting, all terms cancel except $n^m-k^m$. The second factor is an integer, so $n-k$ divides $n^m-k^m$. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{(a)} This is the case $k=1$ of (b) (with $m$ renamed $k$): $n^k-1=(n-1)\left(n^{k-1}+\cdots+n+1\right)$, and $n-1\ne0$ because $n>1$. $\blacksquare$

\emph{Alternative for (a).} Since $n\equiv1\pmod{n-1}$, we get $n^k\equiv1^k=1\pmod{n-1}$.
