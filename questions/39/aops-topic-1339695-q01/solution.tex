Rotating the triangle about the altitude $AM$ sweeps out a solid cone: the altitude is the axis, the apex $A$ is the tip, and the half-side $MC$ sweeps out the circular base.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real r=3*sqrt(3), h=9;
pair A=(0,h), B=(-r,0), C=(r,0), M=(0,0);
draw(xscale(r)*yscale(1.4)*arc((0,0),1,180,360),linewidth(0.9));
draw(xscale(r)*yscale(1.4)*arc((0,0),1,0,180),gray+dashed);
draw(B--A--C,linewidth(0.9));
draw(A--M,red+dashed); draw(M--C,blue);
draw((0,0.8)--(0.8,0.8)--(0.8,0),red);
dot(A);dot(M);
label("$A$",A,N); label("$M$",M,SW);
label("$6\sqrt{3}$",(A+C)/2,NE); label("$9$",(0,4.5),W,red); label("$3\sqrt{3}$",(2.0,0),N,blue+fontsize(8pt));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The base radius is half the side, $r=3\sqrt3$, and the height is the altitude, $h=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\cdot6\sqrt3=9$; the slant height is the side $6\sqrt3$. Hence
\[
V=\dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2h=\dfrac{1}{3}\pi\cdot27\cdot9=\boxed{81\pi}.
\]
