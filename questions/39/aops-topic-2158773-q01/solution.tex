Let $g=\gcd(m,n)$.

\textbf{(a)} \emph{If there is a solution $x$:} then $m\mid x-a$ and $n\mid x-b$, so $g$ divides both $x-a$ and $x-b$, hence their difference $(x-b)-(x-a)=a-b$.

\emph{If $g\mid a-b$:} write $b-a=gk$. By B\'ezout's identity there are integers $u,v$ with $mu+nv=g$. Put $x=a+muk$. Then $x\equiv a\pmod m$, and
\[x-b=muk-(b-a)=muk-(mu+nv)k=-nvk,\]
which is divisible by $n$. So $x$ is a solution. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{(b)} Here $\gcd(4,6)=2$ does not divide $1-2=-1$, so there is \textbf{no solution}. (Directly: $x\equiv1\pmod4$ forces $x$ odd and $x\equiv2\pmod6$ forces $x$ even.)
