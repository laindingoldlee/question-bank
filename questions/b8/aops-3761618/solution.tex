For positive exponents, work modulo $8$ and $125$. The exponent $E=7^{67}$ exceeds $3$, so $6^E\equiv0\pmod8$. Euler\textquotesingle s theorem gives period dividing $100$ modulo $125$. Since $7^4\equiv1\pmod{100}$, $E\equiv7^3\equiv43\pmod{100}$. Repeated squaring gives $6^{43}\equiv41\pmod{125}$. The eight residues $41+125j$, $0\le j<8$, have exactly one divisible by $8$, namely $416$. Thus the last three digits are $\boxed{416}$.
