\textbf{Each round halves the circle.} The counting continues without interruption, and members alternately say odd and even numbers. In the first round $96$ members speak, the even-numbered ones leave, and $48$ remain: those who said $1,3,5,\dots,95$. Since $96$ is even, the second round again starts with an odd number ($97$) said by the member who said $1$, and again every second member leaves. The same happens while the number of members is even: $96\to48\to24\to12\to6\to3$.

\textbf{Who is left after these five rounds.} After round $1$ the members who said $1,3,5,\dots$ (numbers $\equiv1\pmod2$) remain; after round $2$, those who said $1,5,9,\dots$ ($\equiv1\pmod4$); after round $3$, $1\pmod8$; after round $4$, $1\pmod{16}$; after round $5$, those whose first-round numbers are $\equiv1\pmod{32}$, namely $1$, $33$, $65$.

\textbf{The last rounds with three members.} The counting continues with the member who said $1$ saying an odd number, then $33$'s member an even number (out), then $65$'s member odd, then $1$'s member even (out). Only the member who said $65$ remains.

The survivor said $\boxed{65}$ in the first round. (This is the Josephus problem with every second person removed: for $n=2^m+\ell$ with $0\le\ell<2^m$, the survivor is number $2\ell+1$; here $96=64+32$ gives $65$.) The answer $33$ in the post is not correct: the member who said $33$ is eliminated in the last stage.
