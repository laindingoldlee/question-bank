\textbf{(a)} Since $AB\parallel DC$, triangles $VAB$ and $VDC$ are similar (corresponding angles), with ratio $\dfrac{b_1}{b_2}$. Let $d$ be the distance from $V$ to line $AB$; then the distance from $V$ to line $DC$ is $d+h$, and the heights are in the same ratio:
\[
\dfrac{d}{d+h}=\dfrac{b_1}{b_2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b_2d=b_1d+b_1h\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d=\dfrac{b_1h}{b_2-b_1}.
\]
The trapezoid is triangle $VDC$ with triangle $VAB$ removed, so
\[
[ABCD]=\dfrac{1}{2}b_2(d+h)-\dfrac{1}{2}b_1d=\dfrac{1}{2}\big((b_2-b_1)d+b_2h\big)=\dfrac{1}{2}\big(b_1h+b_2h\big)=\dfrac{1}{2}h(b_1+b_2). \qquad\blacksquare
\]

\textbf{(b)} In a parallelogram $b_1=b_2$, the legs are parallel and never meet, so there is no point $V$ (and the formula for $d$ would divide by $b_2-b_1=0$). The area formula is still true there, as base times height, but it needs a different proof.
