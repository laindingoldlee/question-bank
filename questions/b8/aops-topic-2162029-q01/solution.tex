Write $\gamma=\angle ACB$ and let $\Gamma$ be the circumcircle of $ABC$.

\textbf{Two angles at $F$.} Since $\angle BEC=\angle BFC=90^\circ$, the points $B$, $C$, $E$, $F$ lie on the circle with diameter $BC$, so (exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral) $\angle AFE=\angle ACB=\gamma$. Similarly $A$, $F$, $D$, $C$ lie on the circle with diameter $AC$, so $\angle BFD=\gamma$, and hence $\angle AFD=180^\circ-\gamma$. Consequently, any ray from $F$ along the line $FE$ or along the line $FD$ makes an angle $\gamma$ or $180^\circ-\gamma$ with the ray $FA$, and the sine of that angle is $\sin\gamma$.

\textbf{The two possible positions of $P$.} The point $F$ lies on side $AB$, inside $\Gamma$, so the line $EF$ meets $\Gamma$ at two points, one on each side of $F$. Since $E$ is on the same side of line $AB$ as $C$, one of them (beyond $E$) is on the same side of $AB$ as $C$; for it $\angle APB=\angle ACB=\gamma$ (Case 1). The other (beyond $F$) is on the opposite side, and for it $\angle APB=180^\circ-\gamma$ (Case 2).

\textbf{The circle $\omega$ through $A$, $F$, $P$.} Since $F$ lies between $A$ and $B$, the power of $B$ with respect to $\omega$ is $BF\cdot BA>0$, so $B$ is outside $\omega$, and the line $BP$ meets $\omega$ again at a point $Q'$ on the ray $BP$ with
\[BP\cdot BQ'=BF\cdot BA .\]
Then $\dfrac{BF}{BP}=\dfrac{BQ'}{BA}$, and the triangles $BFQ'$ and $BPA$ share the angle at $B$, so they are similar (SAS) and
\[\angle BFQ'=\angle BPA .\]
\textbf{$Q'$ lies on line $DF$, so $Q'=Q$.} The point $Q'$, on the ray from $B$ through $P$, is on the same side of $AB$ as $P$.
\begin{itemize}
\item Case 1: $\angle BFQ'=\angle BPA=\gamma$, and $Q'$ is on the side of $C$. The ray $FD$ also makes the angle $\angle BFD=\gamma$ with $FB$ on that side, so $Q'$ is on ray $FD$.
\item Case 2: $\angle BFQ'=180^\circ-\gamma$, and $Q'$ is on the side opposite to $C$. The ray opposite to $FD$ makes the angle $180^\circ-\gamma$ with $FB$ on that side, so $Q'$ is on that ray.
\end{itemize}
In both cases $Q'$ lies on line $DF$ and on line $BP$, so $Q'=Q$. Thus $A$, $F$, $P$, $Q$ all lie on $\omega$ (shown dashed in both sketches).

\textbf{Conclusion.} Let $\rho$ be the radius of $\omega$. Applying the extended law of sines in the triangles $AFP$ and $AFQ$, both inscribed in $\omega$,
\[AP=2\rho\sin\angle AFP,\qquad AQ=2\rho\sin\angle AFQ .\]
Since $P$ is on line $FE$ and $Q$ is on line $FD$, both sines equal $\sin\gamma$. Hence $AP=AQ$. $\blacksquare$
