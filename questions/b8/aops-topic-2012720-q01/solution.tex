Measure positions in blocks east of the house. The first block is not random, so after one block Gracie is at position $1$. The remaining seven blocks are decided by seven independent fair flips, so each sequence of seven moves has probability $\dfrac{1}{2^7}=\dfrac{1}{128}$.

She stops after exactly eight blocks when, during moves $2$ to $7$, her position stays at least $1$, and move $8$ takes her from position $1$ to $0$. So moves $2$ to $7$ form a six-step walk from $1$ back to $1$ that never goes below $1$, followed by a west step.

A six-step walk from $1$ to $1$ that never drops below $1$ is, after shifting down by $1$, a sequence of three up-steps and three down-steps whose running total is never negative. These are counted by the Catalan number $C_3=5$; explicitly, writing $U$ for east and $D$ for west, they are
\[UUUDDD,\quad UUDUDD,\quad UUDDUD,\quad UDUUDD,\quad UDUDUD.\]
Hence the probability is
\[\boxed{\dfrac{5}{128}}.\]
