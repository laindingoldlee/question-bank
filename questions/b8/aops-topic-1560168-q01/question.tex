A security key is a string of $6$ digits (repeats allowed, leading zeros allowed). You type a fixed key, and then a random key is generated, each of its $6$ digits chosen independently and uniformly from $0,1,\dots,9$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] For a fixed position $n$ ($1\le n\le6$), what is the probability that the $n$th digit of the random key equals the $n$th digit of your key?
\item[(b)] What is the probability that exactly one of the six positions matches?
\item[(c)] A student suggests that the answer to (a) is $\dfrac{1}{60}$ or $\dfrac{6\cdot9^5}{10^6}$. Explain what is wrong with each suggestion.
\end{enumerate}
