A positive integer with digit sum two is either $2\cdot10^a$ or $10^a+10^b$ with $a>b$. Divisibility by eleven excludes the first form and requires $a-b$ odd in the second. Write $a-b=2k+1$. Then $n=\dfrac{10^b(10^{2k+1}+1)}{11}$ has alternating digits $90\cdots901$: exactly $k$ nines and one one. Its digit sum is $9k+1=100$, so $k=11$. Therefore $n$ has $\boxed{12}$ nonzero digits.
