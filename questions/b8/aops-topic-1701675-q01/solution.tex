Let the distance be $d$ feet. The speed of light is $c=\dfrac{3\times10^8}{0.3048}\approx9.8425\times10^8$ feet per second. The flash is seen after $\dfrac {d}{c}$ seconds and the thunder is heard after $\dfrac{d}{1088}$ seconds, so
\[
\dfrac{d}{1088}-\dfrac {d}{c}=10\ \Longrightarrow\ d=\dfrac{10\cdot1088\,c}{c-1088}=10880\cdot\dfrac{c}{c-1088}.
\]
Since $\dfrac{c}{c-1088}=1+\dfrac{1088}{c-1088}\approx1+1.105\times10^{-6}$,
\[
d\approx10880+0.012=\boxed{10880.012\text{ feet}\approx2.06\text{ miles}}.
\]
Ignoring the travel time of the light would give $10880$ feet, so the correction is only about $0.012$ feet (about $4$ millimetres): the light's travel time, about $1.1\times10^{-5}$ seconds, is negligible.
