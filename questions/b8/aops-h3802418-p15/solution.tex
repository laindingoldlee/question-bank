Place the unique multiple of three, namely $3$, in a fixed position of the cyclic list. The other residues are two $1$s and two $2$s. The edges touching $3$ contribute zero, leaving the three products along the four remaining positions. A pair of equal residues contributes $1$ modulo $3$; unequal residues contribute $-1$. If $t$ of these three neighbouring pairs differ, their sum is $3-2t\equiv t\pmod3$. Thus $t=0$ or $3$. The first is impossible with two of each residue, so the residues must alternate. There are two alternating patterns and $2!2!$ assignments of the actual digits, giving $8$ lists with $3$ in the fixed position. Multiplying by its five possible positions gives $\boxed{40}$.
