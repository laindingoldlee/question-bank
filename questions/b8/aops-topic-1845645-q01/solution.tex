Divide by $2$: we must show $\dfrac{1}{a}+\dfrac{1}{b}+\dfrac{1}{c}+q\ge6$, where $q=ab+bc+ca>0$. Note $\dfrac{1}{a}+\dfrac{1}{b}+\dfrac{1}{c}=\dfrac{q}{abc}$.

\textbf{Step 1: $abc\le\dfrac{q^2}{9}$.} For any reals $X,Y,Z$ we have $(X+Y+Z)^2\ge3(XY+YZ+ZX)$, since the difference is $\dfrac{1}{2}\left[(X-Y)^2+(Y-Z)^2+(Z-X)^2\right]\ge0$. With $X=ab$, $Y=bc$, $Z=ca$, the right side is $3abc(a+b+c)=9abc$. So $q^2\ge9abc$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Hence $\dfrac{q}{abc}\ge\dfrac{9}{q}$, and by AM-GM,
\[
\dfrac{1}{a}+\dfrac{1}{b}+\dfrac{1}{c}+q\ge\dfrac{9}{q}+q\ge2\sqrt{\dfrac{9}{q}\cdot q}=6 .
\]
Multiplying by $2$ gives the inequality. Equality needs $q=3$ and $q^2=9abc$, i.e.\ $a=b=c=1$. $\blacksquare$
