Let \(f=a^2+2a-2\) and \(g=a^3-5a+3\). Note \(g(a)\ne 0\) for integers \(a\ge2\): \(g(2)=1\) and \(g\) is increasing for \(a\ge2\) since \(g'(a)=3a^2-5>0\).

Polynomial division gives
\[
a^3-5a+3=(a-2)(a^2+2a-2)+(a-1).
\]
So any common divisor \(d\) of \(f\) and \(g\) divides \(g-(a-2)f=a-1\). Also
\[
a^2+2a-2=(a-1)(a+3)+1,
\]
so \(d\) divides \(f-(a-1)(a+3)=1\). Therefore \(\gcd(f,g)=1\) for every integer \(a\), and the fraction is always in lowest terms.

\[
\boxed{\text{every integer } a\ge 2}
\]
(For example \(a=2\) gives \(\dfrac{6}{1}\) and \(a=3\) gives \(\dfrac{13}{15}\).)
