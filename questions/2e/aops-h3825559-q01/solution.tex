Let $g(x)=f(x)-x^2$. The supplied values give $g(2)=g(3)=g(4)=0$, so $2,3,4$ are distinct roots of $g$. By the factor theorem, their three linear factors divide $g$ as a product.

If $\deg f\le2$, then $\deg g\le2$ unless $g=0$. A nonzero polynomial of degree at most $2$ cannot have three distinct roots. Therefore $g=0$ and $\boxed{f(x)=x^2}$.
