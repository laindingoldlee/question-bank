By unique factorization, the positive divisors of $n$ are exactly the numbers
\[p_1^{e_1}p_2^{e_2}\cdots p_m^{e_m},\qquad 0\le e_i\le k_i,\]
and different exponent choices give different divisors. Now expand the product
\[\left(1+p_1+\cdots+p_1^{k_1}\right)\left(1+p_2+\cdots+p_2^{k_2}\right)\cdots\left(1+p_m+\cdots+p_m^{k_m}\right).\]
Each term of the expansion is obtained by choosing one power $p_i^{e_i}$ from each bracket, so the terms are exactly the products $p_1^{e_1}\cdots p_m^{e_m}$, each appearing once. Hence this product is the sum of all divisors of $n$.

Finally, each bracket is a geometric series: since $(p-1)(1+p+\cdots+p^k)=p^{k+1}-1$ and $p\ne1$,
\[1+p_i+\cdots+p_i^{k_i}=\dfrac{p_i^{k_i+1}-1}{p_i-1}.\]
Multiplying these gives the stated formula. $\blacksquare$
