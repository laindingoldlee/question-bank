By the binomial theorem, for all numbers $x$ and $y$,
\[(x+y)^n=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dbinom nk x^{n-k}y^k.\]
Put $x=1$ and $y=-1$. The left side is $(1-1)^n=0^n=0$, because $n>0$, and the right side is
\[\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dbinom nk(-1)^k=\dbinom n0-\dbinom n1+\dbinom n2-\cdots+(-1)^n\dbinom nn.\]
So the alternating sum is $0$. $\blacksquare$

(The condition $n>0$ is needed: for $n=0$ the sum is $\dbinom00=1$. In words, for $n>0$ a set with $n$ elements has as many subsets with an even number of elements as with an odd number.)
