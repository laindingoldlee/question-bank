Set $u=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt x}$, $v=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt y}$, and $w=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt z}$. The operation satisfies $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{a\odot b}}=\dfrac{1}{2}(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt a}+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt b})$. Thus the first two equations become $u+v=w$ and $\dfrac{u+w}{2}=\dfrac{2v}{3}$. Substitution yields $v=6u,w=7u$. The last equation becomes $\dfrac{v+w}{2}=\dfrac{u}{\sqrt k}$, hence $\dfrac{13u}{2}=\dfrac{u}{\sqrt k}$. Since $u>0$, $\boxed{k=\dfrac{4}{169}}$.
