Call a positive integer \emph{Pythagorean} if it is a sum of the squares of two positive integers (for example $2=1^2+1^2$ and $5=1^2+2^2$). Show that there are Pythagorean numbers $M>N$ such that $M+N$ is Pythagorean but $M-N$ is not.
