For $k=1,\ldots,n$, let $X_k=1$ if Bob can see the $k$th person from the front and $X_k=0$ otherwise. Bob sees the $k$th person exactly when that person is the tallest of the first $k$ people. In a uniformly random order, each of these $k$ people is equally likely to be the tallest of them, so $P(X_k=1)=\dfrac{1}{k}$.

By linearity of expectation, the expected number of people Bob sees is
\[E[X_1+\cdots+X_n]=\boxed{1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{n}}=\sum_{k=1}^n\dfrac{1}{k} .\]
For example, for $n=3$ this is $\dfrac{11}{6}$: of the $6$ orders, the numbers of visible people are $3,2,2,2,1,1$, with total $11$.
