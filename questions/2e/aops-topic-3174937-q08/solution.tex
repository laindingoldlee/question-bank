For one civilization, track the number $w$ of successes and current consecutive-failure count $s$. In each century, success probability is $p_s=\min(\dfrac{2^s}{5},1)$. A state $(w,s)$ moves to $(w+1,0)$ with probability $p_s$ and to $(w,s+1)$ otherwise. Starting at $(0,0)$, four iterations give probabilities of $1,2,3,4$ successes equal to $\dfrac{360}{625},\dfrac{236}{625},\dfrac{28}{625},\dfrac{1}{625}$; zero successes is impossible because the fourth attempt after three failures is certain. Since $A$ starts one level ahead, it remains strictly ahead exactly when its number of advances is at least that of $B$. Independence and identical distributions give probability $\dfrac{1+P(\text{equal counts})}{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1+\dfrac{360^2+236^2+28^2+1}{625^2}\right)=\dfrac{288353}{390625}\approx0.73818$. To two significant figures this is $\boxed{0.74}$. The recurrence above accounts for every four-century history.
