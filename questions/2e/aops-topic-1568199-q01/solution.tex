Each remainder is one less than the divisor, so if $x$ is such a number, then $x+1$ is divisible by $3$, by $4$ and by $5$. Since $3$, $4$, $5$ are pairwise relatively prime, this means $60\mid x+1$. Conversely, if $60\mid x+1$, then $x\equiv-1$ modulo $3$, $4$ and $5$, which gives the required remainders.

So the numbers are exactly those with $x\equiv59\pmod{60}$: $59,119,179,\dots$, and the smallest is $\boxed{59}$.

This is the Chinese remainder theorem in a simple case: congruences modulo pairwise relatively prime numbers ($3$, $4$, $5$) are together equivalent to a single congruence modulo their product ($60$).
