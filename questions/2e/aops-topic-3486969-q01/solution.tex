Every exclusion is possible. The following explicit constructions settle both existence and completeness; the center is left blank. For each displayed grid, the top row, bottom row, left column, and right column have the common sum listed. \[E=1:\quad\begin{pmatrix}2&5&9\\8&\ &4\\6&7&3\end{pmatrix}\quad(S=16).\]\[E=2:\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&6&8\\9&\ &4\\5&7&3\end{pmatrix}\quad(S=15).\]\[E=3:\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&5&9\\8&\ &4\\6&7&2\end{pmatrix}\quad(S=15).\]\[E=4:\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&6&9\\7&\ &2\\8&3&5\end{pmatrix}\quad(S=16).\]\[E=5:\quad\begin{pmatrix}2&7&6\\9&\ &1\\4&3&8\end{pmatrix}\quad(S=15).\]\[E=6:\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&4&9\\8&\ &3\\5&7&2\end{pmatrix}\quad(S=14).\]\[E=7:\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&4&9\\8&\ &2\\5&6&3\end{pmatrix}\quad(S=14).\]\[E=8:\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&4&9\\6&\ &3\\7&5&2\end{pmatrix}\quad(S=14).\]\[E=9:\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&4&8\\7&\ &3\\5&6&2\end{pmatrix}\quad(S=13).\]Thus $\boxed{\text{no exclusion makes the task impossible}}$.
