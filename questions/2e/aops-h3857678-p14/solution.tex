Neither $p=2$ nor $p=3$ works by direct reduction. For other primes, Fermat reduces the exponent of three to 6 and that of two to $-6$ modulo $p-1$. Multiplication by the invertible $2^6$ shows equivalence to $6^6\equiv1\pmod p$. Now $6^6-1=(216-1)(216+1)=5\cdot43\cdot7\cdot31$. These four distinct primes all qualify, giving $\boxed{4}$, choice D.
