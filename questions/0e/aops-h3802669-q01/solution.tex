(a) A real polynomial must also have the conjugate root $\sqrt2-i$, so the least degree is two, attained by $\boxed{x^2-2\sqrt2x+3}$.
(b) Multiplying by its $\sqrt2\mapsto-\sqrt2$ counterpart gives
\[
\boxed{q(x)=(x^2+3)^2-8x^2=x^4-2x^2+9}.
\]
To prove minimality, observe that $\alpha^{-1}=\dfrac{\sqrt2-i}{3}$, so
\[
\sqrt2=\dfrac{\alpha+\dfrac{3}{\alpha}}{2},\qquad i=\dfrac{\alpha-\dfrac{3}{\alpha}}{2}.
\]
Thus $\mathbb Q(\alpha)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,i)$, which has degree four over $\mathbb Q$: the real field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ has degree two, and adjoining the nonreal square root $i$ doubles it. Therefore no nonzero rational polynomial of degree below four vanishes at $\alpha$. Dividing any such polynomial by the monic $q$ leaves a rational remainder of degree below four that also vanishes at $\alpha$, so that remainder is zero. This proves the divisibility claim.
