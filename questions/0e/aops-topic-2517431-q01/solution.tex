It suffices to show that $10\mid a^{n+4}-a^n=a^{n-1}\left(a^5-a\right)$, and for that it suffices to show $10\mid a^5-a$. Factor
\[a^5-a=a\left(a^4-1\right)=(a-1)a(a+1)\left(a^2+1\right).\]
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Divisible by $2$:} $(a-1)a$ is a product of two consecutive integers.
\item \textbf{Divisible by $5$:} if $a\equiv0,1,4\pmod5$ then $5$ divides $a$, $a-1$ or $a+1$. If $a\equiv2$ or $3\pmod5$, then $a^2\equiv4\pmod5$, so $5\mid a^2+1$.
\end{itemize}
Since $\gcd(2,5)=1$, $10\mid a^5-a$, hence $10\mid a^{n+4}-a^n$ for all $n\ge1$, and $a^{n+4}$ and $a^n$ have the same units digit. $\blacksquare$

The cycle length is $1$, $2$ or $4$: for example the units digits of the powers of $5$ are $5,5,\dots$, of $9$ are $9,1,9,1,\dots$, and of $2$ are $2,4,8,6,\dots$. The condition $n\ge1$ is needed: $2^0=1$, but $2^4=16$ ends in $6$.
