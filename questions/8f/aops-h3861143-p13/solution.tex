Let $D=p+15q$. Reduction modulo $D$ gives $D\mid15q^2(q+15)$. Since $\gcd(D,q)=1$, this implies $D\mid15(q+15)$. If the quotient were one, $p=225$, not prime. Thus $2(p+15q)\le15(q+15)$, giving $2p+15q\le225$. Therefore $q\in\{2,3,5,7,11,13\}$ and $2\le p\le\dfrac{225-15q}{2}$. Checking primes in these six finite ranges in the original divisibility condition leaves only $(p,q)=(5,7)$. It gives $k=\dfrac{25+15\cdot343}{110}=47$. The required sum is $\boxed{47}$.
