Write the roots as $u=\dfrac{1}{3},r,s$. Since $c=urs>0$, $r,s$ have the same sign. They cannot both be negative: then $-\dfrac{1}{3}<r+s<0$ from $a>0$, while $b=rs+\dfrac{r+s}{3}\le\dfrac{(r+s)^2}{4}+\dfrac{r+s}{3}<0$, a contradiction. Thus all roots are positive. Put $a=u+r+s$. The condition is $u^2r^2s^2=\dfrac{1}{8}a(a-2u)(a-2r)(a-2s)$. At most one of the last three factors can be nonpositive; positivity of the product forces all three positive. Hence $u,r,s$ are side lengths of a triangle. By Heron\textquotesingle s formula, the right side is twice its squared area, so $urs=\sqrt2\,\Delta$. Its circumradius is therefore $R=\dfrac{urs}{4\Delta}=\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt2}$. Place the fixed side of length $u=\dfrac{1}{3}$ at endpoints $B=(-\dfrac{1}{6},h)$ and $C=(\dfrac{1}{6},h)$ on this circle centred at the origin. The other vertex is $A=(X,Y)$ with $X^2+Y^2=R^2$. Label $r=AC,s=AB$. Subtracting squared distances gives $r^2-s^2=-\dfrac{2}{3}X\le\dfrac{2}{3}R=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{6}$. Equality occurs at $A=(-R,0)$, a nondegenerate triangle, so all positive coefficients and the original condition are attained. The maximum is $\boxed{\dfrac{\sqrt2}{6}}$.
