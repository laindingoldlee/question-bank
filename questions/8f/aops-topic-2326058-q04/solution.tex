Let $X_k$ be the number of candies $A$ has after $k$ rounds. In each round $A$ gives away exactly one candy and receives a candy from $B$ with probability $\dfrac12$ and from $C$ with probability $\dfrac12$. By linearity of expectation, the expected change in one round is
\[-1+\dfrac12+\dfrac12=0,\]
whatever happened before. Hence $E[X_{k+1}]=E[X_k]$ for every $k$, and
\[E[X_{917}]=X_0=\boxed{500}.\]
(The probabilities $\dfrac23$ and $\dfrac13$ with which $A$ gives to $B$ or $C$ do not matter for $A$; they do matter for $B$ and $C$, whose expected counts change by $+\dfrac16$ and $-\dfrac16$ per round.)
