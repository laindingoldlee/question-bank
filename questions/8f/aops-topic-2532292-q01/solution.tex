The three primes are integers and the sum $157.5$ is not, so the fourth term $x$ is not an integer; it equals $157.5$ minus an integer, so $2x$ is an odd integer.

In a proportion the product of the extremes equals the product of the means, so the four terms split into two pairs with equal products. Let $p$ be the prime paired with $x$ and $q,r$ the other two primes. Then
\[xp=qr,\qquad 2x=\dfrac{2qr}{p}.\]
If $p$ were odd, then $p$ would divide $qr$ and $\dfrac{2qr}{p}$ would be even. So $p=2$, and $2x=qr$ is odd, so $q$ and $r$ are odd primes and $x=\dfrac{qr}{2}$. The sum condition becomes
\[2+q+r+\dfrac{qr}{2}=157.5\iff qr+2q+2r+4=315\iff(q+2)(r+2)=315=3^2\cdot5\cdot7.\]
Take $q\le r$, so $q+2$ is a divisor of $315$ with $5\le q+2\le\sqrt{315}$, i.e. $q+2\in\{5,7,9,15\}$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $q+2=5$: $q=3$, $r=63-2=61$, both prime;
\item $q+2=7$: $q=5$, $r=45-2=43$, both prime;
\item $q+2=9$: $q=7$, $r=35-2=33$, not prime;
\item $q+2=15$: $q=13$, $r=21-2=19$, both prime.
\end{itemize}
Each of these works, so the possible sets of terms are
\[\boxed{\{2,3,61,91.5\},\quad\{2,5,43,107.5\},\quad\{2,13,19,123.5\}}\]
with proportions
\[2:3=61:91.5,\qquad 2:5=43:107.5,\qquad 2:13=19:123.5.\]
(Check: $2\cdot91.5=183=3\cdot61$, $2\cdot107.5=215=5\cdot43$, $2\cdot123.5=247=13\cdot19$, and each set sums to $157.5$.) The terms of each proportion may of course be rearranged into other valid proportions.
