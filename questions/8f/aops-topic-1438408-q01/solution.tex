The bisector from $B$ meets the opposite side $AC$, so $AC$ is the shortest side, the "$2$" of the ratio. Then the other two sides are $\dfrac{3}{2}\cdot10=15$ and $\dfrac{4}{2}\cdot10=20$. Say $AB=20$ and $BC=15$ (the other choice only swaps the two pieces). By the angle bisector theorem,
\[
\dfrac{AD}{DC}=\dfrac{AB}{BC}=\dfrac{20}{15}=\dfrac{4}{3} ,
\]
so $AD=\dfrac{4}{7}\cdot10=\dfrac{40}{7}$ and $DC=\dfrac{30}{7}$. The longer subsegment has length $\boxed{\dfrac{40}{7}}$ inches.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),C=(10,0);
real x=(400-225+100)/20; pair B=(x,sqrt(400-x^2));
pair D=(40/7,0);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(B--D,blue);
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);dot(D);
label("$A$",A,SW); label("$B$",B,N); label("$C$",C,SE); label("$D$",D,S);
label("$20$",(A+B)/2,NW); label("$15$",(B+C)/2,E);
label("$\dfrac{40}{7}$",(A+D)/2,S); label("$\dfrac{30}{7}$",(D+C)/2,S);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}
