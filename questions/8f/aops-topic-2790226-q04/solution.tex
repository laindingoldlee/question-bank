Every point sends one arrow. If every point receives at least one, the total arrow count forces every point to receive exactly one, so the directed graph is a union of directed cycles. No cycle can have length three or more: at each vertex its outgoing distance is smaller than the incoming distance, since the outgoing neighbour is the unique closest point. This would force a strictly decreasing cycle of lengths, impossible. Therefore all cycles are two-cycles and the number of points must be even. Six points are possible: place them on a line at coordinates $0,1,10,12,30,33$. All fifteen pairwise distances are distinct, and the nearest-neighbour pairs are $(0,1),(10,12),(30,33)$. Thus (a) yes, and (b) no.
