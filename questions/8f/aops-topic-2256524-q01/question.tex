A ray of light enters a circle at point $A$ and travels along a chord to the point $B$ on the circle, where the arc $AB$ (measured counterclockwise from $A$) has an \emph{irrational} degree measure $\theta$. The light is reflected again and again inside the circle, hitting the circle at $C$, $D$, $E$, $\dots$, so that $AB=BC=CD=DE=\cdots$ and $A,B,C,D,\dots$ follow each other counterclockwise. Let $Q$ be the point of the circle diametrically opposite $A$. Prove that the light never touches $Q$, but that it hits the circle at points arbitrarily close to $Q$.
