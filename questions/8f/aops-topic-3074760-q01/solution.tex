First bound every composite weight below $887$. If $n$ is composite but not a perfect power, its prime-factor sum is at most $\dfrac{n}{2}+2\le501.5$: for any factorization $n=ab$ with $a,b\ge2$, the prime-factor sums of $a,b$ are at most $a,b$, and $a+b\le\dfrac{ab}{2}+2$. Adding a possible triangular contribution of at most $333$ gives less than $887$. If $n=a^e$ is a perfect power, $2\le a\le31$ and $2\le e\le9$. Its prime-factor sum is at most $ea\le279$. The total of all perfect-power bonuses is bounded above by\[2(31)+3(9)+4(5)+5(3)+6(3)+7(2)+8(2)+9(2)=190.\]Even with a triangular bonus its weight is at most $279+190+333=802<887$. A prime greater than $3$ is not triangular, since $\dfrac{t(t+1)}{2}$ is composite for $t>2$, and is not a perfect power, so its weight is itself. Trial division by primes up to $31$ shows that the fifteen largest primes below $1000$ are\[997,991,983,977,971,967,953,947,941,937,929,919,911,907,887.\]All have larger weights than every composite, and every other prime is smaller. Their sum, and the required maximum, is $\boxed{14217}$.
