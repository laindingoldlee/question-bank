Each trailing zero comes from a factor $10=2\cdot5$, and $100!$ contains more factors $2$ than factors $5$, so the number of trailing zeros is the exponent of $5$ in $100!$. Among $1,\dots,100$ there are $\left\lfloor\dfrac{100}{5}\right\rfloor=20$ multiples of $5$, and $\left\lfloor\dfrac{100}{25}\right\rfloor=4$ of them are multiples of $25$ and contribute a second factor $5$; there are no multiples of $125$. So the exponent of $5$ is $20+4=24$, and $100!$ ends in $\boxed{24}$ zeros.
