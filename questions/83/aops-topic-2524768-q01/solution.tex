\textbf{(a)} The $6^6$ outcomes $(d_1,\dots,d_6)$ are equally likely. The map
\[(d_1,\dots,d_6)\mapsto(7-d_1,\dots,7-d_6)\]
sends outcomes to outcomes, is its own inverse (so it is a bijection), and changes a sum $s$ into $42-s$. Hence exactly as many outcomes have sum $42-x$ as have sum $x$, and both probabilities equal $k$. (The distribution is symmetric about $21$, not about $18$.)

\textbf{(b)} Take $x=6$ and $x=36$. A sum of $6$ needs all six dice to show $1$, and a sum of $36$ needs all to show $6$, so in both cases $k=\dfrac{1}{6^6}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item For $x=36$: the probability of a sum of $36-36=0$ is $0$.
\item For $x=6$: by (a), the probability of a sum of $30$ equals that of a sum of $12$. Writing $d_i=1+e_i$, a sum of $12$ means $e_1+\cdots+e_6=6$ with $0\le e_i\le5$; there are $\dbinom{11}{5}=462$ solutions with $e_i\ge0$, minus the $6$ with some $e_i=6$, so $456$ outcomes. The probability is $\dfrac{456}{6^6}>0$.
\end{itemize}
The two values of $x$ have the same $k$ but different probabilities for $36-x$, so that probability is not a function of $k$. The correct relation is the one in (a): $P(42-x)=P(x)=k$.
