The roots form conjugate pairs $re^{\pm i\alpha},se^{\pm i\beta}$ with $r,s>0$ and $|\cos\alpha|,|\cos\beta|<1$. The constant term gives $rs=10$, while the $x^2$ coefficient gives $r^2+s^2+40\cos\alpha\cos\beta=69$. Thus $r^2+s^2>29$, so $2(r+s)>2\sqrt{29+20}=14$. Conversely let $r^2+s^2$ decrease to 29 from above, keeping $rs=10$, and choose equal cosines with square $\dfrac{69-r^2-s^2}{40} < 1$. These give nonreal roots and approach the bound. Hence the infimum is $\boxed{14}$.
