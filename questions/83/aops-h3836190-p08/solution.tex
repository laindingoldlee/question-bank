Every edge of the intersection lies on an edge line of one of the original polygons. There are at most $4+3=7$ such lines, so seven is an upper bound. A triangle cutting off three different corners of a square while retaining portions of all four square sides attains seven. Thus $\boxed{7}$, choice C.
