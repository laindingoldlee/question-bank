A number is divisible by $99$ exactly when it is divisible by both $9$ and $11$.

\textbf{Divisibility by $9$:} the digit sum $3+y+4+5+z+8=20+y+z$ is a multiple of $9$. Since $0\le y+z\le18$, this means $y+z=7$ or $y+z=16$.

\textbf{Divisibility by $11$:} the alternating sum of digits from the right, $8-z+5-4+y-3=6+y-z$, is a multiple of $11$. Since $-9\le y-z\le9$, this means $y-z=-6$ or $y-z=5$.

The numbers $y+z$ and $y-z$ have the same parity. The pairs with equal parity are $(y+z,y-z)=(7,5)$, giving $y=6$, $z=1$, and $(16,-6)$, giving $y=5$, $z=11$, which is not a digit. Hence the only answer is $\boxed{y=6,\ z=1}$, and indeed $364518=99\cdot3682$.
