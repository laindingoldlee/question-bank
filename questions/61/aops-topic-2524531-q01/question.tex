There are $25$ people and $6$ balls of different colours. Each person independently chooses one of the $6$ colours uniformly at random (with replacement, so several people may choose the same colour).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Find the expected number of (unordered) pairs of people who chose the same colour.
\item[(b)] A student tries to solve (a) by saying ``there are at least $10$ pairs'' and counting, for each possible number of pairs, the ways to split the people into disjoint same-colour pairs and leftover single people, and gets a huge number. Explain what is wrong with this approach.
\end{enumerate}
