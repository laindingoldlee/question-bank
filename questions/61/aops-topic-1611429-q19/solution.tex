\textbf{Volume.} Take $ABC$ as the base: it is a right triangle of area $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot2\cdot3=3$, and $AD\perp$ plane $ABC$, so the height is $AD=4$ and
\[
V=\dfrac{1}{3}\cdot3\cdot4=4 .
\]
\textbf{Area of $BCD$.} By the Pythagorean theorem in the three right faces,
\[
BC^2=4+9=13,\qquad BD^2=4+16=20,\qquad CD^2=9+16=25 .
\]
Heron's formula in the form $16K^2=2\left(a^2b^2+b^2c^2+c^2a^2\right)-\left(a^4+b^4+c^4\right)$ gives
\[
16K^2=2(13\cdot20+20\cdot25+25\cdot13)-(169+400+625)=2170-1194=976,
\]
so $K^2=61$ and $[BCD]=\sqrt{61}$.

\textbf{Height.} With $BCD$ as the base and $AH$ the height from $A$ (dashed red),
\[
4=V=\dfrac{1}{3}\cdot\sqrt{61}\cdot AH\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad AH=\dfrac{12}{\sqrt{61}}=\boxed{\dfrac{12\sqrt{61}}{61}}.
\]
(Equivalently, $\dfrac{1}{AH^2}=\dfrac{1}{AB^2}+\dfrac{1}{AC^2}+\dfrac{1}{AD^2}=\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{1}{9}+\dfrac{1}{16}=\dfrac{61}{144}$.)
