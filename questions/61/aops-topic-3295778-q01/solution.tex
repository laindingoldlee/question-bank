Let $F(A,B)$ denote the probability. Set $F(A,0)=1$ and $F(A,B)=0$ when $A<2,B>0$. For $a=\min(3,A)$ and $b=\min(2,B)$, enumerate all $6^{a+b}$ equally likely roll vectors. Sort each side decreasingly; let $L$ count compared positions where the attacking die is no larger than the defending die, and put $k=\min(a,b)$. Then $F(A,B)=6^{-(a+b)}\sum_{\text{roll vectors}}F(A-L,B-k+L)$. Every term has fewer total units, so the boundary values determine the recurrence uniquely. The desired general answer is $F(n,n)$. For $n=2$, a first-round loss of either attacking unit prevents any future attack, so victory requires both defending units to be lost immediately. Among the $6^4=1296$ rolls, exactly $295$ have both sorted attacking dice strictly greater. One way to count is to sum over the minimum $u$ and maximum $v$ of the defender rolls: for $1\le u\le v\le6$ their multiplicity is $1$ if $u=v$, otherwise $2$, and the winning attacking pairs number $(6-u)^2-(v-u)^2$. The sum is $295$. Thus $\boxed{F(2,2)=\dfrac{295}{1296}}$.
