Trace the ancestry of the ten positions backward through the random copy events. If there are $k$ distinct ancestral positions, one event merges two exactly when its recipient is one of these $k$ positions and its donor is one of the other $k-1$, with probability $\dfrac{k (k-1)}{100}$. All initial letters are distinct, so a configuration at time $t$ is monochromatic exactly when its ten ancestral lines have merged by time zero. Reversing a finite sequence of independent uniform copy events does not change its law, so the consensus-time tail probabilities equal those of this backward coalescence time. The expected time is therefore $\sum_{k=2}^{10} \dfrac{100}{k(k-1)} = 100 (1 - \dfrac{1}{10}) = 90$. Thus $m=90,n=1$ and $\boxed{m+n=91}$.
