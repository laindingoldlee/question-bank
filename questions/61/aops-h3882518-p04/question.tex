Wynn and Alexander receive distinct numbers $\dfrac{1}{w}$ and $\dfrac{1}{a}$, where $w,a$ are positive integers. Each knows only their own number and these rules. Both reason perfectly and make these truthful public statements in order:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Wynn cannot determine whose number is larger.
\item Alexander cannot determine whose number is larger.
\item Wynn still cannot determine whose number is larger.
\item Alexander still cannot determine whose number is larger.
\item Wynn still cannot determine whose number is larger.
\item Alexander can now determine whose number is larger.
\item Wynn can now determine both numbers.
\end{enumerate}
Their sum is $\dfrac{m}{n}$ in lowest terms. Find $m+n$.
