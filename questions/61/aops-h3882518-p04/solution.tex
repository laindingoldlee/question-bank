The first statement excludes $w=1$, so $w\ge2$. Alexander's ignorance then excludes $a=1,2$, so $a\ge3$. The next three statements similarly give, in order, $w\ge4$, $a\ge5$, and $w\ge6$. At this point Alexander knows the comparison only if $a=5$ or $6$: larger $a$ would allow a distinct Wynn denominator on either side of $a$.

Wynn can distinguish these two possibilities exactly when $w=6$, since then distinctness forces $a=5$. If $w>6$, both remain possible. The numbers are $\dfrac{1}{6},\dfrac{1}{5}$, whose sum is $\dfrac{11}{30}$. Thus $\boxed{m+n=41}$.
