First, the equivalence: if $m^n\mid k$ with $m>1$, $n\ge2$, then for any prime $\ell\mid m$ we get $\ell^2\mid k$; conversely $\ell^2\mid k$ lets $\sqrt{k}$ be simplified. So root-simple means squarefree.

\textbf{(a)} Every prime is root-simple, and there are infinitely many primes. The student's argument needs one correction: $z!+1$ is not divisible by \emph{any} prime $\ell\le z$ (each such $\ell$ divides $z!$, so it would divide $(z!+1)-z!=1$). Not being divisible by \emph{every} such prime is far weaker and proves nothing. With the correction: any prime factor of $z!+1$ is greater than $z$, so for every prime $z$ there is a larger prime, and the primes are infinite.

\textbf{(b)} For every odd prime $p$, the number $2p$ is composite and squarefree (its prime factors $2$ and $p$ each appear once). There are infinitely many odd primes, so infinitely many such numbers.

\textbf{(c)} For every odd prime $p$, the pair $(2,p)$ consists of squarefree numbers with $\gcd(2,p)=1$, so there are infinitely many such pairs. $\blacksquare$
