Suppose $21\mid a^2+b^2$ with positive integers $a,b$.

\textbf{Modulo $3$:} squares are $\equiv0$ or $1$, so $a^2+b^2\equiv0\pmod3$ forces $a^2\equiv b^2\equiv0$, that is, $3\mid a$ and $3\mid b$.

\textbf{Modulo $7$:} squares are $\equiv0,1,2,4$. If $7\nmid a$, then $b^2\equiv-a^2\in\{6,5,3\}\pmod 7$, which is not a square residue. So $7\mid a$ and then $7\mid b$.

Hence $21\mid a$ and $21\mid b$, so $a^2+b^2\ge21^2+21^2=882$. Since $882=21\cdot42$, the answer is
\[
\boxed{882=21^2+21^2}.
\]
(If the two squares must be different, the smallest is $21^2+42^2=2205$.)
