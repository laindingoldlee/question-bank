Let $H$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $A$ to the line $BC$. Since $ABC$ is a triangle, $A$ is not on the line $BC$, so $AH>0$.

\textbf{Two basic inequalities.} If $H\ne B$, then triangle $AHB$ has a right angle at $H$, and by the Pythagorean theorem $AB^2=AH^2+HB^2>HB^2$, so $AB>HB$. If $H=B$, then $AB>0=HB$. Either way $AB>HB$, and in the same way $AC>HC$.

\textbf{Case 1: $H$ lies on the segment $BC$.} Then $BC=BH+HC<AB+AC$.

\textbf{Case 2: $H$ lies outside the segment $BC$.} Say $C$ lies between $B$ and $H$ (the case where $B$ lies between $C$ and $H$ is the same with $B$ and $C$ swapped). Then
\[BC<BH<AB<AB+AC.\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(1.3,2.2), B=(0,0), C=(4.5,0), H=(1.3,0);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.8));
draw(A--H,dashed);
real s=0.17; draw(H+(s,0)--H+(s,s)--H+(0,s));
dot(H);
label("$A$",A,N); label("$B$",B,SW); label("$C$",C,SE); label("$H$",H,S);
\end{asy}

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(5.2,2.0), B=(0,0), C=(3.6,0), H=(5.2,0);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.8));
draw(C--H,dashed); draw(A--H,dashed);
real s=0.17; draw(H+(-s,0)--H+(-s,s)--H+(0,s));
dot(H);
label("$A$",A,N); label("$B$",B,SW); label("$C$",C,S); label("$H$",H,S);
\end{asy}

In both cases $AB+AC>BC$. $\blacksquare$

Applying this to each vertex gives all three inequalities $AB+AC>BC$, $AB+BC>AC$ and $AC+BC>AB$.
