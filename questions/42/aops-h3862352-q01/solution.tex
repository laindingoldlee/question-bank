Write $a=du,b=dv$ with $d=\gcd(a,b)$ and $\gcd(u,v)=1$. Then
\[
\gcd(a-b,a+b)=d\,\gcd(u-v,u+v).
\]
The last gcd divides $2$; it equals $2$ if $u,v$ are both odd and $1$ if they have opposite parity. Thus the pair is strange exactly when $a,b$ contain different powers of $2$ in their prime factorizations.

Among $1,\ldots,30$, the counts with exact powers $2^0,2^1,2^2,2^3,2^4$ are $15,8,4,2,1$. The number of pairs with equal powers is $15^2+8^2+4^2+2^2+1^2=310$. Subtracting from $30^2$ gives $\boxed{590}$.
