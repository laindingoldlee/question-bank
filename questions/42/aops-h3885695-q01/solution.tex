No. Take the triangle with vertices $(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)$ and $P=(\dfrac{1}{4},\dfrac{1}{4})$. Each move averages the coordinates with integer coordinates. Consequently both coordinates always remain dyadic rationals, meaning fractions whose denominators are powers of $2$. The interior point $Q=(\dfrac{1}{3},\dfrac{1}{3})$ is therefore unreachable. More generally, there are only countably many finite sequences of moves but uncountably many interior target points.

\end{asy}
