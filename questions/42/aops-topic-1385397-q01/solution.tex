Let her lose $p$ pennies, $d$ dimes and $q$ quarters with $p+d+q=3$. Every such triple is possible (she has at least $3$ of each kind), and there are $\dbinom{5}{2}=10$ triples. The amounts $p+10d+25q$ are all different: they are
\[
3,\ 12,\ 21,\ 30,\ 27,\ 36,\ 45,\ 51,\ 60,\ 75
\]
cents for $(p,d,q)=(3,0,0),(2,1,0),(1,2,0),(0,3,0),(2,0,1),(1,1,1),(0,2,1),(1,0,2),(0,1,2),(0,0,3)$. (In general, two triples with the same sum $3$ and the same amount would need $9(d-d')+24(q-q')=0$, that is $3(d-d')=-8(q-q')$; then $8\mid d-d'$ with $|d-d'|\le3$, so $d=d'$ and then $q=q'$.) So there are $\boxed{10}$ amounts.
