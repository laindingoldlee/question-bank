Rewrite the equations as
\[
a^2-bc=-3,\qquad b^2-ca=-14,\qquad c^2-ab=30 .
\]
\textbf{Subtract in pairs.} The first minus the second gives
\[
a^2-b^2+ca-bc=(a-b)(a+b)+c(a-b)=(a-b)(a+b+c)=11 .
\]
The second minus the third gives, in the same way,
\[
(b-c)(a+b+c)=-44 .
\]
\textbf{Use that $11$ is prime.} Since $a+b+c\ge3$ is a positive divisor of $11$, we get $a+b+c=11$ and $a-b=1$. Then $b-c=-\dfrac{44}{11}=-4$. So $a=b+1$ and $c=b+4$, and $3b+5=11$ gives $b=2$, $a=3$, $c=6$.

\textbf{Check.} $3^2+3=12=2\cdot6$, $2^2+14=18=6\cdot3$ and $6^2=36=3\cdot2+30$.

The only solution is $\boxed{(a,b,c)=(3,2,6)}$.
