If $n\le0$ then $4n\le0\le(a-b)^2$, and there is nothing to prove. So let $n\ge1$.

Since $ab=n^2+n+1>0$, the numbers $a$ and $b$ have the same sign, and replacing them by $-a,-b$ changes neither $ab$ nor $(a-b)^2$. So we may assume $a,b>0$, and by symmetry $a\le b$.

\textbf{Step 1: $a\le n<b$.} We have $a^2\le ab=n^2+n+1<(n+1)^2$, so $a<n+1$, that is, $a\le n$. Then $b=\dfrac{n^2+n+1}{a}\ge\dfrac{n^2+n+1}{n}>n$.

\textbf{Step 2: $a+b\ge2n+2$.} Since $n-a\ge0$ and $b-n>0$,
\[(n-a)(b-n)\ge0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad n(a+b)\ge ab+n^2=2n^2+n+1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a+b\ge2n+1+\dfrac{1}{n}>2n+1 .\]
As $a+b$ is an integer, $a+b\ge2n+2$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Therefore
\[(a-b)^2=(a+b)^2-4ab\ge(2n+2)^2-4\left(n^2+n+1\right)=4n .\qquad\blacksquare\]

Equality occurs, for example, for $n=1$, $(a,b)=(1,3)$ and for $n=4$, $(a,b)=(3,7)$.
