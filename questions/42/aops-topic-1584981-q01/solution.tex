Let the square $ABCD$ have side $s$. The diagonal $AC$ is the hypotenuse of the right isosceles triangle $ABC$:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real s=(sqrt(2)+sqrt(3))/sqrt(2);
pair A=(0,0),B=(s,0),C=(s,s),D=(0,s);
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(A--C,blue+linewidth(0.9));
label("$A$",A,SW);label("$B$",B,SE);label("$C$",C,NE);label("$D$",D,NW);
label("$s$",(A+B)/2,S);label("$s$",(B+C)/2,E);
pair m=(A+C)/2;label("$\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}$",m+0.07*dir(135),dir(135),blue);
real r=0.13;draw((s-r,0)--(s-r,r)--(s,r));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

By the Pythagorean theorem $AC^2=s^2+s^2=2s^2$, so the area is
\[
s^2=\dfrac{AC^2}{2}=\dfrac{\left(\sqrt2+\sqrt3\right)^2}{2}=\dfrac{2+2\sqrt6+3}{2}=\boxed{\dfrac{5}{2}+\sqrt6}.
\]
(The formula ``area $=\dfrac{d^2}{2}$'' for a square with diagonal $d$ is the same computation; it also follows because the square is a rhombus whose diagonals both have length $d$.)
