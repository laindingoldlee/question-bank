At position $1$, the only increase is by $1$, so four increases are necessary. At position $2$, one move reaches at most $4$, and $2\to4\to5$ works. At positions $3,4$, one increase suffices. Position $5$ needs no move. For positions $6,7,8$, one halving gives $3,4,4$ respectively, while an increase cannot reach $5$; hence two moves are necessary and sufficient. Positions $9,10$ reach $5$ by one halving. Summing the independent lower bounds and using these constructions gives $\boxed{16}$ moves.
