An equilateral triangle is divided into small congruent equilateral triangles by lines parallel to its sides, with $n$ rows of small triangles (each side of the big triangle is $n$ times the side of a small triangle). The figure shows $n=5$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real h=sqrt(3)/2;int n=5;
pair V(int i,int j){return (j-i/2,-i*h);}
for(int i=0;i<n;++i)for(int j=0;j<=i;++j)draw(V(i,j)--V(i+1,j)--V(i+1,j+1)--cycle,linewidth(0.7));
draw(V(0,0)--V(n,0)--V(n,n)--cycle,linewidth(1.1));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

For even $n$ the total number of triangles of all sizes in the figure is $\dfrac{n(n+2)(2n+1)}{8}$, but this expression is not an integer when $n$ is odd.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Find the number of triangles of all sizes when $n=5$ and when $n=7$.
\item[(b)] Prove that for every odd $n$ the number of triangles is $\dfrac{n(n+2)(2n+1)-1}{8}$.
\end{enumerate}
