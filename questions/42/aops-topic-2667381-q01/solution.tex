\textbf{(a)} $x^2-17x+72=(x-8)(x-9)$. With $\gcd(3,8)=1$ and $\gcd(3,9)=3$ we get $3x-8$ and $x-3$, and indeed $(3x-8)(x-3)=3x^2-17x+24$.

\textbf{(b)} Since $r+s=-b$ and $rs=ac$,
\[(ax-r)(ax-s)=a^2x^2-a(r+s)x+rs=a\left(ax^2+bx+c\right).\]
Write $ax-r=g\,P(x)$ and $ax-s=h\,Q(x)$, where $P(x)=\dfrac agx-\dfrac rg$ and $Q(x)=\dfrac ahx-\dfrac sh$ have integer coefficients. Then $a\left(ax^2+bx+c\right)=gh\,P(x)Q(x)$.

\emph{Claim: $a\mid gh$.} For a prime $p$ let $\alpha,\rho,\sigma$ be the exponents of $p$ in $a,r,s$ (infinite for $0$). From $rs=ac$, $\rho+\sigma\ge\alpha$. Then $\min(\alpha,\rho)+\min(\alpha,\sigma)\ge\alpha$: this is clear if $\rho\ge\alpha$ or $\sigma\ge\alpha$, and otherwise the left side is $\rho+\sigma\ge\alpha$.

So $gh=ka$ for a positive integer $k$, and $ax^2+bx+c=k\,P(x)Q(x)$. Hence $k$ divides $a$, $b$ and $c$, so $k=1$ when $\gcd(a,b,c)=1$, and $ax^2+bx+c=P(x)Q(x)$. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{(c)} $x^2+4x+4=(x+2)^2$, so $r=s=-2$ and $g=h=2$. The method gives $(x+1)(x+1)=x^2+2x+1$, which is only half of $2x^2+4x+2$. The common factor $\gcd(2,4,2)=2$ is lost, so it must be taken out first: $2x^2+4x+2=2(x+1)^2$.
