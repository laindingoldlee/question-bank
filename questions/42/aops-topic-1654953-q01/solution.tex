Rachel's car cannot contain Alex or Jeff, so Alex and Jeff ride together in the other car. Rachel is in either car ($2$ choices; the count is the same for both). Let $S$ be the set of the five free people (Marcus, Sara, William, Ryan, Andrew) who join Rachel.

\textbf{Seats.} Rachel's car holds $1+|S|\le5$ people and the other holds $2+(5-|S|)\le5$, so $2\le|S|\le4$.

\textbf{Drivers.} Suppose $S$ contains $j$ of the three possible drivers Marcus, Sara, William and $k$ of the non-drivers Ryan, Andrew. Rachel's car has $1+j$ possible drivers and the other car has $2+(3-j)=5-j$. The number of ways to pick $S$ with given $j,k$ is $\dbinom3j\dbinom2k$, and we need $2\le j+k\le4$.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|c|c|c}
$j$ & allowed $k$, weight $\sum\dbinom2k$ & $\dbinom3j(1+j)(5-j)$ & product\\ \hline
$0$ & $k=2$: $1$ & $5$ & $5$\\
$1$ & $k=1,2$: $3$ & $24$ & $72$\\
$2$ & $k=0,1,2$: $4$ & $27$ & $108$\\
$3$ & $k=0,1$: $3$ & $8$ & $24$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
This gives $5+72+108+24=209$ configurations for each choice of Rachel's car, so the total is $2\cdot209=\boxed{418}$.
