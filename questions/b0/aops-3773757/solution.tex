Let $\theta = \dfrac{2 \pi}{7}$ and $t = 1 + 2 \cos \theta = \dfrac{b}{a}$. The identity $1+2\cos\theta+2\cos2\theta+2\cos3\theta=0$ follows from summing the seventh roots of unity and taking real parts. Expressing the multiple-angle cosines in $2\cos\theta=t-1$ gives $t^3-2t^2-t+1=0$. But $\dfrac{S}{a^5} = 1 - 4 t^2 + 3 t^4 - t^5 = - (t^2-t-1) (t^3-2t^2-t+1) = 0$. Therefore $\boxed{S=0}$.
