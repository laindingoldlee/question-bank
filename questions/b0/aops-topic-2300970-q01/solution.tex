The conditions say
\[x^2(y+z)=a^3,\qquad y^2(z+x)=b^3,\qquad z^2(x+y)=c^3,\qquad xyz=abc.\]
Multiplying the first three,
\[x^2y^2z^2(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)=a^3b^3c^3=(abc)^3=(xyz)^3,\]
and dividing by $x^2y^2z^2\ne0$ gives $(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)=xyz$.

Adding the first three instead,
\[a^3+b^3+c^3=x^2y+x^2z+y^2z+y^2x+z^2x+z^2y.\]
Expanding, $(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)=x^2y+x^2z+y^2z+y^2x+z^2x+z^2y+2xyz$, so
\[(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)=a^3+b^3+c^3+2xyz.\]
Combining the two results, $xyz=a^3+b^3+c^3+2xyz$, so $a^3+b^3+c^3+xyz=0$, and since $xyz=abc$,
\[a^3+b^3+c^3+abc=0.\ \blacksquare\]
(Such numbers exist: for example $x=1$, $y=-2$, $z=\dfrac{3+\sqrt{17}}{2}$ satisfy $(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)=xyz$, and then $a,b,c$ are the real cube roots of $x^2(y+z)$, $y^2(z+x)$, $z^2(x+y)$.)
