Let $s_2(m)$ be the number of ones in the binary expansion of $m$. Counting factors of $2$ in $m!$ gives $v_2(m!)=\sum_{j\ge1}\lfloor\dfrac{m}{2^j}\rfloor=m-s_2(m)$: each binary digit $2^k$ contributes $2^k-1$ to this sum. Hence $v_2(C_n)=2n-s_2(2n)-2(n-s_2(n))-v_2(n+1)=s_2(n)-v_2(n+1)$. Adding one to $n$ replaces its trailing run of $v_2(n+1)$ ones by zeroes and adds one new one. Thus $s_2(n+1)=s_2(n)+1-v_2(n+1)$, and $v_2(C_n)=s_2(n+1)-1$. This vanishes exactly when $n+1$ has one binary one, that is, is a power of two. Therefore $\boxed{C_n\text{ is odd exactly for }n=2^k-1,\ k\ge0}$.
