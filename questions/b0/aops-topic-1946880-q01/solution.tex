Besides the banana there are $4$ fruits: $a$ apples and $p$ pears with $a+p=4$, $a\ge1$, $p\ge1$. They cost $50a+60p=200+10p$ cents, so the banana costs
\[
275-200-10p=75-10p \text{ cents},\qquad p\in\{1,2,3\}.
\]
This gives $65$, $55$ or $45$ cents. The banana is cheaper than a pear ($60$ cents), which rules out $65$. So the banana cost $\boxed{45\text{ or }55}$ cents (with $3$ pears and $1$ apple, or $2$ pears and $2$ apples).
