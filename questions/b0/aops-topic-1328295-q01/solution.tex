(a) Choosing $2$ of $n+1$ objects is the same as choosing the $n-1$ to leave out, so $\dbinom{n+1}2=\dbinom{n+1}{n-1}$ (or: both equal $\dfrac{(n+1)!}{2!\,(n-1)!}$).

(b) Pairing the first and last terms, $2T_n=(1+n)+(2+(n-1))+\dots+(n+1)=n(n+1)$, so $T_n=\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}=\dbinom{n+1}{2}$.

(c) Count the $2$-person committees from $n+1$ people numbered $1,\dots,n+1$ by the higher-numbered member. If that member is $k+1$ ($k=1,\dots,n$), the other member is one of $1,\dots,k$: $k$ choices. So $\dbinom{n+1}2=1+2+\dots+n=T_n$. $\blacksquare$

(In Pascal's triangle, the entries $\dbinom{k}{2}$ run down the third diagonal $1,3,6,10,\dots$, and the hockey-stick identity $\dbinom22+\dbinom32+\dots$ is the same fact.)
