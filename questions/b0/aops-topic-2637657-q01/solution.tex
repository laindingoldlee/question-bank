\emph{Verification.} $\left(\sqrt6+\sqrt2\right)^2=6+2+2\sqrt{12}=8+4\sqrt3$, and $\sqrt6+\sqrt2>0$, so it is the positive square root of $8+4\sqrt3$. $\blacksquare$

\emph{Finding it.} Look for $\sqrt{8+4\sqrt3}=\sqrt a+\sqrt b$. Squaring gives $a+b+2\sqrt{ab}=8+4\sqrt3$, so try $a+b=8$ and $2\sqrt{ab}=4\sqrt3$, i.e. $ab=12$. Then $a,b$ are the roots of $t^2-8t+12=0$, namely $6$ and $2$, giving $\boxed{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}$.
