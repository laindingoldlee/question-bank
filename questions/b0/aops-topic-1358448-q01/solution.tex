Let $x=7+4\sqrt3$, $y=7-4\sqrt3$, so $x+y=14$ and $xy=49-48=1$. Put $s_n=x^n+y^n$. Then $s_0=2$, $s_1=14$ and $s_{n+1}=14s_n-s_{n-1}$, so every $s_n$ is an even integer and $t_n=\dfrac{s_n}{2}$ is an integer with
\[
t_0=1,\quad t_1=7,\quad t_{n+1}=14t_n-t_{n-1}.
\]
(Binomial theorem gives the same: $t_n=\sum_j\dbinom{n}{2j}7^{n-2j}48^j$.)

Work modulo $4$ and modulo $25$.

\emph{Mod $4$:} $t_{n+1}\equiv2t_n-t_{n-1}$, starting $1,3$: the sequence is $1,3,1,3,\dots$ (since $2\cdot3-1=5\equiv1$ and $2\cdot1-3=-1\equiv3$). For odd $n$, $t_n\equiv3\pmod4$.

\emph{Mod $25$:} $t_{n+1}\equiv14t_n-t_{n-1}$ gives
\[
1,7,22,1,17,12,1,2,2,1,12,17,1,22,7,\ 1,7,\dots
\]
After $15$ steps the pair $(1,7)$ returns, so the sequence has period $15$ modulo $25$. As $1335=15\cdot89$, $t_{1335}\equiv t_0=1\pmod{25}$.

Then $t_{1335}\equiv3\pmod4$ and $\equiv1\pmod{25}$, which gives $t_{1335}\equiv\boxed{51}\pmod{100}$.
