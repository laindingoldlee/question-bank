The only moments you can recognise are ``now'' and the moments when a candle burns out, so the times you can measure from now are exactly the burn-out times.

\textbf{Every burn-out time is a sum of the burning times of distinct candles.} A candle lit now burns out at its own burning time. A candle lit when another candle burns out at time $T$ finishes at $T$ plus its own burning time; by induction $T$ is the sum of the times of a chain of candles lit one after another, and the new candle is not in that chain because each candle is lit only once. Hence every measurable time is a sum of some of the numbers $1,3,4$ (each used at most once):
\[1,\ 3,\ 4,\ 1+3=4,\ 1+4=5,\ 3+4=7,\ 1+3+4=8 .\]

\textbf{All of these can be measured:} $1$, $3$ and $4$ directly; $5$ by the $1$-hour candle then the $4$-hour candle; $7$ by the $3$-hour candle then the $4$-hour candle; $8$ by all three one after another.

So the measurable times from now are $1,3,4,5,7,8$ hours. Among the options, $2$ hours and $6$ hours cannot be measured, and the longer of these is $6$ hours: answer $\boxed{\textbf{(E)}}$.

(The words ``from now'' matter. If you light the $1$-hour and $3$-hour candles together, the time from the first burn-out to the second is $2$ hours; and lighting the $1$-hour candle together with the $3$-hour candle followed by the $4$-hour candle gives burn-outs at $1$ and $7$ hours, $6$ hours apart. But neither interval starts now.)
