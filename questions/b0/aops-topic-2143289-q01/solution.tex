Days $1$ to $14$ give $14\cdot5=70$ problems, and days $12$ to $31$ give $20\cdot3=60$ problems. Let $s$ be the number solved on the overlap, days $12$, $13$ and $14$. Then
\[n=70+60-s=130-s .\]
Since the overlap is part of each period, $0\le s\le60$. Every such $s$ is possible: solve $s$ problems on day $12$, $70-s$ on day $1$, $60-s$ on day $15$, and none on the other days. So $n$ takes every value from $130-60=70$ to $130$, which is $\boxed{61}$ values.
