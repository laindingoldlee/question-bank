The second condition makes $\tau(n)$ prime, say $ q>2 $. In the factorisation formula for $\tau(n)$ only one factor can exceed one, so $ n=p^{q-1}$ for a prime $ p $. Since $ 2^{10}>1000 $, the only possible $ q $ are 3, 5, 7, giving exponents 2, 4, 6. Prime squares contribute the eleven primes up to 31; fourth powers contribute $ p=2,3,5 $; sixth powers contribute $ p=2,3 $. These powers are distinct by unique factorisation. Total $\boxed{16}$.
