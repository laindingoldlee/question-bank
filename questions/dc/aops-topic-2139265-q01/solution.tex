Let $O_1,O_2,O_3$ be the centres of the three circles and $P$ the centre of the fourth. Then $O_iO_j=r_i+r_j$ and $PO_i=r+r_i$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real r1=1.3, r2=1.1, r3=1.0;
pair O1=(0,0), O2=(r1+r2,0);
real a=r1+r3, c=r2+r3, d=r1+r2;
real x3=(a^2-c^2+d^2)/(2d);
pair O3=(x3,sqrt(a^2-x3^2));
real s=sqrt(r1*r2*r3*(r1+r2+r3));
real r=r1*r2*r3/(r1*r2+r2*r3+r3*r1+2*s);
pair[] X=intersectionpoints(circle(O1,r1+r),circle(O2,r2+r));
pair P=(X[0].y>0)?X[0]:X[1];
draw(circle(O1,r1),linewidth(0.9)); draw(circle(O2,r2),linewidth(0.9)); draw(circle(O3,r3),linewidth(0.9));
draw(O1--O2--O3--cycle,gray+dashed);
filldraw(circle(P,r),paleyellow,red+linewidth(0.9));
draw(P--O1,blue); draw(P--O2,blue); draw(P--O3,blue);
dot(O1); dot(O2); dot(O3); dot(P,red);
label("$O_1$",O1,SW); label("$O_2$",O2,SE); label("$O_3$",O3,N);
draw(P--P+(1.0,0.45),red); label("$P$",P+(1.0,0.45),E,red+fontsize(9pt));
label("$r_1$",O1+r1*dir(200),W); label("$r_2$",O2+r2*dir(-20),E); label("$r_3$",O3+r3*dir(160),W);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\textbf{An area equation.} A triangle with sides $x+y$, $y+z$, $z+x$ has semiperimeter $x+y+z$, so by Heron's formula its area is $\sqrt{(x+y+z)\,xyz}$. The gap is bounded by arcs lying inside triangle $O_1O_2O_3$, so $P$ is inside that triangle, and the triangles $PO_2O_3$, $PO_3O_1$, $PO_1O_2$ fill it. Hence
\[\sqrt{r_1r_2r_3\left(r_1+r_2+r_3\right)}=\sqrt{r\,r_2r_3\left(r+r_2+r_3\right)}+\sqrt{r\,r_3r_1\left(r+r_3+r_1\right)}+\sqrt{r\,r_1r_2\left(r+r_1+r_2\right)}.\]

\textbf{Curvatures.} Put $k=\dfrac{1}{r}$, $k_i=\dfrac{1}{r_i}$ and $\sigma=\sqrt{k_1k_2+k_2k_3+k_3k_1}$. Since $\dfrac{r+r_2+r_3}{r\,r_2r_3}=k_2k_3+kk_3+kk_2$ and so on, dividing the equation by $r\,r_1r_2r_3$ gives
\[k\sigma=k_1\sqrt{k(k_2+k_3)+k_2k_3}+k_2\sqrt{k(k_3+k_1)+k_3k_1}+k_3\sqrt{k(k_1+k_2)+k_1k_2}.\qquad(*)\]

\textbf{The solution.} Try $k=k_1+k_2+k_3+2\sigma$. Then
\[k(k_2+k_3)+k_2k_3=(k_2+k_3)^2+k_1(k_2+k_3)+2\sigma(k_2+k_3)+k_2k_3=(k_2+k_3+\sigma)^2,\]
because $(k_2+k_3+\sigma)^2=(k_2+k_3)^2+2\sigma(k_2+k_3)+\sigma^2$ and $\sigma^2=k_1k_2+k_2k_3+k_3k_1$. The same holds for the other two square roots, so the right side of $(*)$ becomes
\[k_1(k_2+k_3+\sigma)+k_2(k_3+k_1+\sigma)+k_3(k_1+k_2+\sigma)=2\sigma^2+\sigma(k_1+k_2+k_3)=\sigma k ,\]
and $(*)$ holds. It has no other positive solution: after dividing by $k$, the right side becomes $\displaystyle\sum k_1\sqrt{\dfrac{k_2+k_3}{k}+\dfrac{k_2k_3}{k^2}}$, which strictly decreases as $k$ increases, while the left side is the constant $\sigma$.

So $k=k_1+k_2+k_3+2\sqrt{k_1k_2+k_2k_3+k_3k_1}$ (this is Descartes' circle theorem), and in terms of the radii
\[\boxed{r=\dfrac{r_1r_2r_3}{r_1r_2+r_2r_3+r_3r_1+2\sqrt{r_1r_2r_3\left(r_1+r_2+r_3\right)}}} .\]
For example, three unit circles give $r=\dfrac{1}{3+2\sqrt3}=\dfrac{2\sqrt3-3}{3}\approx0.155$.

\emph{Remark.} There can be a second circle tangent to all three, outside the gap, with curvature $k_1+k_2+k_3-2\sigma$; when this number is positive, that circle is also externally tangent to all three (for example $r_1=r_2=1$, $r_3=\dfrac{1}{10}$). That is why the question asks for the circle in the gap.
