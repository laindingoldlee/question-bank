Let $p_n(k)$ be the probability of exactly $k$ makes in the first $n$ shots ($n\ge2$). We prove by induction that $p_n(k)=\dfrac{1}{n-1}$ for $k=1,2,\dots,n-1$.

For $n=2$: exactly one make, with probability $1=\dfrac11$.

Suppose it is true for $n$. To have $k$ makes after $n+1$ shots, either she had $k-1$ makes after $n$ shots and made the next (probability $\dfrac{k-1}{n}$), or she had $k$ makes and missed (probability $1-\dfrac kn=\dfrac{n-k}{n}$). For $1\le k\le n$,
\[p_{n+1}(k)=\dfrac1{n-1}\cdot\dfrac{k-1}{n}+\dfrac1{n-1}\cdot\dfrac{n-k}{n}=\dfrac{1}{n-1}\cdot\dfrac{n-1}{n}=\dfrac1n,\]
where a term with $k-1=0$ or $k=n$ simply vanishes (those numbers of makes were impossible after $n$ shots, and the formula gives the factor $0$ anyway). This completes the induction.

So after $100$ shots each number of makes from $1$ to $99$ is equally likely, and the probability of exactly $50$ makes is $\boxed{\dfrac1{99}}$.
