Since $1000=8\cdot125$, find the remainders modulo $8$ and modulo $125$ and combine them.
\begin{itemize}
\item Modulo $8$: $2^{2011}\equiv0$.
\item Modulo $125$: $\varphi(125)=100$ and $\gcd(2,125)=1$, so by Euler's theorem $2^{100}\equiv1$ and $2^{2011}=\left(2^{100}\right)^{20}\cdot2^{11}\equiv2^{11}=2048\equiv48 \pmod{125}$.
\end{itemize}
The number $48$ is divisible by $8$ and is $48$ modulo $125$, so by the Chinese remainder theorem the remainder is $\boxed{48}$.

\textbf{General approach.} To find $a^n \bmod 1000$, split $1000=8\cdot125$. Modulo $125$, reduce the exponent modulo $\varphi(125)=100$ when $\gcd(a,5)=1$ (if $5\mid a$ and $n\ge3$ the remainder is $0$). Modulo $8$, use $\varphi(8)=4$ or the fact that $a^n\equiv0$ when $a$ is even and $n\ge3$. Then combine the two remainders.
