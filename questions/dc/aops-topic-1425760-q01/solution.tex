Let $T$ be the time at which Johnny normally reaches school; the train passes the school at time $T$ every day.

On the late day Johnny reaches school at $T+250$ seconds. At $12$ mph he walks one mile in $\dfrac{1}{12}$ hour $=300$ seconds, so he is one mile from school at time
\[
T+250-300=T-50\text{ seconds}.
\]
At that moment the train passes him, so the train is one mile before the school at time $T-50$ seconds and at the school at time $T$. It covers one mile in $50$ seconds, so its speed is
\[
\dfrac{1\text{ mile}}{50\text{ s}}=\dfrac{3600}{50}\text{ mph}=\boxed{72\text{ mph}}.
\]
(If the train passed him one \emph{hour} before he reached school, as the original wording says, he would be $12$ miles from school at time $T+250-3600$ seconds, and the train would cover $12$ miles in $3350$ seconds, giving the unlikely speed $\dfrac{864}{67}\approx12.9$ mph.)
