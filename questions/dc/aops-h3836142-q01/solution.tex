The observed equality follows from $AB\parallel DC$ and alternate interior angles. It compares angles at different vertices, so it does not establish that $\angle ABD=\angle DBC$.

For example take $A=(0,0),B=(4,0),C=(4,3),D=(0,3)$. The diagonal makes an angle $\arctan\dfrac{3}{4}$ with $BA$, which is not $45^\circ$, so it does not bisect the right angle at $B$.

For a rectangle of width $w$ and height $h$, the two angles at $B$ are equal exactly when the diagonal has slope magnitude $\dfrac{h}{w}=1$. Thus the diagonal is an angle bisector exactly when $\boxed{w=h}$, that is, when the rectangle is a square.

\end{asy}
