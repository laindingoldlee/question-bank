Take an equilateral triangle of side $2$ and drop an altitude. It bisects the base, giving a right triangle with hypotenuse $2$, short leg $1$ and, by Pythagoras, long leg $\sqrt{4-1}=\sqrt3$ opposite the $60^\circ$ angle. So $\sin60^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$.

Take an isosceles right triangle with legs $1$; its hypotenuse is $\sqrt2$ and its acute angles are $45^\circ$. So $\sin45^\circ=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$ and $\dfrac{1}{\sin45^\circ}=\sqrt2$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(2,0),C=(1,sqrt(3)); draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9)); draw(C--(1,0),dashed); label("$60^\circ$",A,(2.5,0.8)); label("$2$",(A+C)/2,NW); label("$1$",(0.5,0),S); label("$\sqrt3$",(1,0.8),E);
pair P=(3,0),Q=(4.5,0),R=(4.5,1.5); draw(P--Q--R--cycle,linewidth(0.9)); label("$45^\circ$",P,(2.8,0.7)); label("$1$",(3.75,0),S); label("$1$",(4.5,0.75),E); label("$\sqrt2$",(3.75,0.75),NW);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}
