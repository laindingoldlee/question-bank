\textbf{(a)} Going once around the table we meet $12$ chairs and $12$ gaps, so the circumference must be at least
\[
12\cdot16+12\cdot8=288\text{ inches}.
\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair O=(0,0);real r=2.2;
draw(circle(O,r),linewidth(1));
for(int i=0;i<12;++i){
  real a=30*i;
  draw(arc(O,r,a-10,a+10),red+linewidth(3));
}
draw(arc(O,r+0.35,10,20),Arrows(TeXHead));label("$8$",(r+0.4)*dir(15),dir(15),fontsize(8pt));
draw(arc(O,r+0.35,-10,10),blue,Arrows(TeXHead));label("$16$",(r+0.4)*dir(0),E,blue+fontsize(8pt));
draw(-r*dir(70)--r*dir(70),dashed);label("$d$",0.45*r*dir(70),dir(-20));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

(Red: chairs; the gaps between them are at least $8$ inches.) The smallest diameter is
\[
d=\dfrac{288}{\pi}\approx91.673\text{ inches}\approx\boxed{91.7\text{ inches}}.
\]

\textbf{(b)} $91.7$ inches is about $7.64$ feet, less than both $12$ and $14$ feet, so \textbf{yes}, the table fits (with about $4.4$ feet to spare across the $12$-foot width, i.e.\ about $2.2$ feet on each side, which is tight once the chairs are pulled out).
