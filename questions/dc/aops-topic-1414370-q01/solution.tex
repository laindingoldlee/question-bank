\textbf{Largest.} At most $1182$ houses can have all three, since only $1182$ have a turtle. This is attained: let every turtle house also have a dog and a cat (possible since $1182\le1651\le1820$, e.g.\ the cat houses are among the dog houses and the turtle houses among the cat houses). So $x=1182$.

\textbf{Smallest.} The numbers of houses \emph{without} a dog, a cat, a turtle are $197$, $366$ and $835$. A house fails to have all three only if it misses at least one, so at most $197+366+835=1398$ houses miss some pet, and at least $2017-1398=619$ have all three. This is attained by letting $1398$ different houses each miss exactly one pet ($197$ miss only the dog, $366$ only the cat, $835$ only the turtle) and the other $619$ have all three. So $y=619$.

Hence $x-y=1182-619=\boxed{563}$.
