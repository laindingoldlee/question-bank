For $n\le63$, the original sum is at most $\dfrac{63(64)}{2}-2=2014$, too small. For $n\ge65$, erasure removes at most $n-1$, leaving at least $\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}-1\ge2079$, too large. Hence $n=64$, whose original sum is 2078. The erased amount is 58. A two-digit number with tens digit $a$ and units digit $b$ loses $9a+b$; the only number at most 64 losing 58 is 64 itself ($64-6=58$). Answer $\boxed{64}$.
