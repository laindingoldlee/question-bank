Show that there are no positive integers $a$, $b$, $c$ such that
\[\left(\dfrac {a}{c}\right)^{\dfrac{3}{2}}=1-\left(\dfrac {b}{c}\right)^{\dfrac{3}{2}},\qquad\text{that is,}\qquad a\sqrt a+b\sqrt b=c\sqrt c .\]
You may use Fermat's Last Theorem for exponent $3$: there are no positive integers $x$, $y$, $z$ with $x^3+y^3=z^3$.
