Let the expression be $N+PQ$ with $N$ four-digit and $P,Q$ two-digit. The four-digit number contributes up to about $10^4$; the product is at most $98\cdot76<7500$, so both parts matter, and the thousands digit of $N$ (worth $1000$ per unit) and the tens digits of $P,Q$ (each worth roughly $10$ times the other factor, i.e. up to about $900$ per unit) compete for the largest digits.

Comparing the options for the two largest places: putting $9$ in the thousands place of $N$ and $8,7$ as the tens digits of the factors gives $9xyz+(8a)(7b)$; the remaining digits $2,3,4,5,6$ are best used as $6,5$ in the factors' units ($84\cdot75$ or $85\cdot74$, etc.) and $6,3,2$ in $N$. Checking the few arrangements, the best is
\[
9632+84\cdot75=9632+6300=\boxed{15932}.
\]
An exhaustive check of all $8!$ placements confirms that $15932$ is the maximum.
