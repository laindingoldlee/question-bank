$1000=2^3\cdot5^3$, so $n!$ must contain at least three factors of $2$ and three factors of $5$. Factors of $2$ are plentiful (already $4!$ has three), so the factor $5$ decides. The multiples of $5$ up to $n$ each contribute at least one factor $5$, and $5,10$ give only two, while $5,10,15$ give three. So $14!$ contains $5^2$ but not $5^3$, and $15!$ contains $5^3$ (and plenty of $2$s). The answer is $\boxed{15}$.
