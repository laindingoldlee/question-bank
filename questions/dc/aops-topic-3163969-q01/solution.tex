Twice the first equation minus the second gives $10y+21z=21$. Five times the first minus the third gives $30y+57z=67$. Subtracting the latter from three times the former yields $6z=-4$, so $z=-\dfrac{2}{3}$. Then $10y-14=21$ gives $y=\dfrac{7}{2}$. Finally $3x+14-\dfrac{20}{3}=18$, yielding $x=\dfrac{32}{9}$. Thus the unique solution is $\boxed{(x,y,z)=(\dfrac{32}{9},\dfrac{7}{2},-\dfrac{2}{3})}$. Back-substitution gives the original right-hand sides $18,15,23$.
