If $n^2\equiv n\pmod{1000}$, then by induction $n^k\equiv n$ for every $k\ge1$ (as $n^{k+1}=n^k\cdot n\equiv n\cdot n\equiv n$). Conversely the property for $k=2$ requires $n^2\equiv n$. So we need $1000\mid n(n-1)$.

Since $n$ and $n-1$ are coprime, $8$ divides one of them and $125$ divides one of them. For a three-digit $n$ (so neither $n$ nor $n-1$ is divisible by $1000$) we need either
\begin{itemize}
\item $125\mid n$ and $8\mid n-1$: $n\in\{125,250,\dots,875\}$ with $n\equiv1\pmod8$: $n=625$;
\item $8\mid n$ and $125\mid n-1$: $n\in\{126,251,376,501,626,751,876\}$ with $8\mid n$: $n=376$.
\end{itemize}
(The cases $1000\mid n$ or $1000\mid n-1$ give no three-digit numbers.) Indeed $625^2=390625$ and $376^2=141376$. So the two numbers are $376$ and $625$, and the difference is $625-376=\boxed{249}$.
