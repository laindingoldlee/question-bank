Every roll ends the game with probability $\dfrac16$, so the game ends with probability $1$. Let $a$ be the probability that Player $1$ wins when Player $1$ is about to roll, and $b_j$ the probability that Player $1$ wins when Player $j$ ($2\le j\le5$) is about to roll.

\textbf{From Player $j\ne1$.} With probability $\dfrac16$ Player $j$ rolls a six and wins (Player $1$ loses); with probability $\dfrac16$ the die goes to Player $1$; and with probability $\dfrac16$ each it goes to Players $2,3,4,5$. So
\[b_j=\dfrac16a+\dfrac16\left(b_2+b_3+b_4+b_5\right)\qquad(j=2,3,4,5).\]
The right side is the same for every $j$, so $b_2=b_3=b_4=b_5=b$, which is why one unknown suffices. Then
\[b=\dfrac16a+\dfrac46b\iff b=\dfrac a2.\]

\textbf{From Player $1$.} With probability $\dfrac16$ he rolls a six and wins, with probability $\dfrac16$ he rolls a $1$ and rolls again, and with probability $\dfrac46$ the die goes to another player:
\[a=\dfrac16+\dfrac16a+\dfrac46b=\dfrac16+\dfrac16a+\dfrac13a\iff\dfrac12a=\dfrac16\iff a=\boxed{\dfrac13}.\]
