If $p\equiv1\pmod3$, then $10p-1$ is divisible by $3$ and greater than $3$, impossible. If $p\equiv2\pmod3$, then $2p-1$ is divisible by $3$, so it must equal $3$, giving $p=2$. The remaining prime divisible by $3$ is $p=3$. Both work: their companion primes are $(3,19)$ and $(5,29)$. The sum is $\boxed5$.
