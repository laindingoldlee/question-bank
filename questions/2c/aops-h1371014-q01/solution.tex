Subtracting consecutive four-term sums shows that positions $1,5$ have equal residues modulo three, as do $2,6$ and $3,7$. The multiset of residues has three zeros, two ones, and two twos. Hence the unpaired fourth position must be zero, while the three paired positions use residues $0,1,2$ in any order. Each resulting first four residues sums to zero, so all six patterns work. For each pattern the distinct numbers can be placed in $3!\,2!\,2!=24$ ways. Thus the answer is $\boxed{6\cdot24=144}$.
