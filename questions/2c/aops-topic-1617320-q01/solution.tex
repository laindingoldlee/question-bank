Note $\sqrt{180}=6\sqrt5$. Suppose $\sqrt{N+6\sqrt5}=c+\sqrt d$ with positive integers $c,d$ and an integer $N$. Squaring,
\[
N+6\sqrt5=c^2+d+2c\sqrt d,\qquad\text{so}\qquad 2c\sqrt d=(N-c^2-d)+6\sqrt5 .
\]
Squaring again, with $m=N-c^2-d$,
\[
4c^2d=m^2+180+12m\sqrt5 .
\]
Since $\sqrt5$ is irrational and all other quantities are integers, $m=0$, and then $4c^2d=180$, that is $c^2d=45=3^2\cdot5$. So $(c,d)=(1,45)$ or $(3,5)$, and $N=c^2+d$ is $46$ or $14$.

\textbf{(a)} For $N=54$ neither pair works ($c^2+d\ne54$), so no such $c,d$ exist.

\textbf{(b)} For $N=14$ we need $(c,d)=(3,5)$, and indeed $(3+\sqrt5)^2=9+6\sqrt5+5=14+\sqrt{180}$. Since $3+\sqrt5>0$, $\sqrt{14+\sqrt{180}}=3+\sqrt5$, and
\[
c+d=\boxed{8}.
\]
(The only other value that works is $N=46$: $\sqrt{46+\sqrt{180}}=1+\sqrt{45}$.)
