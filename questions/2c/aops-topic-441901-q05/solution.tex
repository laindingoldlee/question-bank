Call the people $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$ in order around the table, so the neighbouring pairs are $AB$, $BC$, $CD$ and $DA$; the opposite pairs $A,C$ and $B,D$ may agree. There are $6^4=1296$ equally likely outcomes. Count the good ones by whether $A$ and $C$ roll the same number.
\begin{itemize}
\item $A=C$: $6$ choices for $A$ (and $C$), then $B$ and $D$ each avoid that number: $6\cdot5\cdot5=150$.
\item $A\ne C$: $6\cdot5$ choices for $A$ and $C$, then $B$ and $D$ each avoid two numbers: $6\cdot5\cdot4\cdot4=480$.
\end{itemize}
So there are $630$ good outcomes and the probability is
\[
\dfrac{630}{1296}=\boxed{\dfrac{35}{72}}.
\]
