The two feet from $P$ to lines through a vertex $V$ lie on the circle with diameter $PV$. The chord joining them therefore has length $PV$ times the sine of the angle between those lines. Consequently
\[A_1B_1=PB\sin B,\quad B_1C_1=PC\sin C,\quad C_1D_1=PD\sin D,\quad D_1A_1=PA\sin A.\]
Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary, so $\sin D=\sin B$ and $\sin C=\sin A$. Thus the two opposite-side sums are $BD\sin B$ and $AC\sin A$. By the extended sine rule in triangles on the original circumcircle of radius $R$, $BD=2R\sin A$ and $AC=2R\sin B$. Both sums equal $2R\sin A\sin B$, proving the identity.
