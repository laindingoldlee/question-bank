The order of the pairs, and the order within a pair, do not matter.

\textbf{Method 1.} Take the student whose name is first alphabetically: there are $5$ choices for the partner. Among the remaining four students, take the first alphabetically again: $3$ choices for the partner. The last two students form the third pair. So there are $5\cdot3\cdot1=\boxed{15}$ groupings.

\textbf{Method 2.} Choose an ordered list of pairs: $\dbinom62\dbinom42\dbinom22=15\cdot6\cdot1=90$ ways. Each grouping into three pairs is counted $3!=6$ times (once for each order of its pairs), so there are $\dfrac{90}{6}=15$ groupings.
