Put $C=(0,0)$, $D=(40,0)$. Solving $BC=25$ and $BD=39$ gives $B=(\dfrac{44}{5},\dfrac{117}{5})$, taking $B$ above $CD$. Since $P$ is on the perpendicular bisector, write $P=(20,t)$; then $PC=PD$.

The tangent powers of $P$ to the two circles are equal, so $P$ is on their radical axis $AB$. Thus $A$ is the intersection of $PB$ with the $x$-axis. Parametrising that line gives
\[
\overline{PA}\,\overline{PB}
=\dfrac{t}{t-\dfrac{117}{5}}\left[\left(\dfrac{56}{5}\right)^2+
\left(t-\dfrac{117}{5}\right)^2\right],
\]
where the product uses directed distances on the common line. Equating this to $PC^2=400+t^2$ simplifies to
\[
(t+15)(3t-80)=0.
\]
For $t=\dfrac{80}{3}$, the $x$-coordinate of $A$ is $-\dfrac{500}{7}$, outside $CD$. For $t=-15$, it is $\dfrac{125}{8}$, inside $CD$. Therefore
\[
\boxed{PC=\sqrt{20^2+15^2}=25}.
\]
