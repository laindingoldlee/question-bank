Since $10\equiv-1\pmod{11}$, a decimal integer is congruent modulo $11$ to the alternating sum of its digits. Thus that sum must be a multiple of $11$, not necessarily zero. For $153461$, the alternating sum is $1-5+3-4+6-1=0$, so it is divisible by $11$. For $90001$ it is $9-0+0-0+1=10$, so it is not divisible by $11$. For $90002$ it is $11$, so it is divisible by $11$, with quotient $8182$.
