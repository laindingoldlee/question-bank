The four dominoes cover $8$ squares, and these squares form an edge-connected set containing the corner square at $A$ and the corner square at $B$. Going from the square at $A$ to the square at $B$ through edge-adjacent squares needs at least $4$ steps to the right and $3$ steps down, i.e.\ at least $7$ steps through $8$ squares. With only $8$ squares available, the covered squares must form exactly such a shortest path: a staircase of $7$ steps, each to the right or down.

Conversely, every such staircase of $8$ squares is covered in exactly one way by four dominoes: the square at $A$ has only one neighbour on the path, so it must be paired with the second square; then the third square must be paired with the fourth, and so on. Consecutive dominoes then share a side, so each staircase gives exactly one valid arrangement.

A staircase is determined by which $3$ of the $7$ steps go down, so the number of arrangements is
\[
\dbinom73=\boxed{35}.
\]
