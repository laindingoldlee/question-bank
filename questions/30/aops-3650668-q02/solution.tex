A boundary cell has at most five neighbours, so cannot show six. Every interior cell has eight neighbours and can have six of them mined. There are $(19-2)^2=\boxed{289}$ such positions. This asks which positions are individually possible, not how many sixes can coexist.
\begin{asy}
import graph;import geometry;usepackage("amsmath");size(7cm);
draw((0,0)--(5,0)--(5,5)--(0,5)--cycle);filldraw((.5,.5)--(4.5,.5)--(4.5,4.5)--(.5,4.5)--cycle,lightblue,blue);label("$17\times17$ interior",(2.5,2.5));label("$19\times19$ board",(2.5,-.5));
\end{asy}
