There are $\dbinom93=84$ equally likely choices. Lengths $a<b<c$ form a triangle exactly when $a+b>c$ (the other two triangle inequalities hold automatically, since $c$ is the longest).

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(10cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(6,0),C=(27/12,sqrt(16-(27/12)^2));
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
label("$6$",(A+B)/2,S);label("$4$",(A+C)/2,NW);label("$5$",(B+C)/2,NE);
label("$4+5>6$",(3,-1.3));
pair o=(8.5,0);
draw(o--o+(6,0),linewidth(0.9));draw(o--o+2*dir(25),linewidth(0.9));draw(o+(6,0)--o+(6,0)+3*dir(155),linewidth(0.9));
label("$6$",o+(3,0),S);label("$2$",o+dir(25),NW);label("$3$",o+(6,0)+1.5*dir(155),NE);
label("$2+3<6$: no triangle",o+(3,-1.3));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Count by the longest straw $c$: we count the pairs $a<b<c$ with $a+b>c$:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccccc}
c&3&4&5&6&7&8&9\\\hline
\text{pairs }(a,b)&0&1&2&4&6&9&12
\end{array}
\]
For example, for $c=6$ the pairs are $(2,5),(3,4),(3,5),(4,5)$, and for $c=9$ they are the pairs from $\{2,\dots,8\}$ with sum at least $10$: $(2,8),(3,7),(3,8),(4,6),(4,7),(4,8),(5,6),(5,7),(5,8),(6,7),(6,8),(7,8)$. The total is $0+1+2+4+6+9+12=34$, so the probability is
\[
\dfrac{34}{84}=\boxed{\dfrac{17}{42}}.
\]
