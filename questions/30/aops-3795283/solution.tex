Put $m=n+1$. If $m$ is composite, choose a prime divisor $p<m$. Since $p\le n$, $p\mid n!$, whence $p\nmid n!+1$; therefore $m$ cannot divide it. If $m$ is prime, pair each nonzero residue with its multiplicative inverse modulo $m$. Only $1$ and $-1$ are self-inverse (for $m=2$, the result follows directly). Thus $(m-1)!\equiv-1\pmod m$. All and only the solutions are $\boxed{n=p-1\text{ with }p\text{ prime}}$.
