The statement is false: \textbf{no} equilateral triangle has all three vertices at lattice points.

Suppose $A$, $B$, $C$ were lattice points forming an equilateral triangle with side $s$.

\emph{The area is rational.} By the shoelace formula,
\[
[ABC]=\dfrac{1}{2}\left|x_A(y_B-y_C)+x_B(y_C-y_A)+x_C(y_A-y_B)\right|,
\]
which is half an integer, hence rational.

\emph{The area is irrational.} The squared side $s^2=(x_A-x_B)^2+(y_A-y_B)^2$ is a positive integer, and the area of an equilateral triangle is $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}s^2$. Since $\sqrt3$ is irrational and $\dfrac{s^2}{4}$ is a nonzero rational, the area is irrational.

This contradiction shows that such a triangle does not exist. For example, with $A=(0,0)$ and $B=(4,0)$ the third vertex must be $(2,\pm2\sqrt3)$, which is not a lattice point.

\end{asy}
