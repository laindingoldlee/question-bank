Consider the equation
\[x^{\dfrac{3}{2}}+8x^{\dfrac{1}{2}}=-16x^{-\dfrac{1}{2}}.\]
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Show that the equation has no real solution (with $x^{\dfrac{1}{2}}=\sqrt x$ for real $x\ge0$).
\item[(b)] Explain why $x=0$ is not a solution.
\item[(c)] Show that $x=-4$ satisfies the equation if the powers are interpreted with the principal complex square root, $(-4)^{\dfrac{1}{2}}=2i$, $(-4)^{\dfrac{3}{2}}=(-4)\cdot2i$ and $(-4)^{-\dfrac{1}{2}}=\dfrac{1}{2i}$.
\end{enumerate}
