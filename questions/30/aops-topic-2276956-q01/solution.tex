\textbf{(a)} For real numbers, $x^{-\dfrac{1}{2}}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt x}$ requires $x>0$. Multiplying by $\sqrt x>0$ turns the equation into $x^2+8x=-16$, i.e. $(x+4)^2=0$, so $x=-4$, which is not positive. Alternatively: for $x>0$ the left side is positive and the right side is negative. So there is \textbf{no real solution}.

\textbf{(b)} $x^{-\dfrac{1}{2}}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt x}$ is undefined at $x=0$, so the right side is undefined and $0$ is not in the domain. (Putting $0$ into the ``cleared'' equation $x^2+8x+16=0$ fails anyway: $16\ne0$.)

\textbf{(c)} With $(-4)^{\dfrac{1}{2}}=2i$: the left side is $(-4)(2i)+8(2i)=-8i+16i=8i$, and the right side is $-\dfrac{16}{2i}=8i$. So $x=-4$ works only if complex values are allowed.
