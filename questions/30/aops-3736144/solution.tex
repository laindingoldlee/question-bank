There are infinitely many such triangles. Consider sides $2u-1,2u,2u+1$, where $u^2-3v^2=1$ and $u,v$ are positive integers. Heron\textquotesingle s formula gives area squared $3u(u+1)u(u-1)=3u^2(u^2-1)=9u^2v^2$, so the area is $3uv$. Starting from $(u,v)=(2,1)$, define $u\prime=2u+3v$, $v\prime=u+2v$. Direct expansion gives $(u\prime)^2-3(v\prime)^2=u^2-3v^2=1$, and $u\prime>u$. This produces infinitely many distinct triangles: initially $(3,4,5)$, $(13,14,15)$, and so on. Their perimeters are $6u$, positive and unbounded. Therefore $\boxed{\text{there is no finite sum}}$.
