\textbf{Prime factorisation.} The exponent of a prime in the product is the sum of its exponents in the seven factorials:
\begin{itemize}
\item $2$: $0+1+1+3+3+4+4=16$;
\item $3$: $0+0+1+1+1+2+2=7$;
\item $5$: $1+1+1=3$ (from $5!,6!,7!$);
\item $7$: $1$ (from $7!$).
\end{itemize}
So the product is $2^{16}\cdot3^7\cdot5^3\cdot7$.

\textbf{Square divisors.} A divisor $2^a3^b5^c7^d$ is a perfect square exactly when all exponents are even: $a\in\{0,2,\dots,16\}$ ($9$ choices), $b\in\{0,2,4,6\}$ ($4$), $c\in\{0,2\}$ ($2$), $d=0$ ($1$). The number of perfect-square divisors is
\[
9\cdot4\cdot2\cdot1=\boxed{72}.
\]
