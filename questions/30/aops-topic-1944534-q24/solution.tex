The boys are distinct and can be arranged in their $10$ places in $10!$ ways, so $a$ is the number of ways to choose the positions of the girls (in the fixed order $P,D,M,S$) with $pdm$ even. Choosing $4$ of the $14$ positions gives $\binom{14}4=1001$ patterns in all.

A pattern is described by $x+p+d+m+y=10$, where $x$ boys stand before Patricia and $y$ after Sarah, all nonnegative. The product $pdm$ is odd exactly when $p,d,m$ are all odd. Write $p=2p'+1$, $d=2d'+1$, $m=2m'+1$; then $x+y+2(p'+d'+m')=7$. If $p'+d'+m'=j$ ($j=0,1,2,3$), there are $\binom{j+2}2$ choices of $(p',d',m')$ and $8-2j$ choices of $(x,y)$ with $x+y=7-2j$:
\[
1\cdot8+3\cdot6+6\cdot4+10\cdot2=8+18+24+20=70 .
\]
So $a=1001-70=\boxed{\textbf{(A) }931}$.
