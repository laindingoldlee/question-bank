Scale the altitude to one and let the common ratio be $r>1$. Then $AB=r$, $AC=r^2$, and $BC=r^3$. Because the altitude foot lies inside $BC$, the two right triangles give $\sqrt{r^2-1}+\sqrt{r^4-1}=r^3$. Set $t=r^2>1$. Squaring and rearranging gives $2\sqrt{(t-1)(t^2-1)}=t^3-t^2-t+2$. Squaring again and simplifying yields $t^2(t^2-t-1)^2=0$. Thus $t=\dfrac{(1+\sqrt5)}{2}$; direct substitution in the unsquared equation verifies it. The requested squared ratio is $\boxed{\dfrac{(1+\sqrt5)}{2}}$.
