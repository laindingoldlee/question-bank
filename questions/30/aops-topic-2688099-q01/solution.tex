Let $f(x)=C+\dfrac{D-C}{B-A}(x-A)$. Since $\dfrac{D-C}{B-A}>0$, $f$ is increasing, $f(A)=C$ and $f(B)=D$, so $f$ maps $[A,B]$ into $[C,D]$. It has the inverse $g(y)=A+\dfrac{B-A}{D-C}(y-C)$, which maps $[C,D]$ into $[A,B]$, and $f(g(y))=y$, $g(f(x))=x$. Hence $f$ is a bijection from $[A,B]$ to $[C,D]$. $\blacksquare$
