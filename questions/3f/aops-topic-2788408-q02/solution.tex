$\boxed{\text{No}}$. Take an isosceles triangle with base $1000$ cm and height $0.4$ cm (not to scale):
\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
draw((0,0)--(10,0)--(5,0.4)--cycle);draw((5,0)--(5,0.4),dashed);label("base $1000$",(5,0),S);label("height $0.4$",(5,0.4),N);
\end{asy}

its area is $200$ cm$^2$. Each leg is $\sqrt{500^2+0.4^2}>500$ cm, so the altitude to a leg is $\dfrac{2\cdot200}{\text{leg}}<\dfrac{400}{500}=0.8$ cm. All three altitudes are below $1$ cm, but the area is $200$ cm$^2$.
