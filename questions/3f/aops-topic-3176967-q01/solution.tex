The remaining coins are not three unobserved independent fair coins: some were already inspected and are known to be opposite to the removed type. Let $T$ be the stopping position. Then $T=3,4,5$ with probabilities $\dfrac{1}{4},\dfrac{3}{8},\dfrac{3}{8}$, respectively. For $T=3$ there are three unobserved coins, so success probability $\dfrac{1}{8}$; for $T=4$, one remaining coin is already known opposite and two are unobserved, giving $\dfrac{1}{4}$; for $T=5$, two are known opposite and only one is unobserved, giving $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Thus the total is $\dfrac{1}{4}\cdot\dfrac{1}{8}+\dfrac{3}{8}\cdot\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{3}{8}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}=\boxed{\dfrac{5}{16}}$. This also equals $\dfrac{\binom63}{2^6}$, since success means exactly three heads overall.
