Let
\[S=1-\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{3\cdot5}-\dfrac{1}{3\cdot5\cdot7}+\cdots=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-1)^k}{1\cdot3\cdot5\cdots(2k+1)} .\]
For $N\ge1$ let $x_N$ be the finite continued fraction
\[x_N=\dfrac{1}{2+\dfrac{3}{4+\dfrac{5}{6+\cdots+\dfrac{2N-1}{2N}}}},\qquad\text{so that}\quad x_1=\dfrac{1}{2},\quad x_2=\dfrac{1}{2+\dfrac{3}{4}},\quad x_3=\dfrac{1}{2+\dfrac{3}{4+\dfrac{5}{6}}} .\]
The infinite continued fraction $X=\dfrac{1}{2+\dfrac{3}{4+\dfrac{5}{6+\cdots}}}$ is defined as the limit of $x_N$ as $N\to\infty$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Prove that the limit exists and that $X=\dfrac{1}{S}-1$.
\item[(b)] Find $X$ to four decimal places.
\end{enumerate}
