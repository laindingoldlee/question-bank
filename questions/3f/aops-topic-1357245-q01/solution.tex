Use the difference of squares and the sum and difference of cubes:
\[
2^{12}-1=\left(2^6-1\right)\left(2^6+1\right),
\]
\[
2^6-1=\left(2^3-1\right)\left(2^3+1\right)=7\cdot9,\qquad 2^6+1=\left(2^2\right)^3+1=\left(2^2+1\right)\left(2^4-2^2+1\right)=5\cdot13 .
\]
So $2^{12}-1=4095=\boxed{3^2\cdot5\cdot7\cdot13}$. The same ideas work for any $a^n\pm1$: $a^n-1$ is divisible by $a^d-1$ for every divisor $d$ of $n$, and $a^n+1$ is divisible by $a^{\dfrac {n}{m}}+1$ for every odd divisor $m$ of $n$.
