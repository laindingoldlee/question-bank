$\boxed{n+1}$ values suffice: if two degree-at-most-$ n $ polynomials agree at $ n+1 $ distinct inputs, their difference has more roots than its degree and must be zero. Fewer cannot guarantee uniqueness. Given $ k\le n $ sampled integer inputs $ x_1,\ldots,x_k $, add to a candidate polynomial a nonzero integer multiple of $\prod_{j=1}^k(x-x_j)$. It preserves the sampled values and integer coefficients. Choose the multiplier to avoid cancellation of the leading term if $ k=n $; then both polynomials still have degree exactly $ n $. For $ n=0 $, one value is plainly necessary.
