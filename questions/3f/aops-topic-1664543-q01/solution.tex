\textbf{(a)} The speed increases by $75-30=45$ km/h. In one hour ($3600$ seconds) a speed of $45$ km/h covers $45000$ metres, so $45$ km/h is $\dfrac{45000}{3600}=12.5$ metres per second. The speed grows by $12.5$ metres per second in $15$ seconds, so the acceleration is
\[
\dfrac{12.5}{15}=\boxed{\dfrac{5}{6}\ \text{metres per second squared}}:
\]
during the first $15$ seconds the speed grows by $\dfrac{5}{6}$ metre per second every second.

\textbf{(b)} After $15$ seconds the car slows down uniformly from $75$ km/h to $0$ in $50$ seconds, losing $\dfrac{75}{50}=1.5$ km/h each second. At $30$ seconds it has been slowing down for $15$ seconds, so its speed is
\[
75-15\cdot1.5=\boxed{52.5\text{ km per hour}}.
\]
(The answer $90$ km/h comes from continuing the first acceleration past $15$ seconds, but by then the car is already slowing down.)
