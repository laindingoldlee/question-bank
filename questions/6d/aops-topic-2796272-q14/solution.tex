Both ages are two-digit numbers now and in $13$ years. Writing $\overline{SG}=100S+(S+17)=101S+17$, we need $101S+17$ and $101(S+13)+17=101S+1330$ both to be squares; they differ by $1313=13\cdot101$. If $m^2-n^2=1313$ then $(m-n)(m+n)=1313\in\{1\cdot1313,\ 13\cdot101\}$, giving $(m,n)=(657,656)$ (too large) or $(57,44)$. So $101S+17=44^2=1936$ and $S=\boxed{19}$ (Gil is $36$; later $3249=57^2$).
