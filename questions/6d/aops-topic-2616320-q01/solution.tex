For $n=4$: $3!=6$ is not divisible by $4$.

Now let $n>4$ be composite.
\begin{itemize}
\item If $n=ab$ with $1<a<b<n$, then $a$ and $b$ are two different factors among $1,2,\dots,n-1$, so $ab=n$ divides $(n-1)!$.
\item Otherwise $n$ has no such factorisation, which forces $n=p^2$ for a prime $p$; since $n>4$, $p\ge3$. Then $p<2p<p^2=n$ (as $p>2$), so $p$ and $2p$ are two different factors of $(n-1)!$, and $2p^2$, hence $n=p^2$, divides $(n-1)!$.
\end{itemize}
In both cases $n\mid(n-1)!$. $\blacksquare$
