The graph of $y=3f(x-2)$ is obtained from the graph of $y=f(x)$ in two steps.

\textbf{Shift.} $y=f(x-2)$ is the graph of $f$ moved $2$ units to the right. A translation does not change areas, so the region between $y=f(x-2)$ and the $x$-axis still has area $10$.

\textbf{Vertical stretch.} $y=3f(x-2)$ multiplies every height by $3$ while keeping every horizontal position. Every vertical segment from the $x$-axis to the graph becomes $3$ times as long, so (cutting the region into thin vertical strips, each three times as tall and just as wide) the area is multiplied by $3$.

Hence the new area is $3\cdot10=\boxed{30}$ square units.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
import graph;
real f(real x){return 15/16*x*(4-x);}
real g(real x){return 3*f(x-2);}
fill(graph(f,0,4)--cycle,paleblue);
draw((-0.5,0)--(7,0),gray,Arrow(TeXHead));draw((0,-0.5)--(0,12.5),gray,Arrow(TeXHead));
label("$x$",(7,0),E,gray);label("$y$",(0,12.5),N,gray);
draw(graph(f,0,4),blue+linewidth(0.9));
draw(graph(g,2,6),red+linewidth(0.9));
label("$y=f(x)$",(1.0,f(1.0)),W,blue);
label("$y=3f(x-2)$",(5.1,g(5.1)),E,red);
label("$10$",(1.05,1.2),blue);label("$30$",(4.6,5.5),red);
for(int i=2;i<=6;i+=2){draw((i,0)--(i,-0.2));label("$"+string(i)+"$",(i,-0.2),S);}
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

(Illustration with the sample function $f(x)=\dfrac{15}{16}x(4-x)$ on $0\le x\le4$, whose region has area $10$: the red graph is $y=3f(x-2)$, shifted $2$ to the right and three times as tall, enclosing area $30$.)
