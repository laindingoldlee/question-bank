Jack walks for $2$ hours, that is, $120$ minutes. Jill reaches the top at $8{:}13.5$, so she walks for $60+13.5=73.5$ minutes. They cover the same distance, so their speeds are in the inverse ratio of their times:
\[\dfrac{\text{Jill's speed}}{\text{Jack's speed}}=\dfrac{120}{73.5}=\dfrac{240}{147}=\boxed{\dfrac{80}{49}\approx1.63}.\]
(If the distance to the top is $d$ miles, Jill's speed is $\dfrac{d}{73.5\div60}=\dfrac{40d}{49}$ miles per hour, so a speed in miles per hour can only be found once $d$ is known.)
