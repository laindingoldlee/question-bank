Each move changes the column number by $-1$, $0$ or $+1$, and the row number by $-1$, $0$ or $+1$ (not both by $0$). The king must gain $7$ in columns and $7$ in rows in $8$ moves.

\textbf{Columns.} Eight changes from $\{-1,0,1\}$ add up to $7$. A $-1$ is impossible, since the other seven changes add up to at most $7<8$. So exactly one move keeps the column and seven moves increase it by $1$. In the same way, exactly one move keeps the row and seven increase it.

\textbf{The moves.} The move that keeps the column cannot also keep the row (a king must move). So one move is straight up $(0,+1)$, a different move is straight right $(+1,0)$, and the other six moves are diagonal $(+1,+1)$. Every such sequence stays on the board, since the king only moves up and to the right. Choosing which of the $8$ moves is the up move and which of the remaining $7$ is the right move gives
\[8\cdot7=\boxed{56}\]
ways.
