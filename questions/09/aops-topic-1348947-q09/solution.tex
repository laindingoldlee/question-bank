Count in the other order: first choose the $11$ distinguished officers ($\dbinom{79}{11}$ ways), then choose the other officers from the remaining $68$ soldiers; there must be at least one more officer, so any nonempty subset of the $68$ works ($2^{68}-1$ ways). Hence
\[
n=\sum_{k\ge12}\dbinom{79}{k}\dbinom{k}{11}=\dbinom{79}{11}\left(2^{68}-1\right).
\]
Modulo $1000$: $\dbinom{79}{11}=9036996468045\equiv45$. For $2^{68}$: $2^{10}=1024\equiv24$, $2^{20}\equiv576$, $2^{40}\equiv576^2=331776\equiv776$, $2^{60}\equiv776\cdot576=446976\equiv976$, and $2^{68}=2^{60}\cdot256\equiv976\cdot256=249856\equiv856$. So $2^{68}-1\equiv855$ and
\[
n\equiv45\cdot855=38475\equiv\boxed{475}\pmod{1000}.
\]
