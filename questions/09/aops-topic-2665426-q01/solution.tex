\emph{Factoring.} Clearing denominators, $10b+10c=3bc$. Multiply by $3$ and add $100$:
\[9bc-30b-30c+100=100,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad(3b-10)(3c-10)=100.\]
Both factors are $\equiv2\pmod3$, and both are at least $3-10=-7$. If both were negative, their product would be at most $7\cdot7<100$; so both are positive divisors of $100$ that are $\equiv2\pmod3$, namely $2,5,20,50$. The factor pairs are $(2,50),(5,20),(20,5),(50,2)$, giving
\[(b,c)=\boxed{(4,20),\ (5,10),\ (10,5),\ (20,4)}.\]

\emph{Bounding (another way).} The equation is $\dfrac1b+\dfrac1c=\dfrac3{10}$. If $b\le c$, then $\dfrac3{10}\le\dfrac2b$ gives $b\le6$, and $\dfrac1b<\dfrac3{10}$ gives $b\ge4$. Then $b=4$ gives $\dfrac1c=\dfrac1{20}$, $b=5$ gives $\dfrac1c=\dfrac1{10}$, and $b=6$ gives $\dfrac1c=\dfrac2{15}$, which is impossible. The same four ordered pairs result.
