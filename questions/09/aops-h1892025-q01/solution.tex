Modulo $8$, odd $m$ would give $n^2\equiv3$, impossible. Write $m=2k$. Then $n$ is odd and at least $3$, and $(n-2)(n+2)=5\cdot3^{2k}$. The two positive odd factors are coprime because their gcd divides $4$. Therefore each entire prime power must belong to one factor. If a factor is $1$, then $n=3$, $k=0$. Otherwise the factors are $5$ and $3^{2k}$; their difference $4$ forces $3^{2k}=9$, so $n=7$, $k=1$. Both work. The required sum is $\boxed{10}$.
