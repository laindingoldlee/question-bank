Once a black ball is kept, at least one black ball remains in the hand forever: the only removal operation itself adds a black ball. If a white ball is ever kept, it either remains until the end or its removal introduces a black ball. Thus success requires that every kept ball be red and that no black draw occur after the first red.

Initial black draws simply repeat the empty-hand state. The first nonblack ball is red with probability $\dfrac{3}{7}$. After it is kept, the next two draws must be red, with probabilities $\dfrac{2}{9}$ and $\dfrac{1}{8}$. Therefore the answer is
\[
\boxed{\dfrac{3}{7}\cdot\dfrac{2}{9}\cdot\dfrac{1}{8}=\dfrac{1}{84}}.
\]
