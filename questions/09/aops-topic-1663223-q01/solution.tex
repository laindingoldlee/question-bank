\textbf{First form.} Since $(\sqrt2+1)(\sqrt2-1)=1$, we have $\dfrac{1}{1+\sqrt2}=\sqrt2-1$, and so
\[
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+\sqrt2}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{1+\sqrt2}}=\sqrt{\sqrt2-1}.
\]

\textbf{Second form.} Multiply the numerator and the denominator by $\sqrt{1+\sqrt2}$:
\[
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+\sqrt2}}=\dfrac{\sqrt{1+\sqrt2}}{1+\sqrt2}=\left(\sqrt2-1\right)\sqrt{1+\sqrt2}.
\]

\textbf{They are equal.} Both are positive, so it is enough to compare their squares:
\[
\left[\left(\sqrt2-1\right)\sqrt{1+\sqrt2}\right]^2=\left(\sqrt2-1\right)^2\left(\sqrt2+1\right)=\left(\sqrt2-1\right)\cdot\left[\left(\sqrt2-1\right)\left(\sqrt2+1\right)\right]=\sqrt2-1=\left(\sqrt{\sqrt2-1}\right)^2 .
\]
So $\boxed{\sqrt{\sqrt2-1}=\left(\sqrt2-1\right)\sqrt{1+\sqrt2}}$, and both are correct answers; both have a rational denominator (namely $1$).
