\textbf{(a)} Sort the remainders modulo $100$ into the $51$ classes
\[\{0\},\quad\{50\},\quad\{1,99\},\quad\{2,98\},\quad\dots,\quad\{49,51\}\]
($2$ one-element classes and $49$ two-element classes). Among $52$ integers, by the pigeonhole principle, two of them, $a$ and $b$, have remainders in the same class. If their remainders are equal, $100\mid a-b$. Otherwise their remainders are $r$ and $100-r$, and $100\mid a+b$. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{(b)} $\boxed{\text{No}}$. Take the $51$ integers $0,1,2,\dots,50$. For two different ones, $a<b$, the difference satisfies $1\le b-a\le50$ and the sum satisfies $1\le a+b\le49+50=99$, so neither is divisible by $100$. (These are one integer from each of the $51$ classes.)
