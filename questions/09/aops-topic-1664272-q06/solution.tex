Note that $x+y>0$: otherwise $x=y=0$ and $xy+yz+zx=0\ne1$. Using $xy+yz+zx=1$,
\[
1+z^2=xy+yz+zx+z^2=(z+x)(z+y),\qquad 1+\dfrac{z}{x+y}=\dfrac{x+y+z}{x+y}.
\]
So the inequality says
\[
x+y+z\ge K(x+y)(y+z)(z+x).
\]
\textbf{$K=1$ works.} The identity $(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)=(x+y+z)(xy+yz+zx)-xyz$ gives $(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)=x+y+z-xyz\le x+y+z$, since $xyz\ge0$.

\textbf{No $K>1$ works.} Take $z=0$, $x=y=1$. Then $xy+yz+zx=1$, the left side is $1+0=1$ and the right side is $K(1+0)=K$, so $K\le1$.

The largest value is $\boxed{\textbf{(B)}\ 1}$.
