(a) The bottom layer has $k=0$ and $i+j\le3$, giving $4+3+2+1=\boxed{10}$ cubes. For a cube with $i+j+k<3$, only its negative-coordinate faces can be exposed, one for each zero coordinate. For a cube with $i+j+k=3$, all three positive-coordinate faces are also exposed. (b) Exactly three painted faces occur at $(0,0,0)$ and $(1,1,1)$, so there are $\boxed2$. (c) Exactly one painted face requires sum less than 3 and exactly one zero coordinate, giving the three permutations of $(0,1,1)$. Thus there are $\boxed3$.

\end{asy}
