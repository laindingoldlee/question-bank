Let $BD=p$ and $BE=q$. The quadrilateral $ADEC$ is triangle $ABC$ with triangle $DBE$ removed, so $[DBE]=\dfrac{1}{4}[ABC]$:
\[\dfrac{1}{2}pq\sin B=\dfrac{1}{4}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot12\cdot9\sin B,\qquad\text{so}\qquad pq=27 .\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");

pair B=(0,0), C=(9,0), A=12*(cB,sB);
real t=3*sqrt(3);
pair D=t*(cB,sB), Ep=(t,0);
filldraw(A--D--Ep--C--cycle,paleyellow,linewidth(0.9));

label("$12$",(A+B)/2+(-0.4,0.6),W); label("$9$",(7.3,0),S); label("$13$",(A+C)/2,NE);

By the law of cosines in triangle $ABC$,
\[\cos B=\dfrac{12^2+9^2-13^2}{2\cdot12\cdot9}=\dfrac{56}{216}=\dfrac{7}{27}.\]
Then, by the law of cosines in triangle $DBE$ and $p^2+q^2\ge2pq$ (AM--GM),
\[DE^2=p^2+q^2-2pq\cos B\ge2pq-2pq\cos B=54\left(1-\dfrac{7}{27}\right)=40,\]
with equality when $p=q=\sqrt{27}=3\sqrt3\approx5.20$. This is allowed, since $3\sqrt3<9=BC$ and $3\sqrt3<12=AB$. So the minimum length of $DE$ is $\boxed{2\sqrt{10}}$.
