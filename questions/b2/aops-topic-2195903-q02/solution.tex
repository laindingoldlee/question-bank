Call the sum $S$. For $n\ge1$ the ratio of the $(n+1)$st term to the $n$th term is $\dfrac{n+1}{4n}\le\dfrac{2}{4}\le\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the terms shrink at least as fast as a geometric series with ratio $\dfrac{1}{2}$; the series converges and we may manipulate it term by term.

Dividing by $4$ shifts every term one place to the right:
\[S=\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{2}{16}+\dfrac{3}{64}+\cdots,\qquad \dfrac{S}{4}=\phantom{\dfrac{1}{4}+{}}\dfrac{1}{16}+\dfrac{2}{64}+\cdots.\]
Subtracting, each numerator drops by $1$:
\[S-\dfrac{S}{4}=\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{1}{16}+\dfrac{1}{64}+\cdots=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{4}}{1-\dfrac{1}{4}}=\dfrac{1}{3}.\]
So $\dfrac{3}{4}S=\dfrac{1}{3}$ and
\[S=\dfrac{4}{3^2}=\boxed{\dfrac{4}{9}}.\]
