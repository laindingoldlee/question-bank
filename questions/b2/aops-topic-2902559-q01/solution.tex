$5\mid\overline{abc}$ and $5\mid\overline{bcd}$ give $c,d\in\{0,5\}$, so $\{c,d\}=\{0,5\}$. Reversed, the number ends in $\overline{ba}$, so $4\mid\overline{ba}$ and $a,b$ are both even or $b$ odd and $a\in\{2,6\}$.

If $c=5$, $d=0$, then $\overline{cde}=50e$ is prime only for $e\in\{3,9\}$. If $c=0$, $d=5$, then $\overline{5e}$ is prime for $e\in\{3,9\}$. Then $9\mid a+b+c$ and $9\mid e+f+g$, and the alternating sum $a-b+c-d+e-f+g\equiv0\pmod{11}$, with all digits distinct.

Checking the few remaining cases leaves exactly one number: $\boxed{6305981}$. Check: $630$ and $981$ are multiples of $9$; $630$ and $305$ of $5$; $059=59$ is prime; $6-3+0-5+9-8+1=0$; and $1895036$ ends in $36$.
