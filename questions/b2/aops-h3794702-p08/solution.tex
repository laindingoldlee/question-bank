Write the exponents of $2,5,11$ in $n$ as $a,b,c$. Their contributions to the divisor count of $110n^3$ are $3a+2,3b+2,3c+2$, all at least $2$. Since $110=2\cdot5\cdot11$ has exactly three prime factors counted with multiplicity, these contributions must be $2,5,11$ in some order, and no other prime can divide $n$. Thus $a,b,c$ are $0,1,3$ in some order. In $81n^4$, the prime $3$ contributes five choices of exponent; the other contributions are $1,5,13$. The divisor count is $5\cdot1\cdot5\cdot13=\boxed{325}$.
