Write $n=\sum_k d_k10^k$ with digits $d_k$. Since $10\equiv1\pmod9$, we have $10^k\equiv1\pmod 9$ for every $k\ge0$, so
\[
n=\sum_k d_k10^k\equiv\sum_k d_k=S(n)\pmod 9 .
\]
This holds for every positive integer, in particular for $n+1$: $S(n+1)\equiv n+1\pmod 9$. Therefore
\[
S(n+1)\equiv n+1\equiv S(n)+1\pmod 9 .\qquad\blacksquare
\]
(For example $S(1999)=28$ and $S(2000)=2$, and indeed $2\equiv29\pmod9$: the carries lower the digit sum by multiples of $9$.)
