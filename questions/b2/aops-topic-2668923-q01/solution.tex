Let $f(x)=ax^2+bx+c$ and suppose $r_1,r_2,r_3$ are distinct roots. Since $f(r_1)=0$,
\[f(x)=f(x)-f(r_1)=a\left(x^2-r_1^2\right)+b(x-r_1)=(x-r_1)\,g(x),\qquad g(x)=a(x+r_1)+b.\]
For $i=2,3$ we have $0=f(r_i)=(r_i-r_1)g(r_i)$ with $r_i\ne r_1$, so $g(r_2)=g(r_3)=0$. Subtracting, $a(r_2-r_3)=0$, so $r_2=r_3$ because $a\ne0$, a contradiction. Hence there are at most two distinct roots. $\blacksquare$
