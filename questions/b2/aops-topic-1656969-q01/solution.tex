\textbf{$f(2)$.} The squares $1,4,9,16$ are not sums of two distinct positive squares (the possible sums below $17$ are $5,10,13$), while $25=9+16$. So $f(2)=25$.

\textbf{$f(25)$.} A sum of $25$ distinct positive squares is at least
\[
1^2+2^2+\cdots+25^2=\dfrac{25\cdot26\cdot51}{6}=5525 .
\]
The smallest square that is at least $5525$ is $75^2=5625$ (as $74^2=5476$). It is attainable: we must raise the sum by $100$, and replacing $24^2$ by $26^2$ does this, since $26^2-24^2=(26-24)(26+24)=100$. Thus
\[
1^2+2^2+\cdots+23^2+25^2+26^2=5625=75^2 ,
\]
with $25$ distinct squares. Hence $f(f(2))=f(25)=\boxed{5625}$.
