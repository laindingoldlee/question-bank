Treat trees of the same kind as identical; since all arrangements of the actual trees are equally likely, every pattern of birch and non-birch positions is also equally likely (each corresponds to the same number of arrangements of the real trees).

There are $\dbinom{12}{5}=792$ ways to choose the birch positions. For no two to be adjacent, place the $7$ non-birch trees first; they create $8$ gaps (including the two ends), and the birches go into $5$ distinct gaps: $\dbinom85=56$ ways. The probability is $\dfrac{56}{792}=\dfrac{7}{99}$, so $m+n=\boxed{106}$.

The non-birch trees are not "dividers placed anywhere": in the gap method they are simply the $7$ positions not chosen for birches, and the gaps are between consecutive non-birch trees. Putting all of them at one end would leave a single gap holding all the birches, which is exactly what choosing $5$ \emph{distinct} gaps rules out. The order of maples and oaks among themselves multiplies both the favourable and the total counts by the same factor $\dbinom73$, so it cancels.
