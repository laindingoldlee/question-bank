For $z>0$,
\[1-\dfrac{1}{2+z}=\dfrac{1+z}{2+z}=\dfrac{1}{1+\dfrac{1}{1+z}}.\]
Taking $z=\dfrac{1}{3+\dfrac{1}{4+\dfrac{1}{5+\dfrac{1}{6+\dfrac{1}{7}}}}}$ gives $(x_1,\ldots,x_7)=(1,1,3,4,5,6,7)$ and sum $\boxed{27}$. The Euclidean algorithm uniquely determines a finite positive simple continued fraction with last entry greater than 1. Its only alternative positive representation splits the final 7 into $6+\dfrac{1}{1}$, requiring eight entries. Thus the seven-entry answer is unique.
