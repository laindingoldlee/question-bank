A rotation through $\alpha$ turns every direction through $\alpha$: if $A,B$ are two points of $\ell_1$ and $A',B'$ their images, then the vector $\overrightarrow{A'B'}$ is the vector $\overrightarrow{AB}$ turned through $\alpha$ (a rotation about $P$ is a rotation about the origin followed by a translation, and translations do not change vectors).

So a direction vector $\mathbf u$ of $\ell_1$ is turned into a direction vector $\mathbf u'$ of $\ell_2$ with $\angle(\mathbf u,\mathbf u')=\alpha$.
\begin{itemize}
\item Since $0^\circ<\alpha<90^\circ$, $\mathbf u'$ is not parallel to $\mathbf u$, so the lines are not parallel and therefore meet at a point $X$.
\item At $X$ the lines form angles $\alpha$ and $180^\circ-\alpha$ (the angles between the direction vectors $\pm\mathbf u$ and $\pm\mathbf u'$). As $\alpha<90^\circ$, the acute one is $\alpha$.
\end{itemize}
$\blacksquare$

\textbf{A synthetic picture} (for $P$ not on $\ell_1$). Let $Q$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $P$ to $\ell_1$, and $Q'$ its image, so $PQ'\perp\ell_2$ and $\angle QPQ'=\alpha$:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(7cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair P=(0,3);real al=60;
pair Q=(0,0);pair Q2=rotate(al,P)*Q;pair d2=dir(al);
real t=-Q2.y/d2.y;pair X=Q2+t*d2;
draw((-2.5,0)--(5,0),linewidth(1));label("$\ell_1$",(5,0),E);
draw(X-1.4*d2--X+4.2*d2,blue+linewidth(1));label("$\ell_2$",X+4.2*d2,E,blue);
draw(P--Q,dashed);draw(P--Q2,blue+dashed);
real m=0.22;draw(Q+(m,0)--Q+(m,m)--Q+(0,m));
pair u=unit(P-Q2),v=-d2;draw(Q2+m*v--Q2+m*(u+v)--Q2+m*u,blue);
draw(arc(X,0.6,0,al),red);label("$\alpha$",X+0.9*dir(al/2),red);
draw(arc(P,0.7,-90,-90+al),red);label("$\alpha$",P+1.0*dir(-90+al/2),red);
dot(P);label("$P$",P,N);dot(Q);label("$Q$",Q,S);dot(Q2);label("$Q'$",Q2,dir(al-90),blue);dot(X);label("$X$",X,SE);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

In quadrilateral $PQXQ'$ the angles at $Q$ and $Q'$ are right angles, so the angles at $P$ and $X$ add to $180^\circ$: the angle $QXQ'$ equals $180^\circ-\alpha$, and its supplement, the acute angle between the lines, is $\alpha$. (If $P$ lies on $\ell_1$, then $X=P$ and the statement is just the definition of the rotation.)
