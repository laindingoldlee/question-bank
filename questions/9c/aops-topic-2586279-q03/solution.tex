The general term is $\dfrac{n}{n+1}x^{n+1}=x^{n+1}-\dfrac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}$ for $n\ge1$. For $|x|<1$ both resulting series converge:
\[\sum_{n\ge1}x^{n+1}=\dfrac{x^2}{1-x},\qquad\sum_{n\ge1}\dfrac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}=\sum_{m\ge2}\dfrac{x^m}{m}=-\ln(1-x)-x,\]
using the series $-\ln(1-x)=x+\dfrac{x^2}{2}+\dfrac{x^3}{3}+\cdots$ for $|x|<1$ (obtained by integrating $\dfrac{1}{1-x}=1+x+x^2+\cdots$ term by term). Therefore the sum is
\[\dfrac{x^2}{1-x}+x+\ln(1-x)=\boxed{\dfrac{x}{1-x}+\ln(1-x)}.\]
For example, at $x=\dfrac12$ the sum is $1-\ln2\approx0.307$.
