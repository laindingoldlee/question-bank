For $a<0$, the nonzero coefficients alternate signs, so neither polynomial is a tower. For $a=0$, both are constant towers, so this value is excluded.

For $a>0$, list the degree-$n$ coefficients in increasing powers as $c_k=\binom nk a^k$. Their ratios
\[
\dfrac{c_{k+1}}{c_k}=a\dfrac{n-k}{k+1}
\]
decrease with $k$. All ratios are at least $1$ exactly when $a\ge n$, and all are at most $1$ exactly when $a\le\dfrac{1}{n}$. Hence the degree-$n$ polynomial is a tower precisely for $0<a\le\dfrac{1}{n}$ or $a\ge n$.

The required set is consequently $(\dfrac{1}{11},\dfrac{1}{10}]\cup[10,11)$. Its total length is $\dfrac{1}{110}+1=\dfrac{111}{110}$, giving $\boxed{m+n=221}$.
