The digits split as $1+3$, $2+2$ or $3+1$ between numerator and denominator. A one-digit over three-digit fraction is at most $\dfrac{9}{102}$, and a three-digit over one-digit fraction is at least $\dfrac{102}{9}$; both are far from $1$. So both numbers have two digits, and $\dfrac ND$ differs from $1$ by $\dfrac{|N-D|}{D}$.

To have $|N-D|=1$, the numbers must be consecutive with all four digits distinct. Consecutive two-digit numbers usually share a tens digit, so the smaller must end in $9$: $\overline{a9}$ and $\overline{(a+1)0}$. The pair uses digits $a,9,a+1,0$, which are distinct when $a+1\ne9$, i.e. $a\le7$. The largest denominator is obtained from $79$ and $80$.

For $|N-D|=1$ the best is therefore $\dfrac{79}{80}$ with distance $\dfrac{1}{80}$ (better than $\dfrac{80}{79}$, distance $\dfrac{1}{79}$). If $|N-D|\ge2$ then, since $D\le98$, the distance is at least $\dfrac{2}{98}>\dfrac{1}{80}$. The closest fraction is $\boxed{\dfrac{79}{80}}$.
