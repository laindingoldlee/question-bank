The positions of the red balls form a random $4$-element subset of the $12$ positions, so there are $\binom{12}{4}=495$ equally likely outcomes.

\textbf{Counting good subsets.} Fix position $1$.
\begin{itemize}
\item Position $1$ is red: positions $2$ and $12$ are blue, and the other $3$ red balls go in positions $3$ to $11$ (a row of $9$) with no two adjacent: $\binom{9-3+1}{3}=\binom73=35$ ways.
\item Position $1$ is blue: the $4$ red balls go in positions $2$ to $12$ (a row of $11$) with no two adjacent: $\binom{11-4+1}{4}=\binom84=70$ ways.
\end{itemize}
(Here $\binom{m-k+1}{k}$ counts the ways to choose $k$ pairwise non-adjacent places in a row of $m$: removing one place after each of the first $k-1$ chosen places turns them into an arbitrary choice from $m-k+1$ places.) So there are $35+70=105$ good subsets, and the probability is
\[
\dfrac{105}{495}=\boxed{\dfrac{7}{33}}.
\]
