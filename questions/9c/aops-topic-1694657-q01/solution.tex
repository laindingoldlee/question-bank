$P(x)^2-9=0$ means $P(x)=3$ or $P(x)=-3$. Let $U$ be the set of integer roots of $P(x)-3$ and $V$ the set of integer roots of $P(x)+3$; these sets are disjoint. Each of $P(x)\mp3$ has degree $2015$, so $|U|\le2015$ and $|V|\le2015$. If $U$ or $V$ is empty, we are done.

Suppose both are non-empty, say $U=\{u_1,\dots,u_m\}$ and $v\in V$. By the factor theorem (dividing by monic linear factors keeps integer coefficients),
\[
P(x)-3=(x-u_1)(x-u_2)\cdots(x-u_m)\,Q(x)
\]
with $Q$ a polynomial with integer coefficients. Putting $x=v$:
\[
-6=(v-u_1)(v-u_2)\cdots(v-u_m)\,Q(v).
\]
So $v-u_1,\dots,v-u_m$ are $m$ \emph{distinct} non-zero integers whose product divides $6$. At most two of them have absolute value $1$, and the others have absolute value at least $2$, and at most two of them can have absolute value $2$, etc.; five distinct non-zero integers have product of absolute value at least $1\cdot1\cdot2\cdot2\cdot3=12>6$. Hence $m\le4$. In the same way (with the roles of $3$ and $-3$ exchanged) $|V|\le4$. Then the total number of distinct integer roots is $|U|+|V|\le8\le2015$.

In every case there are at most $2015$ distinct integer roots. $\blacksquare$
