Use coordinates with $O=(0,0,0)$, so the centres of the unit cubes are the points $(x,y,z)$ with $x,y,z\in\{0,1,\dots,9\}$, and the opposite corner cube has centre $(9,9,9)$. A jump changes one coordinate by $\pm1$.

The squared distance from $O$ is $x^2+y^2+z^2$. Changing $x$ to $x+1$ increases it. Changing $x\ge1$ to $x-1$ decreases it, since $(x-1)^2<x^2$; and from $x=0$ the coordinate cannot decrease inside the cube. So the allowed jumps are exactly the jumps $+1$ in one coordinate.

Hence a route to $(9,9,9)$ is a sequence of $27$ jumps, $9$ in each direction, in any order. The number of routes is
\[
\dfrac{27!}{9!\,9!\,9!}=\boxed{227873431500}.
\]
