Let $H$ be the foot of the altitude from $A$, so $AH=7$ and $AH\perp BC$. The area is
\[
[ABC]=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot BC\cdot AH=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot15\cdot7=\boxed{\dfrac{105}{2}}.
\]
Such a triangle exists: put $B=(0,0)$, $C=(15,0)$, $A=(4,7)$, so $H=(4,0)$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair B=(0,0),C=(15,0),A=(4,7),H=(4,0);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(A--H,dashed);
real s=0.5; draw(H+(s,0)--H+(s,s)--H+(0,s));
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);dot(H);
label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,SW);label("$C$",C,SE);label("$H$",H,S);
draw((0,-1.6)--(15,-1.6),Arrows(TeXHead));draw((0,-0.3)--(0,-1.9),gray);draw((15,-0.3)--(15,-1.9),gray);
label("$15$",(7.5,-1.6),S);label("$7$",(4,3.5),E);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Then $AB^2=16+49=65$ and $AC^2=121+49=170$, so the sides $\sqrt{65}$, $\sqrt{170}$, $15$ are all different. The angles at $B$ and $C$ are acute because the foot $H$ lies strictly between $B$ and $C$, and the angle at $A$ is acute because $AB^2+AC^2=235>225=BC^2$. (The area does not depend on the choice of $A$: any point at height $7$ above $BC$ gives the same area.)
