For large $x$, $f(x)\approx\sqrt[3]{8x^3}=2x$, so $a=2$. To find $b$, compare with $(2x+b)^3=8x^3+12bx^2+6b^2x+b^3$: the $x^2$ terms agree when $12b=-7$, so take $b=-\dfrac{7}{12}$.

To justify this, let $u=f(x)$ and $v=2x+b$. Using $u^3-v^3=(u-v)(u^2+uv+v^2)$,
\[
u-v=\dfrac{u^3-v^3}{u^2+uv+v^2}=\dfrac{(-7-12b)x^2+(-4-6b^2)x+(-2-b^3)}{u^2+uv+v^2}.
\]
With $b=-\dfrac{7}{12}$ the numerator is linear in $x$, while the denominator is about $12x^2$ for large $x$ (since $u,v\approx2x$). So $u-v\to0$. (For any other $b$ the quotient tends to $\dfrac{-7-12b}{12}\ne0$.) Hence $a=2$, $b=-\dfrac{7}{12}$ and
\[
a+b=\boxed{\dfrac{17}{12}} .
\]
