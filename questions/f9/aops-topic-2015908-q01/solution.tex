Order the numbers $a<b<c<d$ (they are distinct because they form a set). The six pair sums add to $3(a+b+c+d)$, so $a+b+c+d=\dfrac{66}{3}=22$.

The smallest sum must be $a+b=7$ and the next smallest $a+c=9$ (any other pair is larger than one of these). Likewise the largest is $c+d=15$ and the next largest $b+d=13$. The remaining two sums, $10$ and $12$, are $a+d$ and $b+c$ in some order; we cannot assume which, so there are two cases.
\begin{itemize}
\item $a+d=10$, $b+c=12$: from $a+b=7$, $a+c=9$, $b+c=12$ we get $a=2$, $b=5$, $c=7$, $d=8$. All pair sums: $7,9,10,12,13,15$. Product $2\cdot5\cdot7\cdot8=560$.
\item $a+d=12$, $b+c=10$: then $a=3$, $b=4$, $c=6$, $d=9$. Pair sums: $7,9,12,10,13,15$, as required. Product $3\cdot4\cdot6\cdot9=648$.
\end{itemize}
Both sets consist of integers from $1$ to $9$, so both are possible, and the smallest product is $\boxed{560}$.

The poster's assignment $a+d=10$, $b+c=12$ happens to give the smaller product, but it is not justified without the casework: the other assignment gives a second valid set, which is why the question asks for the \emph{smallest} product.
