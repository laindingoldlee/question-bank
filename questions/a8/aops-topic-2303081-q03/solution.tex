In each flip, independently of the past:
\begin{itemize}
\item with probability $\dfrac12\cdot0.37=0.185$ the coin shows AoPS and he stops (selling to AoPS);
\item with probability $\dfrac12\cdot0.58=0.29$ it shows Ben and he stops (selling to Ben);
\item with probability $1-0.185-0.29=0.525$ he reflips, and the situation is exactly as at the start.
\end{itemize}
Let $p$ be the probability that he sells to AoPS. Conditioning on the first flip,
\[p=0.185+0.525\,p,\qquad\text{so}\qquad p=\dfrac{0.185}{0.475}=\boxed{\dfrac{37}{95}}\approx0.389.\]
(Equivalently, $p$ is the share of AoPS among the two ways of stopping: $\dfrac{0.185}{0.185+0.29}$.)
