\textbf{(a)} For $n\ge2$ the list contains two consecutive integers $k$ and $k+1$. Any common divisor of all the numbers divides $(k+1)-k=1$, so the greatest common divisor is $\boxed{1}$.

\textbf{(b)} For the product of $k,k+1,\dots,k+n-1$,
\[k(k+1)\cdots(k+n-1)=\dfrac{(k+n-1)!}{(k-1)!}=n!\dbinom{k+n-1}{n},\]
and the binomial coefficient is an integer, so $n!$ divides every such product. Taking $k=1$, the product is $n!$ itself, so no larger integer divides all the products. Hence $d=\boxed{n!}$.
