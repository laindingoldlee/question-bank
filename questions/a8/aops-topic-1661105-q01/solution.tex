Note that $10a+b=9a+(a+b)$ and $10b+a=9b+(a+b)$. If $a+b$ divides both numbers, it divides $9a$ and $9b$, hence divides $\gcd(9a,9b)=9\gcd(a,b)=9$. So $a+b\in\{3,9\}$ (it is at least $2$). It remains to check the candidates directly:
\begin{itemize}
\item $a+b=3$: $(1,2)$ and $(2,1)$, with $\gcd(12,21)=3$;
\item $a+b=9$ with $\gcd(a,b)=1$: $(1,8),(2,7),(4,5)$ and their reverses, with $\gcd(18,81)=9$, $\gcd(27,72)=9$, $\gcd(45,54)=9$. The pair $(3,6)$ is excluded as $\gcd(3,6)=3$.
\end{itemize}
So there are $\boxed{8}$ ordered pairs $(a,b)$ (that is, $4$ unordered pairs $\{a,b\}$).
