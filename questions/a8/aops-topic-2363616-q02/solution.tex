\textbf{(a)} Just as a square of side $n$ has area $n^2$ and a cube volume $n^3$, the four-dimensional box $[0,n]^4$ has hypervolume $n\cdot n\cdot n\cdot n=\boxed{n^4}$ (for integer $n$ it is made of $n^4$ unit tesseracts).

\textbf{(b)} For any two points of the tesseract, each difference $|x_i-y_i|$ is at most $n$, so their distance is at most $\sqrt{4n^2}=2n$. The opposite vertices $(0,0,0,0)$ and $(n,n,n,n)$ are at distance exactly $\sqrt{4n^2}=\boxed{2n}$, so this is the longest diagonal.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair e1=(1,0), e2=(0,1), e3=(0.35,0.42), e4=(1.25,0.55);
pair P(int a,int b,int c,int d){return a*e1+b*e2+c*e3+d*e4;}
for(int a=0;a<2;++a) for(int b=0;b<2;++b) for(int c=0;c<2;++c) for(int d=0;d<2;++d){
  if(a==0) draw(P(0,b,c,d)--P(1,b,c,d),gray);
  if(b==0) draw(P(a,0,c,d)--P(a,1,c,d),gray);
  if(c==0) draw(P(a,b,0,d)--P(a,b,1,d),gray);
  if(d==0) draw(P(a,b,c,0)--P(a,b,c,1),gray);
  dot(P(a,b,c,d));
}
draw(P(0,0,0,0)--P(1,1,1,1),red+linewidth(1));
label("$(0,0,0,0)$",P(0,0,0,0),SW,fontsize(8pt)); label("$(n,n,n,n)$",P(1,1,1,1),NE,fontsize(8pt));
\end{asy}

(The pattern: a square of side $n$ has diagonal $n\sqrt2$, a cube $n\sqrt3$, a tesseract $n\sqrt4=2n$.)
