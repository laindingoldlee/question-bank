Let the sequence be $1,r,r^2,\dots,r^m=1024$ with $m\ge1$. The second term $r$ is a positive integer, and $r^m=1024=2^{10}$, so $r$ divides $2^{10}$ and $r=2^d$ for some $d\ge1$. Then $2^{dm}=2^{10}$, so $dm=10$ and $d$ is a divisor of $10$. Conversely each divisor $d$ of $10$ gives the sequence with ratio $2^d$ and $m=\dfrac{10}{d}$ steps. The divisors $d=1,2,5,10$ give the ratios
\[
2,\quad 4,\quad 32,\quad 1024,
\]
that is, the sequences $1,2,4,\dots,1024$; $1,4,16,64,256,1024$; $1,32,1024$; and $1,1024$. This is where the book's list $2^{10},2^5,2^2,2^1$ comes from. There are $\boxed{4}$ sequences.
