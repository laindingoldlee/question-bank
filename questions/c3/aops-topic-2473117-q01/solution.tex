\textbf{(a)} Since $n$ is odd, $n^2$ is odd, so $n^2=b+(b+1)=2b+1$ has the integer solution $b=\dfrac{n^2-1}{2}$, and $a=\dfrac{n^2+1}{2}$; as $n\ge3$, $b\ge4>0$. By the difference of squares,
\[a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b)=1\cdot n^2=n^2,\]
so $n^2+b^2=a^2$, and $(n,b,a)$ is a Pythagorean triple of positive integers. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{(b)} With $n=2k+1$ we get $n^2=4k^2+4k+1$, so
\[b=\dfrac{n^2-1}{2}=2k^2+2k,\qquad a=2k^2+2k+1.\]
(Directly: $a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b)=4k^2+4k+1=n^2$.) For $n=7$, $k=3$: $b=24$, $a=25$, and indeed $49+576=625$. The triple is $\boxed{(7,24,25)}$.
