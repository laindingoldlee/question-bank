Use coordinates $(x,y,z)$, with $z$ as the height. For $i=1,\dots,n$, let body $B_i$ be the union of
\begin{itemize}
\item the \emph{lower bar} $L_i=[0,2n-1]\times[2i-2,2i-1]\times[0,1]$, running in the $x$-direction at height $0$ to $1$, and
\item the \emph{upper bar} $U_i=[2i-2,2i-1]\times[0,2n-1]\times[1,2]$, running in the $y$-direction at height $1$ to $2$.
\end{itemize}
The top view below shows the case $n=3$: the pale strips are the lower bars and the dark strips, drawn on top, are the upper bars.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
int n=3; pen[] c={red,blue,heavygreen};
for(int i=0;i<n;++i) filldraw(box((0,2i),(2n-1,2i+1)),0.35*c[i]+0.65*white,c[i]);
for(int i=0;i<n;++i) filldraw(box((2i,-0.0),(2i+1,2n-1)),c[i]+0.25*white,black);
for(int i=0;i<n;++i) label("body $"+string(i+1)+"$",(2n-1,2i+0.5),E,c[i]);
for(int i=0;i<n;++i) label(string(i+1),(2i+0.5,2n-1),N,c[i]);
label("pale strips: lower bars (height $0$ to $1$)",(n-0.5,-0.6),S,fontsize(8pt));
label("dark strips: upper bars (height $1$ to $2$)",(n-0.5,-1.4),S,fontsize(8pt));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\textbf{Each body is connected.} $L_i$ and $U_i$ both lie over the unit square $[2i-2,2i-1]\times[2i-2,2i-1]$, and there $L_i$ (heights $0$ to $1$) and $U_i$ (heights $1$ to $2$) share the square face at height $1$.

\textbf{Disjoint interiors.} Different lower bars occupy different strips $2i-2\le y\le2i-1$, which do not overlap, and different upper bars occupy different strips in $x$. A lower bar and an upper bar lie in the layers $0\le z\le1$ and $1\le z\le2$, which meet only in the plane $z=1$.

\textbf{Every two bodies touch.} For $i\ne j$, the lower bar $L_i$ and the upper bar $U_j$ both lie over the unit square $[2j-2,2j-1]\times[2i-2,2i-1]$, where the top of $L_i$ and the bottom of $U_j$ coincide. So $B_i$ and $B_j$ share a face of area $1$. $\blacksquare$

(In the plane the analogous statement is false: at most four regions can pairwise share boundary segments, by the four-colour theorem or because the complete graph on five vertices is not planar. This is the difference the poster noticed between two and three dimensions.)
