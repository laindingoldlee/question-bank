\emph{Small cases.} The digit sums of $0!,1!,\dots,10!$ ($1,1,2,6,24,120,720,5040,40320,362880,3628800$) are
\[1,1,2,6,6,3,9,9,9,27,27.\]
So $n=6,7,8$ work and no other $n\le10$ does.

\emph{$n\ge11$.} Then $11\mid n!$. By the divisibility rule for $11$, if $O$ and $E$ are the sums of the digits in the odd and even positions (counted from the right), then $11\mid O-E$. If the digit sum were $9$, then $O+E=9$, so $|O-E|\le9$ and hence $O-E=0$. But then $O+E=2O$ is even, not $9$. So no $n\ge11$ works.

The answer is $\boxed{n=6,7,8}$.
