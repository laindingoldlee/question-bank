\textbf{(a)} Integers are the case $q=1$. For odd $q$, $\sqrt[q]{-2}=-\sqrt[q]2$, so
\[(-2)^{\dfrac pq}=(-1)^p\left(\sqrt[q]2\right)^p=(-1)^p\,2^{\dfrac pq}.\]
For this to equal the positive number $2$, $p$ must be even and $2^{\dfrac pq}=2$, i.e. $\dfrac pq=1$ and $p=q$. But $q$ is odd and $p$ is even, so $p\ne q$. Hence $(-2)^x=2$ has no solution, and $\log_{-2}2$ has no value. (Also, $(-2)^x$ jumps between positive and negative values, so it is not an increasing or decreasing function whose inverse could be the logarithm.)

\textbf{(b)} $1^x=1$ for every $x$. So $1^x=2$ has no solution, while $1^x=1$ has every real solution: $\log_12$ does not exist and $\log_11$ is not a single number. $\blacksquare$
