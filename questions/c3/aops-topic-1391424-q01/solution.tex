Each team consists of three people sitting next to each other at the new table of six. The team without James consists of three of the five friends who are consecutive around the table; they were consecutive among the five friends before James sat down. The other team is James together with the remaining two friends, who are also adjacent among the five.

So a pair of teams is determined by a block of three consecutive friends among the five seated in a circle, and there are $5$ such blocks. Each block does occur: James sits in either gap next to the complementary pair of friends (but not between them), and then the table reads block, then James and the pair. Different blocks give different pairs of teams. Hence there are $\boxed{5}$ possible pairs of teams.
