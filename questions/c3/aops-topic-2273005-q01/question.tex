Let $s=(s_1,s_2,\ldots,s_n)$ be a sequence of $n\ge2$ distinct integers. A rearrangement $p$ of $s$ is called \emph{completely shuffled} if for any two of the integers, their order in $p$ is the opposite of their order in $s$ (if $x$ comes before $y$ in $s$, then $y$ comes before $x$ in $p$). Prove that $s$ has exactly one completely shuffled rearrangement.
