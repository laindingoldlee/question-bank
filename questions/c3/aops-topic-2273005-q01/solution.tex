\textbf{Existence.} The reversed sequence $(s_n,s_{n-1},\ldots,s_1)$ is completely shuffled: if $i<j$, then $s_i$ comes before $s_j$ in $s$, and $s_j$ comes before $s_i$ in the reversal.

\textbf{Uniqueness.} Let $p$ be completely shuffled and fix an index $i$. The integers that come \emph{before} $s_i$ in $p$ are exactly those that come \emph{after} $s_i$ in $s$, namely $s_{i+1},\ldots,s_n$. There are $n-i$ of them, so $s_i$ is in position $n-i+1$ of $p$. Thus the position of every element is forced, and $p$ must be the reversal. $\blacksquare$
