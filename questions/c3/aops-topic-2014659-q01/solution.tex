The squares are labelled, so rotations or reflections of the grid give different paintings. Choose the red square ($4$ ways), then the green square among the remaining three ($3$ ways); the other two squares are blue. That gives
\[
4\cdot3=\boxed{12}
\]
ways. (Equivalently, $\dfrac{4!}{2!\,1!\,1!}=12$ arrangements of the multiset $\{\text{blue},\text{blue},\text{red},\text{green}\}$.)
