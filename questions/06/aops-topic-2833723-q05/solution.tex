Each of the triangle's three sides meets the boundary of the convex hexagon at most twice, giving an upper bound 6. To attain it, inscribe a regular hexagon in a unit circle and use the equilateral triangle with vertices $ 1.5\operatorname{dir}(90^\circ)$, $ 1.5\operatorname{dir}(210^\circ)$, and $ 1.5\operatorname{dir}(330^\circ)$. Each triangle side cuts through the hexagon twice, with distinct intersections. Hence the maximum is $\boxed6 $.
\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");

path h=dir(0);for(int k=1;k<6;++k)h=h--dir(60*k);draw(h--cycle);pair A=1.5dir(90),B=1.5dir(210),C=1.5dir(330);draw(A--B--C--cycle,blue);label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,SW);label("$C$",C,SE);
\end{asy}
