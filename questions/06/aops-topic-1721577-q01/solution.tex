Let $S_n=\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}+\dots+\dfrac{1}{2^n}$ (the sum of $\dfrac{1}{k}$ for $2\le k\le2^n$).

\textbf{Base case.} $S_1=\dfrac{1}{2}\ge\dfrac{1}{2}$.

\textbf{Inductive step.} Suppose $S_n\ge\dfrac {n}{2}$. Then
\[
S_{n+1}=S_n+\left(\dfrac{1}{2^n+1}+\dfrac{1}{2^n+2}+\dots+\dfrac{1}{2^{n+1}}\right).
\]
The bracket has $2^{n+1}-2^n=2^n$ terms, each at least $\dfrac{1}{2^{n+1}}$, so it is at least $2^n\cdot\dfrac{1}{2^{n+1}}=\dfrac{1}{2}$. Hence $S_{n+1}\ge\dfrac {n}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{n+1}{2}$.

By induction the inequality holds for all $n\ge1$. $\blacksquare$
