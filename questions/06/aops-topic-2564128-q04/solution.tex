If a prime $p$ divides every number in the list, then $p$ is a common divisor, so the gcd is at least $p>1$. Conversely, if the gcd $g$ is greater than $1$, then any prime factor of $g$ divides $g$, and $g$ divides every number in the list, so that prime divides all of them. Hence the gcd is $1$ exactly when no prime divides all the numbers. $\blacksquare$

Example: $6=2\cdot3$, $10=2\cdot5$ and $15=3\cdot5$. No prime divides all three, so $\gcd(6,10,15)=1$, but $\gcd(6,10)=2$, $\gcd(6,15)=3$ and $\gcd(10,15)=5$.
