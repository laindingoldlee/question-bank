\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Let $r$ and $s$ be the roots of a quadratic $x^2+bx+c$ with $c\ne0$. Prove that
\[\dfrac{r+s}{rs}=\dfrac1r+\dfrac1s,\]
and express this common value in terms of $b$ and $c$.
\item[(b)] A student claims that for every polynomial with nonzero roots, the sum of the roots divided by the product of the roots equals the sum of the reciprocals of the roots. Show that the claim is false for $x^3-6x^2+11x-6=(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)$, and give a correct formula for $\dfrac1r+\dfrac1s+\dfrac1t$ when $r,s,t$ are the roots of $x^3+bx^2+cx+d$ with $d\ne0$.
\end{enumerate}
