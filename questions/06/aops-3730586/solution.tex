For each $v=0,1,\ldots,\lfloor\dfrac{n}{2}\rfloor$, there is exactly one choice $u=n-2v$. Thus $\boxed{a_n=\lfloor\dfrac{n}{2}\rfloor+1}$. As formal power series, $\dfrac{1}{1-x}=\sum_{u\ge0}x^u$ and $\dfrac{1}{1-x^2}=\sum_{v\ge0}x^{2v}$. In their product, each choice $(u,v)$ contributes $x^{u+2v}$, so the coefficient of $x^n$ counts exactly the desired solutions.
