If both primes were odd, their sum would be even and greater than $2$, not prime. Thus one is $2$ and the other is an odd prime $p$. We require $p-2,p,p+2$ all prime. One of these three odd numbers is divisible by $3$, so it must equal $3$. The possibility $p=3$ fails because $p-2=1$; the valid choice is $p=5$, giving $3,5,7$. The product of the original two primes is $\boxed{10}$.
