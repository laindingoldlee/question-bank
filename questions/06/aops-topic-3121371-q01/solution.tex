Substitute $x=t-\dfrac{b}{3a}$ to obtain $t^3+pt+q=0$, where $p=\dfrac{3ac-b^2}{3a^2}$ and $q=\dfrac{2b^3-9abc+27a^2d}{27a^3}$. Put $\Delta=\dfrac{q^2}{4}+\dfrac{p^3}{27}$. If $p=q=0$, the sole root is $x=-\dfrac{b}{3a}$ with multiplicity 3. Otherwise choose a nonzero one of $-\dfrac{q}{2}\pm\sqrt\Delta$, let $U$ be any of its cube roots, and define $V=-\dfrac{p}{3U}$. The two numbers under the cube roots have product $-\dfrac{p^3}{27}$, so $U^3+V^3=-q$ and $UV=-\dfrac{p}{3}$. For $\omega=e^{\dfrac{2\pi i}{3}}$, each $t_k=\omega^kU+\omega^{-k}V$ satisfies $t_k^3+pt_k+q=0$, by expanding the cube. Moreover their sum is 0, their pairwise-product sum is $-3UV=p$, and their product is $U^3+V^3=-q$. Thus their monic root polynomial is exactly $t^3+pt+q$, including multiplicities. All roots are $\boxed{x_k=\omega^kU+\omega^{-k}V-\dfrac{b}{3a},\ k=0,1,2}$. Choosing $U,V$ independently can violate $UV=-\dfrac{p}{3}$; that compatibility is essential.
