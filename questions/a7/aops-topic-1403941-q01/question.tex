Let $a$ and $b$ be positive integers and call $n\ge0$ \emph{representable} if $n=ax+by$ for some nonnegative integers $x,y$. The Chicken McNugget theorem says that if $\gcd(a,b)=1$, then the largest non-representable integer is $ab-a-b$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Show that if $d=\gcd(a,b)>1$, there is no largest non-representable integer.
\item[(b)] Show that in that case the largest non-representable \emph{multiple of $d$} is $\dfrac{ab}{d}-a-b$.
\end{enumerate}
