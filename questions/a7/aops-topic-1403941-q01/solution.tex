\textbf{(a)} Every representable number $ax+by$ is a multiple of $d$. So every integer that is not a multiple of $d$ (for instance every number $\equiv1\pmod d$) is non-representable, and there are infinitely many of them: no largest one exists.

\textbf{(b)} Write $a=da'$ and $b=db'$ with $\gcd(a',b')=1$. For a multiple $n=dm$ of $d$,
\[
dm=ax+by\iff m=a'x+b'y ,
\]
so $dm$ is representable by $a,b$ exactly when $m$ is representable by $a',b'$. By the Chicken McNugget theorem for the coprime pair $a',b'$, the largest non-representable $m$ is $a'b'-a'-b'$. So the largest non-representable multiple of $d$ is
\[
d(a'b'-a'-b')=\dfrac{ab}{d}-a-b .
\]
For example, with $a=6$, $b=10$ ($d=2$), the largest even number that is not of the form $6x+10y$ is $\dfrac{60}{2}-16=14$.
