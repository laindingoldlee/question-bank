\textbf{(a)} The sum includes $\dfrac12+\dfrac14+\dfrac16+\cdots=\dfrac12\left(1+\dfrac12+\dfrac13+\cdots\right)$. The harmonic series diverges, since its terms can be grouped as
\[1+\dfrac12+\left(\dfrac13+\dfrac14\right)+\left(\dfrac15+\cdots+\dfrac18\right)+\cdots,\]
where each bracket (from $\dfrac1{2^k+1}$ to $\dfrac1{2^{k+1}}$) is at least $2^k\cdot\dfrac1{2^{k+1}}=\dfrac12$. So the partial sums exceed any bound, and the sum is infinite.

\textbf{(b)} Including $n=1$, the sum over all $n=2^a3^b$ factors as a product of two geometric series:
\[\sum_{a\ge0}\sum_{b\ge0}\dfrac1{2^a3^b}=\left(\sum_{a\ge0}\dfrac1{2^a}\right)\left(\sum_{b\ge0}\dfrac1{3^b}\right)=2\cdot\dfrac32=3.\]
(All terms are positive, so the order of summation does not matter.) Removing the term $1$ for $n=1$ gives $\boxed{2}$.
