Label the vertices $0,1,\dots,5$ in order, with centre $O$ and circumradius $1$. There are $9$ diagonals: $3$ long ones ($03$, $14$, $25$) through $O$, and $6$ short ones ($02$, $13$, $24$, $35$, $40$, $51$).

Any $4$ vertices $a<b<c<d$ form a convex quadrilateral whose diagonals $ac$ and $bd$ cross inside the hexagon, and every crossing pair of diagonals arises this way. So there are $\dbinom64=15$ crossing pairs. We sort them:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{long with long} ($\{0,1,3,4\}$, $\{0,2,3,5\}$, $\{1,2,4,5\}$): all three cross at $O$, giving $1$ point;
\item \textbf{long with short} ($\{0,1,2,4\}$, $\{0,1,3,5\}$, $\{0,2,3,4\}$, $\{0,2,4,5\}$, $\{1,2,3,5\}$, $\{1,3,4,5\}$): the long diagonal is the perpendicular bisector of the short one (for example $14$ and $02$), so they cross at the midpoint of the short diagonal, at distance $\dfrac12$ from $O$; the six short diagonals give $6$ different midpoints;
\item \textbf{short with short} ($\{0,1,2,3\}$, $\{1,2,3,4\}$, $\{2,3,4,5\}$, $\{0,3,4,5\}$, $\{0,1,4,5\}$, $\{0,1,2,5\}$): these are the six inner vertices of the six-pointed star formed by the short diagonals, at distance $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$ from $O$, all different.
\end{itemize}

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair[] V; for(int k=0;k<6;++k) V.push(dir(60k));
draw(V[0]--V[1]--V[2]--V[3]--V[4]--V[5]--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
path[] D;
for(int i=0;i<6;++i) for(int j=i+2;j<6;++j) if(!(i==0 && j==5)){ path p=V[i]--V[j]; D.push(p); draw(p, (j-i==3) ? red : blue); }
for(int i=0;i<D.length;++i) for(int j=i+1;j<D.length;++j){ pair[] X=intersectionpoints(D[i],D[j]); for(pair x : X) if(abs(x)<0.99) dot(x,black+3pt); }
draw(circle((0,0),0.5),gray+dotted); draw(circle((0,0),1/sqrt(3)),gray+dotted);
for(int k=0;k<6;++k) label("$"+string(k)+"$",dir(60k),dir(60k));
label("$O$",(0,0),dir(15),fontsize(8pt));
\end{asy}

Points at different distances from $O$ are different, and the points in each group are different, so the number of distinct interior intersection points is
\[1+6+6=\boxed{13}.\]
(If no three diagonals met at a point the answer would be $15$; the triple point $O$ accounts for the difference.)
