In Lewis Carroll's poem \emph{The Hunting of the Snark}, the Butcher wants to show that $1+2=3$. He takes three as ``the subject to reason about'', adds seven and ten, multiplies by one thousand diminished by eight, divides the result by nine hundred and ninety-two, and finally subtracts seventeen.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Carry out the Butcher's calculation. What is the result?
\item[(b)] Show that if any number $x$ is used in place of three, the calculation always gives back $x$.
\end{enumerate}
