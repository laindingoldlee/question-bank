\textbf{(a)} Take $x=4$, $a=6$, $b=2$, $c=2$, $d=2$. Then $6\equiv2\pmod4$ and $2\equiv2\pmod4$, but $\dfrac{a}{c}=3$ and $\dfrac{b}{d}=1$, and $3\not\equiv1\pmod4$. So the statement is false in general.

\textbf{(b)} Let $u=\dfrac{a}{c}$ and $v=\dfrac{b}{d}$, so $a=cu$ and $b=dv$. Since $d\equiv c\pmod x$,
\[
cu=a\equiv b=dv\equiv cv\pmod{x},
\]
so $x$ divides $c(u-v)$. Because $\gcd(c,x)=1$, it follows that $x$ divides $u-v$, that is $\dfrac{a}{c}\equiv\dfrac{b}{d}\pmod{x}$. $\blacksquare$

So division in congruences is allowed only by numbers coprime to the modulus.
