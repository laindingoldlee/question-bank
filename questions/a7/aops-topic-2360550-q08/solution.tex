The right side is $\dfrac{a+b}{ab}$, so the equation says $ab=(a+b)^2$, that is, $a^2+ab+b^2=0$. Dividing by $b^2$ and writing $t=\dfrac ab$:
\[t^2+t+1=0,\qquad t=\boxed{\dfrac{-1\pm i\sqrt3}{2}}.\]
These values are not real, so $a$ and $b$ cannot both be real. (Indeed $a^2+ab+b^2=\left(a+\dfrac b2\right)^2+\dfrac34b^2>0$ for real $b\ne0$.) For example, $b=1$ and $a=\dfrac{-1+i\sqrt3}{2}$ satisfy the equation.
