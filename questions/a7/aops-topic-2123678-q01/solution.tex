By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
\[(ax+by+cz)^2\le\left(a^2+b^2+c^2\right)\left(x^2+y^2+z^2\right)=16\cdot25=400,\]
so $ax+by+cz\le20$. Here equality holds, and equality in Cauchy-Schwarz forces $(a,b,c)$ to be a multiple of $(x,y,z)$. To see this directly and find the multiple, compute
\[\left(a-\dfrac{4}{5}x\right)^2+\left(b-\dfrac{4}{5}y\right)^2+\left(c-\dfrac{4}{5}z\right)^2=\left(a^2+b^2+c^2\right)-\dfrac{8}{5}\left(ax+by+cz\right)+\dfrac{16}{25}\left(x^2+y^2+z^2\right)=16-32+16=0 .\]
A sum of squares of real numbers is $0$ only if every square is $0$, so
\[a=\dfrac{4}{5}x,\qquad b=\dfrac{4}{5}y,\qquad c=\dfrac{4}{5}z .\]
Therefore $a+b+c=\dfrac{4}{5}(x+y+z)$ and
\[\dfrac{a+b+c}{x+y+z}=\boxed{\dfrac{4}{5}}.\]
(The hypothesis $x+y+z\ne0$ is needed: for example $(x,y,z)=\left(\dfrac{5}{\sqrt2},-\dfrac{5}{\sqrt2},0\right)$ and $(a,b,c)=\dfrac{4}{5}(x,y,z)$ satisfy all three equations, but $x+y+z=0$.)
