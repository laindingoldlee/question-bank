\textbf{Hook lengths.} In the first row, the boxes have (right-arm, leg) of $(3,2)$, $(2,1)$, $(1,0)$, $(0,0)$, so their hook lengths are $6,4,2,1$. In the second row the hook lengths are $3,1$, and the single box of the third row has hook length $1$:
\[
\begin{array}{cccc}6&4&2&1\\3&1&&\\1&&&\end{array}
\]
The product is $6\cdot4\cdot2\cdot1\cdot3\cdot1\cdot1=144$, so the number of tableaux is
\[
\dfrac{7!}{144}=\dfrac{5040}{144}=\boxed{35}.
\]

\textbf{Direct check.} The largest number $7$ must sit in a box with nothing to its right and nothing below it: the end of row $1$, the end of row $2$, or the box of row $3$. Removing it leaves a standard tableau of shape $(3,2,1)$, $(4,1,1)$ or $(4,2)$ respectively. By the same formula (or by the same recursion) these shapes have
\[
\dfrac{6!}{5\cdot3\cdot1\cdot3\cdot1\cdot1}=16,\qquad\dfrac{6!}{6\cdot3\cdot2\cdot1\cdot2\cdot1}=10,\qquad\dfrac{6!}{5\cdot4\cdot2\cdot1\cdot2\cdot1}=9
\]
tableaux, and $16+10+9=35$, in agreement.
