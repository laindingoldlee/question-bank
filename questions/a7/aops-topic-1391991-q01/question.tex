A \emph{standard Young tableau} of shape $(4,2,1)$ is a filling of a left-justified array of boxes with rows of lengths $4$, $2$, $1$ (top to bottom) by the numbers $1,2,\dots,7$, each used once, so that the numbers increase from left to right along each row and from top to bottom down each column.

The \emph{hook} of a box consists of the box itself, all boxes to its right in its row, and all boxes below it in its column; its hook length is the number of boxes in the hook. The hook length formula says that the number of standard Young tableaux of a shape with $n$ boxes is $\dfrac{n!}{\prod h}$, the product being over the hook lengths of all boxes. Use it to count the standard Young tableaux of shape $(4,2,1)$, and explain why the answer is plausible by a direct count of where the number $7$ can go.
