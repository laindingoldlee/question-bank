Let $2n+1=a^2$ and $3n+1=b^2$.

\emph{Divisibility by $8$.} $a$ is odd, so $a^2\equiv1\pmod8$ and $8\mid2n$, i.e. $4\mid n$. Then $3n+1$ is odd, so $b$ is odd, $b^2\equiv1\pmod8$ and $8\mid3n$, so $8\mid n$.

\emph{Divisibility by $5$.} Squares are $0$, $1$ or $4$ mod $5$, and $a^2+b^2=5n+2\equiv2\pmod5$. The only way to get $2$ as a sum of two of $0,1,4$ mod $5$ is $1+1$. So $a^2\equiv1$, hence $2n\equiv0$ and $n\equiv0\pmod5$.

Therefore $40\mid n$. Since $n=40$ works ($81=9^2$ and $121=11^2$), the smallest such $n$ is $\boxed{40}$. $\blacksquare$

(The next ones are $3960$ and $388080$.)
