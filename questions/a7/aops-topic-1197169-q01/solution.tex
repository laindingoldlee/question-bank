\textbf{(a)} $\gcd(101,1000)=1$, so $101$ is invertible modulo $1000$ and we may cancel it: $x\equiv1\pmod{1000}$, i.e. $\boxed{x=1+1000n}$.

\textbf{(b)} $12321\equiv321\pmod{1000}$, so the congruence is $321x\equiv321\pmod{1000}$. Since $\gcd(321,1000)=1$, cancel again: $x\equiv1\pmod{1000}$, i.e. $\boxed{x=1+1000n}$.

(In general $ax\equiv c\pmod m$ has solutions iff $d=\gcd(a,m)$ divides $c$, and then the solutions form one class modulo $\dfrac {m}{d}$; e.g. $4x\equiv4\pmod{1000}$ gives $x\equiv1\pmod{250}$.)
