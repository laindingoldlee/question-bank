No. In a non-degenerate triangle each side is shorter than the sum of the other two (the triangle inequality is strict). Here the two shorter sides add up to exactly the longest:
\[(x-1)+1=x .\]
So if we try to build the triangle $ABC$ with $AB=x$, $AC=x-1$ and $CB=1$, the point $C$ must lie on the segment $AB$, and the ``triangle'' collapses into a segment, however large $x$ is.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0), B=(6,0), C=(5,0);
draw(A--B,linewidth(1));
draw(A--C,blue+linewidth(2.5)); draw(C--B,red+linewidth(2.5));
dot(A); dot(B); dot(C);
label("$A$",A,S); label("$B$",B,S); label("$C$",C,S);
label("$x-1$",(A+C)/2,N,blue); label("$1$",(C+B)/2,N,red);
label("$x$",(A+B)/2,(0,-3));
\end{asy}

\emph{Remark.} This answers the question that inspired it, whether there is a triangle with sides $\infty$, $\infty-1$ and $1$: $\infty$ is not a real number, so such side lengths have no meaning, and for every real $x$ the corresponding triangle is degenerate.
