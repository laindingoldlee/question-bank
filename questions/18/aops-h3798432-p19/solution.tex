Let $\alpha=\angle BAC$. Triangle ABC gives $\angle ABC=134^\circ-\alpha$, so $\angle KBC=56^\circ-\alpha$. In triangle BKC, $\angle BKC=78^\circ+\alpha$. Its supplementary angle $CKD$ is $102^\circ-\alpha$. Because $CK=DK$, the base angle $KCD$ is $39^\circ+\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$. The bisector of BAC makes angle $\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$ with AC. Comparing the directions of these two lines therefore gives the acute difference $(39^\circ+\dfrac{\alpha}{2})-\dfrac{\alpha}{2}=\boxed{39^\circ}$.
