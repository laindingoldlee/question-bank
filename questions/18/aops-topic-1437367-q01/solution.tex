Both can be done by forming the number $71$ from the digits $7$ and $1$ and using the two $5$'s to make $25$ or $5$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $46=71-5\times5$.
\item[(b)] $66=71-\sqrt{5\times5}$.
\end{enumerate}
Each uses $1$, $5$, $5$, $7$ exactly once and only the allowed operations. (These are two of the twelve numbers $46, 66, 67, 78, 83, 87, 89, 91, 92, 94, 97, 99$ that the poster could not reach without factorials; the trick is that the digits may be written next to each other in any order, so $71$ is available just like $17$ and $75$.)
