(a) The equation $1\cdot x=0$ has the unique solution $x=0$, so $\dfrac{0}{1}=0$. But $0\cdot x=1$ has no solution, so zero has no multiplicative inverse. (b) A rational number is a quotient of integers with nonzero denominator. Both displayed zero-denominator expressions are undefined, not additional rational values. For $\dfrac{0}{0}$, the equation $0\cdot x=0$ is true for every $x$, so it selects no unique quotient. (c) The proof of $\dfrac{a}{a}=1$ multiplies by the inverse of $a$, which exists only for $a\ne0$. Products containing $\dfrac{1}{0}$ cannot be manipulated as ordinary numbers. (d) The distributive law forces $0\cdot z=(0+0)z=0z+0z$, hence $0z=0$ after subtraction. An alleged inverse $z$ with $0z=1$ would therefore imply $0=1$. Complex numbers also obey these field laws and do not repair division by zero.
