Give each person the set of days on which they are invited. To guarantee revelation for every ordered witness-criminal pair, no person's set may be a subset of another's. Thus the 69 sets must form an antichain. For $d$ days, an antichain has at most $\binom d{\lfloor \dfrac{d}{2}\rfloor}$ members: in each ordering of the days, at most one antichain member is an initial segment, so summing $|S|!(d-|S|)!$ over the family gives at most $d!$, implying the bound. For 7 days the bound is 35, while for 8 it is 70. Choose any 69 distinct 4-element subsets of 8 days; equal-sized distinct sets are incomparable. Thus $\boxed8$ days suffice and are necessary. Adaptive questioning cannot improve the worst case: along the all-silent branch it also produces a fixed collection of invitation sets, to which the same argument applies.
