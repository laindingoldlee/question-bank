Write $R_k=\underbrace{11\cdots1}_{k}$. Splitting the $100$ ones into the first $98$ and the last $2$,
\[
R_{100}=R_{98}\cdot100+11 .
\]
Since $98=7\cdot14$, the number $R_{98}$ consists of $14$ blocks of seven ones:
\[
R_{98}=1111111\cdot\left(10^{91}+10^{84}+\cdots+10^7+1\right),
\]
so $R_{98}$, and hence $R_{98}\cdot100$, is divisible by $1111111$. Therefore $R_{100}=1111111\cdot q+11$ for an integer $q$, and since $0\le11<1111111$, the remainder is $\boxed{11}$.
