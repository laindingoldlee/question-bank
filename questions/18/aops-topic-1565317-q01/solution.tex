Any integer root divides $18$. Testing, $x=1$ is a root: $1+2-10-8+33-18=0$. Synthetic division gives
\[
x^5+2x^4-10x^3-8x^2+33x-18=(x-1)\left(x^4+3x^3-7x^2-15x+18\right).
\]
The quartic vanishes at $x=1$ again ($1+3-7-15+18=0$):
\[
x^4+3x^3-7x^2-15x+18=(x-1)\left(x^3+4x^2-3x-18\right),
\]
and the cubic vanishes at $x=2$ ($8+16-6-18=0$): $x^3+4x^2-3x-18=(x-2)(x^2+6x+9)=(x-2)(x+3)^2$. Therefore
\[
x^5+2x^4-10x^3-8x^2+33x-18=(x-1)^2(x-2)(x+3)^2 .
\]
The positive roots are $1$ (a double root) and $2$, and the sum of the distinct positive roots is $1+2=\boxed{3}$. (Counting $1$ twice, with multiplicity, would give $4$.)
