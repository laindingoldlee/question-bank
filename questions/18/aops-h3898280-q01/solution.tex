Call the vertices $A,B,C,D$ in side order. Since $BC^2+CD^2=BD^2$, angle $C$ is right. Take $C=(0,0),B=(\sqrt{a+3},0),D=(0,\sqrt{a+2})$. Equal lengths $AC=AD$ imply $A=(u,\dfrac{\sqrt{a+2}}{2})$. Using $AC^2=2a+5$ gives $u^2=\dfrac{7a+18}{4}$. Using $AB^2=a$ gives $u\sqrt{a+3}=a+4$. Consequently $4(a+4)^2=(7a+18)(a+3)$, or $(a-1)(3a+10)=0$. Since $a>0$, $a=1$. Then $A=(\dfrac{5}{2},\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2})$, $B=(2,0)$, $D=(0,\sqrt3)$. Angle $B$ is $120^\circ$ and angle $C$ is $90^\circ$; positive dot products of the adjacent side vectors at $A,D$ show both are acute. The difference is $\boxed{30^\circ}$.
