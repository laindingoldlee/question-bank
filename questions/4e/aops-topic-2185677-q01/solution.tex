Because the boxes look alike, a placement is determined only by which balls share a box. There are two possibilities: both balls in the same box, or the two balls in different boxes. The second needs at least two boxes. Hence the number of ways is
\[\boxed{1\text{ if }n=1,\quad\text{and}\quad2\text{ if }n\ge2}.\]
(For comparison, with $n$ distinguishable boxes there would be $n^2$ ways: $n$ with both balls together and $n(n-1)$ with them apart. Identifying placements that differ only by relabelling the boxes collapses the first group to $1$ and the second group to $1$.)
