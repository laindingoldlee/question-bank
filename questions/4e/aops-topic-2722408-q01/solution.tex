By the hockey-stick identity, $T_n=\dbinom{n+1}{2}$, $A_n=\dbinom{n+2}{3}$ and $B_n=\dbinom{n+3}{4}$. So
\[\dfrac{B_n}{T_n}=\dfrac{(n+3)(n+2)(n+1)n\cdot2}{24\,(n+1)n}=\dfrac{(n+2)(n+3)}{12}.\]
We need $12\mid(n+2)(n+3)$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $3$ divides the product iff $n\equiv0$ or $1\pmod3$.
\item Exactly one factor is even, so $4$ divides the product iff $n+2$ or $n+3$ is a multiple of $4$, i.e. $n\equiv2$ or $1\pmod4$.
\end{itemize}
Together, $n\equiv1,6,9,10\pmod{12}$. Since $2021=12\cdot168+5$, the count is $4\cdot168+1=\boxed{673}$ (the extra one is $n=2017\equiv1$).
