For odd primes, $1+p+\cdots+p^e$ is odd exactly when $e$ is even, while the factor for $2$ is always odd. Thus $\sigma(n)$ is odd precisely when $n$ is a square or twice a square. Among the $1011$ odd numbers up to $2022$, exclude the $22$ odd squares, leaving $989$ cases with odd $n$ and even $\sigma(n)$. For even $n$, include $22$ even squares and $31$ twice-squares. These sets are disjoint. The total is $989+22+31=\boxed{1042}$.
