Suppose a triangle with integer sides has an angle $\theta$ between sides $b$ and $c$, opposite side $a$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(5,0),C=(1.6,2.4);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(arc(A,0.6,0,degrees(C-A)),red);
label("$\theta$",0.95*dir(degrees(C-A)/2),red);
label("$b$",(A+B)/2,S);label("$c$",(A+C)/2,NW);label("$a$",(B+C)/2,NE);
label("$A$",A,SW);label("$B$",B,SE);label("$C$",C,N);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

By the law of cosines,
\[
\cos\theta=\dfrac{b^2+c^2-a^2}{2bc},
\]
which is a rational number.

\textbf{$\theta=30^\circ,45^\circ,135^\circ,150^\circ$ are impossible.} Their cosines are $\pm\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ and $\pm\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$, which are irrational.

\textbf{$\theta=60^\circ$ or $120^\circ$ gives an irrational area.} Then $\sin\theta=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ and
\[
\text{area}=\dfrac{1}{2}bc\sin\theta=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}\,bc ,
\]
which is irrational because $bc$ is a positive integer. (Such triangles exist, for example the equilateral ones or $3,5,7$ with a $120^\circ$ angle, but none has integer area.)

So no triangle has all three properties, and
\[
N=\boxed{0}.
\]
