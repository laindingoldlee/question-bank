Minimising the average of the four distances is the same as minimising their sum.

The four points are the vertices of the convex quadrilateral $dacb$ (going $d\to a\to c\to b$, the slopes of the sides $\dfrac{1}{3},\dfrac{2}{3},\dfrac{5}{2}$ increase, and $a$ and $c$ lie below the line $db$, $y=x+2$). Its diagonals are $ab$ and $cd$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair a=(0,0),bb=(5,7),c=(3,2),d=(-3,-1),p=(5/9,7/9);
draw(d--a--c--bb--cycle,gray);
draw(a--bb,blue+dashed);draw(c--d,blue+dashed);
dot(a);dot(bb);dot(c);dot(d);dot(p,red);
label("$a\,(0,0)$",a,SE);label("$b\,(5,7)$",bb,NE);label("$c\,(3,2)$",c,SE);label("$d\,(-3,-1)$",d,SW);
label("$p$",p,NW,red);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

For every point $p$, the triangle inequality gives
\[
ap+bp\ge ab\qquad\text{and}\qquad cp+dp\ge cd,
\]
with equality exactly when $p$ lies on segment $ab$, respectively on segment $cd$. So the sum is always at least $ab+cd$, and it equals $ab+cd$ only at the point common to both diagonals.

The line $ab$ is $y=\dfrac{7}{5}x$ and the line $cd$ is $y=\dfrac{1}{2}x+\dfrac{1}{2}$. They meet where $\dfrac{7}{5}x=\dfrac{1}{2}x+\dfrac{1}{2}$, that is $x=\dfrac{5}{9}$, $y=\dfrac{7}{9}$. This point is on both segments ($0<\dfrac{5}{9}<3$ and $-3<\dfrac{5}{9}<3$). Hence
\[
p=\boxed{\left(\dfrac{5}{9},\dfrac{7}{9}\right)}.
\]
