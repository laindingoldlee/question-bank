The two trips have the same length, so the times are inversely proportional to the speeds:
\[\text{walking time}:\text{riding time}=40:8=5:1.\]
The total time is $30$ minutes, so she walked for $\dfrac56\cdot30=\boxed{25\text{ minutes}}$ (and rode for $5$ minutes). The distance is
\[8\cdot\dfrac{25}{60}=\boxed{\dfrac{10}{3}\text{ km}}\approx3.33\text{ km}.\]
(Check with algebra: $\dfrac d8+\dfrac{d}{40}=\dfrac{6d}{40}=\dfrac12$ gives $d=\dfrac{10}{3}$.) Relative speed is not needed: there is only one traveller, and the useful idea is that for a fixed distance, time is inversely proportional to speed.
