Let $H$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $A$ to line $BC$.

\emph{Triangle $ADH$ is equilateral.} In right triangle $AHB$, $\angle B=30^\circ$, so $AH=\dfrac12AB=AD$ and $\angle BAH=60^\circ$. Also $D$ is the midpoint of the hypotenuse $\overline{AB}$, so $DH=DA=DB$. Thus $AD=DH=AH$, and $\angle ADH=60^\circ$; triangle $DHB$ is isosceles with $\angle DHB=\angle DBH=30^\circ$.

\emph{Locating $C$.} Since $\angle ADC=45^\circ<60^\circ=\angle ADH$, ray $DC$ lies between rays $DA$ and $DH$, so $H$ lies between $B$ and $C$ and $\angle HDC=60^\circ-45^\circ=15^\circ$. In triangle $DHC$, $\angle DHC=180^\circ-30^\circ=150^\circ$, so $\angle HCD=180^\circ-150^\circ-15^\circ=15^\circ=\angle HDC$. Hence $HC=HD=AH$.

\emph{Conclusion.} Right triangle $AHC$ is isosceles, so $\angle HAC=45^\circ$, and
\[\angle BAC=\angle BAH+\angle HAC=60^\circ+45^\circ=\boxed{105^\circ}.\]
(Check: $\angle C=180^\circ-105^\circ-30^\circ=45^\circ$.)
