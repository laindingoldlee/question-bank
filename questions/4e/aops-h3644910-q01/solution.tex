The right side is nonnegative, so $0\le y\le\sqrt a$. Squaring once gives
\[
\sqrt{x+a}=x-a-y^2+2y\sqrt a.
\]
Put $k=x-a-y^2\in\mathbb Z$. Squaring again, the irrational term is $4ky\sqrt a$. Since $a$ is not a square and all other terms are integers, $ky=0$.

If $y>0$, then $k=0$ and $x=a+y^2$. The squared equation becomes $2a+y^2=4ay^2$, or $y^2=2\dfrac{a}{(4a-1)}<1$, impossible for a positive integer $y$. Thus $y=0$.

Now $k=x-a=\sqrt{x+a}\ge0$ is an integer. Substituting $x=a+k$ gives $k^2=k+2a$, so $a=\dfrac{k(k-1)}{2}$. The largest such number below $100$ is $91$, corresponding to $k=14$; it is not a square. Taking $x=105,y=0$ verifies the original equation because $x+a=196$. Therefore $\boxed{a=91}$.
