Of the $36$ equally likely outcomes, $25$ have no $1$, $10$ have exactly one $1$, and $1$ has two $1$'s. So you win money with probability
\[\dfrac{10+1}{36}=\boxed{\dfrac{11}{36}}.\]
With a $\$100$ bet the net gain is $+100$, $+200$ or $-100$ dollars, so its expected value is
\[\dfrac{10}{36}\cdot100+\dfrac{1}{36}\cdot200-\dfrac{25}{36}\cdot100=\dfrac{1000+200-2500}{36}=\boxed{-\dfrac{325}{9}\text{ dollars}}\approx-\$36.11.\]
On average you lose about $\$36$ per $\$100$ bet.
