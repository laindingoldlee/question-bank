\textbf{(a)} The hundreds digit is \(1\), \(2\) or \(9\) and the tens and units digits are any of \(0,1,2,9\), independently: \(3\cdot4\cdot4=48\) numbers. Since each position varies independently and uniformly, the mean is the number formed by the mean digits:
\[
100\cdot\dfrac{1+2+9}{3}+10\cdot\dfrac{0+1+2+9}{4}+\dfrac{0+1+2+9}{4}=400+30+3=\boxed{433}.
\]
(Directly: the sum is \(16\cdot100\cdot12+12\cdot10\cdot12+12\cdot12=20784\), and \(\dfrac{20784}{48}=433\).)

\textbf{(b)} With distinct digits there are \(3\cdot3\cdot2=18\) numbers. Mean is still additive by position, but the positions are no longer uniform over \(\{0,1,2,9\}\). The hundreds digit is \(1,2,9\) each \(6\) times, mean \(4\). For the tens digit: given the hundreds digit \(h\), the tens digit is one of the other three digits, each twice. Summing over \(h\), the tens digits used total \(2[(12-1)+(12-2)+(12-9)]=2\cdot24=48\), so the mean tens digit is \(\dfrac{48}{18}=\dfrac{8}{3}\); by symmetry the units digit has the same mean. So the mean is
\[
100\cdot4+10\cdot\dfrac{8}{3}+\dfrac{8}{3}=400+\dfrac{88}{3}=\boxed{\dfrac{1288}{3}=429\dfrac{1}{3}}.
\]
So a mean of \(429\dfrac{1}{3}\) is the correct answer when digits may not repeat, and \(433\) when they may; the usual reading of the problem allows repetition, giving \(433\).
