\textbf{When March has five Mondays.} March has $31=4\cdot7+3$ days, so the weekdays of March $1$, $2$, $3$ occur five times and the others four times. So March has five Mondays exactly when March $1$ is a Saturday, Sunday or Monday.

\textbf{How March $1$ moves.} From March $1$ of year $y-1$ to March $1$ of year $y$ there are $366$ days if $y$ is a leap year and $365$ otherwise, so the weekday moves forward by $2$ or by $1$. In $2001$--$2100$ the leap years are $2004,2008,\dots,2096$ ($2100$ is not a leap year).

Going backwards from Saturday $2014$, the weekdays of March $1$ for $2001,\dots,2016$ are
\[\text{Th, F, \textbf{Sa}, \textbf{M}, Tu, W, Th, \textbf{Sa}, \textbf{Su}, \textbf{M}, Tu, Th, F, \textbf{Sa}, \textbf{Su}, Tu}.\]

\textbf{Years $2001$--$2084$.} Split these years into groups of four, $4j+1,\dots,4j+4$. Within a group, if March $1$ of the first year is weekday $w$, the four years have weekdays $w$, $w+1$, $w+2$, $w+4$ (the last year of the group is a leap year). From one group to the next the weekday moves by $1+1+2+1=5$. So seven consecutive groups ($28$ years) start on $w$, $w+5$, $\dots$, $w+30$, which are all different modulo $7$; hence for each of the four positions in a group, the seven groups use every weekday once, and every weekday occurs exactly $4$ times in the $28$ years. It also follows that the pattern repeats every $28$ years ($7\cdot5=35\equiv0$). So in $2001$--$2084$ (three blocks of $28$) each weekday occurs $12$ times as March $1$, and Saturday, Sunday or Monday occurs $3\cdot12=36$ times.

\textbf{Years $2085$--$2100$.} The years $2085$--$2099$ repeat $2001$--$2015$, in which the bold years $2003$, $2004$, $2008$, $2009$, $2010$, $2014$ and $2015$ give $7$ good years. March $1$, $2099$ is a Sunday (like $2015$), and $2100$ is not a leap year, so March $1$, $2100$ is a Monday: $1$ more.

The total is $36+7+1=\boxed{44}$.
