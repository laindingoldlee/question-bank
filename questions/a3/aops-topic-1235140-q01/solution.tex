\emph{Proof 1 (area).} The area is $\dfrac{1}{2}bc\sin A=\dfrac{1}{2}ca\sin B=\dfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C$. Dividing by $\dfrac{1}{2}abc$ gives $\dfrac{\sin A}{a}=\dfrac{\sin B}{b}=\dfrac{\sin C}{c}$.

\emph{Proof 2 (altitude).} Drop the altitude from $C$, of length $h$. Then $h=b\sin A=a\sin B$ (this also holds when an angle is obtuse, since $\sin(180^\circ-x)=\sin x$), so $\dfrac{\sin A}{a}=\dfrac{\sin B}{b}$; similarly for $C$.

\emph{Proof 3 (circumcircle).} With circumradius $R$, the chord $BC=a$ subtends the inscribed angle $A$, so $a=2R\sin A$ (move $A$ along the arc to the end of the diameter through $B$, which keeps the angle). Hence each ratio equals $\dfrac{1}{2R}$. $\blacksquare$
