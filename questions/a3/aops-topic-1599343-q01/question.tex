Let $s_n=\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2^2}+\dfrac{1}{2^3}+\dots+\dfrac{1}{2^n}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Show that $s_n=1-\dfrac{1}{2^n}$, so that every $s_n$ is less than $1$.
\item[(b)] A student argues: ``every partial sum is less than $1$, so the infinite sum $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2^2}+\dfrac{1}{2^3}+\cdots$ must be less than $1$.'' Explain the error, and prove that the infinite sum equals $1$.
\end{enumerate}
