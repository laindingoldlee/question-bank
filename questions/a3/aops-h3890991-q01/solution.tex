First suppose $0<x<y$. Write $x=da,y=db$ with $\gcd(a,b)=1$ and $a<b$. Taking positive $d$th roots gives $d^{b-a}a^b=b^a$. A prime divisor of $a$ would divide the left side but not the right, so $a=1$. Hence $d^{b-1}=b$. We have $d\ge2$; otherwise $b=1$. For $b\ge3$, $2^{b-1}>b$ (starting at 3 and then doubling), impossible. Thus $b=2,d=2$, yielding $(x,y)=(2,4)$ and its reversal.

If both integers are negative, write $x=-a,y=-b$ with positive distinct $a,b$. Equality of magnitudes gives $a^b=b^a$, so $\{a,b\}=\{2,4\}$. Both exponents are even, and both negative pairs work.

If the signs differ, write $x=a>0,y=-b<0$. Positivity forces $a$ even, and the magnitude equality implies $a^b b^a=1$, impossible for even $a\ge2$. If one entry is zero, a negative exponent is undefined, while a positive other entry gives 0 on one side and 1 on the other.

Thus the complete list is $\boxed{(2,4),(4,2),(-2,-4),(-4,-2)}$.
