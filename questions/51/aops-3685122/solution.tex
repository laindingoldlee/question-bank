Put the base centres at $(\pm\dfrac{3}{2},0)$ and the apex at $(0,H)$. By symmetry a line normal can be taken as $(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)$, $0\le\theta\le90^\circ$. A line intersecting both unit disks can have normal offset at most $1-(\dfrac{3}{2})\sin\theta$, and requires $\sin\theta\le\dfrac{2}{3}$. Its least distance to the apex is therefore $H\cos\theta+(\dfrac{3}{2})\sin\theta-1$. This positive concave function attains its minimum on the interval at an endpoint. The minimum radius is $\min(H-1,\dfrac{H\sqrt5}{3})$. Thus (a) $\boxed{\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{2}-1}$, attained by a horizontal common tangent; (b) $\boxed{2\sqrt5}$, attained by an internal common tangent of the base disks.
