\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Prove that $0.999\ldots=1$, where $0.999\ldots$ means the limit of $0.9,\ 0.99,\ 0.999,\ \dots$.
\item[(b)] More generally, let $d$ be a digit from $0$ to $8$ and let $u$ be a terminating decimal whose last digit is $d$ in the $k$-th place after the point. Prove that the decimal obtained by following $u$ with infinitely many $9$s equals $u+10^{-k}$. (For example $5.3999\ldots=5.4$ and $156.7999\ldots=156.8$.)
\item[(c)] A student argues: ``$1-0.999\ldots=0.000\ldots1$, and since the $1$ comes after infinitely many zeros we never reach it, so $0.000\ldots1=0$.'' What is wrong with this argument, and how can it be repaired?
\end{enumerate}
