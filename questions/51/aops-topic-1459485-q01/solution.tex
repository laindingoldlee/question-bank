Let the original number be $10t+u$, with tens digit $t$ and units digit $u$. Then
\[
10u+t=\dfrac{3}{8}(10t+u)\iff80u+8t=30t+3u\iff77u=22t\iff7u=2t .
\]
Since $t\le9$, $2t\le18$, and $7u=2t$ forces $u$ to be even and $7\mid t$: so $t=7$, $u=2$. The number is $\boxed{72}$; indeed $27=\dfrac{3}{8}\cdot72$.

Shortcut: the reversed number is $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of the original, so the original is a multiple of $8$ and the reversal a multiple of $3$ (hence so is the original). Among the two-digit multiples of $24$, namely $24,48,72,96$, only $72$ works.
