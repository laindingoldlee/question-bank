\emph{Construction.} In the transitive tournament (player $i$ beats player $j$ whenever $i<j$), each pair $\{i,i+1\}$ is dominant: everyone above beats both, everyone below loses to both. That gives $2015$ dominant pairs.

\emph{Bound.} Let $w(X)$ be the number of wins of $X$. If $\{A,B\}$ is dominant and $A$ beats $B$, every other player treats $A$ and $B$ alike, so $w(A)=w(B)+1$. Call $B$ a \emph{lower partner} of $A$.

\emph{Claim: no player has two lower partners.} Suppose $A$ beats $B$ and $B'$ with $\{A,B\}$, $\{A,B'\}$ dominant. Since $\{A,B'\}$ is dominant and $B$ loses to $A$, $B$ loses to $B'$. Since $\{A,B\}$ is dominant and $B'$ loses to $A$, $B'$ loses to $B$. Contradiction. In the same way no player has two upper partners.

Now the dominant pairs form a graph with no cycle: in a cycle, take the player with the fewest wins; both cycle neighbours have one more win, so both are upper partners of it, which is impossible. A graph on $2016$ vertices with no cycle has at most $2015$ edges. Hence the maximum is $\boxed{2015}$.
