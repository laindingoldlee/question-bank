\textbf{(a)} No. Take $m=2$, $n=3$ and $x=\dfrac{3}{2}$. Then $\gcd(2,3)=1$ and $mx=2\cdot\dfrac{3}{2}=3$, so $\dfrac{mx}{n}=\dfrac{3}{3}=1$ is an integer, but $x$ is not even an integer, let alone a multiple of $3$.

\textbf{(b)} Let $t=\dfrac{mx}{n}$, an integer, so $mx=nt$. Since $\gcd(m,n)=1$, Bezout's identity gives integers $u$ and $v$ with $um+vn=1$. Multiplying by $x$,
\[x=u(mx)+v(nx)=u(nt)+v(nx)=n\,(ut+vx),\]
and $ut+vx$ is an integer. So $x$ is a multiple of $n$. $\blacksquare$

(Equivalently: $n$ divides $mx$ and shares no prime factor with $m$, so every prime power dividing $n$ must divide $x$.)

\textbf{(c)} Take $m=2$, $n=4$ and $x=2$. Then $\dfrac{mx}{n}=\dfrac{4}{4}=1$ is an integer, but $2$ is not a multiple of $4$.
