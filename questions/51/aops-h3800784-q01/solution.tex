Scale $AB=AC=1$. Then the base angles of $ABC$ are $40^\circ$, and $BC=2\sin50^\circ$ by bisecting the vertex angle. Thus $AD=2\sin50^\circ$.
Consider a triangle whose angles at $A,C,D$ are $100^\circ,50^\circ,30^\circ$ and whose side $AC$ is $1$. The law of sines gives $AD=\dfrac{\sin50^\circ}{\sin30^\circ}=2\sin50^\circ$. It therefore has the same two sides $AC,AD$ and included angle as the actual triangle $ACD$, so they are congruent. Hence $\angle ACD=50^\circ$ and $\boxed{\angle BCD=50^\circ-40^\circ=10^\circ}$.
\begin{asy}
texpreamble("\usepackage{amsmath,amssymb}");
size(240);
defaultpen(fontsize(9pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(1,0),C=dir(100),D=(2sin(50pi/180),0);draw(A--D--C--cycle);draw(B--C);label("$A$",A,(0,-1));label("$B$",B,(0,-1));label("$C$",C,(-1,1));label("$D$",D,(1,-1));label("$100^\circ$",A+(.15,.2));label("$40^\circ$",C+(.12,-.25));label("$10^\circ$",C+(.5,-.3));
\end{asy}
