Here are three valid choices. In every row, use two cubic dice both labelled $(1,2,3,5,6,7)$. The two octahedral dice can be: (i) $(1,2,3,3,4,4,5,6)$ and $(1,2,4,4,5,5,7,8)$; (ii) $(1,3,3,4,5,6,6,8)$ and $(1,2,2,3,4,5,5,6)$; or (iii) $(1,2,2,3,3,4,4,5)$ and $(1,3,4,4,6,6,7,9)$. To verify, associate polynomial $D(t)=\sum t^{\text{face}}$ with a die, counting repeated labels with multiplicity. For each of these rows, multiplying the four polynomials gives exactly $(t+t^2+\cdots+t^6)^2(t+t^2+\cdots+t^8)^2$. The coefficient of $t^s$ counts outcomes totalling s, and all sets have $6^28^2$ equally likely outcomes. Thus the complete sum distributions agree. All four dice in each row are nonstandard. The computation audit enumerates these factorizations; under positive integer labels there are more than three possible choices, so these are three examples, not an exhaustive classification.
