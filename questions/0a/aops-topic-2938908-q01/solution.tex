Let the diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ meet at $P$ (inside, by convexity).
\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(4,0),C=(4.5,3),D=(0.8,2.5);pair P=extension(A,C,B,D);draw(A--B--C--D--cycle);draw(A--C);draw(B--D);dot(P);
label("$A$",A,SW);label("$B$",B,SE);label("$C$",C,NE);label("$D$",D,NW);label("$P$",P,S);
\end{asy}

\emph{Upper bound.} In triangles $ABC$ and $ACD$: $AC<AB+BC$ and $AC<CD+DA$, so $2AC<p$, the perimeter. Similarly $2BD<p$. Adding, $AC+BD<p$.

\emph{Lower bound.} In triangle $APB$, $AB<AP+PB$. Similarly $BC<BP+PC$, $CD<CP+PD$ and $DA<DP+PA$. Adding, $p<2(AC+BD)$. $\blacksquare$
