Let $O$ be the midpoint of $AB$. Complete the right triangle to a rectangle $ACBD$ by taking $D$ with $\vec{D}=\vec{A}+\vec{B}-\vec{C}$; it has right angles at all four vertices. The diagonals of a rectangle are equal and bisect each other, so $O$, the midpoint of diagonal $AB$, is also the midpoint of diagonal $CD$, and
\[
OC=\dfrac{1}{2}CD=\dfrac{1}{2}AB=OA=OB .
\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair ft(pair P,pair U,pair V){return U+dot(P-U,V-U)/dot(V-U,V-U)*(V-U);}
path ra(pair P,pair u,pair v,real s=0.2){return P+s*unit(u)--P+s*unit(u)+s*unit(v)--P+s*unit(v);}
pair O=(0,0); real R=2;
pair A=R*dir(180),B=R*dir(0),C=R*dir(62);
draw(circle(O,R),linewidth(0.8));
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
pair D=-C;
draw(A--D--B,red+dashed);draw(C--D,red+dashed);
draw(ra(C,A-C,B-C,0.2));
dot(O);dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);dot(D,red);
label("$O$",O,1.6*dir(-60));label("$D$",D,dir(242),red);label("$A$",A,W);label("$B$",B,E);label("$C$",C,N);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

So $O$ is equidistant from $A$, $B$ and $C$. Three non-collinear points lie on exactly one circle, and its centre is the unique point equidistant from them; hence $O$ is the centre of the given circle. Since the centre $O$ lies on chord $AB$, the chord $AB$ is a diameter. $\blacksquare$

(Alternatively, by the inscribed angle theorem the arc $AB$ not containing $C$ measures $2\cdot90^\circ=180^\circ$, so it is a semicircle.)
