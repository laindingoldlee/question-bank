Set $B=(0,0)$, $C=(6,0)$. The side lengths give $A=(\dfrac{9}{4},5\dfrac{\sqrt 7}{4})$. The circumcenter lies at $(3,\dfrac{1}{\sqrt7})$ and the circumradius is $\dfrac{8}{\sqrt7}$. Since the internal bisector meets the midpoint of arc $BC$ not containing $A$, we have $D=(3,-\sqrt7)$.

Triangles $DBC$ and $DMN$ are similar, with height ratio
\[
\dfrac{\sqrt7+5\dfrac{\sqrt 7}{4}}{\sqrt7}=\dfrac{9}{4}.
\]
Thus $MN=(\dfrac{9}{4})6=\dfrac{27}{2}$. The line intersections are $M=(-\dfrac{15}{4},5\dfrac{\sqrt 7}{4})$ and $N=(\dfrac{39}{4},5\dfrac{\sqrt 7}{4})$, so $A$ lies between them. The two requested triangles have the same altitude $5\dfrac{\sqrt 7}{4}$, and their bases total $AM+AN=MN$. Therefore
\[
[ANC]+[MAB]=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{27}{2}\cdot\dfrac{5\sqrt7}{4}
=\dfrac{135\sqrt7}{16}.
\]
The answer is $\boxed{158}$.
