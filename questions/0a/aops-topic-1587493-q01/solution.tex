The parabola crosses the $x$-axis at the zeros of $(x-6)(x+12)$, namely $x=6$ and $x=-12$. A parabola is symmetric about the vertical line through its vertex, so the two $x$-intercepts are mirror images in that line, and the vertex lies halfway between them:
\[
x=\dfrac{6+(-12)}{2}=-3 .
\]
(Check by expanding: $y=x^2+6x-72=(x+3)^2-81$, so the vertex is $(-3,-81)$.)

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(7cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
import graph;
real f(real x){return (x-6)*(x+12)/9;}
draw((-15,0)--(9,0),gray,Arrow(TeXHead));draw((0,-10.2)--(0,5.5),gray,Arrow(TeXHead));
label("$x$",(9,0),E,gray);label("$y$",(0,5.5),N,gray);
draw(graph(f,-14.2,8.2),blue+linewidth(1));
draw((-3,4.5)--(-3,-9),dashed);
dot((-12,0));dot((6,0));dot((-3,-9));
label("$(-12,0)$",(-12,0),SW);label("$(6,0)$",(6,0),SE);label("$(-3,-81)$",(-3,-9),SW);
label("$x=-3$",(-3,4.5),NW);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

(The vertical scale of the figure is compressed.) The answer is $\boxed{\text{(B) }-3}$.
