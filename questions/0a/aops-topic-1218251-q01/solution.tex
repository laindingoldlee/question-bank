Each flip moves one egg between the two players, in either direction with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$. So Emily's expected number of eggs never changes: her egg count is a fair game (a martingale). The process ends with probability $1$ (each duel is a fair random walk that is absorbed with probability $1$), and at the end Emily has either $15$ or $0$ eggs. Her expected final count equals her initial count:
\[15\cdot P(\text{Emily wins})=1,\qquad P=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{15}}.\]
The pairing rule and the heads/tails convention do not matter, as long as each egg transfer is equally likely in both directions.
