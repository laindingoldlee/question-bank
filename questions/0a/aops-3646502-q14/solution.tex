For an odd prime let $A=(p-1)!$. Wilson gives $A=-1+pw$, so $A^p\equiv-1\pmod{p^2}$. Also $H_{p-1}\equiv0\pmod p$, by pairing each reciprocal with its negative. Rewrite the expression as $A^pH_{p-1}+\dfrac{(A^p+1)}{p}+1$. All denominators in the first term are invertible modulo $p$, and the middle term is an integer divisible by $p$. Thus $g(p)=1$. Direct substitution also gives $g(2)=1$. There are 25 primes at most 100, hence the sum is $\boxed{25}$.
