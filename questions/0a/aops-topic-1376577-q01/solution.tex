Let $s=x+y+z$. Then $x=s-(y+z)$, so
\[
\dfrac{x^2}{y+z}=x\cdot\dfrac{s-(y+z)}{y+z}=s\cdot\dfrac{x}{y+z}-x ,
\]
and similarly for the other two terms. Adding,
\[
\dfrac{x^2}{y+z}+\dfrac{y^2}{z+x}+\dfrac{z^2}{x+y}=s\left(\dfrac{x}{y+z}+\dfrac{y}{z+x}+\dfrac{z}{x+y}\right)-(x+y+z)=s\cdot1-s=\boxed{0}.
\]
Such numbers exist: for instance, with $y=1$ and $z=2$ the condition becomes $\dfrac{x}{3}+\dfrac{1}{x+2}+\dfrac{2}{x+1}=1$, which has a real root between $-2$ and $-1$ (the left side tends to $+\infty$ as $x\to-2^+$ and to $-\infty$ as $x\to-1^-$).
