Let the altitudes from $A$ and $B$ have the same length $h$, so they fall on sides $a=BC$ and $b=CA$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(5,0),C=(1.5,3);
pair Ha=B+dot(A-B,unit(C-B))*unit(C-B), Hb=A+dot(B-A,unit(C-A))*unit(C-A);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(1)); draw(A--Ha,blue); draw(B--Hb,blue);
label("$A$",A,SW);label("$B$",B,SE);label("$C$",C,N); label("$h_a$",(A+Ha)/2,NW); label("$h_b$",(B+Hb)/2,NE);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The area of the triangle is both $\dfrac{1}{2}ah$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}bh$, so $a=b$: the triangle is isosceles with $CA=CB$. $\blacksquare$
