No. Label rows and columns 1 through 8. Remove $(2,1),(2,3),(1,4)$, which have odd row-plus-column parity, and $(8,2),(8,4),(8,6)$, which have even parity. The remaining squares are connected. However, squares $(1,1)$ and $(1,3)$ each have only one remaining neighbour, namely $(1,2)$. Each would have to share a domino with that same square, impossible. Equal counts of the two colours are necessary but not sufficient.
\begin{asy}
import geometry;
usepackage("amsmath");
size(270);
for(int i=1;i<=8;++i)for(int j=1;j<=8;++j){bool gone=(i==2&&(j==1||j==3))||(i==1&&j==4)||(i==8&&(j==2||j==4||j==6));if(!gone)filldraw((j,-i)--(j+1,-i)--(j+1,-i-1)--(j,-i-1)--cycle,(i+j)%2==0?white:lightgray,black);}label("A",(1.5,-1.5));label("B",(2.5,-1.5));label("C",(3.5,-1.5));label("A and C both need B",(5,-10));
\end{asy}
