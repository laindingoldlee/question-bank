The divisor sum is multiplicative over relatively prime prime powers. Each nontrivial factor $\sigma(p^e)$ is at least $3$ and must divide $28$, so it can only be $4,7,14,$ or $28$.

The prime-power possibilities are $\sigma(3)=4$, $\sigma(2^2)=7$, and $\sigma(13)=14$. None gives $28$: exponent $1$ would require $p=27$; exponent $2$ would require $p^2+p+1=28$, which has no integer solution; and exponents at least $3$ give only $\sigma(2^3)=15$ below $28$, since $\sigma(2^4)=31$ and $\sigma(3^3)=40$.

The only product of the available nontrivial divisor sums equalling $28$ is $4\cdot7$, corresponding to $n=3\cdot2^2=\boxed{12}$.
