A maximizing list has no $1$: combining it with another part increases the product. No part $k\ge5$ is needed, because replacing it by $3,k-3$ increases the product, as $3(k-3)>k$. A part $4$ may be replaced by $2,2$ without changing the product. Thus a maximum is achieved using only twos and threes.

There can be at most two twos, since replacing three twos by two threes increases the product from $8$ to $9$. The sum condition modulo $3$ then forces exactly two twos and thirty-two threes. Therefore the maximum is
\[
\boxed{4\cdot3^{32}}.
\]
