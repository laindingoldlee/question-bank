The quadrilateral is convex (each turn along $(0,0)\to(1,2)\to(3,4)\to(6,5)\to(0,0)$ is clockwise), so the diagonal from $(0,0)$ to $(3,4)$ splits it into two triangles.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair O=(0,0),P=(1,2),Q=(3,4),R=(6,5);
for(int i=0;i<=6;++i) draw((i,0)--(i,5.5),gray(0.85));
for(int j=0;j<=5;++j) draw((0,j)--(6.5,j),gray(0.85));
draw((0,0)--(6.8,0),Arrow); draw((0,0)--(0,5.8),Arrow);
fill(O--P--Q--cycle,palecyan); fill(O--Q--R--cycle,paleyellow);
draw(O--P--Q--R--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(O--Q,red+dashed);
dot(O);dot(P);dot(Q);dot(R);
label("$(0,0)$",O,SW,fontsize(9pt)); label("$(1,2)$",P,W,fontsize(9pt)); label("$(3,4)$",Q,NW,fontsize(9pt)); label("$(6,5)$",R,NE,fontsize(9pt));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

For a triangle with vertices $(0,0)$, $(x_1,y_1)$, $(x_2,y_2)$ the area is $\dfrac{1}{2}\left|x_1y_2-x_2y_1\right|$. So
\[
[(0,0),(1,2),(3,4)]=\dfrac{1}{2}\left|1\cdot4-3\cdot2\right|=1,\qquad
[(0,0),(3,4),(6,5)]=\dfrac{1}{2}\left|3\cdot5-6\cdot4\right|=\dfrac{9}{2}.
\]
The area is $1+\dfrac{9}{2}=\boxed{\dfrac{11}{2}}$.

The same computation in one step is the shoelace formula:
\[
\dfrac{1}{2}\left|(0\cdot2-1\cdot0)+(1\cdot4-3\cdot2)+(3\cdot5-6\cdot4)+(6\cdot0-0\cdot5)\right|=\dfrac{1}{2}\left|0-2-9+0\right|=\dfrac{11}{2}.
\]
