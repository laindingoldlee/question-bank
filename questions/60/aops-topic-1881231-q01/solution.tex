Let the original speed be $v$ km per hour and the distance $d$ km. At speed $\dfrac45v$, a distance $D$ takes $\dfrac{5D}{4v}$ hours instead of $\dfrac Dv$, i.e. $\dfrac{D}{4v}$ hours longer.

\textbf{Actual journey.} After the accident $d-v$ km remain. The delay is the stop plus the slowdown:
\[\dfrac12+\dfrac{d-v}{4v}=2\iff d-v=6v\iff d=7v.\]

\textbf{Accident $80$ km later.} Then $d-v-80$ km remain:
\[\dfrac12+\dfrac{d-v-80}{4v}=1\iff d-v-80=2v\iff d=3v+80.\]

So $7v=3v+80$, giving $v=20$ and $d=140$. The original speed is $\boxed{20\text{ km per hour}}$ and the distance is $\boxed{140\text{ km}}$.

\emph{Check.} On time the trip takes $7$ h. Actually: $1+\dfrac12+\dfrac{120}{16}=9$ h, $2$ h late. In the second case the accident is at $100$ km after $5$ h: $5+\dfrac12+\dfrac{40}{16}=8$ h, $1$ h late.
