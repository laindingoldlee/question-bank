Joining the centre $O$ to the vertices and edge midpoints cuts the hexagon into $12$ congruent right triangles $OMV$, each with a right angle at the midpoint $M$ of an edge and a vertex $V$, with $MV$ half a side. By symmetry it suffices to work in one of them, where the nearest marked point is $M$ or $V$.

Points nearer to $V$ than to $M$ lie beyond the perpendicular bisector of $MV$, which is parallel to $OM$ and halfway between $M$ and $V$. It cuts off from triangle $OMV$ a triangle similar to it with ratio $\dfrac{1}{2}$ (the shaded parts below), so with $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of its area.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair[] V; for(int k=0;k<6;++k) V.push(dir(60k));
for(int k=0;k<6;++k){ pair A=V[k],B=V[(k+1)%6],M=(A+B)/2; pair Pa=(3A+B)/4,Pb=(A+3B)/4;
 fill(A--Pa--A/2--cycle,lightblue); fill(B--Pb--B/2--cycle,lightblue); draw((0,0)--A,gray); draw((0,0)--M,gray); draw(Pa--A/2,dashed); draw(Pb--B/2,dashed);}
draw(V[0]--V[1]--V[2]--V[3]--V[4]--V[5]--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
for(int k=0;k<6;++k){ dot(V[k],blue); dot((V[k]+V[(k+1)%6])/2,red); }
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Hence the probability is $\boxed{\dfrac{1}{4}}$.
