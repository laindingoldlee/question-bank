\textbf{Casework.} Since the boxes are indistinguishable, a distribution is determined by how the set of balls is split into at most $3$ groups. List the possible group sizes:
\begin{itemize}
\item $5,0,0$: $1$ way;
\item $4,1,0$: choose the single ball, $5$ ways;
\item $3,2,0$: choose the pair, $\dbinom52=10$ ways;
\item $3,1,1$: choose the three balls together, $\dbinom53=10$ ways;
\item $2,2,1$: choose the single ball ($5$ ways) and split the other four into two pairs ($3$ ways), $15$ ways.
\end{itemize}
In total $1+5+10+10+15=\boxed{41}$.

\textbf{Counting with labelled boxes and correcting.} If the boxes are labelled, each ball has $3$ choices: $3^5=243$ distributions. Every unlabelled distribution with three nonempty groups, or with two nonempty groups and one empty box, corresponds to exactly $3!=6$ labelled ones (the three ``groups'', counting the empty one, can be given the labels in $6$ ways, and they are all different). The single distribution with all five balls together corresponds to only $3$ labelled ones (choose the box that gets them). Hence
\[243=6\cdot(41-1)+3\cdot1,\qquad\text{so the answer is}\qquad\dfrac{243-3}{6}+1=41.\]
(Stars and bars counts identical balls, so it does not apply here: with labelled boxes it would give $\dbinom72=21$, the number of ways to put $5$ \emph{identical} balls into $3$ labelled boxes.)
