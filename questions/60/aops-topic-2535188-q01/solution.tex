Let $f(n)$ be the number of tilings of a $3\times n$ rectangle (with $3$ rows and $n$ columns), and $f(0)=1$. Look at the leftmost column.
\begin{itemize}
\item If it is covered by one vertical brick, the rest is a $3\times(n-1)$ rectangle: $f(n-1)$ tilings.
\item Otherwise, each of its three cells is covered by a horizontal brick starting in that column, so three horizontal bricks fill the first three columns exactly, leaving a $3\times(n-3)$ rectangle: $f(n-3)$ tilings.
\end{itemize}
Hence $f(n)=f(n-1)+f(n-3)$ for $n\ge3$, with $f(0)=f(1)=f(2)=1$. Then
\[f(3),f(4),\dots,f(17)=2,3,4,6,9,13,19,28,41,60,88,129,189,277,406.\]
So there are $\boxed{406}$ tilings.
