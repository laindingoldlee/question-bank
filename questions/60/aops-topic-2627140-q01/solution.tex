Take a regular tetrahedron $ABCD$ with edge $2$ and let $M$ be the midpoint of $\overline{AB}$. In the equilateral faces $ABC$ and $ABD$, the medians $CM$ and $DM$ are perpendicular to $AB$ and have length $\sqrt3$, so the dihedral angle along $AB$ is $\theta=\angle CMD$:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(2.4,-0.5),C=(3.3,0.7);pair G=(A+B+C)/3;pair D=(G.x-0.1,2.6);pair M=(A+B)/2;
draw(A--B--D--cycle,linewidth(0.8));draw(B--C--D,linewidth(0.8));draw(A--C,dashed);
draw(D--M);draw(C--M,dashed);
draw(arc(M,0.35,degrees(C-M),degrees(D-M)));label("$\theta$",M+0.55*dir((degrees(C-M)+degrees(D-M))/2));
label("$A$",A,W);label("$B$",B,S);label("$C$",C,E);label("$D$",D,N);label("$M$",M,S);
label("$\sqrt3$",(D+M)/2,W);label("$\sqrt3$",M+0.63*(C-M),rotate(-90)*unit(C-M),fontsize(8pt));label("$2$",(D+C)/2,NE);
\end{asy}

By the law of cosines in triangle $CMD$ with $CD=2$:
\[4=3+3-2\cdot3\cos\theta,\qquad\cos\theta=\boxed{\textbf{(C) }\dfrac13}.\]
