Let each child receive $c$ candies and work backwards. Before the third child came, Mr.\ Cook ate half of what was left and the remaining $c$ went to the third child, so there were $2c$ candies after the second child left. Before that the second child took $c$, so there were $3c$, and this was half of what there was after the first child left: $6c$. Before the first child took $c$ there were $7c$, which was half of the original amount. So he bought
\[
14c
\]
candies. This is always a multiple of $7$, while for $c=1$ the number $14$ is not a multiple of $3$, $4$, $6$ or $11$. The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(D)}}$.
