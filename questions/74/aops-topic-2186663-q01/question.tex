I take a three-year loan of $\$10{,}000$ at an interest rate of $6\%$ compounded annually. At the end of each year the year's interest is added to the amount I owe, and I immediately make a payment; for the following year I am charged interest on the amount I still owe after that payment. My three payments are equal, and the loan is completely paid off by the third payment.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Find the amount of each payment, to the nearest cent.
\item[(b)] One solution says that $\dfrac{x}{1.06}$ dollars is the \emph{present value} of a payment of $x$ dollars made at the end of the first year, and that the loan is paid off exactly when the present values of the three payments add up to $\$10{,}000$. Explain why.
\end{enumerate}
