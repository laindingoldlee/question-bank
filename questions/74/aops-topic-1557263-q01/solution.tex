\textbf{Why the right angle is at $C$.} $C$ lies on the circle and $AB$ is a diameter, so $\angle ACB$ is inscribed in a semicircle and $\angle ACB=90^\circ$ (Thales' theorem). The angle at $B$ is not a right angle: $B$ is an end of the diameter, and the right angle of a triangle inscribed in a semicircle is always at the third point, the one on the arc.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(-2,0),B=(2,0),D=(5,0),E=(5,5),O=(0,0),C=(24/37,70/37);
draw(circle(O,2),linewidth(0.9));
draw(A--D,linewidth(0.9));draw(D--E,linewidth(0.9));draw(A--E,linewidth(0.9));
draw(B--C,blue+linewidth(0.9));
real s=0.3; draw(D+(-s,0)--D+(-s,s)--D+(0,s));
pair u=unit(A-C),v=unit(B-C); draw(C+s*u--C+s*u+s*v--C+s*v,blue);
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);dot(D);dot(E);dot(O);
label("$A$",A,SW);label("$B$",B,SE);label("$C$",C,NW);label("$D$",D,SE);label("$E$",E,NE);label("$O$",O,S);
label("$2$",(-1,0),S);label("$3$",(3.5,0),S);label("$5$",(5,2.5),E);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\textbf{Similar triangles.} Triangles $ACB$ and $ADE$ share the angle at $A$, and $\angle ACB=\angle ADE=90^\circ$, so they are similar (AA). Here $AB=4$, $AD=4+3=7$, $DE=5$ and $AE=\sqrt{7^2+5^2}=\sqrt{74}$. Hence
\[
\dfrac{AC}{AD}=\dfrac{BC}{DE}=\dfrac{AB}{AE}=\dfrac{4}{\sqrt{74}},\qquad AC=\dfrac{28}{\sqrt{74}},\qquad BC=\dfrac{20}{\sqrt{74}} .
\]
\textbf{Area.}
\[
[ABC]=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot AC\cdot BC=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{560}{74}=\boxed{\dfrac{140}{37}}.
\]
