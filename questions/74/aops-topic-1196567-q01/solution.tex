There are $4^4=256$ equally likely placements. Count those whose cookie counts are a rearrangement of
\begin{itemize}
\item $(1,1,1,1)$: $4!=24$;
\item $(2,1,1,0)$: choose the cookie with $2$ chips ($4$) and the empty one ($3$), then assign chips in $\dfrac{4!}{2!}=12$ ways: $144$;
\item $(2,2,0,0)$: choose the two cookies with chips ($6$), then split the chips $2$--$2$ in $\dbinom42=6$ ways: $36$.
\end{itemize}
The probability is $\dfrac{24+144+36}{256}=\dfrac{204}{256}=\boxed{\dfrac{51}{64}}$.

(Counting the $\dbinom73=35$ distributions of identical chips gives $\dfrac{19}{35}$, but those $35$ outcomes are not equally likely, which is why that answer is wrong.)
