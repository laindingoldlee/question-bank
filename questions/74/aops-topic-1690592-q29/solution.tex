The square has side $6$ and centre $M=(-2,1)$.

\textbf{The line must pass through $M$.} A line cutting the square into two trapezoids crosses two opposite sides. Say it crosses the left and right sides at heights $p$ and $q$ above the bottom side. The two pieces have areas $\dfrac{(p+q)\cdot6}{2}$ and $\dfrac{(12-p-q)\cdot6}{2}$; congruent pieces have equal areas, so $p+q=6$, and the midpoint of the two crossing points is at height $3$ above the bottom and halfway across, i.e.\ at the centre $M$. (The same argument works if the line crosses the top and bottom sides.)

\textbf{Every line through $M$ works.} The half-turn about $M$ maps the square to itself and the line to itself, and swaps the two pieces, so they are congruent.

So the line is the line through $O=(0,0)$ and $M=(-2,1)$, with slope $\dfrac{1-0}{-2-0}=\boxed{-\dfrac{1}{2}}$. It crosses the left side at $(-5,2.5)$ and the right side at $(1,-0.5)$, so the pieces are indeed trapezoids.
