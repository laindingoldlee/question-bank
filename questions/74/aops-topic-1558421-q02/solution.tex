Numbers $\equiv3\pmod4$ are $3,7,11,15,\dots$; the first that is $\equiv1\pmod7$ is $15$. By the Chinese remainder theorem the conditions are equivalent to $n\equiv15\pmod{28}$.

In the range $200\le n\le500$: $n=15+28k$ with $185\le28k\le485$, i.e.\ $7\le k\le17$ (since $28\cdot6=168<185$, $28\cdot7=196$, $28\cdot17=476\le485<504$). That gives the numbers $211,239,\dots,491$, which are $17-7+1=\boxed{11}$ integers.
