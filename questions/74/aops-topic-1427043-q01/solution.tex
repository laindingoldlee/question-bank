July and August have $31+31=62$ days, that is $8$ weeks and $6$ days. They contain $9$ Sundays exactly when the first Sunday of July is one of July $1,\dots,6$; call it July $s$.

The Sundays of July are $s, s+7, s+14,\dots$ and those of August start on August $s+4$ (since $s+35-31=s+4$). Consecutive Sundays alternate in parity. Checking the six cases:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
s & \text{July Sundays} & \text{August Sundays} & \text{odd dates}\\ \hline
1 & 1,8,15,22,29 & 5,12,19,26 & 3+2=5\\
2 & 2,9,16,23,30 & 6,13,20,27 & 2+2=4\\
3 & 3,10,17,24,31 & 7,14,21,28 & 3+2=5\\
4 & 4,11,18,25 & 1,8,15,22,29 & 2+3=5\\
5 & 5,12,19,26 & 2,9,16,23,30 & 2+2=4\\
6 & 6,13,20,27 & 3,10,17,24,31 & 2+3=5
\end{array}
\]
So $s=2$ or $s=5$. If $s=2$, August $6$ is a Sunday and August $7$ is a \textbf{Monday}; if $s=5$, August $2$ is a Sunday, so August $9$ is a Sunday and August $7$ is a \textbf{Friday}. Both cases really occur (for example in $2017$ and in $2015$). The answer is
\[
\boxed{\text{Monday or Friday}}.
\]
