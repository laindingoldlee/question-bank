Let $P$ be the product on the left and $N=\overline{abcd}$.

\textbf{Reductions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item $P$ is symmetric in $a,b,c,d$: it depends only on the multiset $M=\{a,b,c,d\}$ of digits. So we look for multisets $M$ for which $P(M)$ is a four-digit number whose digits are exactly $M$; such a number is a solution, and every solution arises this way.
\item At most one digit is $0$: if two digits were $0$, their sum would be a zero factor and $P=0$.
\item Since $N\equiv a+b+c+d\pmod 9$, we need $P(M)\equiv a+b+c+d\pmod9$.
\item $P$ increases when any digit increases, so for each choice of the three smallest digits only a short range of largest digits gives $1000\le P\le9999$. For example, if the smallest digit is at least $1$ and the next is at least $3$, then $P\ge(1+3)^3(3+3)^3>9999$; with smallest digit $0$, the other three digits $q\le r\le t$ give $P=qrt(q+r)(q+t)(r+t)$, which already exceeds $9999$ when $q\ge3$ and $t\ge4$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The check.} Running through the multisets in this way, there are $45$ with $1000\le P\le9999$, and only eight of them satisfy $P\equiv a+b+c+d\pmod9$:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccccc}
M&\{0,1,2,6\}&\{0,1,3,5\}&\{0,2,3,4\}&\{0,3,3,3\}&\{1,1,1,7\}&\{1,1,2,5\}&\{1,2,2,4\}&\{1,2,3,3\}\\\hline
P&2016&2880&5040&5832&4096&4536&6480&7200
\end{array}
\]
Only for $M=\{0,1,2,6\}$ are the digits of $P$ the elements of $M$. So the only solution is
\[
\boxed{2016}:\qquad(2+0)(2+1)(2+6)(0+1)(0+6)(1+6)=2\cdot3\cdot8\cdot1\cdot6\cdot7=2016 .
\]
