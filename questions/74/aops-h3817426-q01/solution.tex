Divisibility by $3$ is preserved in both directions: adding $3$ preserves it, and dividing an even integer by $2$ preserves it because $2$ is coprime to $3$. Thus a multiple of $3$ cannot reach $1$.
For every odd $n>3$, the next two steps are $n\mapsto n+3\mapsto\dfrac{n+3}{2}<n$. An even $n>1$ decreases immediately. Therefore, grouping odd steps with the following halving, every orbit eventually reaches $1$ or $3$. If the starting number is not divisible by $3$, it cannot reach $3$, so it reaches $1$. Multiples of $3$ eventually enter $3\mapsto6\mapsto3$. Hence precisely the $\boxed{\text{positive multiples of }3}$ never reach $1$.
