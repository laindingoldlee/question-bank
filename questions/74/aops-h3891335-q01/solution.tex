There are $n(n-1)\cdots(n-r+1)=\dfrac{n!}{(n-r)!}$ ordered selections of $r$ distinct elements. Each unordered subset produces exactly $r!$ such selections, one for each ordering. Dividing gives $\dfrac{n!}{r!(n-r)!}$. For $r=0$, both the empty subset count and the formula (using $0!=1$) equal $1$.
