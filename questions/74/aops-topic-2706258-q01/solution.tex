$\angle BEC=90^\circ$ because $BE\perp AC$, and $\angle BFC=90^\circ$ because $CF\perp AB$. A point $X$ with $\angle BXC=90^\circ$ lies on the circle with diameter $BC$ (converse of Thales' theorem). So $E$ and $F$, as well as $B$ and $C$, lie on that circle. $\blacksquare$ (The segment $FC$ is not a diameter in general; it is an altitude.)

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(1.2,3),B=(0,0),C=(4,0);
pair Ef=A+dot(B-A,C-A)/dot(C-A,C-A)*(C-A),F=A+dot(C-A,B-A)/dot(B-A,B-A)*(B-A);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.8));draw(B--Ef);draw(C--F);draw(circle((B+C)/2,2),dashed);
dot(Ef);dot(F);label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,SW);label("$C$",C,SE);label("$E$",Ef,NE);label("$F$",F,NW);
\end{asy}
