Let $a$, $b$ and $c$ be nonzero digits ($1$ to $9$), and let $\overline{abc}$, $\overline{bca}$ and $\overline{cab}$ be the three-digit numbers formed by writing the digits in these orders. Which of the following is NOT always true about the sum $S=\overline{abc}+\overline{bca}+\overline{cab}$?

\textbf{(a)} $S$ is divisible by $3$.\quad \textbf{(b)} $S$ is divisible by $2$.\quad \textbf{(c)} $S>330$.\quad \textbf{(d)} $S$ is divisible by $37$.\quad \textbf{(e)} $\dfrac{S}{a+b+c}$ is an integer.
