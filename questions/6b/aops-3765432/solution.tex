Let the common value be $k$. Multiply each equality of numerator to $k$ times denominator by that denominator, and sum cyclically. The sum on the left is $(x+y-1)(x-y)+(y+z-1)(y-z)+(z+x-1)(z-x)=0$ by cancellation. Thus $k((x-y)^2+(y-z)^2+(z-x)^2)=0$. The squared sum is positive by the domain restrictions, so $k=0$. Then $x+y=y+z=z+x=1$, forcing $x = y = z = \dfrac{1}{2}$, which violates the domain. Therefore no real solution exists.
