Call the sum $S$. For $n\ge1$ the ratio of the $(n+1)$st term to the $n$th term is $\dfrac{n+1}{10n}\le\dfrac{2}{10}\le\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the terms shrink at least as fast as a geometric series with ratio $\dfrac{1}{2}$; the series converges and we may manipulate it term by term.

Dividing by $10$ shifts every term one place to the right:
\[S=\dfrac{1}{10}+\dfrac{2}{100}+\dfrac{3}{1000}+\cdots,\qquad \dfrac{S}{10}=\phantom{\dfrac{1}{10}+{}}\dfrac{1}{100}+\dfrac{2}{1000}+\cdots.\]
Subtracting, each numerator drops by $1$:
\[S-\dfrac{S}{10}=\dfrac{1}{10}+\dfrac{1}{100}+\dfrac{1}{1000}+\cdots=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{10}}{1-\dfrac{1}{10}}=\dfrac{1}{9}.\]
So $\dfrac{9}{10}S=\dfrac{1}{9}$ and
\[S=\dfrac{10}{9^2}=\boxed{\dfrac{10}{81}}.\]
