(a) The three vertices of an equilateral triangle of side one include two of the same color by the pigeonhole principle. (b) Suppose every unit-distance pair has equal colors. Any two points at distance at most two can be joined by two unit segments, by taking an intersection of their unit circles; their colors must therefore agree. For arbitrary points, subdivide the segment joining them into pieces of length at most two and apply that conclusion successively. Every point would have the same color, contradicting the hypothesis. Thus an unlike-colored unit pair exists.
