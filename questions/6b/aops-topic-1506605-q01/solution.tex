Since $\angle A=90^\circ$ and $BC=2AB$, $\cos B=\dfrac{1}{2}$: $\angle ABC=60^\circ$ and $\angle ACB=30^\circ$. Hence
\[
\angle ABD=20^\circ,\ \angle DBC=40^\circ,\qquad\angle ACE=10^\circ,\ \angle ECB=20^\circ .
\]

\textbf{Angles at $D$, $E$, $F$.} In right triangle $ABD$, $\angle ADB=70^\circ$, so $\angle FDC=180^\circ-70^\circ=110^\circ$. In right triangle $AEC$, $\angle AEC=80^\circ$, so $\angle FEB=100^\circ$. In triangle $FBC$, $\angle BFC=180^\circ-40^\circ-20^\circ=120^\circ$, so $\angle DFE=120^\circ$ (vertical angles) and $\angle DFC=\angle BFE=60^\circ$.

\textbf{Collinearity.} Reflections preserve distances from points of the mirror, so $CH=CF=CK$. Also $\angle HCA=\angle ACF=10^\circ$ and $\angle KCB=\angle BCF=20^\circ$, so $\angle HCK=10^\circ+10^\circ+20^\circ+20^\circ=60^\circ$. Thus triangle $CHK$ is isosceles with apex angle $60^\circ$, i.e.\ equilateral, and $\angle CHK=60^\circ$.

Let line $HK$ meet line $AC$ at $D'$. In triangle $CHD'$, $\angle HCD'=10^\circ$ and $\angle CHD'=60^\circ$, so $\angle HD'C=110^\circ$. Reflecting in $AC$ (which fixes $C$ and $D'$ and sends $H$ to $F$) gives $\angle FD'C=110^\circ$. But also $\angle FDC=110^\circ$, with $D$ and $D'$ on line $AC$ between $A$ and $C$ and $F$ on the same side: the lines $D'F$ and $DF$ make equal corresponding angles with $AC$, so they are parallel; as both pass through $F$, they coincide, and $D'=D$. Hence $D$ lies on $HK$. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{Isosceles triangle.} By the law of sines in triangles $FDC$ ($\angle FCD=10^\circ$, $\angle FDC=110^\circ$), $FEB$ ($\angle FBE=20^\circ$, $\angle FEB=100^\circ$) and $FBC$:
\[
FD=\dfrac{FC\sin10^\circ}{\sin110^\circ},\qquad FE=\dfrac{FB\sin20^\circ}{\sin100^\circ},\qquad\dfrac{FC}{FB}=\dfrac{\sin40^\circ}{\sin20^\circ}=2\cos20^\circ .
\]
Using $\sin110^\circ=\cos20^\circ$ and $\sin100^\circ=\cos10^\circ$,
\[
FD=\dfrac{2\cos20^\circ\,FB\sin10^\circ}{\cos20^\circ}=2FB\sin10^\circ,\qquad FE=\dfrac{FB\cdot2\sin10^\circ\cos10^\circ}{\cos10^\circ}=2FB\sin10^\circ .
\]
So $FD=FE$, and triangle $DEF$ is isosceles with apex $F$ (its base angles are $30^\circ$, as $\angle DFE=120^\circ$). $\blacksquare$
