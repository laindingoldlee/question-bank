\textbf{(a)} Add and subtract $x^2$ to make a difference of squares:
\[
x^4+x^2+1=(x^4+2x^2+1)-x^2=(x^2+1)^2-x^2=\boxed{(x^2+x+1)(x^2-x+1)}.
\]
Each quadratic factor has discriminant $1-4=-3<0$, so neither factors further over the integers.

\textbf{(b)} The same trick with $x^4$ in place of $x^2$:
\[
x^8+x^4+1=(x^4+1)^2-x^4=(x^4+x^2+1)(x^4-x^2+1)=\boxed{(x^2+x+1)(x^2-x+1)(x^4-x^2+1)}.
\]
The factor $x^4-x^2+1$ is irreducible over the integers. It has no rational root ($\pm1$ give $1$). If it were a product of two monic integer quadratics $(x^2+px+q)(x^2-px+r)$ (the $x^3$ coefficients cancel), then $qr=1$, so $q=r=\pm1$, and the $x^2$ coefficient $q+r-p^2=-1$ gives $p^2=3$ when $q=r=1$ and $p^2=-1$ when $q=r=-1$, both impossible.
