A multiple of $1000$ needs at least three factors of both $2$ and $5$. The factorial valuation formula
\[
v_5\binom nk=\sum_{j\ge1}\left(\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{5^j}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\dfrac{k}{5^j}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\dfrac{n-k}{5^j}\right\rfloor\right)
\]
has summands $0$ or $1$. Hence $n<125$ cannot work. If $n+k<132$, it remains to check $125\le n\le130$ and $1\le k\le131-n$. Among these pairs, those with $v_5\binom nk\ge3$ are exactly
\[
\begin{array}{c|l|l}
n&k&v_2\binom nk\text{ in the same order}\\\hline
125&1,2,3,4,6&0,1,1,0,2\\
126&2,3,4&0,2,0\\
127&3,4&0,0
\end{array}
\]
None has three factors of $2$. Finally $\binom{128}{4}=10668000$ is divisible by $1000$, so the smallest sum is $\boxed{132}$.
