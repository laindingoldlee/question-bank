Let the numbers be $a$ and $b$, with $m=\lceil a\rceil$ and $n=\lceil b\rceil$, so $mn=100$. For any real $t$, $\lfloor t\rfloor=\lceil t\rceil$ if $t$ is an integer and $\lfloor t\rfloor=\lceil t\rceil-1$ otherwise.

\emph{$m,n>0$.} Then $0\le\lfloor a\rfloor\le m$ and $0\le\lfloor b\rfloor\le n$, so $X\le mn=100$.

\emph{$m,n<0$.} Then $\lfloor a\rfloor$ and $\lfloor b\rfloor$ are negative with $\left|\lfloor a\rfloor\right|\le|m|+1$ and $\left|\lfloor b\rfloor\right|\le|n|+1$, so
\[X\le(|m|+1)(|n|+1)=|m||n|+|m|+|n|+1=101+|m|+|n| .\]
Since $|m|$ and $|n|$ are positive integers with product $100$, $|m|+|n|\le1+100$, so $X\le202$.

Equality holds for $a=-1.5$ and $b=-100.5$: $\lceil a\rceil\lceil b\rceil=(-1)(-100)=100$ and $\lfloor a\rfloor\lfloor b\rfloor=(-2)(-101)=202$. The greatest possible value is $\boxed{202}$.

(If the numbers had to be positive, the answer would be just $100$, from two integers.)
