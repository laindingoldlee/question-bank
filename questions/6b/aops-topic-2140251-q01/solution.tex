Use the identity
\[a^3+b^3+c^3-3abc=(a+b+c)\left(a^2+b^2+c^2-ab-bc-ca\right),\]
which can be checked by expanding the right side. Also
\[a^2+b^2+c^2-ab-bc-ca=\dfrac{1}{2}\left[(a-b)^2+(b-c)^2+(c-a)^2\right]\ge0 .\]
Since $a+b+c>0$, it follows that $a^3+b^3+c^3-3abc\ge0$. Equality holds exactly when $(a-b)^2+(b-c)^2+(c-a)^2=0$, that is, when $a=b=c$. $\blacksquare$

\emph{On the QM--AM--GM--HM chain.} The inequality is exactly the AM--GM inequality for the three numbers $a^3$, $b^3$, $c^3$: $\dfrac{a^3+b^3+c^3}{3}\ge\sqrt[3]{a^3b^3c^3}=abc$. So quoting AM--GM for three numbers proves it at once, and the identity above is a self-contained proof of that case of AM--GM.
