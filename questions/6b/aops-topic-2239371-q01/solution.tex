\textbf{(a)} Let the roots be $r$ and $s$, both negative. Then
\[ax^2+bx+c=a(x-r)(x-s)=ax^2-a(r+s)x+ars ,\]
so $b=a\cdot(-(r+s))$ and $c=a\cdot rs$, where $-(r+s)>0$ and $rs>0$. Hence $b$ and $c$ have the same sign as $a$: all three coefficients have the same sign.

They need not be positive: $-x^2-3x-2=-(x+1)(x+2)$ has the negative roots $-1$ and $-2$, and all its coefficients are negative. The coefficients are positive exactly when $a>0$ (and one can always make $a>0$ by multiplying by $-1$).

\textbf{(b)} No: $x^2+x+1$ has positive coefficients but no real roots (its discriminant is $1-4=-3<0$). What is true is this: if $a,b,c>0$ and the quadratic has real roots, they are negative, because for $x\ge0$ we have $ax^2+bx+c\ge c>0$.
