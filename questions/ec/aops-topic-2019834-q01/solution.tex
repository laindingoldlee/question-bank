By symmetry suppose $a\le b$ and let $d=b-a\ge0$. Since $5$ does not divide $2^a$ or $3^a$,
\[
5\mid 2^a(1+2^d)\iff 2^d\equiv-1\pmod5,\qquad 5\mid 3^a(1+3^d)\iff 3^d\equiv-1\pmod5 .
\]
The powers of $2$ modulo $5$ are $1,2,4,3,1,\ldots$ and those of $3$ are $1,3,4,2,1,\ldots$ (period $4$); each equals $4\equiv-1$ exactly when $d\equiv2\pmod4$. So both conditions hold exactly when $b-a\equiv2\pmod4$ (this is symmetric in $a$ and $b$).

Among $1,\ldots,1000$ each residue class mod $4$ contains exactly $250$ numbers. For any $a$, exactly $250$ values of $b$ satisfy $b\equiv a+2\pmod4$. The probability is
\[
\dfrac{1000\cdot250}{1000^2}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{4}}.
\]
