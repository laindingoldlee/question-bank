For a function $f$, the graph of $y=f\left(\dfrac {x}{2}\right)$ is the graph of $y=f(x)$ stretched horizontally by a factor of $2$, and the graph of $y=\dfrac{1}{2}f(x)$ is the graph of $y=f(x)$ shrunk vertically by a factor of $2$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Show that for $f(x)=mx$ (any constant $m$) the two transformations give the same graph.
\item[(b)] Give a function for which they give different graphs, so that ``a horizontal stretch is the same as a vertical shrink'' is false in general.
\item[(c)] Prove that if $f$ is a polynomial with $f\left(\dfrac {x}{2}\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}f(x)$ for all real $x$, then $f(x)=mx$ for some constant $m$.
\end{enumerate}
