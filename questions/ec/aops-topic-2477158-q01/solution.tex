\textbf{Claim 1.} If $2^k$ people ($k\ge0$) sit in a circle and the first person to act holds the beam, then that person survives.

\emph{Proof by induction on $k$.} For $k=0$ there is nothing to do. For $k\ge1$, label the people $1,2,\dots,2^k$ clockwise, starting with the holder. In the first round the odd-numbered people eliminate $2,4,\dots,2^k$ in turn, and person $2^k-1$, after eliminating $2^k$, passes the beam to person $1$. Now $2^{k-1}$ people remain, and person $1$ holds the beam and acts first. By the induction hypothesis, person $1$ survives. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{The general case.} Write $2014=2^{10}+990$ with $990<2^{10}=1024$. After the first $990$ eliminations, persons $2,4,\dots,1980$ have gone, and the beam is with person $1981$ (note $1981\le2014$). At that moment exactly $2014-990=1024=2^{10}$ people remain, and person $1981$ holds the beam and acts next. By Claim 1, person $1981$ survives.

The last survivor is person $\boxed{1981}$.
