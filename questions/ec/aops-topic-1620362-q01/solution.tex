Let $S(m)$ denote the digit sum of $m$. When we double $n$ digit by digit, a digit $d$ becomes $2d$; if $d\ge5$, then $2d\ge10$ and there is a carry, which replaces $10$ in one place by $1$ in the next and lowers the digit sum by $9$ (doubled digits are at most $18$ and a carry adds at most $1$, so no new carries arise). Hence
\[
S(2n)=2S(n)-9c,
\]
where $c$ is the number of digits of $n$ that are at least $5$. So $S(2n)=S(n)$ exactly when $S(n)=9c$.

Write $n=100h+10t+u$ with $h\in\{0,1\}$. The hundreds digit $h\le1$ never carries, so $c$ counts the digits among $t,u$ that are at least $5$, and $c\le2$, $S(n)\le19$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $c=0$: $S(n)=0$ is impossible for $n\ge1$.
\item $c=1$, $S(n)=9$. If $h=0$: $t+u=9$ gives $10$ numbers $9,18,\dots,90$, and in each exactly one of $t,u$ is at least $5$. If $h=1$: $t+u=8$ gives $9$ pairs, of which $(4,4)$ has no digit $\ge5$ and the other $8$ have exactly one. That is $10+8=18$ numbers.
\item $c=2$, $S(n)=18$ with $t,u\ge5$. If $h=0$: $t+u=18$ gives $99$. If $h=1$: $t+u=17$ gives $189$ and $198$. That is $3$ numbers.
\end{itemize}
In total there are $18+3=\boxed{21}$ such $n$. They are the multiples of $9$ below $200$ except $144$: since $S(n)\equiv n\pmod9$ is necessary, the multiples of $9$ are the only candidates, and $144$ fails because none of its digits is at least $5$, so $S(288)=18\ne9$.
