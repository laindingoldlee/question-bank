Every year from $2000$ to $2100$ contains the digit $0$. So we only need the years $19ab$ with digits $a,b\in\{1,2,\dots,9\}$, whose digit product is $9ab$.

\textbf{Divisibility by $9$.} The year must be divisible by $9$, so its digit sum $1+9+a+b=10+a+b$ is a multiple of $9$, i.e.\ $a+b=8$ or $a+b=17$. The candidates are
\[
1917,\ 1926,\ 1935,\ 1944,\ 1953,\ 1962,\ 1971,\ 1989,\ 1998 .
\]

\textbf{Checking the rest of the product.}
\begin{itemize}
\item $1917$ and $1971$: the product is $63$, so $7$ must divide the year; but $1917=7\cdot273+6$ and $1971=7\cdot281+4$.
\item $1926$ and $1962$: the product is $108=4\cdot27$, so $4$ must divide the year; but $26$ and $62$ are not multiples of $4$.
\item $1935$ and $1953$: the product is $135=27\cdot5$. The year $1953$ is not a multiple of $5$, and $1935=9\cdot215$ with $215$ not a multiple of $3$, so $27\nmid1935$.
\item $1944$: the product is $144=16\cdot9$, but $1944=8\cdot243$ with $243$ odd, so $16\nmid1944$.
\item $1989$ and $1998$: the product is $648=8\cdot81$, but $1989$ is odd and $1998=2\cdot999$ is not a multiple of $8$.
\end{itemize}
No candidate works, so the answer is $\boxed{0}$: no year from $1901$ to $2100$ is divisible by the product of its digits. (The same holds for the years $1900$ to $2099$.)
