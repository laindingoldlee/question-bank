(a) By the binomial theorem the term with $x^3y^3$ is $\dbinom63(2x)^3(3y)^3=20\cdot8\cdot27\,x^3y^3$, so the coefficient is $\boxed{4320}$.

(b) Expanding $(x+y+z)^4$ means choosing one of $x,y,z$ from each of the $4$ factors. The term $x^2yz$ arises from the choices in which $x$ is picked twice, $y$ once and $z$ once: the number of such choices is the number of arrangements of $x,x,y,z$, namely $\dfrac{4!}{2!\,1!\,1!}=\boxed{12}$.

The distinct terms are the monomials $x^iy^jz^k$ with $i+j+k=4$; by stars and bars there are $\dbinom{6}{2}=\boxed{15}$ of them.
