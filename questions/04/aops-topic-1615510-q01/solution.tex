For $ax^2+bx+c=0$ the roots have sum $-\dfrac {b}{a}$ and product $\dfrac {c}{a}$ (Vieta's formulas). Here the sum is $\dfrac {m}{2}=6$ and the product is $\dfrac {n}{2}=10$, so $m=12$, $n=20$ and $m+n=\boxed{32}$.

(The roots are not real: $2x^2-12x+20=0$ has discriminant $144-160<0$, and the roots are $3\pm i$. That is why the argument ``the roots cannot be negative'' does not settle anything; Vieta's formulas hold for complex roots too.)
