Since $0^{12}=0\not\equiv16$, we have $x\not\equiv0$. By Fermat's little theorem $x^{16}\equiv1\pmod{17}$, so $x^{12}\cdot x^4\equiv1$, i.e. $x^{12}$ is the inverse of $x^4$. Since $16\equiv-1$ is its own inverse,
\[x^{12}\equiv-1\iff x^4\equiv-1\pmod{17}.\]
A polynomial congruence of degree $4$ modulo a prime has at most $4$ solutions. And there are $4$:
\[2^4=16,\qquad 15^4=(-2)^4=16,\qquad 8^4=4096=17\cdot240+16,\qquad 9^4=(-8)^4\equiv16.\]
So there are exactly $\boxed{4}$ solutions, $x\equiv2,8,9,15\pmod{17}$.
