We have $M=(3,0),N=(0,3)$, $BC=10$, and $BM=3$, so
\[
K=B+\dfrac{3}{10}(C-B)=\left(\dfrac{21}{5},\dfrac{12}{5}\right).
\]
Circle $MNK$ is $x^2+y^2-4x-4y+3=0$. At $y=0$ its roots are $x=3,1$, so $P=(1,0)$. Substituting line $BC$, $4x+3y=24$, gives the second point $Q=(3,4)$.

Now $MN$ is $x+y=3$ and $PQ$ is $y=2(x-1)$, so $T=\left(\dfrac{5}{3},\dfrac{4}{3}\right)$. Line $CT$ has equation $y=8-4x$ and meets $AB$ at $(2,0)$. This is the midpoint of $M=(3,0)$ and $P=(1,0)$, proving the claim.
