The square has the same area as the rectangle, $9\cdot16=144$, so its side is $12$. One piece is not enough, because the rectangle itself is not a square. So at least $2$ pieces are needed.

\textbf{Two pieces suffice: a staircase cut.} Cut the rectangle (with corners $(0,0)$ and $(16,9)$) along the staircase
\[
(4,9)\to(4,6)\to(8,6)\to(8,3)\to(12,3)\to(12,0),
\]
whose steps are $4$ across and $3$ down. Keep piece $1$ (the left part) fixed and slide piece $2$ (the right part) $4$ units left and $3$ units up:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(11cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
path L=(0,0)--(12,0)--(12,3)--(8,3)--(8,6)--(4,6)--(4,9)--(0,9)--cycle;
path R=(4,9)--(16,9)--(16,0)--(12,0)--(12,3)--(8,3)--(8,6)--(4,6)--cycle;
pair sh=(21,0);
filldraw(L,paleblue,linewidth(0.9));filldraw(R,palered,linewidth(0.9));
filldraw(shift(sh)*L,paleblue,linewidth(0.9));filldraw(shift(sh+(-4,3))*R,palered,linewidth(0.9));
label("$1$",(3,3));label("$2$",(13,6));
label("$1$",sh+(3,3));label("$2$",sh+(9,9));
label("$16$",(8,0),S);label("$9$",(0,4.5),W);
label("$12$",sh+(6,0),S);label("$12$",sh+(0,6),W);
draw((4,6.7)--(8,6.7),linewidth(0.5),Arrows(TeXHead,size=2));label("$4$",(6,6.7),N);draw((8.7,3)--(8.7,6),linewidth(0.5),Arrows(TeXHead,size=2));label("$3$",(8.7,4.5),E);
label("$\Longrightarrow$",(18.5,4.5));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\textbf{Why the pieces fit.} The translation by $(-4,3)$ moves each step of the staircase onto the step above and to the left of it, so the moved staircase edge of piece $2$ runs along the staircase edge of piece $1$ from $(4,9)$ down to $(8,3)$, and the bottom edge of piece 2 now lies along the top of the last step of piece 1, at height $3$ from $x=8$ to $x=12$. The moved piece $2$ fills the top-left gap $0\le x\le4$, $9\le y\le12$ (its old top-left step) and the strip above piece $1$. Its right edge moves from $x=16$ to $x=12$ and its top edge from $y=9$ to $y=12$. Piece 1 occupies $0\le x\le12$ up to the staircase. Together they fill exactly the square $0\le x\le12$, $0\le y\le12$, with no overlap (the total area is still $144$).

So the least number of pieces is $\boxed{2}$.

(If the pieces were required to be rectangles, as the post's description suggests, a simple solution uses $4$ pieces: cut off a $9\times4$ strip, cut it into three $3\times4$ rectangles, and lay them end to end, as a $3\times12$ strip, on the remaining $9\times12$ rectangle.)
