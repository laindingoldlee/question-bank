There are $\dbinom{16}{6}=8008$ equally likely choices. A choice containing both impostors consists of the $2$ impostors and $4$ of the other $14$ people, so there are $\dbinom{14}{4}=1001$ of them. The probability is
\[\dfrac{\dbinom{14}{4}}{\dbinom{16}{6}}=\dfrac{1001}{8008}=\boxed{\dfrac18}.\]
(Check: the probability that a given impostor is chosen is $\dfrac{6}{16}$, and then that the other one is chosen too is $\dfrac{5}{15}$; $\dfrac{6}{16}\cdot\dfrac{5}{15}=\dfrac18$.)

The student's numerator $\dbinom{16}{4}$ chooses the $4$ other people from all $16$ people, including the impostors themselves; they must be chosen from the $14$ non-impostors. The student's value $\dfrac{1820}{8008}=\dfrac{5}{22}$ is too large.
