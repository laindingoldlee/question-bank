Let $BC=a$, $AC=2a$ and $CD=d$. The bisector splits the $120^\circ$ angle into two $60^\circ$ angles, and $[ABC]=[ACD]+[BCD]$:
\[\dfrac12\cdot a\cdot2a\sin120^\circ=\dfrac12\cdot2a\cdot d\sin60^\circ+\dfrac12\cdot a\cdot d\sin60^\circ.\]
Since $\sin120^\circ=\sin60^\circ$, this gives $2a^2=3ad$, so $d=\dfrac23a$. $\blacksquare$

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real a=1;pair C=(0,0),B=a*dir(0),A=2a*dir(120);
pair D=extension(C,C+dir(60),A,B);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.8));draw(C--D,dashed);
label("$A$",A,NW);label("$B$",B,E);label("$C$",C,S);label("$D$",D,NE);
label("$60^\circ$",C+0.25*dir(30),fontsize(7pt));label("$60^\circ$",C+0.25*dir(90),fontsize(7pt));
\end{asy}

(With the post's hypothesis $AB=2\cdot BC$ instead, $AC=\dfrac{\sqrt{13}-1}{2}\,BC$ and $CD=\dfrac{7-\sqrt{13}}{6}\,BC\ne\dfrac23BC$, so that version is false.)
