For an object $t$, let $a(t)$ be the number of objects in its old box and $c(t)$ the number in its new box. Each nonempty old box with $a$ objects contributes $a\cdot\dfrac1a=1$ to $\sum_t\dfrac1{a(t)}$, and similarly for the new boxes. So
\[\sum_t\dfrac{1}{a(t)}=\#\{\text{nonempty old boxes}\}\le n,\qquad\sum_t\dfrac{1}{c(t)}=n+1,\]
and therefore
\[\sum_t\left(\dfrac1{c(t)}-\dfrac1{a(t)}\right)\ge1.\]
Every term is less than $1$, since $\dfrac1{c(t)}\le1$ and $\dfrac1{a(t)}>0$. So at least two terms are positive, and a positive term means $c(t)<a(t)$: the object's new box contains fewer objects than its old box. $\blacksquare$
