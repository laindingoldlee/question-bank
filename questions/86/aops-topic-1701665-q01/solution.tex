Regard the equation as a quadratic in $x$: $x^2-3y\,x+y^2=0$. Its solutions are
\[
x=\dfrac{3y\pm\sqrt{9y^2-4y^2}}{2}=\dfrac{3\pm\sqrt5}{2}\,y .
\]
For a positive integer $y$, $\dfrac{3\pm\sqrt5}{2}\,y$ is irrational (otherwise $\sqrt5=\dfrac{2x}{y}-3$ would be rational). So $x$ cannot be an integer, and there are $\boxed{\text{no solutions}}$ in positive integers. (If $0$ is allowed, the only solution in nonnegative integers is $x=y=0$.)

Equivalently, $\left(\dfrac {x}{y}\right)^2-3\cdot\dfrac {x}{y}+1=0$ would make $\dfrac {x}{y}$ a rational root of $t^2-3t+1$, whose discriminant $5$ is not a perfect square.
