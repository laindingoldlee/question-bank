Ignore cell boundaries and tangencies, which change only a set of area zero. If $P$ lies in either middle-column cell, the disk intersects that cell, its two horizontal neighbours, and its vertical neighbour, so at least four cells. In a corner cell, say $0<x,y<1$, it always intersects that cell and its horizontal and vertical neighbours. It cannot reach the far column, and it reaches the diagonal cell exactly when $(1-x)^2+(1-y)^2<1$. Thus admissible centres in this corner cell have area $1-\dfrac{\pi}{4}$. Four congruent corners yield $\boxed{4-\pi}$.
