Let $k$ be a positive integer. A calculator states that
\[
x^{8k}-16=\left(x^{2k}-2\right)\left(x^{2k}+2\right)\left(x^{4k}+4\right).
\]
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Prove that this factorisation is correct.
\item[(b)] Factor $x^{4k}+4$ as a product of two polynomials with integer coefficients, each of degree $2k$, and hence write $x^{8k}-16$ as a product of four such polynomials.
\end{enumerate}
