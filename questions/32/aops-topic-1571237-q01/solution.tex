Write $H_m=1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{m}$, so $A_n=\dfrac{1}{2}H_n$.

\textbf{(a)} The harmonic series diverges: grouping $1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\left(\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{4}\right)+\left(\dfrac{1}{5}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{8}\right)+\cdots$, each group is at least $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so $H_{2^k}\ge1+\dfrac {k}{2}\to\infty$. Hence $A_n=\dfrac{1}{2}H_n\to\infty$. Also $\dfrac{1}{2k+1}>\dfrac{1}{2k+2}$, so $B_n>\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{2}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{n+1}\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(H_{n+1}-1\right)\to\infty$.

\textbf{(b)} Pair the terms with the same $k$:
\[
A_n-B_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\dfrac{1}{2k}-\dfrac{1}{2k+1}\right).
\]
On the other hand the alternating series, stopped after the term $\dfrac{1}{2n+1}$, is
\[
1-\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}-\cdots-\dfrac{1}{2n}+\dfrac{1}{2n+1}=1-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\dfrac{1}{2k}-\dfrac{1}{2k+1}\right)=1-(A_n-B_n),
\]
and it tends to $\ln2$. Therefore
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}(A_n-B_n)=\boxed{1-\ln2}\approx0.307 .
\]

\textbf{(c)} $A_{2n}-B_n=(A_n-B_n)+(A_{2n}-A_n)$, and
\[
A_{2n}-A_n=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{n+1}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{2n}\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(H_{2n}-H_n\right).
\]
The identity $H_{2n}-H_n=1-\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}-\cdots-\dfrac{1}{2n}$ (subtract $2\left(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{2n}\right)=H_n$ from $H_{2n}$) shows that $H_{2n}-H_n\to\ln2$. Hence
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}(A_{2n}-B_n)=1-\ln2+\dfrac{1}{2}\ln2=1-\dfrac{1}{2}\ln2\approx0.653 .
\]
So matching the terms of the two divergent series in two different ways gives two different limits (and other matchings, such as $A_{n^2}-B_n$, even tend to $\infty$). Subtracting divergent series is not defined: the ``difference'' depends on how the partial sums are paired, so the question as posed has no single answer. Only after a rule is fixed, such as pairing term $k$ with term $k$ as in (b), does it acquire a value, $1-\ln2$.
