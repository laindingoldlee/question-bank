Let
\[
A_n=\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{2n},\qquad B_n=\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{5}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{2n+1}.
\]
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Show that $A_n\to\infty$ and $B_n\to\infty$, so both series $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}+\cdots$ and $\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{5}+\cdots$ diverge.
\item[(b)] Using $1-\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}-\dfrac{1}{4}+\cdots=\ln2$, find $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}(A_n-B_n)$.
\item[(c)] Find $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}(A_{2n}-B_n)$, and explain why ``the difference between the two divergent series'' has no well-defined value.
\end{enumerate}
