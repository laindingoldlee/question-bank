The number of ways to get sum $s$ with $k$ dice is the coefficient of $x^s$ in $\left(x+x^2+\cdots+x^6\right)^k$; it can be computed with stars and bars and inclusion--exclusion.

For three dice and $s=10$, write the rolls as $1+u_i$ with $0\le u_i\le5$ and $u_1+u_2+u_3=7$. Without the upper bound there are $\dbinom92=36$ solutions. If $u_1\ge6$, then $(u_1-6)+u_2+u_3=1$ has $\dbinom32=3$ solutions, and likewise for $u_2$ and $u_3$; two variables cannot both be $\ge6$. So there are $36-9=27$ outcomes, and the probability is $\dfrac{27}{216}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{8}}$.

For $k$ dice the same inclusion--exclusion gives the number of ways to roll sum $s$:
\[\sum_{j\ge0}(-1)^j\dbinom kj\dbinom{s-6j-1}{k-1},\]
where terms with $s-6j-1<k-1$ are $0$.
