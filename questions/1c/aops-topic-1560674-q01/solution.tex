The squares eaten have sides $15,15,9,6,3,3$. The largest square that can be cut from an $x\times y$ rectangle with $x\le y$ is $x\times x$, and it leaves an $x\times(y-x)$ rectangle. Work backwards from the end.
\begin{itemize}
\item Day $6$: the bar is a $3\times3$ square.
\item Day $5$: a $3\times3$ square is cut and a $3\times3$ square is left, so the bar was $3\times6$.
\item Day $4$: a $6\times6$ square is cut, leaving $3\times6$, so the bar was $6\times9$.
\item Day $3$: a $9\times9$ square is cut, leaving $6\times9$, so the bar was $9\times15$.
\item Day $2$: a $15\times15$ square is cut, leaving $9\times15$, so the bar was $15\times24$.
\item Day $1$: a $15\times15$ square is cut, leaving $15\times24$, so the bar was $15\times39$.
\end{itemize}
(At each step the square really is the largest possible, since its side is the shorter side of the bar.)

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(9cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
void sq(real x,real y,real s,string t){
  draw((x,y)--(x+s,y)--(x+s,y+s)--(x,y+s)--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
  label(t,(x+s/2,y+s/2));
}
sq(0,0,15,"$D_1$");sq(15,0,15,"$D_2$");sq(30,0,9,"$D_3$");sq(30,9,6,"$D_4$");sq(36,9,3,"$D_5$");sq(36,12,3,"$D_6$");
draw((0,0)--(39,0)--(39,15)--(0,15)--cycle,linewidth(1.3));
label("$39$",(19.5,0),S);label("$15$",(0,7.5),W);
label("$D_k$: the square eaten on day $k$",(19.5,-4.5));
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The longer side was $\boxed{39\text{ cm}}$.
