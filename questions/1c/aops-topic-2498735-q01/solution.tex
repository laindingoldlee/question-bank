Take the lines as the coordinate axes and both semicircles in the first quadrant. A semicircle with diameter on the $x$-axis tangent to the $y$-axis touches it at an end of its diameter, so the large one is the upper half of $(x-1)^2+y^2=1$, ending at $O$ and at $(2,0)$. Likewise the small one, of radius $r$, is the right half of $x^2+(y-r)^2=r^2$, which also passes through $O$.

Expanding, the circles are $x^2+y^2=2x$ and $x^2+y^2=2ry$, so their second common point $P$ satisfies $x=ry$, i.e. $r=\dfrac xy$.

The large arc measures $180^\circ$, so its two pieces measure $30^\circ$ and $150^\circ$. Write $P=(1+\cos\varphi,\sin\varphi)$, where $\varphi$ is the angle at the centre $(1,0)$ from the direction of $(2,0)$.
\begin{itemize}
\item If $\varphi=30^\circ$, then $r=\dfrac{1+\cos30^\circ}{\sin30^\circ}=2+\sqrt3>1$, not the smaller semicircle.
\item If $\varphi=150^\circ$, then $P=\left(1-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2},\dfrac12\right)$ and $r=\dfrac{1-\dfrac{\sqrt3}2}{\dfrac12}=2-\sqrt3$.
\end{itemize}
In the second case $P$ has $x>0$ and $y>0$, so it lies on both semicircles. The radius is $\boxed{2-\sqrt3}$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real r=2-sqrt(3);
draw((-0.3,0)--(2.3,0)); draw((0,-0.3)--(0,1.3));
draw(arc((1,0),1,0,180),linewidth(0.9));
draw(arc((0,r),r,-90,90),blue+linewidth(0.9));
pair P=(1-sqrt(3)/2,0.5);
draw((1,0)--P,dashed); dot(P); dot((1,0)); dot((0,0));
draw(arc((1,0),0.18,0,150),gray);
label("$150^\circ$",(1,0)+0.3*dir(60),fontsize(7pt));
label("$O$",(0,0),SW); label("$P$",P,N); label("$1$",(1,0)+0.5*dir(150),SW,fontsize(8pt));
label("$r$",(0,r/2),W,blue+fontsize(8pt));
\end{asy}
