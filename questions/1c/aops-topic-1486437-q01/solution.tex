It suffices to show $6<3^{\sqrt3}<7$: then $3^{\sqrt3}$ is not an integer, its floor is $6$, its ceiling is $7$, and $6\cdot7=42$.

\emph{Bounds for $\sqrt3$.} Since $1.7^2=2.89<3<3.0625=1.75^2$, we have $\dfrac{17}{10}<\sqrt3<\dfrac{7}{4}$. As $3^u$ is increasing in $u$,
\[
3^{\dfrac{17}{10}}<3^{\sqrt3}<3^{\dfrac{7}{4}} .
\]

\emph{Lower bound.} $3^{\dfrac{17}{10}}>6$ is equivalent (raising to the $10$th power) to $3^{17}>6^{10}$, that is, $3^{17}>2^{10}3^{10}$, that is, $3^7>2^{10}$: $2187>1024$. True.

\emph{Upper bound.} $3^{\dfrac{7}{4}}<7$ is equivalent (raising to the $4$th power) to $3^7<7^4$: $2187<2401$. True.

Hence $6<3^{\sqrt3}<7$, and the product is $6\cdot7=42$. $\blacksquare$ (Numerically $3^{\sqrt3}\approx6.705$.)
