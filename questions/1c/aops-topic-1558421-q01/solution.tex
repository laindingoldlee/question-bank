\textbf{First congruence.} $5\equiv2\pmod3$, so $2x\equiv1\pmod3$. Multiplying by $2$ (the inverse of $2$ modulo $3$) gives $x\equiv2\pmod3$.

\textbf{Second congruence.} $12\equiv5\pmod7$, so $5x\equiv4\pmod7$. The inverse of $5$ modulo $7$ is $3$ (since $15\equiv1$), so $x\equiv12\equiv5\pmod7$.

\textbf{Combine.} The numbers $\equiv5\pmod7$ are $5,12,19,\dots$; the first one $\equiv2\pmod3$ is $5$. By the Chinese remainder theorem the solution is
\[
\boxed{x\equiv5\pmod{21}}.
\]
Check: $5\cdot5=25\equiv1\pmod3$ and $12\cdot5=60\equiv4\pmod7$.
