\textbf{Exponents.} The exponents of $a$ and $b$ are at most $2$ (from $\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)=4x^2$). Since $\operatorname{lcm}(b,c)$ has exponent $3$, the exponent of $c$ is $3$.

\textbf{Coefficients.} Write the coefficients as $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$, with $\operatorname{lcm}(\alpha,\beta)=4$, $\operatorname{lcm}(\beta,\gamma)=12$, $\operatorname{lcm}(\alpha,\gamma)=6$.
\begin{itemize}
\item Powers of $3$: $\alpha,\beta$ are not divisible by $3$ (their lcm is $4$), so $3\mid\gamma$, and $9\nmid\gamma$.
\item Powers of $2$: $4\nmid\alpha$ and $4\nmid\gamma$ (as they divide $6$), so $4\mid\beta$; then $\operatorname{lcm}(\beta,\gamma)$ has $2$-part $4$ whatever $\gamma$'s $2$-part is, so $\gamma$ may be odd or have $2$-part $2$.
\end{itemize}
Hence $\gamma\in\{3,6\}$, and both occur: $a=2x^2$, $b=4x^2$, $c=3x^3$ and $a=x^2$, $b=4x^2$, $c=6x^3$ satisfy all three conditions. So $c=\boxed{3x^3\text{ or }6x^3}$.
