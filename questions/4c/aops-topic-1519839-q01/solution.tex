Put $x=2$ and $x=\dfrac{1}{2}$:
\[
2f(2)-f\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)=7,\qquad 2f\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)-f(2)=\dfrac{5}{2} .
\]
Doubling the first equation and adding the second eliminates $f\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$: $3f(2)=14+\dfrac{5}{2}=\dfrac{33}{2}$, so
\[
f(2)=\boxed{\dfrac{11}{2}}.
\]
(Then $f\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)=2\cdot\dfrac{11}{2}-7=4$, and $2\cdot4-\dfrac{11}{2}=\dfrac{5}{2}$ checks.) In general, solving the same pair of equations for $x$ and $\dfrac{1}{x}$ gives $f(x)=2x+\dfrac{1}{x}+1$, which indeed satisfies the equation.
