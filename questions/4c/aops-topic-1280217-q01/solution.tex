It suffices to find three of the points $X,Y,Z$ with $\angle XYZ\ge90^\circ$: then by the law of cosines
\[
XZ^2=XY^2+YZ^2-2\,XY\cdot YZ\cos\angle XYZ\ge XY^2+YZ^2\ge2+2=4,
\]
so $XZ\ge2$.
\begin{itemize}
\item If three of the points are collinear, the middle one sees the other two at an angle of $180^\circ$.
\item If the four points are the vertices of a convex quadrilateral, its angles sum to $360^\circ$, so one angle is at least $90^\circ$.
\item Otherwise one point $Y$ lies inside the triangle formed by the other three. The three angles at $Y$ subtended by the sides sum to $360^\circ$, so one is at least $120^\circ$.
\end{itemize}
In every case the required angle exists. $\blacksquare$ The bound is sharp: a square of side $\sqrt2$ has diagonals exactly $2$.
