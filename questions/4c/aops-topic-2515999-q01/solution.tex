\textbf{$B$ is forced.} $B\subseteq\{1,\dots,6\}$ and $6\in B$. Apply (iv) to $12\in A$: $12+b$ with $1\le b\le6$ is a square only for $b=4$, so $4\in B$. Apply (v) to $12$: $12y=4\cdot3y$ is a square exactly when $3y$ is, and for $1\le y\le6$ that means $y=3$. So $3\in B$ and $B=\{3,4,6\}$.

\textbf{The other elements of $A$.} Let $a\in A$, $a\le11$.
\begin{itemize}
\item By (v), one of $3a$, $4a$, $6a$ is a square: $a=3$ ($3a=9$), or $a$ a square ($a=1,4,9$), or $a=6$ ($6a=36$). So $a\in\{1,3,4,6,9\}$.
\item By (iv), one of $a+3$, $a+4$, $a+6$ is a square. This holds for $1$ ($1+3$), $3$ ($3+6$) and $6$ ($6+3$), but fails for $4$ ($7,8,10$) and $9$ ($12,13,15$).
\end{itemize}
So $A=\{12\}\cup$ (two of $1,3,6$), and (iii) holds in each case: $\{1,3,12\}\cap B=\{3\}$, $\{1,6,12\}\cap B=\{6\}$, $\{3,6,12\}\cap B=\{3,6\}$. The solutions are
\[\boxed{B=\{3,4,6\}\ \text{and}\ A\in\bigl\{\{1,3,12\},\ \{1,6,12\},\ \{3,6,12\}\bigr\}}.\]
