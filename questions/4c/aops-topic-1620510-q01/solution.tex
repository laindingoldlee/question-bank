\textbf{(a)} Suppose $f$ satisfies the condition. Replacing $x$ by $\dfrac{1}{x}$ gives
\[
f\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)+\dfrac{1}{x^2}f(x)=\dfrac{1}{x^2}-1 ,
\]
and multiplying by $x^2$,
\[
x^2f\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)+f(x)=1-x^2 .
\]
The left side is the same as in the original condition, so $x^2-1=1-x^2$, that is $x^2=1$, for every $x\ne0$. This is false for $x=2$. Concretely, $x=2$ and $x=\dfrac{1}{2}$ give
\[
f(2)+4f\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)=3\qquad\text{and}\qquad 4f\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)+f(2)=-3
\]
(the second after multiplying by $4$), which contradict each other. Hence no such function exists, and $f(1)+\cdots+f(10)$ is not defined.

\textbf{(b)} With $f(x)=\dfrac{2x^2-1}{1-x^4}$ we get $f(2)=\dfrac{7}{-15}=-\dfrac{7}{15}$ and $f\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)=\dfrac{-\dfrac{1}{2}}{\dfrac{15}{16}}=-\dfrac{8}{15}$, so
\[
f(2)+4f\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)=-\dfrac{7}{15}-\dfrac{32}{15}=-\dfrac{13}{5}\ne3 .
\]
The two equations obtained by the substitution have the same left side and different right sides, so they cannot be solved for $f(x)$ at all; any elimination that seems to produce a formula must contain an algebra slip. (The formula is also undefined at $x=1$, while a solution would need $f(1)+f(1)=0$.)
