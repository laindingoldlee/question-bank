If $a=0$, the second equation gives $0=\dfrac{4}{b^2}$, impossible. Thus $a+b=3$. Multiplying the second equation by $2b^2$ gives $ab^2+a^2b=8$, so $ab=\dfrac{8}{3}$. Hence $a^3+b^3=(a+b)^3-3ab(a+b)=27-24=\boxed3$. The numbers $a,b$ are roots of $t^2-3t+\dfrac{8}{3}$, whose discriminant is $9-\dfrac{32}{3}=-\dfrac{5}{3}<0$. Thus there is no real solution, but the conjugate complex roots do satisfy both original equations.
