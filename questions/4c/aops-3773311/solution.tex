Let $C(j)$ count people of age at most $j$, including $C(0)=0$. Adding report entries reveals $C(j)$ whenever $j$ is a multiple of 3, 4, or 5. Thus age $a$ is detectable when both $a-1$ and $a$ are such boundary indices. If either adjacent boundary is unavailable, $a$ lies in an interval between consecutive available boundaries with at least two ages. Moving one person between age $a$ and another age in that interval leaves all reports unchanged, showing the count is not universally determined. The detectable ages are therefore $\boxed{4,5,6,9,10,16,21,25,28,33,36,40,45,51,52,55,56,57}$. This characterises determination for every possible population; a particular report can reveal extra zero counts.
