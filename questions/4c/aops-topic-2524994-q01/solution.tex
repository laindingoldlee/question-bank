\textbf{(a)} $2\cdot4=8\equiv1\pmod7$, so $2^{-1}\equiv4$. Then $5\cdot2^{-1}\equiv5\cdot4=20\equiv\boxed{6}\pmod7$.

\textbf{(b)} $2\cdot6=12\equiv5\pmod7$. If also $2r'\equiv5$, then $2(r'-6)\equiv0$, and multiplying by $4$ gives $r'\equiv6\pmod7$. So $6$ is the unique residue, which is why it is reasonable to write $\dfrac52\equiv6\pmod7$: it is the residue that behaves like ``$5$ divided by $2$''.
