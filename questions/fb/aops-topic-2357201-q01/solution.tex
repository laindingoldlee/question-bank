The spacing condition puts no lower bound on the area, because the points may lie on a line. Put the students at the points $P_k=(6(k-1),0)$ for $k=1,2,\dots,100$; any two are at least $6$ feet apart, and they fill a segment of length $6\cdot99=594$ feet. The thin rectangle
\[0\le x\le594,\qquad -\dfrac w2\le y\le\dfrac w2\]
contains all of them and has area $594w$ (the picture is not to scale).

draw((0,0.7)--(1,0.7),Arrows(3)); label("$6$",(0.5,0.7),N);

Choosing $w=\dfrac{1}{594}$ gives a classroom of area exactly $1$ square foot. A polygon always has positive area, so no smaller positive integer is possible, and the answer is $\boxed{1}$. (Indeed every positive area, however small, is possible. A real lower bound only appears with extra conditions, for example if each student needed a disc of radius $3$ feet inside the room.)
