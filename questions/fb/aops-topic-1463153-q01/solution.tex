\textbf{(a)} Classify a pentomino by the length $L$ of its longest straight run of squares (in a row or a column).
\begin{itemize}
\item $L=5$: the straight pentomino I.
\item $L=4$: a run of $4$ plus one square beside it (not at an end, or $L$ would be $5$). Beside an end square of the run it gives L; beside a middle square it gives Y. Two shapes.
\item $L=3$: put the run in a row, squares $1,2,3$. The other two squares cannot extend the row. At least one of them is next to the run, in the row above or below.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Both are next to the run, on the same side}: above squares $1,2$ (or $2,3$) gives P, above squares $1,3$ gives U.
\item \emph{Both are next to the run, on opposite sides}: above and below the same end square gives T; above and below the middle square gives X; above one square and below an adjacent one gives F; above square $1$ and below square $3$ gives Z.
\item \emph{Only one is next to the run}, and the fifth square is attached to it. If the fifth square is directly above it, we get a column of three: V (above an end square) or T (above the middle square). Otherwise the fifth square is beside it in the upper row, but not above the run; this happens only for the end squares and gives N.
\end{itemize}
These are eight shapes: P, U, T, X, F, Z, V, N.
\item $L=2$: no square can have neighbours on two opposite sides, so each square has at most $2$ neighbours. A $2\times2$ block is impossible (a fifth square attached to it would create a run of $3$). The squares therefore form a chain, and consecutive steps of the chain must turn (otherwise three are in line), always alternately left and right (two turns the same way close a $2\times2$ block). This is the staircase W.
\end{itemize}
