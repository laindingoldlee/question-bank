Multiply the sum and the difference:
\[\left(\sqrt{x+9}+\sqrt x\right)\left(\sqrt{x+9}-\sqrt x\right)=(x+9)-x=9 .\]
So $\sqrt{x+9}-\sqrt x=\dfrac{9}{18}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}}$.

(Such an $x$ exists: adding and subtracting, $\sqrt{x+9}=\dfrac{37}{4}$ and $\sqrt x=\dfrac{35}{4}$, so $x=\dfrac{1225}{16}$, and indeed $\dfrac{1225}{16}+9=\dfrac{1369}{16}=\left(\dfrac{37}{4}\right)^2$.)
