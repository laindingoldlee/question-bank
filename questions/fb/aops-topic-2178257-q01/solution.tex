Triangle $AED$ has a right angle at $E$, so by the Pythagorean theorem
\[AD=\sqrt{8^2+6^2}=10 .\]

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,0),B=(0,10),C=(10,10),D=(10,0),Ep=(6.4,4.8);
fill(A--Ep--D--C--B--cycle,paleblue);
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,gray+dashed);
draw(A--Ep--D--C--B--cycle,linewidth(1));
pair u=unit(A-Ep), v=unit(D-Ep);
draw(Ep+0.7*u--Ep+0.7*(u+v)--Ep+0.7*v);
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);dot(D);dot(Ep);
label("$A$",A,SW);label("$B$",B,NW);label("$C$",C,NE);label("$D$",D,SE);label("$E$",Ep,N);
label("$8$",(A+Ep)/2,NW);label("$6$",(D+Ep)/2,NE);label("$10$",(A+D)/2,S);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Pentagon $AEDCB$ is the square $ABCD$ with the right triangle $AED$ removed. The square has area $10^2=100$ and the triangle has area $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot8\cdot6=24$, so the pentagon has area
\[100-24=\boxed{76}\text{ square units}.\]
(If $E$ were outside the square, beyond side $AD$, the pentagon would be the square plus the triangle, with area $124$.)
