Let there be $p$ pentagons and $h$ hexagons, and let $V$, $E$, $F$ be the numbers of vertices, edges and faces.
\begin{itemize}
\item $F=p+h$.
\item Each edge lies on exactly two faces, so counting edges face by face gives $2E=5p+6h$.
\item Exactly three faces meet at each vertex, and each face of $k$ sides has $k$ vertices, so $3V=5p+6h$.
\end{itemize}
By Euler's formula $V-E+F=2$ for a convex polyhedron:
\[\dfrac{5p+6h}{3}-\dfrac{5p+6h}{2}+p+h=2.\]
Multiplying by $6$: $2(5p+6h)-3(5p+6h)+6p+6h=12$, i.e. $-(5p+6h)+6p+6h=12$, so $p=\boxed{12}$, whatever the number of hexagons. (The usual ball has $h=20$, $V=60$, $E=90$.)
