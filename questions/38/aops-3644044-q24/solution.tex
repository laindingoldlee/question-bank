Put $C=(0,0),B=(6,0),A=(\dfrac{15}{4},\dfrac{5\sqrt7}{4})$ by the cosine rule. The angle bisector theorem gives $AN:NC=4:6$, hence $N=(\dfrac{9}{4},\dfrac{3\sqrt7}{4})$ and $M=(3,0)$. The perpendicular distance to line $MN$ is $\dfrac{|\det(M,N)|}{|N-M|}=\dfrac{(\dfrac{9\sqrt7}{4})}{(\dfrac{3\sqrt2}{2})}=\dfrac{3\sqrt{14}}{4}$. The perpendicular foot is the midpoint of $MN$, so lies on the segment. Thus $p+q+r=3+14+4=\boxed{21}$.
