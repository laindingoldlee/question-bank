For $x\ne1$, $1+x+\cdots+x^n=\dfrac{x^{n+1}-1}{x-1}$, and $x=1$ is not a root (the sum there is $n+1$). So the roots are exactly the $(n+1)$th roots of unity other than $1$:
\[x=\exp\left(\dfrac{2\pi ik}{n+1}\right),\quad k=1,2,\dots,n.\]
(1) $x=\exp\left(\dfrac{2\pi ik}5\right)$, $k=1,2,3,4$. (2) $x=\exp\left(\dfrac{\pi ik}3\right)$, $k=1,\dots,5$, i.e. $-1,\ \dfrac{1\pm i\sqrt3}2,\ \dfrac{-1\pm i\sqrt3}2$. (3) $\boxed{x=\exp\left(\dfrac{2\pi ik}{n+1}\right),\ 1\le k\le n}$.
