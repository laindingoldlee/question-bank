Both $a$ and $b$ must be nonzero. Multiplying by $21ab$ gives $21b+21a=2ab$. Multiply by $2$ and rearrange:
\[4ab-42a-42b=0\iff 4ab-42a-42b+441=441\iff (2a-21)(2b-21)=441 .\]
Conversely, if integers $a,b$, both nonzero, satisfy $(2a-21)(2b-21)=441$, the steps reverse and $\dfrac{1}{a}+\dfrac{1}{b}=\dfrac{2}{21}$.

So we count factorisations $441=uv$ with $u=2a-21$ and $v=2b-21$. Since $441=3^2\cdot7^2$ is odd, every divisor $u$ is odd, so $a=\dfrac{u+21}{2}$ and $b=\dfrac{v+21}{2}$ are always integers. The number $441$ has $(2+1)(2+1)=9$ positive divisors, so there are $18$ choices of $u$ (positive or negative), each determining $v=\dfrac{441}{u}$.

We must exclude $a=0$ or $b=0$, that is, $u=-21$ or $v=-21$; since $uv=441$, these happen together, for the single choice $u=v=-21$. That leaves
\[18-1=\boxed{17}\]
ordered pairs. They are $(11,231)$, $(12,84)$, $(14,42)$, $(15,35)$, $(21,21)$ and their reverses from the positive divisors, together with $(-210,10)$, $(-63,9)$, $(-21,7)$, $(-14,6)$ and their reverses from the negative divisors.
