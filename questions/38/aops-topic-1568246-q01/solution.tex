\textbf{Factor (Sophie Germain identity).}
\[
y^4+4=\left(y^2+2\right)^2-(2y)^2=\left(y^2-2y+2\right)\left(y^2+2y+2\right).
\]
Both factors are positive ($y^2-2y+2=(y-1)^2+1\ge1$), and their product is a power of the prime $p$, so each is a power of $p$:
\[
y^2-2y+2=p^i,\qquad y^2+2y+2=p^j,\qquad 0\le i<j .
\]
\textbf{Case $i=0$.} Then $(y-1)^2+1=1$, so $y=1$ and $p^x=5$: $p=5$, $x=1$.

\textbf{Case $i\ge1$.} Then $p$ divides both factors, hence their difference $4y$.
\begin{itemize}
\item If $p=2$: $y^2-2y+2$ is even, so $y$ is even, $16\mid y^4$ and $y^4+4\equiv4\pmod{16}$. A power of $2$ that is $\equiv4\pmod{16}$ is $4$ itself, but $y^4+4>4$. Contradiction.
\item If $p$ is odd: $p\mid4y$ gives $p\mid y$, and then $y^2-2y+2\equiv2\pmod p$, so $p\mid2$. Contradiction.
\end{itemize}
So the only solution is
\[
(p,x,y)=\boxed{(5,1,1)} .
\]
