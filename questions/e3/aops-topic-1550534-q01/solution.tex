Measure distance in laps. When Bonnie has run $x$ laps, Annie has run $1.25x$ laps, so Annie is $0.25x$ laps ahead. Annie catches up with Bonnie from behind (that is, draws level with her for the first time after the start) when her lead is exactly one full lap:
\[
0.25x=1\iff x=4 .
\]
At that moment Bonnie has run $4$ laps and Annie $1.25\cdot4=5$ laps. Immediately afterwards Annie's lead exceeds one lap, so she is passing Bonnie. Hence Annie has run $\boxed{5}$ laps when she first passes Bonnie, answer $\textbf{(D)}$.

(On the poster's objection: at the instant when the lead is exactly one lap the two runners are level, and at every later instant, however soon, Annie is ahead. The number of laps ``when she passes'' is therefore the value $5$ at the instant of drawing level; any later instant would give more than $5$, and no first later instant exists. The usual explanation and the poster's ``relative speed'' explanation, in which Annie gains $0.25$ lap per Bonnie-lap and needs to gain $1$ lap, are the same argument.)
