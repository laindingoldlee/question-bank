Let $N=2^4+5^8=625^2+4^2=390641$. Since $\gcd(625,4)=1$, every odd prime $r$ dividing $N$ satisfies $\left(625\cdot4^{-1}\right)^2\equiv-1\pmod r$, so $-1$ is a quadratic residue mod $r$ and $r\equiv1\pmod4$. (Also $N$ is odd and $N\equiv1\pmod5$.)

So the smaller prime factor $q\le\sqrt N<626$ must be one of $13,17,29,37,41,53,61,73,89,97,101,109,113,\dots$. Testing these in order, the first divisor is $113$:
\[390641=113\cdot3457.\]
Every prime factor of $3457$ also divides $N$, so is at least $113$; as $113^2>3457$, the number $3457$ is prime. Hence $p=3457$, $q=113$, and $p-q=\boxed{3344}$.

(No Sophie Germain-type factorisation applies here, because $16$ is not of the form $4b^4$ with $b$ an integer; restricting the trial divisors to primes $\equiv1\pmod4$ is what makes the search short.)
