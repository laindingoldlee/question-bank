Call the doors $C$ (car), $G$ (goat) and $X$ (the radioactive goat, a car with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$). The host never opens $C$ if he can avoid it, and he prefers $G$ (certainly a goat) to $X$ (a goat with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$). Each of your choices has probability $\dfrac{1}{3}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item You chose $C$: the host opens $G$ and shows a goat. Staying wins; switching (to $X$) wins with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
\item You chose $G$: the host must choose between $C$ and $X$ and opens $X$, which shows a goat with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$. If it does, staying loses and switching (to $C$) wins.
\item You chose $X$: the host opens $G$ and shows a goat. Staying wins with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$; switching (to $C$) wins.
\end{itemize}
The probability that a goat is shown is $\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{3}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{5}{6}$. Given that a goat is shown,
\[
P(\text{stay wins})=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{3}\cdot1+\dfrac{1}{6}\cdot0+\dfrac{1}{3}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}}{\dfrac{5}{6}}=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2}}{\dfrac{5}{6}}=\dfrac{3}{5},\qquad
P(\text{switch wins})=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{3}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{6}\cdot1+\dfrac{1}{3}\cdot1}{\dfrac{5}{6}}=\dfrac{\dfrac{2}{3}}{\dfrac{5}{6}}=\dfrac{4}{5} .
\]
So you should $\boxed{\text{switch: }\dfrac{4}{5}\text{ against }\dfrac{3}{5}}$. (The two probabilities add up to more than $1$ because both remaining doors may hide cars.)
