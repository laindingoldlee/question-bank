Let $t=a-\dfrac1a$. Then $a^3-\dfrac1{a^3}=t^3+3t$, and $t^3+3t=22\sqrt2$ is solved by $t=2\sqrt2$ (as $16\sqrt2+6\sqrt2=22\sqrt2$); since $t^3+3t$ is increasing, this is the only real solution.

\textbf{(a)} $a^2-2\sqrt2\,a-1=0$ with $a>0$ gives $a=\sqrt2+\sqrt3$.

\textbf{(b)} $a+\dfrac1a=\sqrt{t^2+4}=\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt3$, so $a^2-\dfrac1{a^2}=\left(a-\dfrac1a\right)\left(a+\dfrac1a\right)=2\sqrt2\cdot2\sqrt3=\boxed{4\sqrt6}\approx9.80$, not $10$.

\textbf{(c)} $a^3+\dfrac1{a^3}=\left(a+\dfrac1a\right)^3-3\left(a+\dfrac1a\right)=24\sqrt3-6\sqrt3=\boxed{18\sqrt3}$. Since $a^2=5+2\sqrt6$ and $\dfrac1{a^2}=5-2\sqrt6$,
\[5a^2+\dfrac{4}{3a^2}=25+10\sqrt6+\dfrac{20-8\sqrt6}{3}=\boxed{\dfrac{95+22\sqrt6}{3}}.\]
