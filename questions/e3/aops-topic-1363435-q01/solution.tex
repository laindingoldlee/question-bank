Think of the deal as placing the $52$ cards into $52$ slots, $13$ slots per player, with all placements equally likely. Follow where the four aces land.

The ace of spades lands somewhere; it does not matter where. Given that, the ace of hearts is equally likely to be in any of the \emph{remaining $51$ slots}, and $39$ of them belong to the other three players: probability $\dfrac{39}{51}$. Given both, the ace of diamonds is equally likely to be in any of the remaining $50$ slots, of which $26$ belong to the two players without an ace: probability $\dfrac{26}{50}$. Finally the ace of clubs is in one of the remaining $49$ slots, $13$ of which belong to the last player: probability $\dfrac{13}{49}$.

The denominators drop by $1$ because each ace already placed occupies a slot that the next ace cannot use. Multiplying,
\[
P=\dfrac{39}{51}\cdot\dfrac{26}{50}\cdot\dfrac{13}{49}=\boxed{\dfrac{2197}{20825}}\approx0.105 .
\]
Check by counting hands: $\dfrac{4!\,\dbinom{48}{12,12,12,12}}{\dbinom{52}{13,13,13,13}}=\dfrac{4!\cdot13^4\cdot48!}{52!}$, which simplifies to the same value.
