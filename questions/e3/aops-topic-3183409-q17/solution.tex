Put $n=2012$. Placing one blue in each of $n-1$ boxes and all remaining cards in the last yields $\dfrac{n-1+\dfrac{1}{n+1}}{n}=\dfrac{n}{n+1}$. To prove optimality, let $k$ boxes contain red. The other $n-k$ boxes each use at least one blue, so at most $k$ blue cards remain in the red-containing boxes. Write their counts $r_i\ge1,b_i\ge0$. If $k\ge2$, the sum of red probabilities is at least $\sum\dfrac{1}{1+b_i}\ge\dfrac{k^2}{k+\sum b_i}\ge\dfrac{k}{2}\ge1$. If $k=1$, its blue count is at most 1 and its red probability at least $\dfrac{n}{n+1}$. Thus in every case the overall red probability is at least $\dfrac{1}{n+1}$. The maximum blue probability is $\boxed{\dfrac{2012}{2013}}$.
