\emph{The frog riddle.} You must lick a female frog to survive. Each frog is male or female with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$, independently, and males and females look alike. Only males croak: while you listen, each male croaks with probability $q$ ($0<q\le1$), independently, and females never croak. On a stump to one side sits a single frog. In a clearing to the other side are two frogs, and you hear a croak from the clearing (so at least one of the two frogs croaked). You can lick both frogs in the clearing, or the one on the stump.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Find the probability of survival if you go to the clearing, in terms of $q$.
\item[(b)] One argument gives $\dfrac{2}{3}$ for the clearing; another gives $\dfrac{1}{2}$, the same as the stump. Explain how each answer arises, and which path you should take.
\end{enumerate}
