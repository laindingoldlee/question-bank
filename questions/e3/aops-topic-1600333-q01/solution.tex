We prove the contrapositive: if $n$ is even, then $n^2$ does not leave remainder $1$ on division by $4$.

If $n$ is even, write $n=2m$ with $m$ an integer. Then $n^2=4m^2$ is a multiple of $4$, so it leaves remainder $0$, not $1$. Hence whenever $n^2$ leaves remainder $1$, $n$ must be odd. $\blacksquare$

(The converse also holds: if $n=2m+1$ then $n^2=4(m^2+m)+1$.)
