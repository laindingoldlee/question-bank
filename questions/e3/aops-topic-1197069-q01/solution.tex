Look at the angle at $(6,7)$: the vectors to the other two points are $(-12,-12)$ and $(-4,4)$, whose dot product is $48-48=0$. So the three points form a right triangle with the right angle at $(6,7)$, and by Thales' theorem the hypotenuse from $(-6,-5)$ to $(2,11)$ is a diameter.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair P1=(6,7),P2=(-6,-5),P3=(2,11),O=(-2,3); draw(circle(O,sqrt(80)),gray); draw(P2--P3,blue); draw(P2--P1--P3);
draw(P1+0.8*unit(P2-P1)--P1+0.8*unit(P2-P1)+0.8*unit(P3-P1)--P1+0.8*unit(P3-P1));
dot(P1);dot(P2);dot(P3);dot(O); label("$(6,7)$",P1,E); label("$(-6,-5)$",P2,SW); label("$(2,11)$",P3,N); label("$(-2,3)$",O,W);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Its length is $\sqrt{8^2+16^2}=\sqrt{320}=8\sqrt5$, so the radius is $\boxed{4\sqrt5}$ (and the centre is the midpoint $(-2,3)$).
