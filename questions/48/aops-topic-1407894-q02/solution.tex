There are $\binom{49}{3}=18424$ sets of three points; we subtract the collinear ones. Every collinear triple is determined by its two outer points $P$ and $Q$ together with a choice of the middle point among the lattice points strictly between them. If $Q-P=(d_x,d_y)$, there are $\gcd(|d_x|,|d_y|)-1$ such points.

(For example, $P=(0,0)$ and $Q=(6,4)$ have only $(3,2)$ between them, since $\gcd(6,4)=2$; $P'$ and $Q'$ in the figure have $3$ points between them.) A displacement $(d_x,d_y)$ with $d_x,d_y\ge0$ fits in $(7-d_x)(7-d_y)$ positions, and twice as many when both are positive (the mirror direction). Group by direction:
\begin{itemize}
\item horizontal and vertical lines: $14$ lines of $7$ points, $14\binom{7}{3}=490$ triples;
\item slopes $\pm1$: in each direction the diagonals have $7,6,6,5,5,4,4,3,3$ points, giving $\binom73+2\left(\binom63+\binom53+\binom43+\binom33\right)=105$, so $210$ in all;
\item slopes $\pm2,\pm\dfrac{1}{2}$: displacement $(2,4)$ fits in $5\cdot3=15$ positions with $1$ middle point, and $(3,6)$ in $4\cdot1=4$ positions with $2$ middle points, so $15+8=23$ per direction and $92$ for the four directions;
\item slopes $\pm3,\pm\dfrac{1}{3}$: displacement $(2,6)$, $5\cdot1=5$ positions per direction, $20$ in all;
\item slopes $\pm\dfrac{3}{2},\pm\dfrac{2}{3}$: displacement $(4,6)$, $3\cdot1=3$ per direction, $12$ in all.
\end{itemize}
Every other direction has $\gcd=1$ for all displacements that fit in the grid. So there are $490+210+92+20+12=824$ collinear triples, and the number of triangles is
\[
18424-824=\boxed{17600}.
\]
