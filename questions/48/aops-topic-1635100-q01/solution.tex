For a positive integer $x$, $\left(2^2\right)^x$ is the product of $x$ factors, each equal to $2^2=2\cdot2$:
\[
\left(2^2\right)^x=\underbrace{(2\cdot2)(2\cdot2)\cdots(2\cdot2)}_{x\text{ pairs}} .
\]
This is a product of $2x$ factors of $2$, which is $2^{2x}$ by the definition of a power. $\blacksquare$

(The same law $\left(a^m\right)^n=a^{mn}$ holds for all integer, rational and real exponents when $a>0$: the definitions of those powers are chosen precisely so that the laws of exponents keep holding. For instance $4^x=\left(2^2\right)^x=2^{2x}$ for every real $x$.)
