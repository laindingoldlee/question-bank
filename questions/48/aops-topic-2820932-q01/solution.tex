$(n-1)(n+1)=n^2-1$ (difference of squares), so $(n-1)(n+1)+1=n^2$. The identity is algebraic, so it holds for all real (indeed complex) $n$, rational or not. In words: $n\cdot n$ and $(n-1)(n+1)$ differ by exactly $1$ because moving one factor down by $1$ and the other up by $1$ changes the product by $-1\cdot1$. $\blacksquare$
