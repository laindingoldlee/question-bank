All $n$ inequalities cannot hold, since summing would give $S>2S$ for the positive total $S$. Nor can exactly $n-1$ hold: rotate the indexing so those at $1,\ldots,n-1$ hold. They imply $a_1>a_2>\cdots>a_{n-1}$, but the last also gives $a_{n-1}>a_n+a_1>a_1$, a contradiction. Thus $A(n)\le n-2$.

This bound is attained by $a_i=2^{n-i}$ for $1\le i\le n$. For $i\le n-2$, the next two terms sum to $\dfrac{3}{4}a_i<a_i$, while the final two inequalities fail. Therefore $A(n)=n-2$. As $n$ runs from $3$ to $2016$, these values are $1,\ldots,2014$, so the answer is $\boxed{2014}$.
