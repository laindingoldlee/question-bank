Reflection in the $y$-axis sends $(x,y)\mapsto(-x,y)$, and reflection in the $x$-axis sends $(x,y)\mapsto(x,-y)$; their composition sends $(x,y)\mapsto(-x,-y)$. The rotation by $180^\circ$ about the origin sends $(x,y)$ to
\[\left(x\cos180^\circ-y\sin180^\circ,\ x\sin180^\circ+y\cos180^\circ\right)=(-x,-y).\]
So the two transformations agree at every point, hence on every figure (and the order of the two reflections does not matter). $\blacksquare$

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair[] T={(1,0.5),(2.5,0.8),(1.5,2)};
path t0=T[0]--T[1]--T[2]--cycle;path t1=reflect((0,0),(0,1))*t0;path t2=reflect((0,0),(1,0))*t1;
filldraw(t0,lightgray);draw(t1,dashed);filldraw(t2,mediumgray);
draw((-3.2,0)--(3.2,0),gray,Arrow);draw((0,-2.6)--(0,2.6),gray,Arrow);
label("$F$",(1.7,1.1),fontsize(8pt));label("$F_1$",(-1.7,1.1),fontsize(8pt));label("$F_2$",(-1.7,-1.1),fontsize(8pt));
dot((1.5,2));dot((-1.5,-2));draw((1.5,2)--(-1.5,-2),dotted);
\end{asy}
