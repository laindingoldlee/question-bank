If $x=y=z=0$ the sum is $0$. Otherwise $x+y+z>0$.

We claim $1+yz\ge\dfrac{x+y+z}{2}$. Indeed $(1-y)(1-z)\ge0$ gives $y+z\le1+yz$, and $x\le1$, so
\[x+y+z\le1+(1+yz)=2+yz\le2+2yz=2(1+yz).\]
Therefore
\[\dfrac{x}{1+yz}\le\dfrac{2x}{x+y+z},\]
and in the same way $\dfrac{y}{1+zx}\le\dfrac{2y}{x+y+z}$ and $\dfrac{z}{1+xy}\le\dfrac{2z}{x+y+z}$. Adding,
\[\dfrac{x}{1+yz}+\dfrac{y}{1+zx}+\dfrac{z}{1+xy}\le\dfrac{2(x+y+z)}{x+y+z}=2.\ \blacksquare\]
Equality holds, for example, at $x=y=1$, $z=0$.
