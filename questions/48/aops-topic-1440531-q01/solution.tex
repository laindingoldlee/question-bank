Let $L(m)$ be the maximum number of pieces of a disc cut by $m$ lines and $B(n)$ the maximum number of pieces of a ball cut by $n$ planes.

\emph{Lines in a disc.} The $m$th line meets the earlier $m-1$ lines in at most $m-1$ points inside the disc, so it is divided into at most $m$ segments, and each segment splits one region into two. So $L(m)\le L(m-1)+m$, giving $L(m)\le1+\left(1+2+\cdots+m\right)=1+m+\dbinom m2$.

\emph{Planes in a ball.} The $n$th plane meets the ball in a disc, which the earlier $n-1$ planes cut along at most $n-1$ lines, into at most $L(n-1)$ pieces; each piece splits one solid piece in two. So $B(n)\le B(n-1)+L(n-1)$, and
\[
B(n)\le\dbinom n0+\dbinom n1+\dbinom n2+\dbinom n3 .
\]
Equality holds when the planes are in general position (no two parallel, no three through a common line, no four through a common point) and all their intersection points lie inside the ball, which can be arranged by choosing the planes close to the centre. For $n=6$:
\[
B(6)=1+6+15+20=\boxed{42}.
\]
