We need $x>0$ and $x\ne1$. Let $t=\log_3x$, so $t\ne0$. Then
\[
\log_x3=\dfrac{1}{t},\qquad\log_{\sqrt3}x=\dfrac{\log_3x}{\log_3\sqrt3}=2t,\qquad\log_3\sqrt x=\dfrac {t}{2} .
\]
The equation becomes $t+\dfrac{1}{t}=2t+\dfrac {t}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}$, that is $\dfrac{1}{t}=\dfrac{3t+1}{2}$, or
\[
3t^2+t-2=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(3t-2)(t+1)=0 .
\]
So $t=\dfrac{2}{3}$ or $t=-1$, both nonzero, and
\[
x=3^{\dfrac{2}{3}}=\sqrt[3]9\qquad\text{or}\qquad x=3^{-1}=\dfrac{1}{3} .
\]
Check for $x=\dfrac{1}{3}$: the left side is $-1-1=-2$ and the right side is $-2-\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}=-2$. For $x=\sqrt[3]9$: the left side is $\dfrac{2}{3}+\dfrac{3}{2}=\dfrac{13}{6}$, and the right side is $\dfrac{4}{3}+\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{13}{6}$. The solutions are $\boxed{x=\sqrt[3]{9}\text{ and }x=\dfrac{1}{3}}$.
