\textbf{Claim: some image appears on every card.} Suppose not. Take any card $K$, with images $X_1,\ldots,X_8$.

Fix one image $X_i$. By assumption some card $L$ does not show $X_i$. Every card showing $X_i$ shares exactly one image with $L$, and that image is not $X_i$. Two different cards showing $X_i$ cannot share the same image $Y$ of $L$, since they would then have the two images $X_i$ and $Y$ in common. So the cards showing $X_i$ meet $L$ in different images, and there are at most $8$ of them; besides $K$, at most $7$ cards show $X_i$.

Every card other than $K$ shares an image with $K$, so it shows at least one of $X_1,\ldots,X_8$. Hence the number of cards other than $K$ is at most $8\cdot7=56$, and the deck has at most $57$ cards. This contradicts $2020>57$, proving the claim.

\textbf{Conclusion.} Let $Z$ be an image on every card. Any two cards share $Z$, and since they share exactly one image, they share nothing else. So every image other than $Z$ appears on only one card. Exactly one image appears on two or more cards, so the maximum (indeed the only possible value) is $\boxed{1}$.

Such a deck exists: give every card the image $Z$ and $7$ new images of its own (using $1+7\cdot2020$ images in all).
