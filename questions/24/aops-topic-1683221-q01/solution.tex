The $n$th partial sum is
\[
S_n=1+2+\cdots+n=\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}.
\]
Given any number $M$, choose an integer $n>\sqrt{2M}$; then $S_n>\dfrac{n^2}{2}>M$. So the partial sums exceed every bound, and the series diverges to $+\infty$: $\boxed{\text{it has no finite sum}}$. $\blacksquare$

(The value $-\dfrac{1}{12}$ that is sometimes quoted comes from assigning a value to the series by analytic continuation of the zeta function; it is not the sum of the series in the ordinary sense.)
