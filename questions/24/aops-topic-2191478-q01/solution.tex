The primes between $100$ and $110$ are $101$, $103$, $107$ and $109$, all odd. The number $k$ of crashed planes is a prime with $2\le k\le23$, so $m=28-k\ge5$ planes survive.

The number of survivors is a sum of $m$ odd numbers. If $m$ is even this sum is even and greater than $2$, so it is not prime. So $m$ is odd, and $m=5$ (with $k=23$) gives the smallest totals; for $m\ge7$ the total is at least $7\cdot101=707$.

With $m=5$, the total is
\[505+2a+6c+8d,\]
where $a$, $c$, $d$ are the numbers of planes with $103$, $107$, $109$ passengers. The total is odd and at least $505$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $505=5\cdot101$ is not prime;
\item $507=3\cdot13^2$ is not prime;
\item $509$ is prime (it is not divisible by $2,3,5,7,11,13,17,19$, and $23^2>509$), and it is reached with three planes of $101$ and two of $103$: $3\cdot101+2\cdot103=509$.
\end{itemize}
So the smallest possible number of survivors is $\boxed{509}$.
