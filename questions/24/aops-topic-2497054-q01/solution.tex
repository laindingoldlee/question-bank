For any real $\alpha$, the point $P=(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)$ is, by definition, the point of the unit circle (centre $O$, radius $1$) reached by turning through $\alpha$ from $(1,0)$.

Drop the perpendicular from $P$ to the $x$-axis, with foot $Q$. If $P$ is not on an axis, $OQP$ is a right triangle with legs $|\cos\alpha|$ and $|\sin\alpha|$ and hypotenuse $OP=1$, so by the Pythagorean theorem
\[\cos^2\alpha+\sin^2\alpha=|\cos\alpha|^2+|\sin\alpha|^2=1.\]
If $P$ is on an axis, one of $\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha$ is $0$ and the other is $\pm1$, and the identity holds directly. $\blacksquare$

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real t=35;
pair O=(0,0), P=dir(t), Q=(P.x,0);
draw(circle(O,1),linewidth(0.8));
draw((-1.25,0)--(1.3,0),Arrow(6)); draw((0,-1.25)--(0,1.3),Arrow(6));
draw(O--P,blue); draw(P--Q,red); draw(O--Q,red);
real s=0.07; draw(Q+(-s,0)--Q+(-s,s)--Q+(0,s));
draw(arc(O,0.25,0,t)); label("$\alpha$",0.35*dir(t/2),fontsize(8pt));
dot(P); label("$(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)$",P,NE,fontsize(8pt));
label("$1$",P/2,NW,blue+fontsize(8pt)); label("$|\sin\alpha|$",(P+Q)/2,E,red+fontsize(7pt)); label("$|\cos\alpha|$",Q/2,S,red+fontsize(7pt));
\end{asy}

Hence $\sin^2\alpha+\cos^2\alpha+\sin^2\alpha+\cos^2\alpha=1+1$ for every $\alpha$, so the claim is correct (if roundabout).
