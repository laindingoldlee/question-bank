A math team has $100$ students, and exactly $50$ of them got a perfect score on a test; assume every set of $50$ students is equally likely to be the set of perfect scorers. Student $S$ asks the coach about his score. The coach does not answer, but instead names $49$ \emph{other} students who got perfect scores, choosing them at random from the other perfect scorers.

$S$ argues: ``Of the $51$ students not named (including me), exactly one got a perfect score, so my chance of a perfect score is now $\dfrac{1}{51}$.'' Find the actual probability that $S$ got a perfect score, given the coach's statement, and explain the error in $S$'s argument.
