\textbf{(a)} Let $\sigma(n)$ be the sum of all positive divisors of $n$. The proper divisors are all the divisors except $n$, so their sum is $\sigma(n)-n$. Hence
\[
\sigma(n)-n>n\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \sigma(n)>2n ,
\]
and the two definitions agree. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{(b)} Check $n=1,\dots,12$ using the sums of proper divisors:
\begin{itemize}
\item primes $2,3,5,7,11$ and $n=1$: the sum is $1$ (or $0$ for $n=1$), less than $n$;
\item $4$: $1+2=3$; $6$: $1+2+3=6$ (perfect, not abundant); $8$: $1+2+4=7$; $9$: $1+3=4$; $10$: $1+2+5=8$;
\item $12$: $1+2+3+4+6=16>12$.
\end{itemize}
So the smallest abundant number is $\boxed{12}$ (equivalently, $\sigma(12)=28>24$).
