For a positive integer $n$ and an integer $k\ge0$, let $\sigma_k(n)$ be the sum of the $k$th powers of the positive divisors of $n$. (So $\sigma_0(n)$ is the number of divisors and $\sigma_1(n)$ is their sum.)
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Compute $\sigma_2(12)$ and $\sigma_3(6)$.
\item[(b)] Let $p,q$ be distinct primes and $a,b$ nonnegative integers. Show that
\[\sigma_k\left(p^aq^b\right)=\left(1+p^k+p^{2k}+\cdots+p^{ak}\right)\left(1+q^k+q^{2k}+\cdots+q^{bk}\right).\]
\item[(c)] Use (b) to compute $\sigma_2(72)$.
\end{enumerate}
