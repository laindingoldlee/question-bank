Notice that $x^3-3x^2+3x=(x-1)^3+1$, so $F(x)=\sqrt[3]{(x-1)^3+1}$.

We need $\sqrt[3]{(g-1)^3+1}=x$, that is, $(g-1)^3+1=x^3$, so $(g-1)^3=x^3-1$ and
\[
g(x)=\boxed{1+\sqrt[3]{x^3-1}} .
\]
For $x>0$ we have $x^3-1>-1$, so $\sqrt[3]{x^3-1}>-1$ and $g(x)>0$, as required. Check: $\left(g(x)-1\right)^3+1=x^3-1+1=x^3$, so $F(g(x))=\sqrt[3]{x^3}=x$. Since $t\mapsto(t-1)^3+1$ is strictly increasing, $F$ is one-to-one, and $g$ is the inverse function of $F$ on the positive reals.
