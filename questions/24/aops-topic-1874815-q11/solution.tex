Let $P_n$ be the number of ways for a product of $n$ factors. Then $P_1=P_2=1$, and $P_3=2$: $(ab)c$ and $a(bc)$.

\emph{Systematic search: look at the last multiplication.} It multiplies the product of the first $k$ factors by the product of the last $n-k$ factors, for some $k=1,\dots,n-1$, and the two parts are then parenthesised independently. Hence
\[
P_4=P_1P_3+P_2P_2+P_3P_1=2+1+2=5,
\]
namely $a(b(cd))$, $a((bc)d)$, $(ab)(cd)$, $(a(bc))d$, $((ab)c)d$, and
\[
P_5=P_1P_4+P_2P_3+P_3P_2+P_4P_1=5+2+2+5=14 .
\]
With $a,b,c,d,e=2,3,4,5,6$ the $14$ ways are:
\begin{itemize}
\item last step $2\cdot(3\cdot4\cdot5\cdot6)$: $2(3(4(5\cdot6)))$, $2(3((4\cdot5)6))$, $2((3\cdot4)(5\cdot6))$, $2((3(4\cdot5))6)$, $2(((3\cdot4)5)6)$;
\item last step $(2\cdot3)\cdot(4\cdot5\cdot6)$: $(2\cdot3)(4(5\cdot6))$, $(2\cdot3)((4\cdot5)6)$;
\item last step $(2\cdot3\cdot4)\cdot(5\cdot6)$: $(2(3\cdot4))(5\cdot6)$, $((2\cdot3)4)(5\cdot6)$;
\item last step $(2\cdot3\cdot4\cdot5)\cdot6$: $(2(3(4\cdot5)))6$, $(2((3\cdot4)5))6$, $((2\cdot3)(4\cdot5))6$, $((2(3\cdot4))5)6$, $(((2\cdot3)4)5)6$.
\end{itemize}
So there are $\boxed{14}$ ways (all of them give $720$, by associativity). The numbers $1,1,2,5,14,\dots$ are the Catalan numbers.
