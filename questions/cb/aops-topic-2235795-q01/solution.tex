The roof is $60$ by $96$ inches. Along one direction, $n$ shingles of length $a$ with overlaps of at least $2$ inches between neighbours cover a length of at most
\[na-2(n-1)=n(a-2)+2,\]
so covering a length $L$ needs $n(a-2)+2\ge L$, i.e. $n\ge\dfrac{L-2}{a-2}$; and this many suffice (spread the extra overlap over the joints).

\emph{Long side ($14$ in) along the $96$ in edge:} $n\ge\dfrac{94}{12}$, so $8$ columns; short side ($8$ in) along the $60$ in edge: $n\ge\dfrac{58}{6}$, so $10$ rows. Total $8\cdot10=80$.

\emph{Long side along the $60$ in edge:} $n\ge\dfrac{58}{12}$, so $5$; short side along the $96$ in edge: $n\ge\dfrac{94}{6}$, so $16$. Total $5\cdot16=80$.

Both orientations need $\boxed{80}$ shingles. (For example, $8$ columns of length $14$ overlap by a total of $8\cdot14-96=16$ inches over $7$ joints, about $2.3$ inches each, and $10$ rows of height $8$ overlap by $80-60=20$ inches over $9$ joints, about $2.2$ inches each.)
