The digits, from left to right, are $7,a,7,b,a,7,a,b,7,b,a,7,b,7,7$: six $7$s, four $a$s and four $b$s plus one more $7$, i.e. seven $7$s, four $a$s and four $b$s.

\textbf{Divisibility by $9$.} The digit sum is $49+4a+4b$, so $4(a+b)\equiv-49\equiv5\pmod9$; multiplying by $7$ (the inverse of $4$), $a+b\equiv35\equiv8\pmod9$. So $a+b=8$ or $17$.

\textbf{Divisibility by $11$.} The digits in the odd positions from the left (positions $1,3,\dots,15$) are $7,7,a,a,7,a,b,7$ and those in the even positions are $a,b,7,b,b,7,7$. For a number with an odd number of digits, the odd positions from the left are the odd positions from the right, so the alternating-sum test gives
\[
(28+3a+b)-(21+a+3b)=7+2a-2b\equiv0\pmod{11},
\]
i.e. $2(a-b)\equiv4$, so $a-b\equiv2\pmod{11}$: $a-b=2$ or $a-b=-9$.

Combining: $a+b=8$, $a-b=2$ gives $a=5$, $b=3$; the other combinations give non-digits or wrong parity ($a+b=17$ with $a-b=2$ or $-9$ has no integer solution with digits, and $a+b=8$, $a-b=-9$ is impossible). So $10a+b=\boxed{53}$.
