Write $P(x)=\prod_{i=1}^m(x-r_i)$ and $Q(x)=\prod_{j=1}^n(x-s_j)$. Then the left side is $\prod_{i,j}(r_i-s_j)$, while the product on the right before its sign factor is $\prod_{j,i}(s_j-r_i)$. Reversing the sign in each of the $mn$ factors proves the identity, even when a common root makes both sides zero. For common nonzero leading coefficient $L$ instead of monicity, the corrected identity is $L^n\prod Q(r)=(-1)^{mn}L^m\prod P(s)$.
