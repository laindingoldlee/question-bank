\textbf{(a)} The first line has slope $\dfrac{9-6}{5-2}=1$ and passes through $(2,6)$: $y=x+4$. The second has slope $\dfrac{-7-(-1)}{5-(-1)}=-1$ and passes through $(-1,-1)$: $y=-x-2$. Setting $x+4=-x-2$ gives $x=-3$, $y=1$. The lines meet at $\boxed{(-3,1)}$.

\textbf{(b)} \textbf{No.} The point $(-3,1)$ has $x=-3$, but the first segment has $2\le x\le5$ and the second has $-1\le x\le5$, so the intersection point of the lines lies on neither segment. Since two non-parallel lines meet only once, the segments do not intersect. (The handbook answer $(-3,1)$ is the intersection of the \emph{lines} through the segments; the problem as worded, with segments, has no intersection point.)
