\textbf{(a)} Take $x=1$: the equation becomes $1+a+2011+b+1=0$, i.e.\ $a+b=-2013$. So for $a=-2013$, $b=0$ the equation $x^4-2013x^3+2011x^2+1=0$ has the real solution $x=1$, but $a+b=-2013<126$. The claim is false.

\textbf{(b)} Let $x$ be a real solution. Then $x\ne0$ (at $x=0$ the left side is $1$). Dividing by $x^2$,
\[
x^2+\dfrac{1}{x^2}+2011=-\left(ax+\dfrac {b}{x}\right).
\]
Put $t=x^2+\dfrac{1}{x^2}$, so $t\ge2$. By the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality,
\[
\left(ax+\dfrac {b}{x}\right)^2\le(a^2+b^2)\left(x^2+\dfrac{1}{x^2}\right)=(a^2+b^2)\,t .
\]
Therefore
\[
a^2+b^2\ge\dfrac{(t+2011)^2}{t}=t+4022+\dfrac{2011^2}{t}\ge4022+2\sqrt{2011^2}=8044,
\]
using AM-GM on $t+\dfrac{2011^2}{t}$. $\blacksquare$
