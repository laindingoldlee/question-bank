\textbf{At most $10^5$.} Two numbers that agree in their first five digits differ in at most one position (the last). So all issued numbers have different first five digits, and there are only $10^5$ possible strings of five digits. Hence at most $100\,000$ numbers can be issued.

\textbf{$10^5$ is possible.} Issue every number $d_1d_2d_3d_4d_5d_6$ whose last digit is a check digit: $d_6$ is the remainder of $d_1+d_2+d_3+d_4+d_5$ modulo $10$. There are $10^5$ of them, one for each choice of $d_1,\dots,d_5$. If two of them differ in exactly one position, that position cannot be among the first five (changing one of $d_1,\dots,d_5$ changes the sum modulo $10$, and so forces a change in $d_6$ as well), and it cannot be only the last one (equal first five digits give equal check digits). So any two issued numbers differ in at least two positions.

The largest possible number is therefore $\boxed{100\,000}$.
