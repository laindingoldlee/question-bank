Let $O$ be the centre and $r$ the radius of the incircle: $r=\dfrac{12}{2\sqrt3}=2\sqrt3$, and $OC=2r=4\sqrt3$ (in an equilateral triangle the centre is the centroid, two thirds of the way along a median of length $6\sqrt3$).

\textbf{Replacing $\dfrac{DC}{2}$ by a distance.} Let $K$ be the point of segment $OC$ with $OK=\dfrac{r^2}{OC}=\sqrt3$. For every point $D$ on the incircle, triangles $ODK$ and $OCD$ share the angle at $O$, and
\[
\dfrac{OK}{OD}=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2\sqrt3}=\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{2\sqrt3}{4\sqrt3}=\dfrac{OD}{OC},
\]
so they are similar (SAS) and $\dfrac{DK}{CD}=\dfrac{1}{2}$. Hence $\dfrac{DC}{2}=DK$ and
\[
BD+\dfrac{DC}{2}=BD+DK\ge BK,
\]
with equality when $D$ lies on segment $BK$. Since $B$ is outside the incircle and $K$ is inside it ($OK<r$), the segment $BK$ does cross the circle, so the bound is attained.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real s=12; pair B=(0,0),C=(s,0),A=(s/2,s*sqrt(3)/2);
real r=s/(2*sqrt(3)); pair O=(s/2,r);
pair K=O+r^2/length(C-O)*unit(C-O);
pair[] X=intersectionpoints(B--K,circle(O,r)); pair D=X[0];
draw(circle(O,r),blue);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(O--C,gray+dashed);
draw(B--D,red); draw(D--K,red); draw(D--C,deepgreen+dashed);
dot(A);dot(B);dot(C);dot(O);dot(K,red);dot(D,red);
label("$A$",A,N); label("$B$",B,SW); label("$C$",C,SE); label("$O$",O,W);
label("$K$",K,N,red); label("$D$",D,NW,red);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

\textbf{Computing $BK$.} Put $B=(0,0)$, $C=(12,0)$, so $O=(6,2\sqrt3)$. The unit vector from $O$ to $C$ is $\dfrac{(6,-2\sqrt3)}{4\sqrt3}=\left(\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2},-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$, so
\[
K=O+\sqrt3\left(\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2},-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)=\left(\dfrac{15}{2},\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{2}\right),\qquad BK=\sqrt{\dfrac{225}{4}+\dfrac{27}{4}}=\sqrt{63}=3\sqrt7 .
\]
The minimum is $\boxed{3\sqrt7}$.
