Dividing $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$ by $\cos^2\theta$ gives $\tan^2\theta+1=\sec^2\theta$, and dividing by $\sin^2\theta$ gives $1+\cot^2\theta=\csc^2\theta$. (Geometrically, the tangent at $T$ to the unit circle meets the axis at $S$ with $OS=\sec\theta$ and $TS=\tan\theta$, and $OTS$ is right-angled at $T$.)

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real t=40;pair O=(0,0),T=dir(t),S=(1/Cos(t),0);
draw(arc(O,1,-10,100),gray);draw(O--S,linewidth(0.8));draw(O--T);draw(T--S,linewidth(1));
draw(T+0.1*dir(t+180)--T+0.1*dir(t+180)+0.1*dir(t-90)--T+0.1*dir(t-90));
label("$O$",O,SW);label("$T$",T,N);label("$S$",S,SE);label("$1$",(O+T)/2,NW,fontsize(8pt));label("$\tan\theta$",(T+S)/2,NE,fontsize(8pt));label("$\sec\theta$",(0.45,0),S,fontsize(8pt));label("$\theta$",0.25*dir(t/2),fontsize(7pt));
\end{asy}

So (a) equals $\boxed{1}$ and (b) equals $\boxed{-1}$.
