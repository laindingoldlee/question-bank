Let $Q(x)=(x+1)P(x)-x$, a polynomial of degree $n+1$ with roots $0,1,\dots,n$. So
\[Q(x)=c\,x(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-n)\]
for a constant $c$. At $x=-1$: $Q(-1)=0\cdot P(-1)+1=1$, while the product equals $c(-1)(-2)\cdots(-(n+1))=c(-1)^{n+1}(n+1)!$. Hence $c=\dfrac{(-1)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}$. Then
\[(n+2)P(n+1)-(n+1)=Q(n+1)=c\,(n+1)!=(-1)^{n+1},\]
so
\[P(n+1)=\dfrac{n+1+(-1)^{n+1}}{n+2}=\boxed{\begin{cases}1,&n\text{ odd},\\[2pt]\dfrac{n}{n+2},&n\text{ even}.\end{cases}}\]
(Check $n=1$: $P(x)=\dfrac x2$ gives $P(2)=1$.)
