A diagonal joins two vertices that are not adjacent. From any vertex $P$ of the octagon, diagonals go to the $8-3=5$ vertices other than $P$ and its two neighbours:

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair[] V; for(int k=0;k<8;++k) V.push(dir(112.5+45*k));
draw(V[0]--V[1]--V[2]--V[3]--V[4]--V[5]--V[6]--V[7]--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
for(int k=2;k<7;++k) draw(V[0]--V[k],red+dashed);
for(int k=0;k<8;++k) dot(V[k]);
label("$P$",V[0],V[0]);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

That gives $8\cdot5=40$ endpoints-with-a-diagonal, but each diagonal is counted twice (once from each end), so the octagon has
\[
\dfrac{8\cdot5}{2}=\boxed{20}
\]
diagonals. (Equivalently, $\dbinom82-8=28-8=20$: all pairs of vertices minus the $8$ sides.)
