Divide scores by $1.5$. A score becomes $4c+b$ with $c,b\ge 0$ integers and $c+b\le 25$. For a fixed $c$, the possible integers form $[4c,25+3c]$. The intervals for $c=0,\ldots,23$ overlap or touch and cover $0$ through $94$. For $c=24$, they give $96,97$; for $c=25$, they give $100$. Thus there are $95+2+1=\boxed{98}$ different scores. Multiplying back by $1.5$ preserves the count.
