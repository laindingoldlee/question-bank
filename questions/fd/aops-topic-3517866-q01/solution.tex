Choose distinct $B,C$ on $l$ and distinct $B^{\prime},C^{\prime}$ on $m$ so that $T=BB^{\prime}\cap CC^{\prime}$ is finite. Choose $D$ on $AB$ in a generic position, and set $D^{\prime}=TD\cap AB^{\prime}$. Finally set $V=CD\cap C^{\prime}D^{\prime}$. Draw $AV$. To prove it is parallel to the given lines, apply Desargues' theorem to triangles $BCD$ and $B^{\prime}C^{\prime}D^{\prime}$. Their corresponding-vertex lines meet at $T$. Hence the intersections of corresponding side lines are collinear. Two are $BD\cap B^{\prime}D^{\prime}=A$ and $CD\cap C^{\prime}D^{\prime}=V$; the third is the common point at infinity of the parallel lines $BC$ and $B^{\prime}C^{\prime}$. Therefore $AV$ has their direction, as required. The construction uses only joins and finite intersections; the point at infinity occurs in the proof, not as a construction operation. Generic auxiliary choices avoid coincident lines and infinite intersections other than the known parallel pair.
