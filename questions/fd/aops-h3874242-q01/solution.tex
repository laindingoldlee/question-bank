Put $M=10^{10}+1$, an odd number. If $M\mid n^2+1$, then $M\mid(M-n)^2+1$ as well. The map $n\mapsto M-n$ stays within $\{1,\ldots,M-1\}$ and is its own inverse. It has no fixed point, because $n=M-n$ would require $2n=M$, impossible for odd $M$. Therefore the qualifying numbers split into disjoint pairs, and their number is even.
