Imagine that each ant carries a flag, and when two ants meet they turn around but swap flags. Then each flag moves at constant speed in a fixed direction: before the meeting it moves with one ant, and after the meeting it continues in the same direction with the other ant. So the set of positions of the ants at any time is exactly the set of positions of $100$ flags, each moving straight along the stick without ever turning.

A flag moving in a fixed direction reaches the end of the stick it is heading for after at most the time needed to cross the whole stick, which is $2$ minutes. A flag leaves the stick exactly when an ant falls off at that point. Therefore after $2$ minutes there are no flags, and hence no ants, left on the stick.

The bound $2$ minutes cannot be improved: if one ant starts at the left end facing right (and the others anywhere), that ant's flag needs the full $2$ minutes, so some ant is still on the stick until the $2$-minute mark.

So all the ants are dead after at most $\boxed{2\text{ minutes}}$, and this time can be needed.
