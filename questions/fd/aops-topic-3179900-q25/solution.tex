Put $B=(0,0),C=(2\sqrt3,0),A=(\sqrt3,3\sqrt5)$. Since $OT\perp AB$ and $C,T,O$ are collinear, $T$ is the perpendicular projection of $C$ onto $AB$, giving $T=(\dfrac{\sqrt3}{8},\dfrac{3\sqrt5}{8})$ and $CT=\dfrac{3\sqrt5}{2}$. The centre lies beyond $T$ away from $C$, so write $O=T+\lambda(T-C)$ with $\lambda>0$. Its radius is $OT=\lambda\dfrac{3\sqrt5}{2}$, and tangency to the x-axis makes the same radius equal its y-coordinate $(1+\lambda)\dfrac{3\sqrt5}{8}$. Thus $4\lambda=1+\lambda$, giving $\lambda=\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $r=\dfrac{\sqrt5}{2}$. The centre is $(-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2},\dfrac{\sqrt5}{2})$, verifying that the tangency point on the axis lies beyond $B$. Therefore $r^2=\dfrac{5}{4}$ and $\boxed{a+b=9}$.
