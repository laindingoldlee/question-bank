Edgar is in lane $1$ or $4$. If Edgar is in lane $1$, Omar must be in lane $5$. Miguel cannot be in lane $4$ because the remaining Mario would then have to occupy lane $2$ and Beto lane $3$, adjacent to Mario; Miguel in lane $2$ forces Mario to lane $4$ and Beto to lane $3$, again impossible; Miguel in lane $3$ leaves no nonadjacent lane for Mario. Hence Edgar is in lane $4$. Omar cannot be in lane $5$, because then Miguel in lane $2$ or $3$ would force Beto adjacent to Edgar or Mario. Thus Omar is in lane $1$. If Miguel were in lane $2$, Mario would have to be in lane $5$, leaving Beto in lane $3$ adjacent to Edgar. Consequently Miguel is in lane $3$, Mario in lane $5$, and Beto in lane $2$. The order is $\boxed{\text{Omar, Beto, Miguel, Edgar, Mario}}$, which satisfies every condition.
