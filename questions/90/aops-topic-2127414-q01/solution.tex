A square with diagonal $4\sqrt2$ has side $4$, so the inscribed circle has radius $2$ and area $4\pi$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair O=(0,0);
pair T1=2*dir(90), T2=2*dir(210), T3=2*dir(330);
fill(circle(O,2),paleblue);
fill(T1--T2--T3--cycle,white);
draw((-2,-2)--(2,-2)--(2,2)--(-2,2)--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw((-2,-2)--(2,2),gray+dashed);
draw(circle(O,2),linewidth(0.9));
draw(T1--T2--T3--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(O--T3,red+linewidth(0.8));
draw(T1--(0,-1),dotted);
dot(O); dot(T1); dot(T2); dot(T3);
label("$O$",O,W); label("$2$",(O+T3)/2,N,red);
label("$4$",(0,-2),S);
label("$4\sqrt{2}$",(2,2),NE,gray);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The centre $O$ of the circle is also the centroid of the equilateral triangle, and the centroid lies $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of the way down each median. So the radius $2$ is $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of the height of the triangle, and the height is $3$. The side of an equilateral triangle with height $3$ is $\dfrac{2\cdot3}{\sqrt3}=2\sqrt3$, so the triangle has area
\[\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot2\sqrt3\cdot3=3\sqrt3 .\]
The required area (shaded) is
\[\boxed{4\pi-3\sqrt3}.\]
