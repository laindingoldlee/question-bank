The discriminant is $8^2-4\cdot10\cdot(-15)=64+600=664$, which is not a perfect square ($25^2=625<664<676=26^2$). A factorisation $(px+q)(rx+t)$ with integers would give rational roots, and then the discriminant would be a perfect square; so there is no integer factorisation.

Over the reals, complete the square:
\[
10\left(10x^2+8x-15\right)=100x^2+80x-150=(10x+4)^2-166 .
\]
Hence
\[
10x^2+8x-15=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{10}\left(10x+4-\sqrt{166}\right)\left(10x+4+\sqrt{166}\right)},
\]
with roots $x=\dfrac{-4\pm\sqrt{166}}{10}$. One factor is, for example, $10x+4-\sqrt{166}$ (or $x-\dfrac{-4+\sqrt{166}}{10}$).
