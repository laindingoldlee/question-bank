The replacement rule is a one-to-one map of $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$ onto itself. So each die's new number is again uniformly distributed on $\{1,\dots,6\}$, and the three new numbers are independent: the new numbers behave exactly like three fair dice. Hence we only need the probability that three dice have sum $15$.

The ordered triples with sum $15$ are the permutations of $(6,6,3)$ ($3$), $(6,5,4)$ ($6$) and $(5,5,5)$ ($1$): $10$ of the $216$ outcomes. The probability is
\[\dfrac{10}{216}=\boxed{\dfrac{5}{108}}.\]
