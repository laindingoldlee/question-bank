There are $2^4=16$ equally likely outcomes.
\begin{itemize}
\item If the quarter is heads, we already have $25$ cents: the other three coins can be anything, $2^3=8$ outcomes.
\item If the quarter is tails, the most the other coins can give is $1+5+10=16$ cents, and we need at least $15$. The penny is worth only $1$ cent, so both the nickel and the dime must be heads ($15$ cents), and the penny can be anything: $2$ outcomes.
\end{itemize}
So $8+2=10$ outcomes work, and the probability is $\dfrac{10}{16}=\boxed{\dfrac{5}{8}}$. (The two cases are disjoint, so their counts are added.)
