Compare the two sides for small $n$:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
n&1&2&3&4\\\hline
2^n&2&4&8&16\\
n!&1&2&6&24
\end{array}
\]
So $2^n>n!$ for $n=1,2,3$ but not for $n=4$.

For $n\ge4$ the inequality stays false: if $n!\ge2^n$ for some $n\ge4$, then $(n+1)!=(n+1)\,n!\ge 5\cdot2^n>2^{n+1}$. By induction $n!>2^n$ for every $n\ge4$. Hence the largest such $n$ is $\boxed{3}$.
