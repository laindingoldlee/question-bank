By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality $\left(a_1b_1+a_2b_2\right)^2\le\left(a_1^2+a_2^2\right)\left(b_1^2+b_2^2\right)$ with $a_1=x$, $a_2=y$, $b_1=\dfrac1{\sqrt{1+y^2}}$, $b_2=\dfrac1{\sqrt{1+x^2}}$,
\[\left(\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{y^2+1}}+\dfrac{y}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}\right)^2\le\left(x^2+y^2\right)\left(\dfrac1{1+y^2}+\dfrac1{1+x^2}\right).\]
Put $u=x^2$ and $v=y^2$, both in $[0,1]$. It suffices to show that the right side is at most $2$:
\[(u+v)\cdot\dfrac{2+u+v}{(1+u)(1+v)}\le2\iff2(u+v)+(u+v)^2\le2+2(u+v)+2uv\iff u^2+v^2\le2,\]
which holds because $u,v\le1$. Taking square roots (the left side of the original inequality is nonnegative) gives the result. $\blacksquare$

Equality requires $u^2+v^2=2$, i.e. $x=y=1$, and indeed $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}+\dfrac1{\sqrt2}=\sqrt2$.
