From the first sentence, $1.5$ hens lay $1.5$ eggs in $1.5$ days, so one hen lays $\dfrac{1.5}{1.5\cdot1.5}=\dfrac23$ egg per day. A hen that lays better by half lays $\dfrac32\cdot\dfrac23=1$ egg per day. Half a score and a half is $10.5$ eggs, and a week and a half is $10.5$ days. So the number of such hens needed is
\[\dfrac{10.5}{1\cdot10.5}=1=\dfrac12+\dfrac12.\]
The answer to ``how many and a half'' is $\boxed{\text{a half}}$: half a hen and a half, i.e. one hen.

(The solution quoted in the post gets $\dfrac{14}{15}$ because it says a better hen lays $7.5$ eggs in a week and a half; at $1$ egg per day it lays $10.5$.)
