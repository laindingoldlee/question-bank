Number the coins $1$ to $13$.

\textbf{Weighing 1:} $1,2,3,4$ against $5,6,7,8$.

\textbf{Case 1: balance.} The fake is among $9,\dots,13$, and $1,\dots,8$ are genuine.
\begin{itemize}
\item Weighing 2: $9,10,11$ against the genuine $1,2,3$.
\item If it balances, the fake is $12$ or $13$. Weighing 3: $12$ against $1$. If unbalanced, $12$ is fake; otherwise $13$ is fake.
\item If $9,10,11$ is heavier, the fake is one of them and heavy. Weighing 3: $9$ against $10$; the heavier is fake, and if they balance, $11$ is fake. The lighter case is symmetric.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Case 2: unbalanced}, say $1,2,3,4$ heavier (the other case is symmetric). Then either one of $1,2,3,4$ is heavy or one of $5,6,7,8$ is light, and $9,\dots,13$ are genuine.
\begin{itemize}
\item Weighing 2: $1,2,5$ against $3,6,9$.
\item Balance: the fake is $4$ (heavy), $7$ or $8$ (light). Weighing 3: $7$ against $8$; the lighter is fake, and if they balance, $4$ is fake.
\item Left heavier: the fake is $1$ or $2$ (heavy) or $6$ (light). Weighing 3: $1$ against $2$; the heavier is fake, and if they balance, $6$ is fake.
\item Right heavier: the fake is $3$ (heavy) or $5$ (light). Weighing 3: $3$ against the genuine $9$; if $3$ is heavier it is fake, and if they balance, $5$ is fake.
\end{itemize}
In every case the counterfeit is found in three weighings. $\blacksquare$
