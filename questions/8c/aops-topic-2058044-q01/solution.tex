Suppose $y$ is an integer. Then $y\mid x$ and $y\mid x+1$, so $y$ divides their difference $(x+1)-x=1$. Hence $y=\pm1$.

These values really occur: for $y=1$ or $y=-1$, both $\dfrac {x}{y}=\pm x$ and $\dfrac{x+1}{y}=\pm(x+1)$ are integers for every integer $x$. So the original claim that $y$ cannot be an integer is false; the correct statement is that $\boxed{y=\pm1}$ are the only integer possibilities. (Non-integer $y$ also occur, e.g.\ $y=\dfrac{1}{2}$.) $\blacksquare$
