\textbf{Upper bound.} A piece counted in $n$ is nonempty, so it has $g\ge1$ green and $3g\ge3$ red cells. It is connected, so some green cell of the piece shares a side with some red cell of the piece. That green cell borders the red region of the board. Put the green square at rows and columns $1$ to $5$; the green cells bordering red cells are those in row $5$ or column $5$, which is $9$ cells. Different pieces contain different such cells, so $n\le9$.

\textbf{Construction.} For each green cell $(5,j)$, $j=1,\dots,5$, take the piece consisting of it and the red cells $(6,j),(7,j),(8,j)$; for each green cell $(i,5)$, $i=1,\dots,4$, take it with $(i,6),(i,7),(i,8)$. These $9$ straight pieces are disjoint and each has $1$ green and $3$ red cells. Cut the rest of the board arbitrarily. So $n=9$ is possible, and the maximum is $\boxed{9}$.
