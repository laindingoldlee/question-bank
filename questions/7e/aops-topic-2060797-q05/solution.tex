\textbf{(a)} The second equation gives $y+z-2\sqrt{yz}=\left(\sqrt y-\sqrt z\right)^2=0$, so $y=z$. The other two equations become
\[\sqrt5\,\sqrt{xy}=x+y+1,\qquad\sqrt3\,\sqrt{xy}=x+y-1 .\]
Write $s=x+y$ and $g=\sqrt{xy}>0$. Subtracting, $\left(\sqrt5-\sqrt3\right)g=2$, so
\[g=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt5-\sqrt3}=\sqrt5+\sqrt3,\qquad xy=g^2=8+2\sqrt{15},\]
and then $s=\sqrt5\,g-1=5+\sqrt{15}-1=4+\sqrt{15}$.

\textbf{(b)} If $x$ and $y$ were real, then $(x-y)^2=s^2-4xy\ge0$. But
\[s^2-4xy=\left(31+8\sqrt{15}\right)-\left(32+8\sqrt{15}\right)=-1<0 .\]
So no real numbers $x,y$ (and hence no positive reals $x,y,z$) satisfy the system. Equivalently, AM-GM would require $s\ge2g$, but $s=4+\sqrt{15}\approx7.873<2g=2\sqrt5+2\sqrt3\approx7.936$. $\blacksquare$

(Formally, the values in (a) give $\dfrac{1}{x}+\dfrac{1}{y}=\dfrac{x+y}{xy}=\dfrac{4+\sqrt{15}}{2\left(4+\sqrt{15}\right)}=\dfrac{1}{2}$, the intended answer, but the only numbers with this sum and product are the non-real numbers $\dfrac{4+\sqrt{15}\pm i}{2}$.)
