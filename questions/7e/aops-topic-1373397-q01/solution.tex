\textbf{The error.} The factor $3$ was dropped: $3\sqrt[3]{x^2y^2z^2}$ is not equal to $\sqrt[3]{(xyz)^2}$. The inequality $x^2+y^2+z^2\ge1$ is true, but it is not sharp: the claimed equality case $x=y=z=1$ gives $x^2+y^2+z^2=3$, not $1$. A lower bound only gives the minimum if it is actually attained.

\textbf{Correct solution.} By AM-GM on the three positive numbers $x^2,y^2,z^2$,
\[
\dfrac{x^2+y^2+z^2}{3}\ge\sqrt[3]{x^2y^2z^2}=\sqrt[3]{(xyz)^2}=1,
\]
so $x^2+y^2+z^2\ge3$, with equality exactly when $x^2=y^2=z^2$, i.e.\ $x=y=z=1$, which satisfies $xyz=1$. Hence the minimum is $\boxed{3}$.
