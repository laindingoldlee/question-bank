Group the terms in pairs and use the difference of squares $k^2-(k-1)^2=\left(k-(k-1)\right)\left(k+(k-1)\right)=k+(k-1)$. For even $n$ the pairs are $(n,n-1),(n-2,n-3),\dots,(2,1)$, so
\[
\left(n^2-(n-1)^2\right)+\left((n-2)^2-(n-3)^2\right)+\cdots+\left(2^2-1^2\right)=n+(n-1)+(n-2)+(n-3)+\cdots+2+1=\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}.
\]
So the formula is correct for even $n$.

For odd $n$ the same pairing leaves the last term $+1^2$ alone:
\[
\left(n^2-(n-1)^2\right)+\cdots+\left(3^2-2^2\right)+1^2=n+(n-1)+\cdots+3+2+1=\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2},
\]
so the same formula holds for odd $n$ too (with the signs alternating from $+n^2$ down to $+1^2$). For example $n=4$: $16-9+4-1=10=\dfrac{4\cdot5}{2}$, and $n=5$: $25-16+9-4+1=15$.
