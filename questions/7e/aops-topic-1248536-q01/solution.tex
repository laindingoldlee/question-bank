Let $q=2^p-1$, a prime, which is odd. The divisors of $N=2^{p-1}q$ are $2^i$ and $2^iq$ for $0\le i\le p-1$, so the sum of divisors is
\[\left(1+2+\cdots+2^{p-1}\right)(1+q)=\left(2^p-1\right)\cdot2^p=2\cdot2^{p-1}\left(2^p-1\right)=2N.\]
Hence $N$ is perfect. $\blacksquare$ (Examples: $p=2,3,5,7$ give $6,28,496,8128$. Euler proved that every \emph{even} perfect number has this form; whether odd perfect numbers exist is unknown.)
