Let $n$ be a positive integer.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Prove that $n^x>0$ for every rational number $x$. (For an integer $q\ge1$, $n^{\dfrac1q}$ is the positive real number whose $q$th power is $n$; for integers $p$ and $q\ge1$, $n^{\dfrac pq}=\left(n^{\dfrac1q}\right)^p$, where a negative power means $t^{-s}=\dfrac1{t^s}$ and $t^0=1$.)
\item[(b)] For an irrational number $x$ and $n\ge2$, $n^x$ is defined as the least upper bound of the numbers $n^r$ with $r$ rational and $r<x$; and $1^x=1$. Prove that $n^x>0$ for every real number $x$.
\end{enumerate}
