\textbf{The formula.} Let $G_n=n\cdot3^{\,n-1}$. Then $G_1=1=F_1$ and $G_2=6\equiv1=F_2\pmod5$. Since both sequences are determined modulo $5$ by their first two terms and the recurrence, it is enough to show $G_{n+1}\equiv G_n+G_{n-1}\pmod5$ for $n\ge2$:
\[G_{n+1}-G_n-G_{n-1}=3^{\,n-2}\bigl(9(n+1)-3n-(n-1)\bigr)=3^{\,n-2}(5n+10)\equiv0\pmod5 .\]
By induction $F_n\equiv n\cdot3^{\,n-1}\pmod5$ for all $n$. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{Divisibility by $5$.} Since $5\nmid3^{\,n-1}$, $5\mid F_n\iff5\mid n$.

\textbf{Period.} $3^4=81\equiv1\pmod5$, so $3^{\,n-1}$ has period $4$ and $n$ has period $5$ modulo $5$; hence $n\cdot3^{\,n-1}$ repeats every $\operatorname{lcm}(4,5)=20$ steps. The remainders for $n=1,\ldots,20$ are
\[1,1,2,3,0,3,3,1,4,0,4,4,3,2,0,2,2,4,1,0,\]
and no smaller period occurs (the zeros sit at $n=5,10,15,20$, and the terms after them, $3,4,2,1$, first return to $1$ after the fourth zero).
