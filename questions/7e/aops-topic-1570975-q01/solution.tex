Classify the colourings by the number $k$ of red faces. The only thing that matters (up to rotation) is how the red faces sit relative to each other.
\begin{itemize}
\item $k=1$: one red face; all such cubes look alike: $1$ cube.
\item $k=2$: the two red faces are either opposite or adjacent (sharing an edge): $2$ cubes.
\item $k=3$: among three faces of a cube, either two are opposite (then the third is adjacent to both, a ``band'' of three faces), or no two are opposite (then the three faces meet at a common vertex): $2$ cubes.
\item $k=4$ and $k=5$: swapping the colours turns these into the cases $k=2$ and $k=1$: $2$ and $1$ cubes.
\end{itemize}
Different values of $k$ give different cubes, and within each value of $k$ the listed types are different (opposite faces stay opposite under rotation). Hence the number of cubes with at least one face of each colour is
\[
1+2+2+2+1=\boxed{8}.
\]
(With the two one-coloured cubes there are $10$ colourings in all; a computer check over the $24$ rotations of the cube confirms both counts.)
