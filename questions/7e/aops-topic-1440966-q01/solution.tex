The square is $24$ inches on a side, with area $24^2=576$ square inches, and each tile covers $6$ square inches, so at least $\dfrac{576}{6}=96$ tiles are needed. Since $24$ is divisible by both $2$ and $3$, $96$ tiles suffice: $12$ columns of $2$-inch-wide tiles, each column made of $8$ tiles $3$ inches tall.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
for(int i=0;i<=12;++i) draw((2i,0)--(2i,24),gray+0.4);
for(int j=0;j<=8;++j) draw((0,3j)--(24,3j),gray+0.4);
draw((0,0)--(24,0)--(24,24)--(0,24)--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
fill((0,0)--(2,0)--(2,3)--(0,3)--cycle,lightcyan); draw((0,0)--(2,0)--(2,3)--(0,3)--cycle,blue);
label("$24$ in",(12,0),S); label("$24$ in",(24,12),E);
label("$2$",(1,3),N,blue); label("$3$",(2,1.5),E,blue);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The answer is $\boxed{96}$ tiles, as posted.
