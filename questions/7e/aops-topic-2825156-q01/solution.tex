Since $7\cdot2=14\equiv1\pmod{13}$, $7^{-1}\equiv2$ and $x\equiv5\cdot2=10\pmod{13}$. Check: $7\cdot10=70=5\cdot13+5$.

A number $\equiv5\pmod{91}$ is $\equiv5\pmod{13}$, not $10$. So the two are $\boxed{\text{not equivalent}}$. (Division modulo $13$ means multiplying by an inverse; it has nothing to do with multiplying the modulus by $7$.)
