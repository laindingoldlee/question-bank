The $8$ equally likely gender patterns of three children are BBB, BBG, BGB, GBB, BGG, GBG, GGB, GGG. The information ``at least one boy'' rules out only GGG, leaving $7$ equally likely patterns, of which exactly one is BBB. Hence
\[
P(\text{all boys}\mid\text{at least one boy})=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{8}}{\dfrac{7}{8}}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{7}}.
\]
(The answer depends on how the information is obtained: if instead we learned that a particular child, say the eldest, is a boy, the probability would be $\dfrac{1}{4}$.)
