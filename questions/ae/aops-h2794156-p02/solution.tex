Use a Hamiltonian cycle: go down the first column, zigzag up and down the remaining columns through rows 2 to 8, then return along the top row. It visits all 64 cells once. Its longest run of cells strictly inside the board has at most 12 cells.

Let $S_i$ be the sum of the 32 entries starting at cyclic position $i$. Each $S_i$ is even, $S_{i+32}=-S_i$, and consecutive values differ by $0$ or $\pm2$, since one entry leaves and one enters. Passing from a value to its negative must encounter zero. Choose that 32-cell window and its complement. Each has sum zero and is edge-connected as a consecutive cycle segment. Both meet the board boundary, since 32 exceeds the longest interior run. Neither can have a hole: cells of the other piece inside a hole could not connect to that piece's boundary cells. This proves the claim.
