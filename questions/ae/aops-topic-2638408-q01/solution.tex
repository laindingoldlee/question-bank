\emph{One polynomial.} The Lagrange interpolation polynomial
\[L(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_i\prod_{j\ne i}\dfrac{x-j}{i-j}\]
satisfies $L(n)=a_n$ for $n=1,\dots,k$: for $x=n$, every product with $i\ne n$ contains the factor $n-n=0$, and the product with $i=n$ equals $1$.

\emph{Infinitely many.} For each real $c$ let
\[P_c(x)=L(x)+c(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-k).\]
The added term vanishes at $x=1,\dots,k$, so $P_c(n)=a_n$ there. At $x=k+1$,
\[P_c(k+1)=L(k+1)+c\cdot k!,\]
so different values of $c$ give different values at $k+1$. Hence there are infinitely many different rules. $\blacksquare$
