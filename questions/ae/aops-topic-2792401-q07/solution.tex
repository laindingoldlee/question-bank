No. Along the path 1,2,\ldots,121, the internal entries between $k^2$ and $(k+1)^2$ number $2k$, for $k=1,\ldots,10$. None lies in the square column, so each such segment stays entirely on one side. At every intermediate square, entry and exit must be horizontal: vertical neighbours in that column are other squares and cannot be consecutive integers. Entry and exit cannot use the same neighbouring cell. Thus the segments alternate sides. One side contains $2(1+3+5+7+9)=50$ cells and the other $2(2+4+6+8+10)=60$. But either side consists of whole 11-cell columns, so its cell count is divisible by 11; neither 50 nor 60 is. Contradiction.
