\textbf{Existence.} Take $a_n=3n-2$, that is, $1,4,7,10,\dots$. This is a geometric series with first term $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and ratio $\dfrac{1}{8}$:
\[
\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{16}+\dfrac{1}{128}+\cdots=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2}}{1-\dfrac{1}{8}}=\dfrac{4}{7}.
\]

\textbf{Uniqueness.} First, the sequence cannot be finite: a finite sum of powers of $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is a fraction whose denominator is a power of $2$, but $\dfrac{4}{7}$ in lowest terms has denominator $7$.

\emph{Lemma.} If $x=\sum_{i\ge1}2^{-c_i}$ for an infinite increasing sequence of positive integers $c_1<c_2<\cdots$, then $2^{-c_1}<x\le2^{1-c_1}$. Indeed, the sum is more than its first term, and it is at most $\sum_{k\ge c_1}2^{-k}=2^{1-c_1}$.

Now apply the lemma to $x=\dfrac{4}{7}$: $2^{-a_1}<\dfrac{4}{7}\le2^{1-a_1}$. For $a_1\ge2$ the right side is at most $\dfrac{1}{2}<\dfrac{4}{7}$, so $a_1=1$. Then
\[
\sum_{i\ge2}2^{-a_i}=\dfrac{4}{7}-\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{1}{14},
\]
and every $a_i$ with $i\ge2$ satisfies $2^{-a_i}\le\dfrac{1}{14}<\dfrac{1}{8}$, so $a_i\ge4$. Put $b_i=a_{i+1}-3$, a positive increasing sequence. Multiplying by $8$,
\[
\sum_{i\ge1}2^{-b_i}=\dfrac{8}{14}=\dfrac{4}{7} .
\]
So $(b_i)$ satisfies the same condition as $(a_i)$. By the argument above $b_1=1$, that is, $a_2=4$; repeating (formally, by induction on $n$, applied to the shifted sequence $a_{i+n}-3n$) gives $a_{n+1}=a_n+3$ for all $n$. Hence $a_n=3n-2$ is the only sequence. $\blacksquare$

In other words, the binary expansion of $\dfrac{4}{7}$ is $0.100100100\ldots_2$, and a number that is not of the form $\dfrac{m}{2^k}$ has only one binary expansion.
