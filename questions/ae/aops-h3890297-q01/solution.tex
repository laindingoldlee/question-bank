If the parallel bases have lengths $a,b$ and the legs have lengths $c,d$, translating one leg gives a triangle with sides $|a-b|,c,d$. Thus nondegeneracy requires $|c-d|<|a-b|<c+d$. Of the six possible base pairs, only $(2,5)$ satisfies this: $(2,3),(2,4),(3,5),(4,5)$ give equality at the lower bound, while $(3,4)$ gives $1<3$ and is impossible.

Put the longer base on a horizontal axis from 0 to 5, and let the shorter base extend from $x$ to $x+2$ at height $h$, with left leg 3 and right leg 4. Then
\[x^2+h^2=9,\qquad (3-x)^2+h^2=16.\]
Subtracting gives $9-6x=7$, so $x=\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $h=\sqrt{9-\dfrac{1}{9}}=\dfrac{4\sqrt5}{3}$. The area is
\[\dfrac{2+5}{2}h=\boxed{\dfrac{14\sqrt5}{3}}.\]
\begin{asy}
texpreamble("\usepackage{amsmath,amssymb}");
size(240);
defaultpen(fontsize(9pt));
real h=4*sqrt(5)/3;pair A=(0,0),B=(5,0),C=(7/3,h),D=(1/3,h);draw(A--B--C--D--cycle);draw(D--(1/3,0),blue+dashed);label("$5$",midpoint(A--B),(0,-1));label("$2$",midpoint(C--D),(0,1));label("$3$",midpoint(A--D),(-1,0));label("$4$",midpoint(B--C),(1,0));label("$h$",(1/3,h/2),(1,0));
\end{asy}
