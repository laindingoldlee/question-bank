\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Ten distinguishable balls are put into three indistinguishable boxes so that one box gets $5$ balls, one gets $3$ and one gets $2$. In how many ways can this be done?
\item[(b)] Five distinguishable balls are put into three indistinguishable boxes so that two boxes get $2$ balls each and one box gets $1$ ball. In how many ways can this be done? Explain why the division needed here differs from part (a).
\end{enumerate}
