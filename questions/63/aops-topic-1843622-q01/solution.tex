Measure time in hours after midnight and position as the fraction of the route covered from New York. Our train is on the track for $13\le t\le23$, at position $\dfrac{t-13}{10}$. A train that leaves Atlanta at time $s$ is at position $1-\dfrac{t-s}{10}$ for $s\le t\le s+10$. They are at the same place when
\[
\dfrac{t-13}{10}=1-\dfrac{t-s}{10}\iff t=\dfrac{s+23}{2}.
\]
This meeting happens while both trains are on the track exactly when $13\le\dfrac{s+23}{2}\le23$ (and then automatically $s\le t\le s+10$), i.e.\ when $3\le s\le23$: the train must leave Atlanta between 3:00 a.m.\ and 11:00 p.m.

The Atlanta departures are at 1:10, 1:40, 2:10, 2:40, 3:10, \dots. Those from 3:00 a.m.\ to 11:00 p.m.\ are 3:10 a.m., 3:40 a.m., \dots, 10:40 p.m. (no departure is exactly at 3:00 a.m.\ or 11:00 p.m., so no train is met exactly at a station). From 3:10 a.m.\ to 10:40 p.m.\ is $19.5$ hours, so the number of these departures is
\[
\dfrac{19.5}{0.5}+1=\boxed{40}.
\]
