Partition the square into 50 vertical strips of width $\dfrac{1}{50}$, assigning boundary points consistently to one adjacent strip. Some strip contains at least 3 points. Any triangle inside a rectangle of width $w$ and height 1 has area at most $\dfrac{w}{2}$: for fixed two vertices, moving the third to a rectangle corner cannot decrease the maximum possible area; repeat for the other vertices, reducing to three corners. Thus the three points in that strip form a triangle of area at most $\dfrac{1}{100}$.
