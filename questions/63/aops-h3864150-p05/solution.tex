Putting $y=0$ in the first equation gives $f(0)=f(x)f(0)$. If $f(0)\ne0$, then $f(x)=1$ for every $x$. If $f(0)=0$, put $y=x$ in the second equation to obtain $f(3x^2)=0$. In particular $f(1)=0$. Then $f(x)=f(x)f(1)=0$ for every $x$. Both constant functions satisfy the original equations. Thus $\boxed{f\equiv0\text{ or }f\equiv1}$.
