For $n=1,\dots,5$ the numbers $3,7,31,211,2311$ are all prime, but for $n=6$:
\[
2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot11\cdot13+1=30031=59\cdot509,
\]
which is composite. So $n=6$ is a counterexample, and the conjecture is false. $\blacksquare$

(What \emph{is} true, from Euclid's proof that there are infinitely many primes, is that $p_n\#+1$ has no prime factor among the first $n$ primes, since it leaves remainder $1$ on division by each of them. Here its prime factors $59$ and $509$ are larger than $13$.)
