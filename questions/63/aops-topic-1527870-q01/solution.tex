First give every person $2$ coins. That uses $8$ coins, and the remaining $12$ coins can be distributed in any way (each person may get zero more). Distributing $12$ identical coins among $4$ people is stars and bars with $12$ stars and $3$ bars:
\[
\dbinom{12+3}{3}=\dbinom{15}{3}=\boxed{455}.
\]
With $16$ stars the student removed only one coin per person (giving $\dbinom{19}{3}$ ways, which allows people with just $1$ coin); with \emph{at least $2$} each, $2\cdot4=8$ coins must be set aside, leaving $12$ stars. (Equivalently, with $b_i=a_i-1\ge1$ the positive version would use $\dbinom{16-1}{3}=\dbinom{15}{3}$, the same answer.)
