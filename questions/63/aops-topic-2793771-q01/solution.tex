Put $m=k-j-1\ge1$. For fixed $m$, the pairs are $k=j+m+1$ with $j\ge1$ and $k\le n$, i.e. $1\le j\le n-m-1$: there are $n-1-m$ of them. So the sum is
\[\sum_{m=1}^{n-2}(n-1-m)m^3=(n-1)\sum_{m=1}^{n-2}m^3-\sum_{m=1}^{n-2}m^4.\]
With $\sum_{m=1}^{N}m^3=\dfrac{N^2(N+1)^2}4$ and $\sum_{m=1}^Nm^4=\dfrac{N(N+1)(2N+1)(3N^2+3N-1)}{30}$ at $N=n-2$, this simplifies to
\[\boxed{\dfrac{n(n-1)(n-2)(3n^2-6n+1)}{60}}.\]
(Check: $n=6$ gives $\dfrac{6\cdot5\cdot4\cdot73}{60}=146$, matching direct summation.)
