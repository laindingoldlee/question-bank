Let $x=\sqrt2-\sqrt5$. Then $x^2=7-2\sqrt{10}$, so $\left(x^2-7\right)^2=40$, i.e.
\[\boxed{x^4-14x^2+9}=0.\]
\emph{Uniqueness.} This polynomial has no rational roots (the candidates $\pm1,\pm3,\pm9$ fail), and it is not a product of two monic integer quadratics: $\left(x^2+px+q\right)\left(x^2-px+r\right)$ would need $p(r-q)=0$, $qr=9$ and $q+r-p^2=-14$; $p=0$ gives $q+r=-14$, $qr=9$, with non-square discriminant $196-36=160$, and $q=r=\pm3$ gives $p^2=20$ or $8$. So it is irreducible over the rationals. If another monic integer quartic had the root $\sqrt2-\sqrt5$, the difference of the two would be a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $3$ with this root, which is impossible for a root of an irreducible quartic.
