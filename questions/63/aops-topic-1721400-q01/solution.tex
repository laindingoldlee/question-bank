\textbf{(a)} $4!+0!+5!+8!+5!=24+1+120+40320+120=40585$. \checkmark

\textbf{(b)} Suppose $N$ has $d$ digits. Each digit's factorial is at most $9!=362880$, so the sum of the digit factorials is at most $362880d$, while $N\ge10^{d-1}$. For $d=8$: $8\cdot362880=2903040<10^7$. For larger $d$ the gap only widens: going from $d$ to $d+1$ multiplies $10^{d-1}$ by $10$ but increases $362880d$ by a factor $\dfrac{d+1}{d}\le\dfrac{9}{8}$. So for every $d\ge8$, $362880d<10^{d-1}\le N$, and $N$ cannot equal its digit-factorial sum. Hence such numbers have at most $7$ digits. $\blacksquare$

(In fact one can sharpen this: a $7$-digit $N$ is at most $7\cdot9!=2540160$. A finite check, best done by computer, shows that the only such numbers are $1,2,145$ and $40585$.)
