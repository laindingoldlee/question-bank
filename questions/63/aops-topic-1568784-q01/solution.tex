Let $M$ and $E$ be the sets of students who like Math and English, so $|M|=27$, $|E|=25$, and let $x=|M\cap E|$.

\textbf{(a)} The students who like neither are those outside $M\cup E$, and $|M\cup E|\ge|M|=27$. So at most $40-27=13$ like neither. This happens when every English lover also likes Math ($E\subseteq M$, possible since $25\le27$): then $|M\cup E|=27$. The answer is $\boxed{13}$.

\textbf{(b)} By inclusion-exclusion, $|M\cup E|=27+25-x\le40$, so $x\ge12$: at least $12$ students like both. Hence at most $40-12=28$ students do not like both. This happens when every student likes at least one subject and exactly $12$ like both ($15$ like only Math, $13$ only English). The answer is $\boxed{28}$.
