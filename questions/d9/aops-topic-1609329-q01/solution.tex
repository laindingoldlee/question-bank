For a fixed base $x$ with $0<x<1$, the function $t\mapsto x^t$ is strictly decreasing: a larger exponent gives a smaller value.

\textbf{$y>x$.} Since $x<1$, we have $x^x>x^1$, i.e.\ $y>x$.

\textbf{$y<1$.} Since $x>0$, we have $x^x<x^0=1$.

\textbf{$x<z<y$.} The exponent of $z$ is $y=x^x$, and we have just shown $x<y<1$. Because $t\mapsto x^t$ is decreasing, $x<y$ gives $x^y<x^x$, i.e.\ $z<y$, and $y<1$ gives $x^y>x^1$, i.e.\ $z>x$.

Therefore
\[
\boxed{x<z<y}.
\]
(For example, with $x=0.95$: $y\approx0.9524$ and $z\approx0.9523$, so the three numbers are close together, but the order is forced by the argument above, which in fact works for every $0<x<1$.)
