If $p$ ends in $2$ or $7$, then $p\equiv2\pmod5$ and $p^2+p-1\equiv4+2-1\equiv0\pmod5$, so $p^2+p-1$ is a multiple of $5$ (and larger than $5$ for $p\ge7$), which explains the exclusion.

For the other $p$: $p=3,4,5,6,8,9,10,11,13$ give $11,19,29,41,71,89,109,131,181$, all prime, but $p=\boxed{14}$ gives $14^2+14-1=209=11\cdot19$. So the conjecture is false. (Even for prime $p$ it fails: $p=23$ gives $551=19\cdot29$.)
