\textbf{(a)} A square is $0$ or $1$ modulo $4$: $(2t)^2=4t^2$ and $(2t+1)^2=4(t^2+t)+1$. So $x^2+y^2$ is $0$, $1$ or $2$ modulo $4$, never $3$. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{(b)} We need $|x|,|y|\le8$, since $9^2>65$. Checking $x^2=0,1,4,\dots,64$, the values $65-x^2$ that are squares are $65-1=64$, $65-16=49$, $65-49=16$ and $65-64=1$. So $\{|x|,|y|\}=\{1,8\}$ or $\{4,7\}$, and the solutions are
\[
(\pm1,\pm8),\ (\pm8,\pm1),\ (\pm4,\pm7),\ (\pm7,\pm4),
\]
with all sign choices: $\boxed{16}$ ordered pairs. (The two essentially different representations come from $65=5\cdot13$, with $5=1^2+2^2$ and $13=2^2+3^2$; for example $(1\cdot2+2\cdot3)^2+(1\cdot3-2\cdot2)^2=64+1$.)
