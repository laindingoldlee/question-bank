Put $t=x-c$, $K=(c-a)(c-b)$, and $H=2c-a-b$. Polynomial division gives
\[
f(x)=H+t+\dfrac{K}{t}.
\]
(i) Here $K<0$. For any prescribed real value $y$, the equation $y=H+t+\dfrac{K}{t}$ is
$t^2-(y-H)t+K=0$. Its discriminant $(y-H)^2-4K$ is positive, and neither root is zero because their product is $K\ne0$. Thus every real $y$ occurs, and the range is $\boxed{\mathbb R}$.

(ii) Here $K>0$. A nonzero real solution exists exactly when $(y-H)^2-4K\ge0$. Equality is allowed and gives $t=\pm\sqrt K$. Therefore the range is
\[
\boxed{(-\infty,H-2\sqrt K]\ \cup\ [H+2\sqrt K,\infty)},
\quad H=2c-a-b,\quad K=(c-a)(c-b).
\]
