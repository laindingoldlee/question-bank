Use the complement: the committee fails only if it is all boys or all girls. So the probability is
\[1-\dfrac{2\dbinom{10}{4}}{\dbinom{20}{4}}=1-\dfrac{2\cdot210}{4845}=\dfrac{4425}{4845}=\boxed{\dfrac{295}{323}} .\]

\textbf{The error.} The student's count $\dbinom{10}1\dbinom{10}1\dbinom{18}2=100\cdot153=15300$ first picks a ``designated'' boy and a ``designated'' girl and then any $2$ others. A committee is then counted once for every choice of designated boy and girl among its members: a committee with $2$ boys and $2$ girls is counted $2\cdot2=4$ times, and one with $3$ boys and $1$ girl (or $1$ and $3$) is counted $3$ times. The true count is
\[\dbinom{10}{3}\dbinom{10}{1}+\dbinom{10}{2}\dbinom{10}{2}+\dbinom{10}{1}\dbinom{10}{3}=1200+2025+1200=4425,\]
and indeed $3\cdot1200+4\cdot2025+3\cdot1200=15300$. Since $15300>4845=\dbinom{20}{4}$, the student's ratio exceeds $1$.
