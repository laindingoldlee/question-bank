Pair the first and last factors and the middle two. Put $u=x^2-5x+4$, so the equation becomes $u(u+2)=120$, or $(u-10)(u+12)=0$.

If $u=10$, then $x^2-5x-6=(x-6)(x+1)=0$, giving $x=6,-1$. If $u=-12$, then $x^2-5x+16=0$, whose discriminant is $25-64<0$. Therefore the real solutions are $\boxed{-1,6}$.
