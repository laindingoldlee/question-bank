Here $\sin\theta-\cos\theta=-\dfrac{1}{2}$. Squaring gives $\sin\theta\cos\theta=\dfrac{3}{8}$, and the nonnegative sum satisfies
\[
\sin\theta+\cos\theta=\sqrt{1+2(\dfrac{3}{8})}=\dfrac{\sqrt7}{2}.
\]
Adding and subtracting yields
\[
\boxed{\sin\theta=\dfrac{\sqrt7-1}{4},\quad
\cos\theta=\dfrac{\sqrt7+1}{4},\quad
\tan\theta=\dfrac{4-\sqrt7}{3}}.
\]
In general, $\sin\theta-\cos\theta=\sqrt2\sin(\theta-\dfrac{\pi}{4})$ has range $[-\sqrt2,\sqrt2]$. Thus a real solution exists exactly when
\[
\boxed{|s-d|\ge\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}}.
\]
