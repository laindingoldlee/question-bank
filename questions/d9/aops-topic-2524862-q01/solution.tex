\textbf{(a)} $X$ compares the \emph{change} $b-a$ with the starting value, so $X$ is the percentage change (positive for an increase, negative for a decrease). $Y$ compares the new value itself with the starting value: $b$ is $Y$ of $a$. Since
\[\dfrac ba=\dfrac{a+(b-a)}{a}=1+\dfrac{b-a}{a},\]
we get $Y=100\%+X$.

\textbf{(b)} From $80$ to $100$: $X=\dfrac{20}{80}\cdot100\%=25\%$ (an increase of $25\%$) and $Y=\dfrac{100}{80}\cdot100\%=125\%$.

From $80$ to $60$: $X=\dfrac{-20}{80}\cdot100\%=-25\%$ (a decrease of $25\%$) and $Y=75\%$.
\[\boxed{X=25\%,\ Y=125\%;\qquad X=-25\%,\ Y=75\%}\]
