\begin{itemize}
\item By (9) and (14), house $1$ is the Norwegian's and house $2$ is blue. By (8), house $3$ drinks milk.
\item Green is immediately left of white (4), and the green owner drinks coffee (5), so green is not house $3$. Houses $1,2$ are ruled out (house $2$ is blue, and house $1$ would force house $2$ white). So green and white are houses $4$ and $5$.
\item Red is the Brit's (1), not house $1$, so red is house $3$; then house $1$ is yellow and smokes Dunhill (7). By (11) house $2$ keeps horses.
\item House $1$'s drink is not tea (the Dane's), coffee, milk, or beer (beer goes with BlueMaster, but house $1$ smokes Dunhill), so it is water. By (15) the Blends smoker lives in house $2$.
\item House $2$ drinks tea or beer. If beer, house $2$ would smoke BlueMaster, contradicting Blends. So house $2$ drinks tea and is the Dane's, and house $5$ drinks beer and smokes BlueMaster.
\item The German smokes Prince (13); the remaining brands for houses $3,4$ are Pall Mall and Prince, and house $3$ is the Brit's, so house $4$ is the German's (Prince) and house $3$ smokes Pall Mall and keeps birds (6). House $5$ is the Swede's, with dogs (2).
\item By (10), cats are next to house $2$, so in house $1$. The remaining pet, fish, is in house $4$.
\end{itemize}
Summary (houses $1$ to $5$): yellow, blue, red, green, white; Norwegian, Dane, Brit, German, Swede; water, tea, milk, coffee, beer; Dunhill, Blends, Pall Mall, Prince, BlueMaster; cats, horses, birds, fish, dogs.

$\boxed{\text{The German keeps the fish.}}$ A computer search confirms that this is the unique solution.
