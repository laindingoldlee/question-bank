\textbf{At most $12$ sides.} Look at a vertex $V$ of the polygon. Its interior angle is less than $180^\circ$, so $V$ cannot lie inside a side of one of the triangles (that would need an angle of at least $180^\circ$ at $V$); hence every triangle that touches $V$ has a corner at $V$, and the angle of the polygon at $V$ is a sum of corner angles of triangles, each $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$ or $90^\circ$. So every angle of the polygon is a multiple of $30^\circ$ and less than $180^\circ$, hence at most $150^\circ$. Each exterior angle is then at least $30^\circ$, and since the exterior angles of a convex polygon add up to $360^\circ$, the polygon has at most $\dfrac{360}{30}=12$ sides.

\textbf{$12$ sides are possible.} Start with a regular hexagon of side $1$. On each side build outwards a rectangle of size $1\times\sqrt3$, and fill each of the six gaps between neighbouring rectangles with an equilateral triangle of side $\sqrt3$ (the gap angle is $360^\circ-120^\circ-90^\circ-90^\circ=60^\circ$, between two edges of length $\sqrt3$). The outline is a convex $12$-gon with every angle $90^\circ+60^\circ=150^\circ$. Now cut:
\begin{itemize}
\item the hexagon into $6$ equilateral triangles from its centre, and each of these by an altitude into two $30^\circ$-$60^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangles;
\item each $1\times\sqrt3$ rectangle by a diagonal into two right triangles with legs $1$ and $\sqrt3$, which are $30^\circ$-$60^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangles;
\item each equilateral gap triangle by an altitude.
\end{itemize}
