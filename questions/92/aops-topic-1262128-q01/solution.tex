A leg from a point at distance $d$ on one axis to a point at distance $\dfrac {d}{2}$ on the next axis has length $\sqrt{d^2+\dfrac{d^2}{4}}=\dfrac{\sqrt5}{2}d$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair[] P={(16,0),(0,8),(-4,0),(0,-2),(1,0),(0,0.5),(-0.25,0)};
draw((-6,0)--(17,0),gray,Arrow); draw((0,-4)--(0,9.5),gray,Arrow);
for(int i=0;i<6;++i) draw(P[i]--P[i+1],red);
dot(P[0]);dot(P[1]);dot(P[2]);dot(P[3]); dot((0,0));
label("$(16,0)$",P[0],S); label("$(0,8)$",P[1],NE); label("$(-4,0)$",P[2],S); label("$(0,-2)$",P[3],SW);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

The starting distances are $16,8,4,2,\dots$, which sum to $32$. So the total path length is $\dfrac{\sqrt5}{2}\cdot32=16\sqrt5$, and at $2$ units per minute it takes $\boxed{8\sqrt5}\approx17.9$ minutes.
