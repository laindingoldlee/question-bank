Let the enclosing radius be $R$. Put the centres of the radius-six and radius-seven circles at $I=(0,0)$ and $J=(13,0)$. The third centre is $K=\left(\dfrac{37}{13},\dfrac{36\sqrt{287}}{13}\right)$, since $IK=47$, $JK=48$. If the enclosing centre is $(u,v)$, the squared-distance equations are $u^2+v^2=(R-6)^2$, $(u-13)^2+v^2=(R-7)^2$, and $\left(u-\dfrac{37}{13}\right)^2+\left(v-\dfrac{36\sqrt{287}}{13}\right)^2=(R-41)^2$. Subtracting gives $u=\dfrac{R+78}{13}$ and $v=\dfrac{\sqrt{287}(2939R+22386)}{67158}$. Substitution yields $41327R^2-1980300R-2965284=0$. Its unique positive root is $R=\dfrac{990150+61992\sqrt{287}}{41327}>41$. For any two inner radii $r_i,r_j$, the angle $\theta$ between their centre rays satisfies $\sin^2\dfrac{\theta}{2}=\dfrac{r_ir_j}{(R-r_i)(R-r_j)}$, by applying the cosine law to the two centres and the enclosing centre. Their tangency points therefore have chord length $2R\sqrt{\dfrac{r_ir_j}{(R-r_i)(R-r_j)}}$. Multiplying the three chord lengths and using triangle area $\dfrac{abc}{4R}$ gives the exact answer $\boxed{\dfrac{3444R^2}{(R-6)(R-7)(R-41)}}$, with $R$ as above. Numerically this is about $545.699921$ square units.
