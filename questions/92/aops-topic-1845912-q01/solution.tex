Write $f(x)=c_nx^n+\cdots+c_1x+c_0$ with integers $c_i$. For integers $x$ and $y$ and every $k\ge1$,
\[
x^k-y^k=(x-y)\left(x^{k-1}+x^{k-2}y+\cdots+y^{k-1}\right),
\]
so $x-y$ divides $x^k-y^k$. Hence
\[
f(x)-f(y)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_k\left(x^k-y^k\right)
\]
is divisible by $x-y$.

If $x$ and $y$ have the same parity, then $x-y$ is even, so $f(x)-f(y)$ is even, i.e.\ $f(x)$ and $f(y)$ have the same parity. Taking $y=0$ for even $x$ and $y=1$ for odd $x$ shows that $f(x)$ has the parity of $f(0)=c_0$ for every even $x$, and the parity of $f(1)=c_0+c_1+\cdots+c_n$ for every odd $x$. $\blacksquare$
