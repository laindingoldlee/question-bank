Every one of the $15$ people is used, since $6+9=15$. Forming the committees amounts to sorting the $15$ people into labelled groups:
\begin{itemize}
\item committee 1: chair ($1$), co-secretaries ($2$), ordinary members ($3$);
\item committee 2: co-chairs ($3$), secretary ($1$), co-treasurers ($2$), ordinary members ($3$).
\end{itemize}
Within a group the order does not matter (``co-'' roles are interchangeable). So the number of ways is the multinomial coefficient
\[
\dfrac{15!}{1!\,2!\,3!\cdot3!\,1!\,2!\,3!}=\dfrac{1307674368000}{864}=\boxed{1513512000}.
\]
(Equivalently: $\dbinom{15}{6}=5005$ ways to choose the first committee, $6\cdot\dbinom52=60$ ways to give the roles in it, and $\dbinom93\cdot6\cdot\dbinom52=84\cdot6\cdot10=5040$ ways to give the roles in the second: $5005\cdot60\cdot5040=1513512000$.)
