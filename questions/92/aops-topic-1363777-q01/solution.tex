The angles of a hexagon add up to $(6-2)\cdot180^\circ=720^\circ$. Let them be $a,a+d,\dots,a+5d$ with $d\ge0$ (the order around the hexagon does not matter). Then
\[
6a+15d=720,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad 2a+5d=240 .
\]
Since $a$ and $a+d$ are integers, $d$ is an integer, and $5d=240-2a$ is even, so $d$ is even. The largest angle is
\[
a+5d=\dfrac{240-5d}{2}+5d=120+\dfrac{5d}{2}.
\]
Convexity requires every angle to be less than $180^\circ$, so $120+\dfrac{5d}{2}<180$, i.e. $d<24$. The largest even $d$ is $22$, giving the angles $65^\circ,87^\circ,109^\circ,131^\circ,153^\circ,175^\circ$ and the largest angle $\boxed{175^\circ}$.

Such a hexagon exists: the exterior angles $115^\circ,93^\circ,71^\circ,49^\circ,27^\circ,5^\circ$ are positive and add up to $360^\circ$, and suitable side lengths close the polygon, as in the sketch (drawn from computed coordinates, with the angles placed in a different order around the hexagon).

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real[] ang={65,175,109,87,153,131};
real[] s={1.0409,1.0362,0.9592,0.9499,0.9751,1.0300};
pair[] P; P.push((0,0)); real th=0;
for(int k=0;k<5;++k){ P.push(P[k]+3*s[k]*dir(th)); th+=180-ang[k+1]; }
draw(P[0]--P[1]--P[2]--P[3]--P[4]--P[5]--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
pair cen=(P[0]+P[1]+P[2]+P[3]+P[4]+P[5])/6;
for(int k=0;k<6;++k){ dot(P[k]); label("$"+string(ang[k])+"^\circ$",P[k]+0.45*unit(cen-P[k]),fontsize(8pt)); }
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}
