No denominator can be $1$, so put $u_i=\dfrac{1}{a_i}\in[\dfrac{1}{20},\dfrac{1}{2}]$. We minimize $\prod u_i$ subject to $\sum u_i=1$, first allowing real $u_i$. This compact feasible set has a minimum. If two coordinates $u\le v$ are strictly between the bounds, replace them by $u-\varepsilon,v+\varepsilon$ for a small positive $\varepsilon$. Their sum is unchanged, but their product changes by $\varepsilon(u-v)-\varepsilon^2<0$, a contradiction. Thus at most one coordinate is interior. If all coordinates were endpoints, the sum would be $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{9k}{20}$ for an integer $k$, never $1$. So there is one interior coordinate $t$ and $k$ coordinates equal to $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Necessarily $k\le1$. The case $k=0$ forces $t=\dfrac{11}{20}$, outside the bounds; hence $k=1$, eight coordinates are $\dfrac{1}{20}$, and $t=\dfrac{1}{10}$. These reciprocals correspond to the permitted integer denominators $2,10,20,\ldots,20$. Consequently the maximum is $2\cdot10\cdot20^8=\boxed{512000000000}$.
