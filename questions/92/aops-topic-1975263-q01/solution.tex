Both $A$ and $C$ are equidistant from $B$ and $D$ ($AB=AD=6$, $CB=CD=5$), so both lie on the perpendicular bisector of $BD$. Let $M$ be the midpoint of $BD$; then $A$, $M$, $C$ are collinear and $AC\perp BD$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real s=sqrt(3);
pair B=(-3,0),D=(3,0),A=(0,3*s),C=(0,-4),M=(0,0);
draw(A--B--C--D--cycle,linewidth(0.9));
draw(B--D);draw(A--C,dashed);
label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,W);label("$C$",C,S);label("$D$",D,E);label("$M$",M,NE);
label("$6$",(A+B)/2,NW);label("$6$",(A+D)/2,NE);label("$5$",(B+C)/2,SW);label("$5$",(C+D)/2,SE);
label("$3\sqrt{3}$",(0,0.9*s),E);label("$4$",(0,-2),E);
dot(M);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

In the equilateral triangle, $AM=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\cdot6=3\sqrt3$. In right triangle $CMB$, $CM=\sqrt{5^2-3^2}=4$. Since $ABCD$ is convex, $A$ and $C$ are on opposite sides of $BD$, so
\[
AC=AM+MC=\boxed{4+3\sqrt3}.
\]
(If $C$ were on the same side of $BD$ as $A$, the quadrilateral would not be convex and $AC$ would be $3\sqrt3-4$.)
