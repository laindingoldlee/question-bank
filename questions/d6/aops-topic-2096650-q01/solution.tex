\textbf{(a)} By the division algorithm, $P(x)=(x-r)^2Q(x)+R(x)$ with $\deg R\le1$. Write $R(x)=a(x-r)+c$. Putting $x=r$ gives $P(r)=c$. Then
\[P(x)-P(r)=(x-r)^2Q(x)+a(x-r)=(x-r)\bigl[(x-r)Q(x)+a\bigr].\]
Comparing with $P(x)-P(r)=(x-r)S(x)$ and cancelling the nonzero polynomial $x-r$ gives $S(x)=(x-r)Q(x)+a$, and putting $x=r$ gives $a=S(r)$. So the remainder is
\[R(x)=S(r)\,(x-r)+P(r).\qquad\blacksquare\]
(For readers who know calculus, $S(r)=P'(r)$, so the remainder is the tangent line $P'(r)(x-r)+P(r)$.)

\textbf{(b)} Here $P(x)=x^{100}$, $r=1$, $P(1)=1$ and
\[x^{100}-1=(x-1)\left(x^{99}+x^{98}+\cdots+x+1\right),\]
so $S(x)=x^{99}+\cdots+x+1$ and $S(1)=100$. The remainder is $100(x-1)+1=\boxed{100x-99}$.
