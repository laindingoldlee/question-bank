We have $a^2=9!\,b^3$, so $\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{\sqrt{9!\,b^3}}{b}=\sqrt{9!\,b}$, and we must make $9!\,b^3$ a perfect square with $b$ as small as possible. Since $9!=2^7\cdot3^4\cdot5\cdot7$, write $b=2^p3^q5^r7^s\cdots$. The exponents in $9!\,b^3$ are $7+3p$, $4+3q$, $1+3r$, $1+3s$ (and $3t$ for any other prime), all of which must be even. So $p$, $r$, $s$ are odd and $q$ and all other exponents are even. The least such $b$ is $2\cdot5\cdot7=70$, and then
\[
\dfrac{a}{b}=\sqrt{9!\cdot70}=\sqrt{2^8\cdot3^4\cdot5^2\cdot7^2}=2^4\cdot3^2\cdot5\cdot7=\boxed{5040}.
\]
(Here $a=5040\cdot70=352800$, and indeed $352800^2=9!\cdot70^3$.) Since $\dfrac{a}{b}=\sqrt{9!\,b}$ increases with $b$, this is the minimum.
