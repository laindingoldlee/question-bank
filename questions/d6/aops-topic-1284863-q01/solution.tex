An integer $c$ is the hypotenuse of an integer right triangle exactly when $c$ has a prime factor $p\equiv1\pmod4$ (such a prime is a sum of two squares and is the hypotenuse of a primitive triangle, which can be scaled up; conversely every primitive hypotenuse has only prime factors $\equiv1\pmod4$).
\begin{itemize}
\item $39=3\cdot13$: $(15,36,39)$.
\item $40=8\cdot5$: $(24,32,40)$.
\item $41$: $(9,40,41)$.
\item $42=2\cdot3\cdot7$ has no prime factor $\equiv1\pmod4$.
\end{itemize}
So the answer is $\boxed{42}$. (A direct check confirms that $42^2-a^2$ is never a positive perfect square for $1\le a\le41$.)
