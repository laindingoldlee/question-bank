\textbf{(a)} Going from $A$ to $B$, the $x$-coordinate changes by $x_2-x_1$ and the $y$-coordinate by $y_2-y_1$. Draw the right triangle with hypotenuse $AB$ and legs parallel to the axes, and the smaller right triangle with hypotenuse $AP$ (dashed below). The two triangles are similar (they share the angle at $A$ and both have a right angle), with ratio $AP:AB=1:3$, so the legs of the small triangle are one third of the legs of the large one.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(8cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(2,2), B=(8,5), P=A+(B-A)/3;
draw((0,0)--(9.4,0),Arrow(6)); draw((0,0)--(0,6),Arrow(6));
label("$x$",(9.4,0),E); label("$y$",(0,6),N);
draw(A--(B.x,A.y)--B,dotted);
draw(A--(P.x,A.y)--P,dashed+blue);
draw(A--B,linewidth(0.9));
dot(A); dot(B); dot(P,blue);
label("$A(x_1,y_1)$",A,W); label("$B(x_2,y_2)$",B,E); label("$P$",P,NW,blue);
label("$\dfrac{x_2-x_1}{3}$",((A.x+P.x)/2,A.y),S,blue+fontsize(8pt));
label("$\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{3}$",(P.x,(A.y+P.y)/2),E,blue+fontsize(8pt));
label("$x_2-x_1$",((A.x+B.x)/2+1.2,A.y),S,fontsize(8pt));
label("$y_2-y_1$",(B.x,(A.y+B.y)/2),E,fontsize(8pt));
\end{asy}

Hence
\[P=\left(x_1+\dfrac{x_2-x_1}{3},\ y_1+\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{3}\right)=\boxed{\left(\dfrac{2x_1+x_2}{3},\ \dfrac{2y_1+y_2}{3}\right)}.\]
In the same way, the point with $AP=\dfrac1nAB$ is
\[P=\left(x_1+\dfrac{x_2-x_1}{n},\ y_1+\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{n}\right)=\left(\dfrac{(n-1)x_1+x_2}{n},\ \dfrac{(n-1)y_1+y_2}{n}\right).\]
For $n=2$ this is the midpoint formula $\left(\dfrac{x_1+x_2}{2},\dfrac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)$; the weights are $\dfrac{n-1}{n}$ on the nearer endpoint $A$ and $\dfrac1n$ on $B$, not $\dfrac1n$ on each.

\textbf{(b)} For $A=(2,2)$ and $B=(8,5)$ (the figure), the correct point is $P=(4,3)$, but the guess gives $G=\left(\dfrac{10}3,\dfrac73\right)$, which is not even on the line $AB$ (on that line, $x=\dfrac{10}3$ gives $y=\dfrac83$). Another example: $A=(3,0)$, $B=(6,0)$ gives $G=(3,0)=A$, while $P=(4,0)$.

In general the guess is right exactly when $\dfrac{x_1+x_2}{3}=\dfrac{2x_1+x_2}{3}$ and $\dfrac{y_1+y_2}{3}=\dfrac{2y_1+y_2}{3}$, that is, when $x_1=y_1=0$: \textbf{only when $A$ is the origin}.
