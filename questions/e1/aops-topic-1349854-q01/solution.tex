Let him have $b$ fives and $c$ tens, with $b,c\ge0$ and $b+c\le9$; the other $9-b-c$ bills are ones. The total is
\[
(9-b-c)+5b+10c=9+4b+9c .
\]
There are $1+2+\dots+10=55$ pairs $(b,c)$. Two pairs give the same total when $4b+9c=4b'+9c'$, i.e. $4(b-b')=9(c'-c)$. Then $9\mid b-b'$, and since $0\le b,b'\le9$ this forces $\{b,b'\}=\{0,9\}$; the pair with $b=9$ has $c=0$, and then $c'=4$. So the only coincidence is $(b,c)=(9,0)$ and $(0,4)$, both giving $\$45$ (nine fives, or four tens and five ones). Hence there are $55-1=\boxed{54}$ different totals.
