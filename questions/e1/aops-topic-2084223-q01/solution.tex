Let \(N\) be such an integer. The conditions \(N\equiv 2\pmod 3\) and \(N\equiv 2\pmod 7\) say that \(N-2\) is divisible by \(3\) and by \(7\), hence by \(21\). So \(N=21k+2\) for some integer \(k\). Then
\[
N\equiv 3\pmod 5\iff 21k+2\equiv k+2\equiv 3\pmod 5\iff k\equiv 1\pmod 5 .
\]
So \(k=5j+1\) and \(N=21(5j+1)+2=105j+23\). Since \(3,5,7\) are pairwise coprime, this is the unique residue class modulo \(105\) given by the Chinese remainder theorem. The integers are exactly those \(N\equiv 23\pmod{105}\), and the smallest positive one is \(\boxed{23}\).
