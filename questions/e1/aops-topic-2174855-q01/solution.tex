Compute the expected winnings of each ticket.
\begin{itemize}
\item \$1 ticket: it wins with probability $\dfrac{1}{10^2}$, so its expected value is $\dfrac{10}{100}=\$0.10$, a loss of \$0.90 per ticket.
\item \$10 ticket: probability $\dfrac{1}{10^3}$, expected value $\dfrac{10000}{1000}=\$10$: on average you break even.
\item \$100 ticket: probability $\dfrac{1}{10^4}$, expected value $\dfrac{10000000}{10000}=\$1000$, an expected profit of \$900 per ticket.
\end{itemize}
So (B) and (E) lose money on average, (A) and (C) give $0$ on average, and (D) and (F) both have expected profit \$900 for your \$100. But (D) is a gamble: with probability $0.9999$ you lose your \$100. With (F), the pool buys $10^4$ tickets at \$100, which is exactly enough to choose every one of the $10^4$ possible sequences of four digits once. Exactly one ticket wins, so the pool is \emph{certain} to receive \$10{,}000{,}000 for its \$1{,}000{,}000, and your share is a guaranteed \$1000 for your \$100.

The best strategy is $\boxed{\text{(F)}}$: it has the largest expected profit (tied with (D)) and no risk.
