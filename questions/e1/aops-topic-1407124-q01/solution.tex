Split $3x^3$ into two equal halves and apply AM-GM to the three nonnegative numbers $\dfrac{3x^3}{2}$, $\dfrac{3x^3}{2}$ and $\dfrac{32}{9}$:
\[
\dfrac{3x^3}{2}+\dfrac{3x^3}{2}+\dfrac{32}{9}\ \ge\ 3\sqrt[3]{\dfrac{3x^3}{2}\cdot\dfrac{3x^3}{2}\cdot\dfrac{32}{9}}
=3\sqrt[3]{8x^6}=6x^2 .
\]
So $3x^3+\dfrac{32}{9}\ge6x^2$, which is the claim. Equality in AM-GM holds exactly when the three numbers are equal, $\dfrac{3x^3}{2}=\dfrac{32}{9}$, that is $x^3=\dfrac{64}{27}$, so $\boxed{x=\dfrac{4}{3}}$. (Check: $3\cdot\dfrac{64}{27}-6\cdot\dfrac{16}{9}+\dfrac{32}{9}=\dfrac{64-96+32}{9}=0$.)

The poster's attempt fails because AM-GM on the two terms $3x^3$ and $\dfrac{32}{9}$ gives a bound involving $x^{\dfrac{3}{2}}$, not $x^2$; the weights have to be chosen so that the product of the terms is a multiple of $x^6$, which is why $3x^3$ is split into two equal parts.
