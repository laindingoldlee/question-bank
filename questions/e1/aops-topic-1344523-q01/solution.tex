Let $m=\min(r,b,g)$. The answer is
\[
\boxed{r+b+g-m+1},
\]
that is, one more than the total of the two largest colour counts.

\textbf{Fewer is not enough.} The machine could first release every gumball of the two most common colours, $r+b+g-m$ gumballs in all, and so far you would be missing the third colour.

\textbf{This many is enough.} If you hold $r+b+g-m+1$ gumballs and one colour is missing, then all of them come from the other two colours, which together have at most $r+b+g-m$ gumballs (the two largest counts at most). That is a contradiction, so all three colours are present.

For example, with $5$ red, $3$ blue and $8$ green gumballs you need $8+5+1=14$.
