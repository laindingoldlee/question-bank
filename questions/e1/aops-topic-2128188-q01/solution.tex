\textbf{(a)} After draining $x$ litres, $15-x$ litres of the $40\%$ mixture remain, containing $0.4(15-x)$ litres of antifreeze; adding $x$ litres of pure antifreeze gives $15$ litres containing $0.6\cdot15=9$ litres of antifreeze. So
\[0.4(15-x)+x=9\iff6+0.6x=9\iff x=\boxed{5}\text{ litres}.\]
(Check: $10$ litres at $40\%$ contain $4$ litres of antifreeze, and $4+5=9$ is $60\%$ of $15$.)

\textbf{(b)} The student's first equation is correct. The first error is in clearing decimals: multiplying $0.4(15-x)+1\cdot x=0.6(15)$ by $100$ gives
\[40(15-x)+100x=60(15),\]
not $40(15-x)+10x$. With the correct equation, $600+60x=900$ and $x=5$. There is also a second slip at the end: $-3x=30$ would give $x=-10$, not $-3$. A negative answer is impossible here, which is a signal that something earlier went wrong.
