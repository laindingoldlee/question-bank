$\sqrt{1-\cos^2A}=\sqrt{\sin^2A}=|\sin A|=\sin A$, since $\sin A>0$ for an acute angle. Hence
\[\sec A\sqrt{1-\cos^2A}=\dfrac{\sin A}{\cos A}=\boxed{\tan A}.\]

usepackage("amsmath");

real s=0.07;draw(X+(-s,0)--X+(-s,s)--X+(0,s));
label("$A$",O,W);label("$1$",(O+Y)/2,NW);label("$\cos A$",(O+X)/2,S);label("$\sin A$",(X+Y)/2,E);
\end{asy}

In a right triangle with hypotenuse $1$ the legs are $\cos A$ and $\sin A$, and $\sqrt{1-\cos^2A}$ is the leg opposite $A$. (For a general angle the expression equals $|\sin A|\sec A$.)
