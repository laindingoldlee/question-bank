Let $R,B$ count the red and blue vertices. Each red vertex has $n$ blue neighbours, while each blue has two red neighbours, so double counting gives $nR=2B$. Since $R+B=2^n$, we get $(n+2)R=2^{n+1}$. Thus $n+2$ must be a power of two. For $n>100$, the smallest possibility is $n=126$. It remains to construct a colouring. Label the $126$ coordinate positions by two copies of each nonzero binary vector of length six. For a cube vertex, define its syndrome to be the coordinatewise sum modulo two of labels at positions where its bit is $1$. Colour it red exactly when its syndrome is zero. Flipping one bit changes the syndrome by its nonzero label, so two red vertices cannot be adjacent. A blue vertex of syndrome $s\ne0$ becomes red exactly by flipping a position labelled $s$; precisely two such positions exist. This is the required colouring, proving the minimum is $\boxed{126}$.
