If the five letters were all different there would be $5!$ arrangements. Each actual word arises from exactly $2!\cdot3!$ of these labelled arrangements (permute the two $a$'s and the three $b$'s among their positions), so the count is $\dfrac{5!}{2!\,3!}=\boxed{10}$. Every word is overcounted by the same factor, which is why we divide; it also equals $\binom52$, choosing the positions of the $a$'s.
