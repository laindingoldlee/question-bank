From $x\sqrt3+1=2x$ we get $1=(2-\sqrt3)x$, so $x=\dfrac{1}{2-\sqrt3}$. Rationalise the denominator, using $(2-\sqrt3)(2+\sqrt3)=4-3=1$:
\[
\dfrac{1}{2-\sqrt3}=\dfrac{2+\sqrt3}{(2-\sqrt3)(2+\sqrt3)}=\dfrac{2+\sqrt3}{1}=2+\sqrt3 .
\]
So \textbf{both are right}: the two answers are the same number, and the book has simply rationalised the denominator. (Check: $(2+\sqrt3)\sqrt3+1=2\sqrt3+4=2(2+\sqrt3)$.)
