Let a dozen eggs normally cost $D$ cents. With the reduction an egg costs $\dfrac{D-x}{12}$ cents, and with the increase $\dfrac{D+x}{12}$ cents. For $x+1$ eggs the difference is one cent:
\[(x+1)\cdot\dfrac{D+x}{12}-(x+1)\cdot\dfrac{D-x}{12}=1\iff\dfrac{2x(x+1)}{12}=1\iff x^2+x-6=0.\]
So $(x+3)(x-2)=0$, and since $x>0$, $x=\boxed{2}$. (The normal price $D$ cancels out.)
