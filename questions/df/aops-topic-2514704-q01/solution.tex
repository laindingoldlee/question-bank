\textbf{(a)} By Vieta's formulas the roots have sum $-b$ and product $c$, so their average is $-\dfrac b2$ and we may write them as $-\dfrac b2+u$ and $-\dfrac b2-u$ (with $u$ possibly complex). Their product is
\[\left(-\dfrac b2+u\right)\left(-\dfrac b2-u\right)=\dfrac{b^2}{4}-u^2=c,\]
so $u^2=\dfrac{b^2}{4}-c$ and $u=\pm\sqrt{\dfrac{b^2}{4}-c}$. The roots are $x=-\dfrac b2\pm\sqrt{\dfrac{b^2}{4}-c}$. Conversely these two numbers have sum $-b$ and product $c$, so they are the roots.

\textbf{(b)} Expanding,
\[\left(y-\dfrac p3\right)^3+p\left(y-\dfrac p3\right)^2+q\left(y-\dfrac p3\right)+r=y^3+\left(-p+p\right)y^2+\cdots=y^3+Py+Q\]
for some constants $P,Q$: the $y^2$ terms $-3\cdot\dfrac p3y^2$ and $py^2$ cancel. Equivalently, the roots in $y$ are the original roots minus their average $-\dfrac p3$, so they sum to $0$. (This ``depressed cubic'' is the starting point of Cardano's formula; unlike the quadratic case, one number $u$ no longer describes all three roots.)

\textbf{(c)} Here $p=-6$, so put $x=y+2$:
\[(y+2)^3-6(y+2)^2+11(y+2)-6=y^3+6y^2+12y+8-6y^2-24y-24+11y+22-6=y^3-y.\]
So $y^3-y=y(y-1)(y+1)=0$, giving $y\in\{-1,0,1\}$, and the roots are
\[\boxed{x=1,\ 2,\ 3},\]
placed symmetrically about their average $2$.
