Regions $A$, $B$, $C$, $J$ and $K$ are ponds. Logs leave pond $A$ and float down flumes (one-way channels, shown as arrows) until they end up in pond $B$ or pond $C$. From $A$ there are flumes to $J$, to $K$ and to $C$; from $J$ there are flumes to $B$ and to $C$; from $K$ there is one flume to $B$ and there are two different flumes to $C$. On leaving a pond, a log is equally likely to use any of the available exit flumes.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real rr=0.45;
pair A=(0,4),J=(-2.6,2),K=(2.6,2),C=(0,2),B=(0,0);
void fl(pair P,pair Q){pair u=unit(Q-P);draw(P+rr*u--Q-rr*u,Arrow(6));}
fl(A,J);fl(A,K);fl(A,C);fl(J,C);fl(J,B);fl(K,B);
draw(K+rr*dir(150)..(1.3,2.45)..C+rr*dir(30),Arrow(6));
draw(K+rr*dir(210)..(1.3,1.55)..C+rr*dir(-30),Arrow(6));
pair[] Pd={A,J,K,C,B};string[] Ln={"$A$","$J$","$K$","$C$","$B$"};
for(int i=0;i<5;++i){filldraw(circle(Pd[i],rr),paleblue,linewidth(0.8));label(Ln[i],Pd[i]);}
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

What is the probability that a log in pond $A$ will end up in pond $B$? Express your answer as a common fraction. (A student listed the six routes $AJB$, $AKB$, $AC$, $AJC$, $AKC$, $AKC$ and answered $\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{1}{3}$. What is wrong with this?)
