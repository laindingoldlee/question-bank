The primes up to $20$ are $2,3,5,7,11,13,17,19$ (eight primes). For $s\in S$ let $T(s)$ be its set of prime factors.

\textbf{Upper bound.} If two elements were both divisible by $p^2$, their greatest common factor would be divisible by $p^2$. So for each of the $8$ primes, at most one element of $S$ is divisible by its square, and at most $8$ elements of $S$ are not squarefree. The squarefree elements are determined by their prime sets, and these sets pairwise intersect (the greatest common factor is greater than $1$). A family of pairwise intersecting subsets of an $8$-element set has at most $2^7=128$ members, because a set and its complement cannot both belong to it, and the $2^8$ subsets split into $128$ such pairs. So $|S|\le128+8=136$.

\textbf{Construction.} Take all $128$ squarefree numbers whose prime set contains $2$, together with
\[
4,\ 2\cdot3^2,\ 2\cdot5^2,\ 2\cdot7^2,\ 2\cdot11^2,\ 2\cdot13^2,\ 2\cdot17^2,\ 2\cdot19^2 .
\]
Every element is even, so any two have a common factor $2$. A prime square $p^2$ divides only one element, so no greatest common factor is divisible by a square. (For example $\gcd(4,2)=2$ and $\gcd(18,6)=6$.)

The maximum is $\boxed{136}$. The student's missing element is $4=2^2$: the square of the prime $2$ may also be used, once.
