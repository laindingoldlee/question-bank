At least one prime is $11$, since $11$ divides their product. Write the others as $p,q$. Then $pq=p+q+11$, so $(p-1)(q-1)=12$. The unordered positive factor pairs are $(1,12),(2,6),(3,4)$, giving $(p,q)=(2,13),(3,7),(4,5)$. The last fails primality. Thus the triples are $\boxed{\{2,11,13\},\{3,7,11\}}$ and the possible largest primes are $\boxed{13,11}$.
