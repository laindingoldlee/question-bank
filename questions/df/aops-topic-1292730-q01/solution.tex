(a) $R(x)=16-x$ satisfies the three conditions, and so does $R(x)=16-x+(x-8)(x-9)(x-10)S(x)$ for any polynomial $S$. For each such $R$ and any $Q$, $P=Q+R$ works, so there are infinitely many pairs.

(b) If $P$ and $Q$ are quadratic, $R$ has degree at most $2$, so it is determined by its three values; $16-x$ fits them, so $R(x)=16-x$. Then $P(x)=x^2+x+1+16-x=\boxed{x^2+17}$.
