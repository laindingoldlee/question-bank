We have $0=0+0i$: its imaginary part is $0$, so it is real, and its real part is $0$, so it is purely imaginary.

Conversely, if $z=a+bi$ is both real and purely imaginary, then $b=0$ and $a=0$, so $z=0$. (The representation $a+bi$ is unique: if $a+bi=c+di$ with $a,b,c,d$ real, then $a-c=(d-b)i$; squaring gives $(a-c)^2=-(d-b)^2$, which forces $a=c$ and $b=d$.) Hence $\boxed{0}$ is the only such number. $\blacksquare$
