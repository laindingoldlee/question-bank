Let $p=x^4y^4$. Squaring the first equation,
\[
4=\left(x^4+y^4\right)^2=x^8+y^8+2x^4y^4=3+2p,\qquad\text{so}\qquad p=\dfrac{1}{2} .
\]
Squaring the second equation in the same way,
\[
x^{16}+y^{16}=\left(x^8+y^8\right)^2-2x^8y^8=9-2p^2=9-\dfrac{1}{2}=\boxed{\dfrac{17}{2}} .
\]
Such real numbers exist: $x^4$ and $y^4$ are the roots $1\pm\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$ of $t^2-2t+\dfrac{1}{2}=0$, which are both positive.
