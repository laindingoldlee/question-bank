A percentage decrease takes a percentage of whatever price is current, so a subtraction made \emph{before} a percentage decrease is itself partly cancelled: subtracting $50$ dollars and then taking $20\%$ off saves only $0.8\cdot50=40$ dollars from the subtraction. To pay the least, every subtraction should come after every percentage decrease.

The percentage decreases are A's $20\%$ and C's $10\%$, and C's $20$-dollar subtraction automatically comes after C's own $10\%$. So the best order is A, then C, then B:
\[1000\xrightarrow{\ \text{A}\ }800\xrightarrow{\ \text{C}\ }800\cdot0.9-20=700\xrightarrow{\ \text{B}\ }650 .\]
In any order the two percentage factors multiply $1000$ to $720$, and each subtraction is reduced by the percentage decreases applied after it; in the order A, C, B nothing is reduced, so no order does better. (For example, C, A, B gives $654$ and B, C, A gives $668$.) The least amount is $\boxed{650}$ dollars.
