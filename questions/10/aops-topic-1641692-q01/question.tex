Let $n=p_1^{e_1}p_2^{e_2}\cdots p_m^{e_m}$, where the $p_i$ are distinct primes and $e_i\ge1$, and let $\varphi(n)$ be the number of integers in $\{1,2,\dots,n\}$ that are relatively prime to $n$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Explain why exactly $\dfrac{n}{p_1}$ of the integers $1,2,\dots,n$ are divisible by $p_1$. (A student objects: ``$\dfrac{n}{p_1}$ has had a factor $p_1$ removed, so how can it be divisible by $p_1$?'' Explain the misunderstanding.)
\item[(b)] Use inclusion-exclusion to prove that $\varphi(n)=n\left(1-\dfrac{1}{p_1}\right)\left(1-\dfrac{1}{p_2}\right)\cdots\left(1-\dfrac{1}{p_m}\right)$.
\end{enumerate}
