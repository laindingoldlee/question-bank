Let the bisector of angle $A$ of triangle $ABC$ meet $BC$ at $D$, and suppose $AD\perp BC$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(4.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
pair A=(0,3.2),B=(-2,0),C=(2,0),D=(0,0);
draw(A--B--C--cycle,linewidth(1));
draw(A--D,blue+linewidth(0.9));
real s=0.22; draw((s,0)--(s,s)--(0,s),blue);
draw(arc(A,0.8,270-32,270),red);draw(arc(A,0.95,270,270+32),red);
dot(D);
label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,SW);label("$C$",C,SE);label("$D$",D,S);
label("$\alpha$",A+1.15*dir(258),red);label("$\alpha$",A+1.3*dir(282),red);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

Compare triangles $ABD$ and $ACD$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\angle BAD=\angle CAD$ (since $AD$ bisects angle $A$);
\item $AD$ is a common side;
\item $\angle ADB=\angle ADC=90^\circ$ (since $AD$ is an altitude).
\end{itemize}
By ASA the triangles are congruent, so $AB=AC$ and the triangle is isosceles. $\blacksquare$

(Equivalently, $\angle B=90^\circ-\alpha=\angle C$, where $\alpha$ is half of angle $A$, and equal base angles give $AB=AC$.)
