The product is $\overline{fagc}+\overline{bbh0}<10000+10000$, a five-digit number, so $f=1$ and the product lies in $[11000,11999]$. Since $abc\cdot e=\overline{1agc}<2000$, we get $10\cdot(abc\cdot d)\ge11000-1999$, so $abc\cdot d=\overline{bbh}\ge901$. Hence $b=9$ and $abc\cdot d\in[990,999]$, with $abc=\overline{a9c}$.

Now check $d$. For $d=1$, $abc\ge990$ gives $a=9=b$. For $d=2$, $abc\in[495,499]$. For $d=3$, $abc\in[330,333]$, whose middle digit is not $9$. For $d=5$, $abc\in[198,199]$, but $a=1=f$ or $c=9=b$. For $d=4,6,7,8,9$, the range $\left[\dfrac{990}d,\dfrac{999}d\right]$ contains no number with middle digit $9$.

So $d=2$ and $abc\in\{495,496,497,498\}$. Then $abc\cdot e$ must be $\overline{14gc}$, which requires $e=3$; only $495\cdot3=1485$ ends in $c$. This gives
\[495\times23=11385,\quad 495\cdot3=1485,\quad 495\cdot2=990,\]
and the unique solution is $\boxed{a=4,b=9,c=5,d=2,e=3,f=1,g=8,h=0}$.
