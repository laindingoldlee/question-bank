Order the five integers as $a\le b\le c\le d\le e$. The ten sums add to $60$, counting each original number four times, so $a+b+c+d+e=15$. The extreme sums give $a+b=-2$, $d+e=14$, hence $c=3$. The two sums $a+c,b+c$ add to $4$ and both occur in the list. Their only possible unordered pairs are $(-2,6)$ and $(1,3)$. The first forces $a=-5,b=3=c$, producing the sum $-2$ twice, contrary to the list. Therefore $a=-2,b=0$. Their product with the other three numbers is $\boxed0$; the full list $-2,0,3,3,11$ verifies consistency.
