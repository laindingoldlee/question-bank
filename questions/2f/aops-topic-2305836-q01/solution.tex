All the series below converge, because their terms are eventually smaller than $\left(\dfrac34\right)^n$.

\textbf{First, $T=\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge1}\dfrac{n}{2^n}$.} Then $\dfrac T2=\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge1}\dfrac{n}{2^{n+1}}=\sum_{n\ge2}\dfrac{n-1}{2^n}$, so
\[T-\dfrac T2=\dfrac12+\sum_{n\ge2}\dfrac{n-(n-1)}{2^n}=\sum_{n\ge1}\dfrac1{2^n}=1,\]
and $T=2$.

\textbf{Now $S=\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge1}\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2^n}$.} In the same way, $\dfrac S2=\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge2}\dfrac{(n-1)n}{2^n}$, and
\[S-\dfrac S2=\dfrac{1\cdot2}{2}+\sum_{n\ge2}\dfrac{n(n+1)-(n-1)n}{2^n}=\sum_{n\ge1}\dfrac{2n}{2^n}=2T=4.\]
Hence $S=\boxed{8}$.
