No. Let $a=29^7$, so $29^{28}=a^4$. By the Sophie Germain identity (complete the square),
\[
a^4+4=a^4+4a^2+4-4a^2=(a^2+2)^2-(2a)^2=(a^2+2a+2)(a^2-2a+2).
\]
Both factors are greater than $1$, since $a^2-2a+2=(a-1)^2+1>1$ for $a>1$. Hence $29^{28}+4$ is \textbf{not prime}; it equals $(29^{14}+2\cdot29^7+2)(29^{14}-2\cdot29^7+2)$.
