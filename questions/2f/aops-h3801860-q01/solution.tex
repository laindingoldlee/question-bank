Suppose $S$ has four positive elements, with the largest four $a>b>c>d>0$. In the triple $a,b,c$, neither sum involving $a$ can lie in $S$, and $b+c>b$, so the only possible sum is $b+c=a$. Applying the same reasoning to $a,b,d$ forces $b+d=a$, contradicting $c\ne d$. Thus there are at most three positive elements. Negating the set proves there are at most three negative elements; zero contributes at most one. Hence $|S|\le7$. The set $\{-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3\}$ works: a triple containing zero works immediately; a mixed-sign triple has a positive-negative sum in the set; and the only all-positive or all-negative triples have $1+2=3$ or $-1-2=-3$. Thus the maximum is $\boxed7$.
