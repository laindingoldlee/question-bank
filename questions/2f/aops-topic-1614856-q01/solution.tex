\textbf{Correct answer.} Use $(a-b)^2=(a+b)^2-4ab$:
\[
(a-b)^2=144-36=108\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad|a-b|=\sqrt{108}=\boxed{6\sqrt3}.
\]
(Indeed the numbers are the roots of $t^2-12t+9=0$, namely $6\pm3\sqrt3$, whose difference is $6\sqrt3$.)

\textbf{The error.} The first step is right: $a^2+b^2=(a+b)^2-2ab=144-18=126$. The mistake is the step ``$a^2+b^2=(a+b)(a-b)$''. In fact $(a+b)(a-b)=a^2-b^2$, a \emph{difference} of squares, not the sum. So $12(a-b)=126$ is false; with the numbers above, $a^2-b^2=12\cdot6\sqrt3=72\sqrt3$, not $126$.
