A largest triangle exists. Fix two of its vertices $B$ and $C$; the area is $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot BC\cdot h$, which is largest when the height $h$ from $A$ is largest, that is when $A$ is the midpoint of an arc $BC$, so $AB=AC$. Hence in a largest triangle every two sides are equal: it is equilateral.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(3cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
draw(circle((0,0),1)); pair A=dir(90),B=dir(210),C=dir(330); draw(A--B--C--cycle,blue+linewidth(0.9)); dot((0,0)); draw((0,0)--A,dashed); label("$10$",(0,0.5),E);
label("$A$",A,N);label("$B$",B,SW);label("$C$",C,SE);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

An equilateral triangle inscribed in a circle of radius $10$ has side $10\sqrt3$, so its area is
\[\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}\cdot300=\boxed{75\sqrt3}\approx129.9.\]
