\textbf{(a)} Since $x\ge y\ge0$, the number $\sqrt x-\sqrt y$ is nonnegative, and
\[\left(\sqrt x-\sqrt y\right)^2=x+y-2\sqrt{xy}.\]
So $\sqrt x-\sqrt y=\sqrt{a-2\sqrt b}$ with $\boxed{a=x+y,\ b=xy}$.

\textbf{(b)} By (a) we need $x+y=a$ and $xy=b$, so $x$ and $y$ are the roots of $t^2-at+b=0$:
\[\boxed{x=\dfrac{a+\sqrt{a^2-4b}}{2},\qquad y=\dfrac{a-\sqrt{a^2-4b}}{2}}.\]
These are real because $a^2\ge4b$, they satisfy $x\ge y$, and $y\ge0$ because $\sqrt{a^2-4b}\le\sqrt{a^2}=a$. Then (a) gives $\sqrt x-\sqrt y=\sqrt{x+y-2\sqrt{xy}}=\sqrt{a-2\sqrt b}$. (The conditions also guarantee $a-2\sqrt b\ge0$, since $a^2\ge4b$ means $a\ge2\sqrt b$.) The formula is useful when $a^2-4b$ is a perfect square, because then $x$ and $y$ are rational.

\textbf{(c)} Here $a=7$, $b=10$, $a^2-4b=9$, so $x=\dfrac{7+3}{2}=5$ and $y=\dfrac{7-3}{2}=2$:
\[\sqrt{7-2\sqrt{10}}=\boxed{\sqrt5-\sqrt2}.\]
(Check: $\left(\sqrt5-\sqrt2\right)^2=7-2\sqrt{10}$.)
