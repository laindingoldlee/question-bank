An equilateral triangle is divided into small congruent equilateral triangles by lines parallel to its sides, so that there are $16$ rows of small triangles (each side of the big triangle is $16$ times the side of a small triangle), as in the figure.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(6cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
real h=sqrt(3)/2;
pair pt(real a,real bb){return (a+bb/2,bb*h);}
for(int i=1;i<16;++i){
  draw(pt(0,i)--pt(16-i,i),linewidth(0.3));
  draw(pt(i,0)--pt(i,16-i),linewidth(0.3));
  draw(pt(i,0)--pt(0,i),linewidth(0.3));
}
draw(pt(0,0)--pt(16,0)--pt(0,16)--cycle,linewidth(0.8));
\end{asy}

How many triangles of any size, whose sides lie along the lines of the figure, point upward (in the same direction as the big triangle)?
