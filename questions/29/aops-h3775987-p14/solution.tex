To determine the whole order, Pat must learn the result of each adjacent-rank pair $(1,2),(2,3),(3,4),(4,5)$. If one is missing, those two adjacent students can be swapped without changing any observed comparison. Conversely, all four give a complete comparison chain. Among the $\binom{10}{6}$ equally likely six-pair samples, favourable ones contain these four and any two of the other six, giving $\dfrac{\binom62}{\binom{10}{6}}=\dfrac{15}{210}=\boxed{\dfrac{1}{14}}$.
