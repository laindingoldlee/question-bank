We use induction on $n$. Order the numbers so that $a_1<a_2<\cdots<a_n$.

\textbf{Base case $n=1$.} $a_1^2\le a_1^3$ because $a_1\ge1$.

\textbf{Inductive step.} Suppose the inequality holds for $n-1$ different positive integers, and let $S=a_1+\cdots+a_{n-1}$ and $a=a_n$. Then
\[(S+a)^2=S^2+2aS+a^2\le\left(a_1^3+\cdots+a_{n-1}^3\right)+2aS+a^2,\]
so it is enough to show $2aS+a^2\le a^3$, i.e. $2S\le a^2-a=a(a-1)$. The numbers $a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1}$ are different positive integers less than $a$, so
\[S\le1+2+\cdots+(a-1)=\dfrac{a(a-1)}{2},\]
which is exactly what we need. This completes the induction.

Equality holds exactly when $\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}$, where it becomes the identity $(1+2+\cdots+n)^2=1^3+2^3+\cdots+n^3$.
