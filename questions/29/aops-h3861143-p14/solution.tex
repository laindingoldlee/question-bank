A stable square has odd side length in cell spacings; its top-right and bottom-left cells are black. These two black cells lie on the same antidiagonal, and their row indices have opposite parity. Conversely any such black pair determines one stable square. Sixteen disjoint pairs must match all thirty-two black cells. The black antidiagonals have lengths $2,4,6,8,6,4,2$; on each, half the row indices have either parity, and every opposite-parity pair is allowed. The number of perfect matchings is therefore $1!2!3!4!3!2!1!=\boxed{3456}$.
