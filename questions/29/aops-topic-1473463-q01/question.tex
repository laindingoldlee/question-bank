When $(a+b+c+d+1)^N$ is expanded and like terms are combined, the resulting expression contains exactly $1001$ terms that include all four variables $a$, $b$, $c$, $d$, each to some positive power. What is $N$?

$\textbf{(A)}\ 9\qquad\textbf{(B)}\ 14\qquad\textbf{(C)}\ 16\qquad\textbf{(D)}\ 17\qquad\textbf{(E)}\ 19$

Also explain why the count of such terms is \emph{not} $\dbinom{N}{0}+\dbinom{N}{1}+\cdots+\dbinom{N}{N-1}$, as one might guess from writing $(a+b+c+d+1)^N=\left((a+b+c+d)+1\right)^N$.
