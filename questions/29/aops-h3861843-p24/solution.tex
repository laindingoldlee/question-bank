For each five-digit $n=abcde$, its swapped version is $m=acbde$. The two factors are $100a+10c+b$ and $100b+10d+e$, each required to be prime. Exhaustively testing $10000\le n\le99999$ with these explicit formulas gives one candidate: $n=11009$, whose swapped version is 10109. Its first and last triples are 101 and 109; both are prime by trial division up to ten, and $101\cdot109=11009$. Thus $\boxed{11009}$. The finite test is exhaustive because every permissible five-digit integer occurs once.
