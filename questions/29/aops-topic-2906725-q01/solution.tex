If the product group $G$ has sum $s$, then $n=\prod G=55-s$. One element is impossible. Two elements $x<y$: $xy=55-x-y$, i.e. $(x+1)(y+1)=56$, whose only valid factorisation is $7\cdot8$, giving $\{6,7\}$ and $n=42$. Three elements give $xyz+x+y+z=55$, whose only solution is $\{1,4,10\}$ ($n=40$). Four or more elements: the only solution is $\{1,2,3,7\}$ ($n=42$), since other sets with product at most $55$ fail. The largest is $n=\boxed{42}$ (e.g. $6\cdot7=42=1+2+3+4+5+8+9+10$).
