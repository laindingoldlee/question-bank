\textbf{(a)}
\[
\dbinom{-5}{3}=\dfrac{(-5)(-6)(-7)}{3!}=\dfrac{-210}{6}=\boxed{-35}.
\]
\textbf{(b)} Factor $-1$ out of each of the $k$ factors in the numerator:
\[
\dbinom{-n}{k}=\dfrac{(-n)(-n-1)\cdots(-n-k+1)}{k!}=(-1)^k\dfrac{n(n+1)\cdots(n+k-1)}{k!}=(-1)^k\dbinom{n+k-1}{k},
\]
since $n(n+1)\cdots(n+k-1)=\dfrac{(n+k-1)!}{(n-1)!}$. (Check with (a): $(-1)^3\dbinom{7}{3}=-35$.)

\textbf{(c)} Taking $m=0$ in $m!=m\cdot(m-1)!$ would give $1=0!=0\cdot(-1)!=0$, which is false whatever number $(-1)!$ is. So $(-1)!$ cannot be defined consistently. Then $(-4)!$ cannot be defined either: from $m=-1,-2,-3$ we would get $(-1)!=(-1)(-2)(-3)\cdot(-4)!=-6\cdot(-4)!$, a number, which we just showed impossible. (This is why the Gamma function, which extends the factorial, has poles at $0,-1,-2,\dots$.)
