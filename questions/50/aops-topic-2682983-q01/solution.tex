\textbf{True.} Let $f$ be one-to-one with domain $A$ and range $B$, so $f^{-1}:B\to A$ is defined by $f^{-1}(y)=x$ exactly when $f(x)=y$. Suppose $f^{-1}(y_1)=f^{-1}(y_2)=x$. Then $y_1=f(x)$ and $y_2=f(x)$, so $y_1=y_2$. Hence $f^{-1}$ is one-to-one. $\blacksquare$ (Its inverse is $f$ itself.)
