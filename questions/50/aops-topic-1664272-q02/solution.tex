Let the concentrations (sugar per litre) be $c_1$ in the $2$-litre jar and $c_2$ in the $5$-litre jar, with $c_1\ne c_2$. After the exchange the jars still hold $2$ and $5$ litres, and the sugar contents are $(2-t)c_1+tc_2$ and $(5-t)c_2+tc_1$. Equal concentrations mean
\[
\dfrac{(2-t)c_1+tc_2}{2}=\dfrac{(5-t)c_2+tc_1}{5}.
\]
Multiplying by $10$: $10c_1-5tc_1+5tc_2=10c_2-2tc_2+2tc_1$, so
\[
(10-7t)c_1=(10-7t)c_2,\qquad (10-7t)(c_1-c_2)=0 .
\]
Since $c_1\ne c_2$, $t=\dfrac{10}{7}$, which is less than $2$, so it is possible: $\boxed{\textbf{(A)}\ \dfrac{10}{7}}$. (Then each jar ends up with the overall concentration $\dfrac{2c_1+5c_2}{7}$.)
