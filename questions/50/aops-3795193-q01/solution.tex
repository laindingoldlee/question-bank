For $0\le x<1$, set $y=\sqrt{1-x^2}$ in the constraint and divide by $y$. Then $f(x)+\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}f(\sqrt{1-x^2})\le\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$. Integrate from zero to one; the substitution $u=\sqrt{1-x^2}$ makes the second integral equal to the first, while the right side integrates to $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. Continuity makes all integrals legitimate as improper limits. Thus $\int_0^1f\le\dfrac{\pi}{4}$. Equality is attained by $f(x)=\sqrt{1-x^2}$: if $x=\sin u,y=\sin v$ with $u , v \in [0 , \dfrac{\pi}{2}]$, then $xf(y)+yf(x)=\sin(u+v)\le1$. The maximum is $\boxed{\dfrac{\pi}{4}}$.
