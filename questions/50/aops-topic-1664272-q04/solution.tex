After the first erasure $80$ numbers remain; the smallest number left at the end is the $11$th smallest of these $80$. Call it $v$.

\textbf{Lower bound.} The $11$ smallest of the $80$ survivors are all at most $v$. A group keeps only $8$ numbers, so these $11$ survivors come from at least $2$ groups. Each of those groups lost its $2$ smallest numbers in the first erasure, and they are smaller than the group's survivors, hence smaller than $v$. So at least $11+2\cdot2=15$ different numbers are at most $v$, and $v\ge15$.

\textbf{$15$ is possible.} Put $1,2,\dots,10$ in one group: it loses $1,2$ and keeps $3,\dots,10$. Put $11,12,13,14,15$ and five numbers greater than $15$ in a second group: it loses $11,12$ and keeps $13,14,15,\dots$. Fill the other groups with the remaining numbers; their survivors are all greater than $15$. Among the $80$ survivors the smallest are $3,4,\dots,10,13,14,15$, and the $10$ smallest of these are erased, so the smallest number left is $15$.

The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(C)}\ 15}$.
