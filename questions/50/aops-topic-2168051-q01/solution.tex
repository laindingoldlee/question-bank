Let $O$ be the centre of the outer circle, let $R$ be the radius of the large shaded circle (centre $C$) and $r=\dfrac {R}{5}$ the radius of the small circles.

\textbf{The large shaded circle is concentric with the outer circle.} A small circle is internally tangent to the outer circle, so its centre is at distance $7-r$ from $O$; it is externally tangent to the large shaded circle, so its centre is at distance $R+r$ from $C$. If $C\ne O$, the circle with centre $O$ and radius $7-r$ and the circle with centre $C$ and radius $R+r$ would meet in at most two points, so there could be at most two small circles. There are four, so $C=O$ and
\[7-r=R+r,\qquad\text{that is}\qquad R+2r=7 .\]
With $R=5r$ this gives $7r=7$, so $r=1$ and $R=5$.

\begin{asy}
usepackage("amsmath");
size(5.5cm);
defaultpen(fontsize(10pt));
fill(circle((0,0),5),lightgray);
for(int k=0;k<4;++k) fill(circle(6*dir(45+90*k),1),lightgray);
draw(circle((0,0),7),linewidth(0.9));
draw(circle((0,0),5));
for(int k=0;k<4;++k) draw(circle(6*dir(45+90*k),1));
pair O=(0,0);
draw(O--5*dir(45),blue+linewidth(1));
draw(5*dir(45)--7*dir(45),red+linewidth(1.2));
dot(O); dot(6*dir(45));
label("$O$",O,SW);
label("$R=5$",2.5*dir(45),SE,blue);
label("$2r=2$",7*dir(45),E,red);
draw(O--7*dir(160),dashed);
label("$7$",3.5*dir(160),N);
label("\mbox{}",(min(currentpicture,user=true)+max(currentpicture,user=true))/2,black); // keeps the text after the figure black when LaTeX typesets the labels inline
\end{asy}

(The centres of the small circles lie on the circle of radius $6$ about $O$; placed $90^\circ$ apart they are $6\sqrt2>2$ apart, so the four small circles do not overlap.)

The shaded area is $\pi\cdot5^2+4\cdot\pi\cdot1^2=29\pi$, and the outer circle has area $49\pi$. The shaded fraction is
\[\boxed{\dfrac{29}{49}}.\]
