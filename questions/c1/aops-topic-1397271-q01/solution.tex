Adding and subtracting the equations gives the equivalent system
\[
\alpha+\beta=2\alpha^2,\qquad \beta-\alpha=2\beta^2 .
\]
From the first, $\beta=2\alpha^2-\alpha=\alpha(2\alpha-1)$. Substituting into the second:
\[
2\alpha^2-2\alpha=2\alpha^2(2\alpha-1)^2,\quad\text{that is}\quad \alpha(\alpha-1)=\alpha^2(2\alpha-1)^2 .
\]
\begin{itemize}
\item If $\alpha=0$, then $\beta=0$, and $(0,0)$ is a solution.
\item If $\alpha\ne0$, divide by $\alpha$: $\alpha-1=\alpha(4\alpha^2-4\alpha+1)$, which simplifies to
\[
4\alpha^3-4\alpha^2+1=0 .
\]
\end{itemize}
Let $g(\alpha)=4\alpha^3-4\alpha^2+1$. Then $g'(\alpha)=12\alpha^2-8\alpha$ vanishes at $0$ and $\dfrac{2}{3}$, with $g(0)=1>0$ and $g\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)=\dfrac{11}{27}>0$; so $g$ is positive for all $\alpha\ge0$ and has exactly one real root $\alpha_0$, which is negative. Since $g(-0.5)=-0.5<0<g(-0.4)=0.104$, it lies between $-0.5$ and $-0.4$; numerically $\alpha_0\approx-0.41964$.

So there are exactly two real solutions:
\[
\boxed{(\alpha,\beta)=(0,0)\quad\text{and}\quad(\alpha_0,\,2\alpha_0^2-\alpha_0)\approx(-0.41964,\ 0.77184),}
\]
where $\alpha_0$ is the real root of $4\alpha^3-4\alpha^2+1=0$. (Over the complex numbers the other two roots of this cubic, $\approx0.70982\pm0.30315i$, give two more pairs in the same way.)
