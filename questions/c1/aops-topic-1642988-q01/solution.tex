Only the rolls showing $1$ or $6$ matter; call them \emph{relevant}. With probability $1$ there are infinitely many relevant rolls, and each relevant roll is a $1$ or a $6$ with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$ each, independently of the others (the other faces just delay things).

Adrian gets ten $1$s before his second $6$ exactly when, among the first $11$ relevant rolls, there is at most one $6$. (If there are at most one $6$ among them, at least ten $1$s come before any second $6$; if there are two or more $6$s among the first $11$ relevant rolls, the second $6$ arrives while there are at most nine $1$s.) Hence
\[
P=\dfrac{\dbinom{11}{0}+\dbinom{11}{1}}{2^{11}}=\dfrac{12}{2048}=\boxed{\dfrac{3}{512}}.
\]
