(a) An ellipse has an equation $Ax^2+Bxy+Cy^2+Dx+Ey+F=0$ with $B^2-4AC<0$, so $AC>0$ and in particular $C\ne0$. Substituting $y=x^2$ gives
\[Cx^4+Bx^3+(A+E)x^2+Dx+F=0,\]
a polynomial of degree exactly $4$, which has at most $4$ real roots. Each root $x$ gives one point $(x,x^2)$, so there are at most $4$ intersection points.

(b) Circles are ellipses, so it is enough to use circles $x^2+(y-k)^2=\rho^2$. On the parabola, $x^2=y$, so we need $y\ge0$ with $y+(y-k)^2=\rho^2$; each root $y>0$ gives two points and $y=0$ gives one.
\begin{itemize}
\item $0$ points: $x^2+(y+5)^2=1$ lies below the $x$-axis.
\item $1$ point: $x^2+(y+1)^2=1$ gives $y^2+3y=0$, so $y=0$ only: the origin.
\item $2$ points: $x^2+y^2=1$ gives $y^2+y-1=0$, with one positive root.
\item $3$ points: $x^2+(y-1)^2=1$ gives $y^2-y=0$: $y=0$ and $y=1$, i.e. $(0,0)$, $(\pm1,1)$.
\item $4$ points: $x^2+(y-2)^2=3$ gives $y^2-3y+1=0$, with two positive roots $\dfrac{3\pm\sqrt5}{2}$.
\end{itemize}

real y1=(3-sqrt(5))/2,y2=(3+sqrt(5))/2;
dot((sqrt(y1),y1)); dot((-sqrt(y1),y1)); dot((sqrt(y2),y2)); dot((-sqrt(y2),y2));
label("$y=x^2$",(1.9,3.6),E); label("$x^2+(y-2)^2=3$",(-sqrt(3),2),W);

\end{asy}
