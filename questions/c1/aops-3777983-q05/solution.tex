First place the two length-3 sides adjacent. Their shared diagonal cannot have length 6, since that would make a degenerate triangle. The other diagonal must be 6. In the triangle with sides 3 and 5 adjacent to that diagonal, the opposite angle has cosine $\dfrac{9+25-36}{30} = - \dfrac{1}{15}$. The supplementary angle between sides 3 and $x$ therefore has cosine $\dfrac{1}{15}$, so $36 = 9 + x^2 - \dfrac{2 x}{5}$. The positive solution is $x = \dfrac{27}{5}$. For the other essentially different order, $3,5,3,x$, the quadrilateral is an isosceles trapezoid and its diagonals have squared length $3^2+5x=36$. Thus it also works, proving $\boxed{x = \dfrac{27}{5}}$.
