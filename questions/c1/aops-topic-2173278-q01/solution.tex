Let the intended number be $10a+b$ and the entered number $10c+b$ (same units digit $b$, tens digits $a\ne c$, with $a,c\ge1$). Then
\[(10c+b)^2-(10a+b)^2=\left(10c-10a\right)\left(10c+10a+2b\right)=20(c-a)\left(5(a+c)+b\right)=2340,\]
so $(c-a)\left(5(a+c)+b\right)=117=3^2\cdot13$. The result increased, so $c>a$, and $1\le a<c\le9$ gives $1\le c-a\le8$; also $5(a+c)+b\le5\cdot17+9=94$. Among the factorizations $1\cdot117$, $3\cdot39$, $9\cdot13$, $13\cdot9$, $39\cdot3$, $117\cdot1$, only $c-a=3$, $5(a+c)+b=39$ fits. Since $a+c$ has the same parity as $c-a$, it is odd, so $a+c=7$ and $b=4$ (the other option, $a+c=6$ with $b=9$, has the wrong parity). Then $a=2$ and $c=5$.
So she meant $24$ and entered $54$; indeed $54^2-24^2=2916-576=2340$. The requested sum is $24+54=\boxed{78}$.

(Shortcut: the sum of the two numbers is $10(a+c)+2b=2\left(5(a+c)+b\right)$, which is $2\cdot39=78$.)
