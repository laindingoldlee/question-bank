Write $n$ in base $3$. Dividing by $3$ and rounding down deletes the last base-$3$ digit, so the terms of the sequence are $n$ with its last $0,1,2,\dots$ digits removed. A positive number is divisible by $3$ exactly when its last base-$3$ digit is $0$. Hence $n$ is lucky if and only if \emph{no base-$3$ digit of $n$ is $0$}, i.e.\ all digits are $1$ or $2$.

Now $1000=729+243+27+1=1101001_3$, a $7$-digit number.
\begin{itemize}
\item Lucky numbers with $1$ to $6$ base-$3$ digits: $2+4+8+16+32+64=126$, all less than $3^6=729\le1000$.
\item $7$-digit lucky numbers are at least $1111111_3>1101001_3=1000$ (they agree in the first two digits, and the third digit $1$ exceeds $0$). So there are none up to $1000$.
\end{itemize}
The number of lucky numbers up to $1000$ is $\boxed{126}$.
